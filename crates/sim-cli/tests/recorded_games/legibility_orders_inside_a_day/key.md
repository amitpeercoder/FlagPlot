# Answer key: legibility of orders inside a day, condition 6 of decision 0156's proof

Written by the team lead of the proof from each case's material alone (`cases.json`, `material`: the number, pulse, kind and words of each event of the seat's day 1), before any reader ran. Where the key states something the material does not show, it says so. The order actually given is in `cases.json` (`order_given`) for the record; it is not in the material and not in this key's answers, except where noted as "not in the material".

The three questions each reader was asked: (i) when the order given inside the day was given, 0600 or 1200; (ii) in which pulse it acted; (iii) what it changed.

How the material tells it. An order given inside a day is taken in at the start of the pulse that follows (decision 0156, rule 4), so it is the one `order_accepted` whose pulse is `morning` or `afternoon`; the others, with pulse `null`, are the orders of dusk taken in as the day opened. Accepted in the morning, it was given at 0600; in the afternoon, at 1200. The words of an `order_accepted` name only the order's number and its unit, never what was ordered, so (iii) is read from the events that follow it.
