# The player's account

A Haiku model, playing alone on Nov 12, 2026 with `sim-cli play` and nothing else. It was also asked to say why things happened and what it would do with one more day, as the legibility test. Its report as it gave it.

Eastern Solomons (allied seat), played with target/debug/sim-cli play only. The game ended after one day. Final result: Allied 1.55 points, minor defeat (margin -3.6). Japanese 5.15 points, minor victory (margin 3.6).

1. **Understanding.** Objectives: sink or cripple Japanese carriers, and keep my own carriers in action. Points: sunk carrier 10, crippled carrier 5, aircraft destroyed 0.05 each. At the start the briefing said "Day 1 of 1". I read that as the whole game being one day, but I did not plan for it. I had no contacts, no enemy bases were listed, and my two carrier forces (task_force:1 with Enterprise, task_force:2 with Saratoga) were idle at (7,-5). Henderson Field (base:1) had no fuel or supply.

2. **Decisions.** A probe order with range 1000 was refused, and the refusal named the limit of 200 nm for the dive bombers. For day 1 I ordered: air_group:2 (VB-6) search north half (270 to 90); air_group:3 (VS-5) search 90 to 270; air_group:6 (VB-3) search 0 to 180; air_group:7 (VS-3) search 180 to 0; all at 200 nm. Fighters air_group:1 and air_group:5 on combat air patrol. No carrier moved, and no strike mission was set. Reason given: no enemy known, so search the whole sea around both carriers.
   My main mistake: I did not set naval_strike on the scout groups or on the strike groups. The strike groups (air_group:4 and air_group:8, the torpedo groups) stayed on rest. I should have set naval_strike before the day so that a sighting in a daylight pulse could launch a strike in that pulse, as the tool text describes.

3. **What happened.** In the afternoon pulse air_group:7 (VS-3) sighted task_force:4 at 200 nm (event 19, chance 0.477, sighted). That produced contact:1, "4 ships estimated, taken for battleship", at (7,-10) (event 20). Japanese raids came in on task_force:1: air_group:12 (12 aircraft) and air_group:15 (15 aircraft) attacked Enterprise (event 29 and others). Enterprise took 6 hits and lost its flight deck to out; air_group:1 lost 3 fighters shot down. Enterprise was counted as a crippled carrier for the Japanese side (5 points). Allied points came only from 31 aircraft destroyed.
   explain on event 20 showed the search chance and its breakdown (aircraft 15, sweep 20 nm, arc 180, distance 200). explain on event 31 showed the hits came from air_group:12 attacking ship:1. explain on event 36 showed the claim of 30 aircraft shot down was built from real shot-down 18, fighter misses 15, false claim chance 0.1, gun misses 118 and false claims 12. The breakdowns were the most useful part.

4. **What I believed before the end.** I believed my search had found the enemy and that I had a contact I could not yet strike. I had no clear picture of whether I had achieved anything. The final result showed no ships sunk for either side, 31 aircraft destroyed counted for the Allies, and 51 claimed. The claim figure is not what scores.

5. **With one more day** I would order: naval_strike on air_group:2, 3, 6 and 7 (the scouts have 200 nm reach, so the strike must be set before the contact appears), and naval_strike on air_group:4 and 8 once a contact is held. I would also keep the fighters on patrol over Enterprise. I would expect a strike to launch in the afternoon pulse on contact:1 and possibly hit the battleship or a carrier. This is a guess; the tools did not let me test it.

6. **Confusing or missing.** (a) The scenario's "Day 1 of 1" was easy to miss and nothing warned that the game was one day long. (b) The history text said "Ryujo was sunk", while the objectives counted 0 ships sunk and 1 carrier crippled. The crippled carrier is Enterprise, counted for the Japanese side; this took some reasoning to see. (c) The contact was labelled "taken for battleship" with no explanation of how that was judged. (d) The enemy_bases list was empty, so there was no way to see the Japanese base positions. (e) The first refused order (range 1000) gave a clear fix. (f) Nothing in the briefing told me which pulse would be the last daylight one for a naval strike.

7. **Rules.** I used only target/debug/sim-cli play. I did not read or search any file, did not build, did not use the web, and did not use --export. Every issue_orders call had a reason. I played until end_day said game_over was true, then read the final briefing. Nothing else was done.
