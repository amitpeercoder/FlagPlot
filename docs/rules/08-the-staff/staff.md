# The staff: The staff, days of supply, levels and addressees

Part of chapter 8, [The staff](README.md). What a staff is and when it is asked, the check of its orders; days of supply; a doctrine at a level; a task force as an addressee.

## The staff

### What a staff is, and when it is asked

A staff is code outside `sim-core`. `sim-core` defines what it is: given a side's view, it decides the day and hands in a `StaffDecisions`:

- **Orders**, each with the intent it serves and its reason.
- **Staff commands**: the commands of the staff's column above, each with its reason.
- **Decisions to give no order**, each a reason.
- **Notices**: structured statements beside these, each resting on one of the reasons above (Notices, below).

A staff is a function of the view: it is asked with a shared reference, so that a staff asked twice on the same view gives the same answer. That is what `replay --check-staff` tests. The function that resolves a day takes each side's staff or none. A side with none is not asked, and a day with no staff and no command is resolved as it was. [0107 items 4, 7 and 8]

A staff is asked once a day, at its place in the day's order (chapter 1, Before the pulses): after the commander's commands are taken in, after forces back from the yard are home, and after the override has taken in the commander's orders to units in a forces limit and held them, and before any other order is taken in. It gets the side's view as it then stands, so it sees the reserves, the leaves and the holds set that day, the orders that held a unit that morning, and the fuel ships and bases held at the end of the day before: nothing is refuelled before the staff is asked (chapter 2, Refuelling in port). It never gets the truth (chapter 1, Truth and belief). [0107, The order of a day]

What a replay hands in, in place of asking, is taken in at the same step as if a staff had given it. If a side has a live staff and something handed in for it, both are taken in, what was handed in first.

The staff's commands are taken in first, each side's in the order handed in, and after each side's commands its decisions to give no order are recorded, and then its notices (Notices, below). When every side's are done, the interrupts are tested (Interrupts, above). Then every order of the day that the override has not already taken in is taken in: the commander's and the staff's together (chapter 1, How orders are numbered and sorted). A staff's order comes after the commander's to the same unit, so the staff's replaces the commander's in a standing slot. A staff's order is marked with the issuer staff and the intent it serves (chapter 1, Who can issue), and is refused as held if it is for something the commander holds (Holds and the override, above). An air group's order serves the cover intent, and not the hold intent above it. A cargo order the staff gives heeds the reserve of a base (chapter 2, Cargo orders). [0107, 0111]

Every runner gives both sides the staff of `sim-staff` (`sim_staff::staffs()`): the doctrine for keeping a base supplied and, after it, the air doctrine for holding a place and covering a base from the air (below). A staff whose side has no intent in force and no commitment to free hands in nothing: nothing is taken in and nothing is saved for that day. [0107, 0115, 0117]

### The forces limit on a staff's orders

A staff's order that is not refused as held (Holds and the override, above) is refused as **outside the forces limit** ("the order is for a unit outside the forces limit of the intent it serves") unless the intent the order serves is in force and:

- for an order to an air group, the group is one the intent may use (The six limits, above): named in every forces limit on its chain, or, with no forces limit on it or above it, at the base it is addressed to, or, for an intent addressed to a task force, aboard that force's ships with every forces limit naming it or having the force inside it; or
- for an order to a land unit, the unit is one the intent may use (decision 0141; Readying a landing, and building up a base, below): named in every forces limit on its chain, or, with no forces limit on it or above it, for a "build up a base", a working unit of the side with men ashore in its base's hex; or, for a "hold a place" with no land unit named in any forces limit on its chain, a fighting unit of the side with men ashore in its base's hex (decision 0144, 4); or
- for an order to a submarine, the intent is a patrol in force and the boat is one it may use (Patrolling waters with submarines, below): named in every forces limit on its chain that names a boat, or, with none naming a boat, any boat of the side's own not sunk (decision 0218, 12 and 34); no other intent's order to a boat is inside; or
- for an order to a task force that the intent is addressed to (decision 0124, 6), the order is for that force, whatever forces limit stands on its chain or above; or
- for any other order, the intent has a forces limit on it or above it that binds the ships (decision 0222, rule 2), and against every such limit on its chain the order is for a task force that the limit lists, or that has at least one ship and every ship of it is a ship the limit lists, or it is the source base's order to form a task force from ships that are in the pool, the source of every limit on the chain that binds the source.

On the chain of a "take a place" each forces limit is read as it binds (Taking a place, With no forces named, item 8): the end's is the commander's for each of the task forces, the land units and the source he named and the staff's choice for each he did not, and a limit under it reads from the end what it does not name of the three. On the chain of an island the same: each limit above an intent binds only what it names of the task forces (with ships), the land units and the source, and the narrowing check of decision 0149 is used under an island too; an island's own limit fills what it does not name from the nearest limit beneath it, and with none beneath it is read as set; and a limit that names no air group does not bind the air groups (Controlling an island, Forces limits under an island). [0149 item 3; 0151 item 4]

A base order other than forming is inside no forces limit, so a staff's order for one is refused. The check does not look at the plan's state or at the area. A refused order is numbered and kept, and recorded as refused with this reason, and nothing else about it is checked. An order that passes is checked as any order is (chapter 1, Every reason an order is refused). [0107, As built]

The check binds the orders a staff hands in. It does not bind an order that a scenario's script gives with the issuer `staff`: that order is a scenario's statement, set down by the scenario's author as a starting condition, and not a decision of a staff made on a view. Scenario 39 is such a scenario. Such an order is checked as any scripted order is. [0107, As built]

A staff command that names ships is checked against the pool and the holds too (`commit_ships`, `add_run`; above). [0107]

### A unit before its day

Before the day from which its side may use it (chapter 1, A day from which a unit may be used), the staff leaves a unit out: it is in no pool, it is no force a task may use, and no doctrine gives it an order. Every doctrine reads the day through one test, the side view's `may_use`, for the day the staff gives orders for. The places that read it: the pool of a forces limit (a ship before its day is in no pool, so no run commits it and nothing is loaded on it), the air groups an intent may use (so the cover, the raid, the strike, the search of waters, the neutralising and the denial of an area use none before its day), the groups aboard a carrier force, a free escort force, a force a surface force that stands by night is formed from, and an intent addressed to a task force. The staff still reads a unit before its day where it reads its own side for anything but choosing what to order. [0140 item 3]

A reason that would have used a unit names its day, in one wording, "U may be used from day N". An intent addressed to a task force that may not be used yet gets no order to the force or to the groups aboard, and one reason: that it is to be served by the force, which may be used from day N. A pool whose every ship waits for its day is not a failed plan: one reason names each ship with its day, and nothing is sent. Where a reason lists what a task may use, a unit left out for its day is named with its day. A forces limit is the commander's, and may name a unit before its day; intake still refuses an order to it with the day, from a staff as from the commander. [0140 items 2 and 3]

**A unit held apart.** A unit of the side's own that is held apart until the day a scenario gives it (`enters`; chapter 1, A unit not yet entered) is not on the map, and the staff reads it as one that may be used from its day: `may_use` is false for it, and its day is the day it enters (or, where it waits for its base, the day after the last day resolved), the later with its own `available_from`. It is in no pool, it is no force a task may use, and no doctrine gives it an order, until it has entered. It is counted as coming, as a unit before its day is (the judgement of more is coming, above; decision 0193): every reader of the staff that asks about the future takes what is held apart as well as what is on the map, namely the units a take counts as its own to use (a task force or a land unit the forces limit names, or of a kind it does not name every transport force but a force with an oiler, A force with an oiler is no lift, below, or every fighting unit, from the entry that holds it), the lifts and the units that "more is coming" counts (a held transport force with fighting men aboard is a lift from its day, read from its entry; a fighting unit held apart is more coming where it enters at the source and its day is in time), and the later dates of a trial (chapter 9). Without this a staff would conclude that nothing more is coming while a division waits to enter. A reason for a force or a unit held apart says it in one wording, "U enters on day N" (the unit named and the day it enters), and no order is given for it: an intent addressed to a task force that is held apart gets no order to the force or the groups aboard, and one reason; a first lift held apart is not lost, and no later lift goes before it has entered; a unit held apart that is out of time is named in the reasons with its day, as a unit before its day is. A forces limit is the commander's and may name a unit held apart; intake refuses an order to it, from a staff as from the commander (chapter 1, A unit not yet entered). A unit that has entered is a unit like any other for the staff. The staff reads only the side's own entries: the enemy's are not reachable through its view. [0195, rules 8 and 13; 0193]

A side sees: its own acceptance or refusal of the order whole, with the reason. [0107, 0124 item 6]

### Reasons

Every order a staff gives and every decision it makes to give none has a **reason**. A reason has five parts:

| Part | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| Serves | The intent, and the plan and the amendment the decision was made under, with the intent's statement: the intent in words as the commander set it and as it stood that day ("keep base:2 (Placeholder base T) at 30 days of supply, a run every 15 days"; "no interval set (the staff takes 15 days)" where he set none; "withdrawn on day N" where it was). The amendment is none for the baseline. Then the **level** that decided (A doctrine belongs to a level, below). Then, for an intent with a parent, the chain: the intents above it, nearest first, each with its number, its maker and its statement as it stood (The intent tree, above) |
| Evidence | Each thing the side held that mattered, in words, with its day |
| Binding | The limit, stock, distance or commitment that shaped the decision, in words |
| Worked | The calculation, in words |
| Would change | What, if different, would give a different decision |

**The limits it speaks of** (decision 0222, rule 5). A reason ends with a list of the limits its binding or its would-change name: each its kind (forces, area, priority, protected, reserve, risk, not before, wait for, after, knowledge, targets, or the source of a forces limit) and the intent it is set on, the nearest on the chain that sets it (for the risk, the one whose risk binds), or the root of the chain where none sets it, the order the commander would set it on; a reserve names its base. It changes no order, no command and no hash; it is recorded with the reason and shown wherever the reason is, and a game saved before it reads its reasons with none. The words are unchanged by it. [0222, rule 5]

A reason is handed in with its order, with its staff command, or with the decision to give no order, and is recorded as a `StaffReason` event. For an order, the event cites the order and the event that accepted or refused it, and is recorded after them. For a staff command it has no order, holds the command as handed in, and cites the command's `CommandTaken` or `CommandRefused`, and is recorded after it and its follow-ups. For a decision to give no order it has no order and no cause. [0107; differences.md row 43] **A reason is kept, not worked out again**: it stays in the log whatever the intent has since become, so it still resolves after its intent is replaced or withdrawn. Its number is the number of its event: a reason has no number of its own. [0107 items 4 and 12]

The staff writes the intent's statement into every reason it hands in, from the side's command state that morning, so the words travel with the reason: every place a reason is shown (the tools `why`, `staff_orders`, `propose`, `preview` and `report`, the replay export and viewer, `run --events` and `sim-cli events`) gives the same words, and a reason made before its intent was changed or withdrawn still says what the intent was then. Where its worked part speaks of a plan with no base in it (a leave given or withheld, a plan ended, a run ended or set aside, a commitment made again), it names the plan with what it is for: "plan:1, the plan to keep base:2 (Placeholder base T) at 30 days of supply,". The statement of a hold intent reads "hold base:1 (name)" and of a cover intent "cover base:1 (name) from the air", of a neutralise intent "neutralise base:9 (name)" and of a raid "raid base:9 (name) from base:1 (name)", of a deny intent "deny the waters within 4 hexes of (q, r)", of a strike-ships task "strike ships from base:1 (name)" and of a search-waters task "search the waters from base:1 (name)", with ", withdrawn on day N" where it was. The statement of every intent above the one served is in the reason's chain, with its maker (The intent tree, above). A game saved before the statement was handed in reads its reasons with none. In the log's own line a reason reads "staff's reason for order N", "staff's reason for the command to ..." or "staff's reason for giving no order", then the serves part in brackets, then the worked part; a count of one there and in a command's own line is "1 ship", not "1 ships". [0107, "As built (step 4)", the mend]

The engine stores what the staff writes in a reason and checks none of it. Evidence names a side's own ships freely and the enemy only as a contact, a claim or an estimate (chapter 3, Belief): that is the staff's to keep, and a staff is given nothing else to name. [0107, truth and belief]

A side sees: its own `StaffReason` whole. The enemy sees none. [0107]

### Notices

A staff hands in notices beside its orders, commands and reasons: that it cannot meet a plan's intent, that a plan waits for a leave, that it has ended a run, that a carrier force asks leave to pursue a find. A notice is structured, so that a standing interrupt can read it without reading words. It has a kind, the plan, the run where the kind is about one (a run ended; else none) and the reason it rests on, which is one of the same hand-in's staff commands or decisions to give no order. [0107, ruling 34]

| Kind | Means | The doctrine hands it in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cannot_meet` | The staff cannot meet the plan's intent | On a morning it weighs a new run for the plan and either can have none, or the run it can have leaves the reading it checks below the floor (The doctrine, Cannot meet). It rests on the day's reason for giving no order about the next run, or, for a cut run, on the reason of the `add_run`. A load cut that still leaves the reading at or above the floor is no notice. For a cover intent, on a morning no fighter group it may order can fly (Covering a base from the air, When it cannot); it rests on that morning's reason for giving no order. For a neutralise intent, on a morning no base qualifies (Neutralising a base, above); for a raid, on a morning it cannot be met (Raiding a base, above); each rests on that morning's reason for giving no order. For a take-a-place intent at preserve the force, on the first morning the ships can carry fewer men than are wanted and no day in reach brings them (Taking a place, Waits for its men), or on the first morning its landing is ready and the check that the place can be held fails, or on the first morning its landing is ready and nothing it may use can look at the place for the count the risk asks for (The need to know), once for the plan (Taking a place, below) |
| `waits_for_leave_to_prepare` | The plan is a draft and waits for the commander's leave to prepare | On a morning the plan is a draft and leave to prepare is withheld, for a supply plan and for the plan of a hold, cover, neutralise or raid root. It rests on the reason that says so |

[0107, ruling 35]

The three notices of a pursuit have no run, name the plan of the order the force serves, and carry what they say as the force, that order and, for the asking and the pursuit, the find; the other notices carry none of it. A notice of a pursuit is recorded after the reason it rests on, which is one of the staff's decisions to give no order. The engine records each notice as a `StaffNotice` event, naming the side, the kind, the plan and the run, with no pulse; a `met` notice of a plan whose intent is a "take a place" names that intent too, and says its words. It is recorded after the staff's commands and the decisions to give no order of the side, in the order handed in, and cites the `StaffReason` it rests on, so a notice leads to its reason and the reason to the evidence. A notice that rests on nothing the hand-in holds is recorded with no cause. The engine tests the standing interrupts on the notices and reads nothing of a reason's words. A notice changes no state, with the exceptions of decision 0220 (a `follows` notice writes the order's kept state, a `contact_lost` notice sets the order lost, and a `force_not_found` notice withdraws the order and what stands beneath it) and two of decision 0214: a `pursues` notice begins the force's pursuit, and a `pursuit_ended` notice ends it (Operating a carrier force, items 33 and 37), and the event of the notice is the event of the change. [0107, ruling 34]

A side sees: its own `StaffNotice`, whole. The enemy sees none. [0107]

## Days of supply

Days of supply is a side's supply stock in a hex over its planned use of supply there for a day. It is worked by `SideView::days_of_supply(hex)` from the side's view only: its own bases, land units, forces, ships and air groups, the map and the figures. It is nothing of the enemy's, changes nothing and draws no random number. [0107 items 31 to 36]

**Where.** It is for the side in a hex, not for a base. A hex where the side holds no base has none. Every base of the side in a hex carries the same figure, because the men of a side are fed from every base it has there. An intent that names a base means its hex. [0107 item 31]

**Stock.** The supply of all the side's bases in the hex, in tons. [0107 item 32]

**Planned use.** The tons the side is planned to spend there on the coming day, in five parts. A part whose figures the scenario does not load is 0, as the rule that charges it charges nothing:

| Part | What | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Men ashore | Each of the side's land units with men ashore in the hex, at its daily charge for its men | Chapter 7, The men's supply |
| Men due | The same charge for men aboard the side's own forces that are ordered to unload in the hex and are due on or before the day, or were due earlier and have not landed | Chapter 7, Landing men. A force is due on the day its passage to the hex ends, forecast from the view as the measuring tool forecasts it. A force already stopped in the hex counts when its unload can work there and men are still aboard |
| Aircraft upkeep | The daily supply for each ready and each damaged aircraft of the groups at the side's bases in the hex | Chapter 5, Daily supply; `air_supply.toml: daily_supply_t_per_aircraft` |
| Repairs | For each base, the smaller of its repairs a day (its figure, and the repairs the work done on aircraft the day before gives it) and the damaged aircraft at it, times the supply of a repair. Counted only where the air upkeep figures are loaded | Chapter 5, Repair; `air_supply.toml: repair_supply_t` |
| Sorties | The sorties of the groups at its bases there that are on a mission that flies whatever is sighted, at each of the day's two daylight pulses: a search group with an arc set, at the cost of a sortie of twice its arc's range, for the aircraft able to fly; a group on combat air patrol, at the cost of a sortie of no distance, for the fighters that fly its patrol share of the aircraft able to fly | Chapter 5, Supply for sorties and What a group can fly |

**A strike mission counts nothing.** Whether a strike group flies depends on what is sighted, and a figure that moved with enemy reports would move for no change in supply. A group at rest counts nothing, and a group aboard a ship is counted at no base. Each part is worked by the engine's own rule for it and by no second formula. [0107 items 32 to 34, rulings 1 and 2]

**The figure.** The stock over the planned use, a fixed-point quotient cut toward zero at six decimals. **No planned use, no figure**: where the planned use is 0 there is no figure, and the answer says so in words ("No supply is planned to be used in this hex today, so there is no days-of-supply figure"). The stock is still given. [0107 item 36]

**When it is read.** At the step where a staff is asked: at the start of a day, after the commands, the returns and the override and before the day's orders are taken in. Read between days it gives the same figure, since nothing between the end of one day and that step changes a stock or a use. An order given today shows in the figure of tomorrow: a group set to patrol today raises the planned use read the next day. [0107 item 31, ruling 3]

**The projection** of the figure over the coming days, and the first day it falls below an intent's floor, are the doctrine's (below, The doctrine). The engine gives it what it counts by its own rule: `SideView::men_due_by_day(hex)`, each force due in the hex with its due day and its men's charge, the rule the men-due part above uses. [0107 items 34 and 35]

A side sees: its own figure, with its stock, its planned use and the five parts, in the agent's `list_bases` and `get_entity` (as `days_of_supply`) and in the briefing's entry for each of its bases (as `planned_days_of_supply`). The briefing's entry for a base also keeps its older `days_of_supply`, which is a different figure: the stock over yesterday's spending, cut to one decimal (glossary, Days of supply). The enemy sees none. [0107 item 47]

## A doctrine belongs to a level

With command levels on, a doctrine reads the picture of the command its intent is addressed to: the area command, a base or a task force (chapter 1, Commands and their pictures). Without report figures every picture is the side's, so this changes nothing a staff does; with them a picture holds what its command held first or was passed, and a staff reason that names a contact names the command whose picture it is (chapter 1). [0134, 0135]

Each doctrine of the staff belongs to one level, the addressee of the intents it serves (The intent tree, above). A doctrine of a higher level forms sub-intents for the level below and gives no order to a unit and sets no setting of one; a doctrine of the lowest level it reads gives the orders. [0120 items 1 to 4]

1. **The level of each doctrine.** Keeping a base supplied is the area's, and gives orders itself (stage 1 built it so, and nothing here changes it). The neutralise doctrine (Neutralising a base, below) is the area's and forms raid tasks, which it gives no order for. Holding a place, covering a base from the air and raiding a base are the base's. Denying an area is the area's and forms strike-ships and search-waters tasks, which it gives no order for, and, for each task force its forces limit names, a stand task or a carrier task; striking ships from a base and searching waters from a base are the base's. Holding a place, at the base's level, forms the same tasks for the forces its forces limit names. Standing at a hex and operating a carrier force are the task force's: its doctrine gives the force and the air groups aboard it their orders, and is the lowest level to give any order to a ship. [0120 item 4; 0123; 0124 items 6 to 8; 0125]
2. **A reason says which level decided.** A reason's serves part holds the level: the addressee of the intent it serves, written "decided at the level of the area" or "decided at the level of base:1", after the intent, plan and amendment and before the chain. It is filled for every reason a staff hands in, from the intent served, so a reason of stage 1 says "decided at the level of the area" too. It is text: no order is changed by it, and the engine never reads it. A game saved before it was handed in reads each reason with none. [0120 item 2]
3. **An air group is a level too.** Below the base is the air group. No intent is given to a group: its level is `Addressee::AirGroup`, and the only reason that names it is the reason of the strike sizing order a strike-ships task gives (Striking ships from a base, above). That reason says "decided at the level of air_group:N" in place of the level of the base, because the group decides the size of its own strikes from the task it is under. A hash and a replay carry it as the other levels. It is text: no order is changed by it. [0123 item 9]
4. **A task force is a level too,** and the one a reason names in "decided at the level of task_force:N" for a stand task or a carrier task. A hash and a replay carry it as the other levels. [0124 item 6]
5. **The picture is still the side's.** A side has one picture and every level reads it. No doctrine reads less of it for being at a lower level. [0120 item 3]
6. **The state of a unit is read at its level.** With report figures, a doctrine addressed to the area (keeping a base supplied, neutralise, deny, and the forming of a force's stand task or carrier task) reads the side's task forces, bases and what they hold through the area's view: as last reported to the area command (chapter 1, Commands and their pictures, State reports). Its forecasts of passages and fuel start from what is held, and a reason says "(as of the morning of day 1)" where the state it rests on is older than the pulse just ended. A doctrine of a base or a task force reads its own units exactly and the units of other commands as the side's view gives them. An order of the area's doctrine that the truth refuses where the held state would have accepted it is refused with "; the area command held ..." and counted apart from the staff's faults. [0136]

## A task force as an addressee

The task force is a level that an intent is addressed to, and three kinds of task are given to it: standing at a hex by night, operating a carrier force, and bombarding a base (below; the third is formed under neutralise and take a place, Bombarding a base). The staff forms them, at the level above, from the forces limit. This section is the rules of being addressed and of forming; each task's own doctrine follows. It draws no random number. [0124 items 6 to 8; 0125 items 1 and 2]

**What it commands.** An intent addressed to a task force of the side may use that force's ships and nothing else, and the air groups aboard those ships where every forces limit on its chain names the group or has the force inside it. The addressee has code 4 and holds the force's number. Two things follow from it:

- A staff's order to the force is inside the forces limit of the intent it serves, whatever limits stand above the intent (The forces limit on a staff's orders, above).
- A commander's order to a force an intent in force is addressed to holds it, as an order to a force in a forces limit does (Holds and the override, above).

**Forming.** Under a deny or a hold intent in force whose plan is released (and, for the carrier task alone, under a "take a place", the third parent, decision 0148), the doctrine of that level forms a task for each task force named in the nearest forces limit on the intent's chain (its own, for a root), in task force number order, where no task of that kind for that force is in force anywhere on the side, whoever made it and wherever it stands in the side's trees (a commander's own task stated as a root counts):

- a **stand task** for a force on the surface combat mission with a ship afloat, at the end's hex: the centre of the waters under a deny intent, the base's hex under a hold intent;
- a **carrier task** for a force on the carrier mission with a carrier afloat that has an air group aboard, at the hex the force is in that morning under a hold intent: the staff does not choose where a carrier force named in a hold's forces limit works, and the reason says it works from where it lies. Under a commander's deny or neutralise, since decision 0222, a carrier force his forces limit names is stationed by the carrier pass as a free one is, unless the limit says `in_place`, when its task is formed here at the hex where it lies (ruling I), (Carriers serving an order for an end, below), and no task is formed for it here; a task of another's making that stands for it (the commander's own, stated as a root) is said as it stands and serves the end. A commander who wants the force at a hex states the task himself, as a root. Under a "take a place" the staff does choose, and forms it at the covering hex (Operating a carrier force, Under take a place, below), on the morning the plan is released or, to look at the place, while it is preparing (item 25 there).

With no task force named by the commander in a forces limit, no such task is formed. A force on any other mission is given no task. The staff hands in a `form_intent` command and a reason for each task it forms; where a task stands it hands in a decision to give no order that names the task, who made it and where it stands, and forms no other. So a force has one task of each kind, and no group is ordered twice for one force. Under a deny intent this runs after the bases' tasks, and a task formed or standing counts as serving the end; under a hold intent it runs first. A task formed is a sub-intent as any is (The intent tree, above), and a commander may state either kind himself as a root: it then needs a plan of its own, made ready and released by the leaves. [0124 items 6 to 8; 0125 items 1 and 2; the architect's ruling of Nov 16, 2026, made on the loading check of the second proof (decision 0128)]

A side sees: its own `IntentFormed`, `StaffReason` and the `CommandTaken` of the command, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0124]
