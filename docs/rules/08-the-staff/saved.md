# The staff: What is saved, what is not built

Part of chapter 8, [The staff](README.md). What a saved game keeps of command state and the staff, and replay --check-staff; what is not built; what is not in this chapter.

## What is saved

A game is saved as it has always been, as what was handed in, and from version 3 of the saved game it holds what step 1 of stage 1 adds (chapter 1, What a saved game holds); version 4, since step 2 of stage 2, adds the intent tree to the commands and the hand-ins (below): the commander's commands by day, and each side's staff hand-in by day, whole. A hand-in is the orders with their intent and reason, the staff's commands and the decisions to give no order with their reasons, and, since step 4, the notices (each its kind's name, its plan, its run or none, and the reason it rests on, as the number of a command or of a decision to give no order of the same hand-in). A hand-in with no notice is written with no `notices` key, as it always was, and a hand-in saved without the key reads as having none; the saved game is still version 3. Only the days and sides that handed in something are kept. The saved game also records the doctrine version beside the rules label. Version 4 writes in a command `set_intent` with `intent_kind` (`cover` or `hold`; left out for keeping a base supplied), the staff's `form_intent` (`parent`, `intent_kind`, `base`), a forces limit with `air_groups` and a `source` that may be left out, and a hold or release of a whole air group (an `air_group` and no `setting`), and in a reason's `serves` the `chain`. A saved game of version 3 or before is refused by the reader, not converted. It holds no command state: the state is rebuilt by taking the commands in again, the commander's `hold` and `release` among them, and the staff's `end_run` among its hand-in, and a commander's `set_interrupt` and `acknowledge` among the commands. It holds no interrupt's status: it is rebuilt by taking the commands in and testing each morning again, and a replay hands the saved notices in as the staff gave them. It holds no override either: the commander's orders are saved as handed in, and the override is taken again when a replay takes them in at step 2. [0107 items 9, 11 and 12]

**Replay.** A replay hands the saved orders, the saved commands and, before each day, the saved hand-in of each side into the engine (`World::submit_staff`), and asks no staff. So a replay of a staffed game works when the doctrine has since changed, and gives every daily hash that was saved. [0107 item 9]

**`replay --check-staff`.** It asks the doctrine's staff as well. On each saved day, each side that the doctrine gives a staff is asked on the view the engine then holds, and what it gives is compared part by part, orders, then commands, then decisions to give no order, then notices, with what was saved. The saved hand-in is what is handed in, whatever the staff gave, so the replay is the saved game and its hashes are still compared. The run stops at the first day and side where they differ and says what differs. A saved hand-in for a side the doctrine gives no staff is a difference too. A difference in the staff is reported before a hash that differs on the same day. If there is none, it says so, naming the doctrine now and the one the game was saved under. It is the test that a staff is a function of the view. [0107 item 10]

## Not built

- **A hold of the commander on a base,** and holds on a setting other than a search arc: they wait for a stage whose doctrine acts on a base or changes a setting. (A hold intent is an intent, and is built.) [0107, rulings 17 and 30]
- **Interrupts of other kinds, and the withdrawal of a numbered one.** The one set kind is a base's days of supply below a number. A numbered interrupt cannot be withdrawn, only set again with another base and number. [0107, ruling 33]
- **A notice on every day the staff weighs no new run.** A cannot-meet notice is handed in only when the staff weighs a new run for the plan: none is handed in for an intent with no forces limit, a base that is not the side's, a pool with no ship afloat or one whose every ship afloat is protected for another intent, a plan that has ended, or a day a run of the plan is still loading or waits to sail. Those days give a reason, or a plan marked failed, and no notice. A preview carries no notice. A run-ended notice is handed in only for a run set aside for a hold: a run of a cancelled plan, which the staff reads as finished, has none. [0107, ruling 35]
- **A second source base** for an intent. [0107 item 19]
- **The recall of a run, an intent for fuel and a phase of a plan**: stage 2. Escort of a run is built (Escorting and covering a run); how many escorts a run is given, an escort sized to the threat, timing a run to the dark and air cover of a run are not. [0107; 0126, What it leaves out]
- **Bombers and fighters sent as escort on purpose** (the strike intent), and a hex of sea as the subject of an intent other than a station. The cover doctrine orders air groups at a base and no ship; the ships that stand at a base's hex or in the waters to deny are the stand task's (Standing at a hex by night, below). A raid is built (Raiding a base), and so are the strike of ships that come within reach and the size of a strike decided at the group's level (decision 0123). Escort as a task of its own is not. [0115, What it leaves for later steps; 0120; 0124]
- **Part of a search group flown,** and searching where nothing is known but something is feared (the area limit's to say). The arc points at what is known. [0115]
- **Sending a share of a search group,** weighed and left out: a group that searches on the days its fatigue allows was enough for stage 2's proof. A neutralise intent whose target is no longer an enemy base on the side's map gives a reason and forms nothing, and the staff does not withdraw it. [0120]
- **A land formation as the addressee of an intent,** a commander taking a staff-made intent over (decision 0232, What it leaves out), the staff withdrawing a sub-intent because what it needs has gone (the cannot-meet notice serves), a ranking of siblings stated and tested beyond the one kind of sub-intent built, and friction between the levels (a delay in passing an order down, a subordinate's narrower view). [0116 item 19; 0117, What it leaves out]
- **Where a carrier force should be, and the enemy's expected places.** The staff keeps a carrier force where it was put and stands a surface force at the centre of the waters or at the base's hex; it does not choose a station from the enemy's reach, close the range on a contact the bombers cannot reach, fuel at sea or keep scarce ships out of the enemy's air reach, which wait for a belief about that reach. A surface force is not kept clear of the enemy's air by day: it steams for home at the morning and is at sea for as long as the passage takes. A damaged ship is not sent to a base that can patch it or to the yard by the staff. A station more than a night's steaming at full speed from the force's home port is not met: the staff hands in the notice that it cannot, and moving a force's home forward is the commander's. [0124, What it leaves out; 0125, What it leaves out]
- **A commander's temper anywhere but in closing.** A rating moves nothing but the closing of a carrier force on a contact of its own: not whether its aircraft strike, not an area it keeps to, not the speed, fuel, search or break-off. Changing a commander in the game, relief and refusal are not built. [0137, Why only this; 0132, What it leaves out]
- **Forces brought together for an operation:** units and ships moved to one base, forces joined, an operation that sails from two bases, a source chosen for its supply, airfield or port beyond the check that the place can be held, and forces kept back for an operation not yet stated. The staff assembles what already lies together (Taking a place, With no forces named). [0149, What it leaves for later]
- **What controlling an island leaves for later.** An island with a place inland, which waits on the march from a beach (stage 4); the order the places are taken in, chosen by the staff (they are taken in the island's order, each as its forces come free, unless the commander writes the order: A node the commander wrote); garrisons sized and moved between places, and defences built under a hold; leaving formed by the staff when a place cannot be held (decision 0150, moved to after the proof); and where a carrier force stands under an island, chosen against the enemy and not only by the bombers' reach of the first place, and carriers of one island answering for another. [0151, What it leaves for later]
- **What the leave to pursue leaves for later.** It is of carrier forces alone, and not of one covering a landing or serving an island. It does not ask about a contact of a kind the order's risk does not close on: a commander who wants every kind closed on says press. A commander's rating does not move ask, go or stay: a man who goes without leave is the question of refusal that decision 0119 left to the designer. The staff does not weigh one order against another, form an order of its own or change or cancel one of the commander's. It does not ask the staff again inside a day (decision 0155, item 4, stands). An unanswered asking never lapses into a yes. It does not mend the closing rule's not reading the area limit (`docs/rules/differences.md`). [0214, What it does not do]
- **What a plan the commander writes leaves out.** Taking over a node the staff has already formed: a place the staff already plans is refused as "Already planned", and he writes with the order or while its plan waits for his leave. Every other pair: a hold under an island, a carrier force's station, a surface force's stand under his own denial or hold, a bombardment, a "ready a landing" under his own take; each is opened by its own plan, with its doctrine read for it. A node of his under a node of the staff's. A notice for each node: a notice is once for its kind and its plan, and the reason of each morning carries the rest. A carrier or bombardment force named for a landing, which stays the island's to place. An "after" that names a node of the staff's: to put one landing after another he writes both. A window of dates for a written part. A seat at a command below the area writing nodes: the seat that may state the order may write under it. The enemy's staff writing nodes: a scenario may give the enemy's commander a written node by the same key, and no file does. [0232, What it leaves out]
- **A carrier force weighing its rounds.** A carrier force goes back for its fuel and weighs nothing of ammunition, under an island as anywhere; only the stand doctrine, the bombard doctrine and the choice of a free escort read rounds (Ammunition as policy). [0151 item 8a; 0127]
