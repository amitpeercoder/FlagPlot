# The staff: Carrier forces

Part of chapter 8, [The staff](README.md). Operating a carrier force; carriers serving an order for an end (carrier.rs).

## Operating a carrier force

The task "operate a carrier force from a hex" and the doctrine at the task force's level that serves it. It is run last in the staff's morning (The day's order, below). It gives orders to a task force and to the air groups aboard its ships, reuses the cover, strike and search pieces of the base's doctrines, and draws no random number. It adds no rule of the engine. [0125 items 1 to 11]

**The task.** An operate-carriers intent names a task force of the side and a hex. Its code is 10. It has no date. It is given to the force. It may use the force's ships and the air groups aboard them. It may stand under a deny intent (one under "control an island" among them, decision 0151), under a hold intent and, since decision 0148, under a "take a place", and a commander may state it himself as a root. [0125 item 1; 0148 item 1; 0151 item 6]

**The groups.** Each morning, for each operate-carriers task in force whose plan is released, in priority order, and whose force is not held, the staff decides for the air groups aboard the force's ships that are not held, in group number order. The risk is the task's risk limit read up the tree, balanced where none is set.

1. **Fighters fly the patrol,** with the patrol share the risk gives (three quarters, a half, a quarter of the aircraft), raised by a quarter and not above 1 on a morning when the force was attacked from the air on any of the three days before (read from the side's own events: an air attack on a ship of the force). The rest of a fighter group are its escorts.
2. **Who searches.** A group that can search (not a fighter, its type has a search range) and cannot attack a ship (its type carries no weapon) searches. Where the force has none, the least tired of the groups that can do both and can fly, then the lowest numbered, searches, chosen each morning, and the others strike; where none of them can fly, the lowest numbered of them (decision 0131, 7, amending decision 0125, 4). A group that searched yesterday and strikes today is given the missions in turn by orders. The reason names each such group's fatigue and the aircraft it can fly. A searching group is set to search once a day at preserve the force alone, and in both daylight pulses at balanced and at press (decision 0131, 6, as amended on Nov 16, 2026: a carrier force searched in the afternoon as well as the morning), the setting ordered after the mission and before the arc, and only where the group's setting differs; the reason says which applied ("at balanced risk a carrier force searches in both daylight pulses"). A search is flown at the type's search range over the arc of the cover's rule (Covering a base from the air, below), taken from the force's hex, with these threats: the enemy's bases on the side's map, the contacts the side holds, and, under a deny intent, the hexes of its waters; and, for a task whose station the staff chose (Carriers serving an order for an end, below) while the force goes there, the way ahead, in the words "the way ahead to the station (q,r) at (q,r)". Where a deny that names the force (its nearest forces limit names it) is not above the force's task and its targets limit has `waters_first` true, its waters are threats too, in the words "the waters to strike first of intent:N at (q,r)" (decision 0190, 29).
3. **The rest strike ships,** by the strike table (Striking ships from a base, above), with strike sizing on: a carrier has more to hit than one force. Where a targets limit binds the force's task, each such bomber's target list is the limit's kinds at every risk, and its waters to strike first are those of the denies that bind it, as Striking ships from a base, What to strike first, says (decision 0190, 3 and 28).
4. **Rest.** A group rests for a day when its fatigue in the morning is above the level the risk gives (30, 45, 60). The last fighter group that can fly is never rested, and neither is the last group that searches; a striking group rests above the level with no exception.
5. **Who is left.** A group that is none of these is given no order.

**The force.** The force's orders come before its groups', in the order readiness, rules of engagement, speed, route, then each group's mission first.

6. **Its place.** Where the force is not at the task's hex and not on its way home, it is routed there, with a speed order first where the speed it has is not the speed of item 11, and given no readiness order. At the hex it is given no route and is set to battle readiness. A force **on its way home** is under way, or halted, with its home port's hex as its last waypoint; it is given no route. A force of a task the staff formed under a commander's deny or neutralise (decision 0202) may be given more than the one point of its hex, and while it is under way on such a route it is given no route and no speed (The way a force takes, below). [0125 item 7; 0130 item 1; 0203 items 23 and 24]
7. **Fuel.** The fuel the force needs is, for each ship, the engine's measure of the passage to the home port at cruise, times the margin the risk gives (the table of Standing at a hex by night). A ship the engine's measure has run dry short of a passage is short of the fuel for it, here, for the passage back and in the sortie of a stand task (Standing at a hex by night): the measure gives no more burnt than is aboard. On a morning a ship is short, the force is routed home at cruise at every risk, with a speed order first where it has another speed and no readiness order, and the reason gives the figures. At home and not at its hex, it fuels (chapter 2, Refuelling in port), and on the first morning every ship has what the passage to the hex and home again needs, at the speed of item 11, with the margin, it is routed back; else it stays. Its home port is the force's own; else the base it is stopped at; with none, the fuel is not weighed and the reason says so. [0125 item 8; 0130 items 1 and 4]
8. **Rules of engagement.** The stance is avoid, since a carrier force does not seek a gun action, and the break-off level is the risk table's.
9. **When it cannot.** On a morning the force has no carrier afloat, or no air group aboard able to fly, the staff hands in that it cannot meet the intent, and gives no order that morning. On a morning a ship of the force has no fuel aboard, or the engine's forecast refuses the passage to the task's hex as out of fuel, the force cannot move: the staff gives it no route and no speed order and does not weigh closing, hands in that it cannot meet the intent with a reason that names the ship out of fuel and the hex where the force lies, and gives the air groups their orders as on any morning (the architect's ruling of Nov 16, 2026, recorded in decision 0131). The stand doctrine has no such hole: it routes a force only on a sortie whose fuel it has measured, and a ship that runs dry is short. [0125 item 10; 0131]
10. **Reasons** carry each group's fatigue against the level, the share and what raised it, the threats and the arc, the fuel aboard against the fuel needed, and the level, "decided at the level of task_force:N".

**Speed and closing the range.** Two rules of the force's commander, after the second proof of stage 2. [0130]

11. **Speed.** A force routed to its hex, to a hex it closes to, or back from its home port to its hex steams at cruise where the engine's measure of the passage at cruise brings it there within the 12 hours of the night, and otherwise at full speed; at preserve the force always at cruise. A speed order is given only where the force has another speed. The fuel for the passage back from home, and for closing (item 14), is measured at the speed chosen. The passage home for fuel is at cruise (item 7). [0130 item 1]
12. **A contact to close on.** On a morning the force is not held, not on its way home and not fuelling at home, and a bomber group aboard (not a fighter, its type carrying a weapon) has an aircraft able to fly, the staff weighs the contacts of ships the side holds. The **reach** is the least strike radius (normal reach) among those bomber groups. A contact is **fresh** when it is no more than 3 pulses old. The kinds closed on are: nothing at preserve the force, a carrier at balanced, any kind at press. A fresh contact whose estimated hex is in the waters to strike first of a deny that binds the force (Striking ships from a base, What to strike first, above) is closed on whatever its kind, at balanced and at press and not at preserve the force (decision 0190, 15). Of the fresh contacts of a kind closed on, or in the waters, that are within a night at full speed (the force's full speed by the engine's measure, times 12 hours) and the reach, by air distance from the force's hex, the staff takes the first: one in the waters before any outside them, then the kind first in the order of importance, then the nearest, then the lowest number. At balanced, then, a force closes on a fresh contact in the waters beyond its reach even where an enemy carrier outside them is within it: that is what the order says. At press a contact in the waters is taken first. With no such deny the waters are empty and the rule is as it was. Where that one is within the reach the force does not close; where it is beyond, the force closes on it. With fresh contacts of a kind closed on and none within a night and the reach, it is too far, and a find may be looked for (items 28 to 41). [0130 item 2; 0214]
13. **Where it goes.** The first hex along the shortest sea route from the force to the contact's hex that is within the reach of the contact: it goes no nearer than its bombers need. It is routed there at the speed of item 11. [0130 item 3]
14. **Fuel first.** It does not close where a ship is short (item 7) of the engine's measure of the passage to that hex and from there to its home port, at the speed chosen, times the risk's margin. With no home port the fuel is not weighed. [0130 item 4]
15. **It stays while something is in reach.** A force that has left its task's hex to close (one not at its hex whose last route ends at a hex that is neither the task's nor its home port's) is given no route on a morning the first contact of item 12 is within the reach, and stays; where that contact is beyond the reach it closes again. On a morning it holds none, in reach or to close on, it is routed back to its task's hex by item 6. [0130 item 5]
16. **Its arc, readiness and reasons.** Its search arc is taken from the hex the force is in that morning. It is set to battle readiness on a morning it closes or stays, as at its hex. The reason of a closing route gives the contact, its kind and age, its distance against the reach and against a night at full speed and the reach, the hex chosen, its distance and the hours to it, and the fuel; on a morning it does not close the reason says why: preserve the force closes on nothing, nothing fresh, nothing of a kind it closes on, already in reach, too far, no sea route, not the fuel, or no bomber group that can fly. These words join the reason it stays on its task where there is one. Where the contact is in the waters the words say so, "in the waters of intent:N that the targets limit set on intent:M puts first". [0130 items 6 to 8; 0190 items 6 and 15]

**Initiative.** One rule reads a commander's rating (chapter 1, Commanders), and it is this one. [0137]

17. **A contact of his own.** In item 12 each fresh contact is weighed at a level of risk: one that is the force's own (chapter 1, A contact of its own) at the level the table gives, one it was told of at the task's risk, whatever the rating. The kinds closed on are read at that level, contact by contact (and the waters to strike first likewise: a contact in them is closed on at any level but preserve the force), and the first contact is taken from those of a kind closed on at their own level. At preserve the force the force closes on nothing unless a fresh contact is weighed at a bolder level. With command levels off, no commander or a steady one, every contact is weighed at the task's risk, and the rule is items 11 to 16 as they were. [0137 items 7 and 8]

| The commander | A contact of his own is weighed at |
| --- | --- |
| Cautious | One level more careful than the task's risk: press as balanced, balanced as preserve the force (which closes on nothing), preserve the force as itself |
| Steady, or none named | The task's risk |
| Bold | One level bolder: preserve the force as balanced, balanced as press, press as itself |

18. **Nothing else moves with it.** The speed (item 11), the fuel and its margin (items 7 and 14), where it goes (item 13), its search, its patrol share, its strike settings and its break-off level are the task's risk's, as before; a bold commander at preserve the force who closes goes at cruise and measures his fuel with preserve the force's margin. He stays inside his task's forces. Nothing here is a refusal. [0137 item 9]
19. **The reason says so.** Where a rating changed how a fresh contact was weighed, the reason for closing or for not closing names the commander, the command, his rating, that the contact is the force's own, the level it was weighed at against the task's risk and the kinds closed on at that level; the binding of a closing route says that a commander of that rating weighs a contact of his own at that level. Where no rating changed how a contact was weighed, no reason's words change. [0137 item 10]

A task already standing is not withdrawn when the force's carrier is lost: item 9 hands in the notice. [0125 items 3 to 11]

**Radio silence.** The force's morning, at preserve the force and where report figures are loaded, begins with the order `set_radio_silence` on for a force not on silence, as Standing at a hex by night, above, has it; at balanced and at press the setting is left as found. [0136 items 18 to 20]

**Under take a place** (decision 0148). Cover for an operation by carriers. It adds no rule of the engine and no state; it draws no random number.

20. **Forming.** Under a "take a place" whose plan is released, the staff forms "operate a carrier force" for each task force on the carrier mission with a carrier afloat that has an air group aboard (a force before its day is left out) that the nearest forces limit on the end's chain names, in task force number order, where no carrier task for that force is in force anywhere on the side, whoever made it. The hex is the **covering hex**: the first hex along the shortest sea route from the hex the force is in that morning to the place's hex that is within the reach, the reach being the least strike radius, at normal reach, among the bomber groups aboard (item 12, with the place for the contact); with no bomber group aboard, or the force already within the reach of the place, the hex the force is in; with no sea route, none is formed and the reason says so. The reason gives the place, the force's hex, the reach and the group that sets it, the hex chosen and its distance from the force and from the place. Since decision 0218 the hex along the route is the first within the reach that lies outside submarine waters the area's picture holds, the reason naming each hex passed over; where all lie in them, the first, and the reason says no place outside serves (Patrolling waters with submarines, rule 16). [0148 items 1, 2 and 9; 0218, rule 55]
21. **The force is then the task's** by the carrier doctrine as it is (items 1 to 19): routed to its hex at the speed of item 11, searching, flying its patrol, resting its groups, weighing its fuel and closing on a contact; the enemy's bases on the side's map, the base at the place among them, are threats of its search already. The task is served after the landing's morning (The day's order), so that on the morning the plan is released it is formed and served at once. [0148 item 3]
22. **The strike on the place.** A bomber group that item 3 sets to strike ships is left to it on a morning the force's picture holds a fresh contact of ships (no more than 3 pulses old) within the group's reach of the hex the force is in. Where a targets limit binds the group, only a contact of a kind on its list counts: a fresh contact of a kind left off the list does not keep the group from the raid. With no targets limit every fresh contact in reach does, as before (decision 0190, 17). On a morning with none, while the task stands under a "take a place" that is not met, the enemy holds a base on the side's map in the place's hex, and that hex is within the group's reach of the force's hex, the groups the raid's rules 1 to 4 send (Raiding a base: which bombers go, whether the raid goes at the task's risk, what the last raid cost) are set to the base strike mission at that base, the lowest-numbered there, and given the raid's held share. A fighter group aboard is the force's cover: it is given no order by this, and the escorts it gives, at preserve the force, are what its patrol share leaves. Where the raid does not go (at preserve the force a group holds back a third and can send 6, fewer than the 12 the risk asks), no group is set to the base strike and item 3's orders stand; the reason gives what the bombers can send against what the risk asks. [0148 items 4, 5 and 9; 0190 item 17]
23. **Back to ships.** On a morning a fresh contact is within a group's reach again, the group is given item 3's naval strike mission again; when the base changes side the engine clears the mission (chapter 7, A base that changes side) and the staff gives no order for it. A fighter's patrol covers its own force and is not flown over a beach. [0148 items 6 and 7]
24. **When the place is taken.** On the first morning the end's plan is complete (the intent is met) the force is routed to its home port at cruising speed, where it has one and is not there or on its way, and the task is given no order after: its groups are left as they are. With no home port it is given no order, and the reason says the commander must move it. A force held by the commander is given none. [0148 item 8]
25. **Moved to look** (decision 0159, item 14, as the architect ruled it on Nov 18, 2026). The task of item 20 is formed sooner, while the end's plan is preparing, on a morning when all of these hold: the need to know is not met (Taking a place, The need to know; so never where none is asked, at press or by the knowledge limit); the side has no fighting man ashore at the place and no group the end may use is on reconnaissance of it; no air group the end's forces limit names that can search reaches the place from where it lies; and the limit names a group that can search, that the commander does not hold, aboard a ship of a carrier force the limit names. The force is the first such in task force number order that may be used, is not held and has no carrier task anywhere; one force a take is moved to look. Its hex is the covering hex of item 20 where such a group aboard reaches the place from it, and otherwise the first hex along the shortest sea route from the force to the place within the longest search range of those groups; with no such hex none is formed and the reason says so; the hex along the route is the first outside submarine waters, as in item 20 (decision 0218, rule 55). So the force moves once, to the hex it would cover the landing from. At balanced (the end's risk) the staff forms it without asking; at preserve the force only where leave to launch is given, and without it it forms nothing, gives the force no order and gives a reason each morning that says the force could look from that hex, the passage and how near the place it would then be, and that the staff waits for leave to launch. The reason of the forming gives what is held and asked, the scouts aboard with their range, the hex and why, the hours of the passage and the distance from there to the place. [0159 item 14]
26. **Before the release.** While the plan is preparing the task is served by items 1 to 19 as on any morning, and the strike on the place (item 22) is not given. A group the take's look keeps on reconnaissance of the place, or sends there that morning, is given no order by the task: its line says it is looking. When the need is met the look does not rest a group aboard the force moved to look: it is left to the task, which sets it as item 3 says. The force stays at its hex to cover the landing and is sent home by item 24, not when the need is met. On the morning the plan is released the task stands and item 20 forms no other. [0159 item 14]

**Under "control an island"** (decision 0151, item 8a). The carriers are the island's, not a take's: the take under an island takes no carrier force (Taking a place, Under "control an island"), and a force is never sent home when a take is met (item 24 does not apply).

25. **Forming.** The staff forms "operate a carrier force" under the island's denial, for each carrier force it may take (decision 0149, item 5) with a carrier afloat that has a group aboard, at the covering hex of item 20 reckoned to the island's first place, in task force order, where no carrier task for the force is in force anywhere on the side (Controlling an island, The tree each morning). [0151 items 6 and 8a]
26. **Served after the landing's morning,** with the carrier tasks of the takes (The day's order, below), by the carrier doctrine as it is (items 1 to 19). It weighs its fuel and not its rounds (Not built). [0151 item 8a]
27. **The strike on the place** (items 22 and 23) applies to such a task for the first take of the island's tree, in the island's order, whose plan is released, whose place is not the side's and is within the reach of a bomber group aboard; the groups are set to the base strike at the enemy's base in that hex as item 22 says. [0151 item 8a]

**A find, and going after it** (decision 0214; the figure `pursuit_nights`; rules label 27, doctrine `supply-53`). The closing rule leaves a contact that is too far for one night without a word. This has the staff look at it again, and ask the commander's leave, or take it, to go after it. It adds one figure, one leave of the commander's, three notices, one standing interrupt, two commands and a stop before the day; it draws no random number. [0214]

28. **What a find is, and where it is looked for.** On a morning a carrier force's closing rule runs (items 12 to 16: the force is not held, not on its way home, not fuelling at home, nothing is out of fuel, and a bomber group aboard has an aircraft able to fly) and gives **too far**, the staff looks for a **find**: the contact the closing rule would take were the night's steaming taken as `pursuit_nights` nights. The rule is worked again whole, with that one thing lifted, and nothing else of it lifted: the kinds the risk closes on (and the commander's rating that reads them contact by contact, items 17 to 19), the freshness of 3 pulses, the reach, the fuel to go and to come back at the risk's margin and the sea route are as they stand. Where the closing rule as it stands closes on a contact, stays or finds nothing fresh of a kind it closes on, no find is looked for. A force that stands under a "take a place" or under the denial of an island has no find: a carrier force that serves a take or an island stays with it. [0214, rules 1 and 3]
29. **Too far, the one kind of find.** The night's steaming is taken as `pursuit_nights` nights: the closing rule is worked again with the force's night at full speed times that many. The contact it then closes on, one beyond the reach and within those nights and the reach, is the find. With `pursuit_nights` at 1 (the frozen step 1 figures) this lifts nothing. A find is of this one kind and is one contact.
30. **What a find must pass.** The contact it gives is no find unless:
    - **It lies inside the order's area limit,** read up the tree from the task: where an area limit is set on the chain, its hexes must hold the contact's estimated hex. The contact is taken first and the area read after: where the one the rule takes lies outside the area the staff looks for no other, and there is no find. Where no area is set there is no limit. The closing rule as it stands does not read the area (`docs/rules/differences.md`); a find is held to it and a close is not.
    - **The force can go there:** the same sea route to a hex within the reach of the contact and the same fuel to go and to come back, at the speed chosen, as a close needs (items 13 and 14), with the speed of item 11. A contact the force cannot go to for want of a sea route or of fuel is no find, and the reason of the day says why it does not close, as it did.
    A find never crosses a hard limit of the order but the area; the forces limit binds as it does. [0214, rules 1 and 2]
31. **Who gives the leave.** For a find the staff reads, in this order, whose leave there is:
    1. **A pursuit of the force by the commander's answer** already stands (item 34): the force pursues the find, and no notice is handed in.
    2. **A pursuit the staff took earlier** stands and the force goes on with it, and no notice is handed in.
    3. **The commander has refused the contact** (item 34): the force goes on with its order, and the reason of the day says that the commander refused leave to pursue that contact. This binds at `go` too.
    4. **The leave to pursue** (The three leaves, above), read up the tree from the task: `stay`, nothing at all changes, no notice and no word beyond the closing rule's own; `go`, the find is pursued by leave taken (item 33); and `ask`, the staff asks (item 32). [0214, rules 4, 5 and 10]
32. **Asking leave.** The staff hands in the notice `asks_leave_to_pursue` (Notices, above) with a reason, and the force is given no order toward the find: it is served as though the contact were too far, so a force at its hex stays and one away from it is routed back to its hex by item 6 (item 15), and nothing of the order is withdrawn or changed. The reason says it is given no route and no speed order today, which is so only for a force at its hex. The staff asks only for a contact the commander's picture holds: the contact's number in the notice is the commander's, which with command levels off is the force's own; with command levels on it is the area command's number for a contact of the same estimated hex and kind, and where the area command holds none the staff does not ask and does nothing. The notice fires the standing interrupt `asks_leave_to_pursue` for the force (Interrupts, above), and a seat that runs time is stopped in the evening before it (chapter 1, The stop in the evening). With no answer there is no leave: the force goes on with its order, and the staff asks again at the next dusk if the find still stands. An unanswered asking never becomes a yes. [0214, rules 6 to 9]
33. **Taking the leave.** At `go`, for a find, and with no pursuit of the force and no refusal of the contact, the staff pursues it. It routes the force as a close does (items 13 and 16, at the speed of item 11) and hands in the notice `pursues` once, on the first morning, with a reason that says the leave to pursue was taken and where it comes from ("the leave to pursue of intent:N is go", or "no leave to pursue is set, so by default it is go"). It begins the force's pursuit, taken, at that notice. On the later mornings the pursuit goes on with no notice. [0214, rule 10]
34. **The commander's answer.** `answer_pursuit` names a force, a contact in the commander's number and yes or no. It is taken in at the first step of the day, before the staff is asked, so an answer given at the stop in the evening is in when the staff is asked that dusk.
    - **Yes** begins a pursuit of the force at once, by answer, naming the contact and the day, and the answer's `CommandTaken` event as its cause; a no kept for that contact is lifted. The staff then pursues, by that leave (item 35), and hands in no asking and no `pursues` notice for the force. The leave is the force's, not the contact's: it stands while the force has something to go after (item 37), and a contact seen again under a new number keeps it. A yes given where no asking was made, or where no find stands, begins a pursuit all the same, and it ends at that morning's test (item 37).
    - **No** keeps the refusal, by force and contact, and ends a pursuit of the force that is under way. The refusal is kept until a yes lifts it: nothing else clears it. The force goes on with its order and is never asked about that contact again; it is asked about another, and a contact seen again under a new number is another. [0214, rules 8, 10 and 12]
35. **A pursuit, and its reason.** A force that pursues is routed as the closing rule routes one (items 13 to 16): to the first hex along the shortest sea route that is within the bombers' reach of the contact, at the speed of item 11, set to battle readiness, with its search arc taken from the hex it is in. The order it leaves is not withdrawn and not changed (item 36). The reason of the closing route says it is a pursuit and by whose leave: by the commander's leave given on day N, in which case the commander's answer, the `CommandTaken` of the yes, is a cause of the order and of the reason; or by leave taken, saying where it comes from; and which order waits. A pursuit by answer is read first (item 31), so a pursuit on the commander's yes goes on at `stay` and past a later refusal of another contact. [0214, rule 10]
36. **The order the force leaves.** It is not withdrawn and not changed, and nothing is proposed in its place. The force is away from its hex; its search and the order's other forces are as the staff's rules leave them. Cancelling or changing the order is the commander's, as now. [0214, rules 11 and 13]
37. **The end of a pursuit.** A force's pursuit, by answer or taken, ends at the first morning the staff serves the force's task and the force has neither a find nor a contact to close on or to stay for: the closing rule closes on nothing, stays on nothing, and gives no find the force can go to. The staff hands in the notice `pursuit_ended` with a reason that names the day and the contact the pursuit began on and says the force goes back to its hex by item 6, and the pursuit is gone with that notice. It ends wherever the closing rule does not run as well: a force that is on its way home (as it is sent for fuel by item 7), or fuelling at home, or fuelling at sea, or with no bomber group aboard able to fly while another group is, has neither a find nor a contact to close on, and its pursuit ends the same morning with the notice. A pursuit of a force that cannot move for want of fuel (item 9), of one whose task is not served that morning (it is held), and of one with no carrier afloat or no group aboard able to fly is neither carried on nor ended, and stands until the force's task is next served and the test is made. A contact seen again under a new number within the bounds keeps the pursuit; the commander may end it sooner with a no (item 34). A force whose pursuit has ended goes on as any force of the task, and may be asked about, or pursue, another find. [0214, rule 12]
38. **The figure.** `pursuit_nights`, in `air_combat.toml` (2; 1 in the frozen step 1 figures, where the closing rule is as it stood and no find is made through the night). A placeholder, a judgement with no row: a force is not sent after a contact it could not reach in two nights. As freshness stands (3 pulses), a pursuit steams one night on a report and goes on only if the contact is seen again. A whole number, at least 1; the loader refuses a float. [0214, rule 14]
39. **Reasons and records.** The asking is a staff reason that gives the find (the contact with its kind, age, where it was reported and its distance), where the force would go and the hours there, the leave and where it was read from, and the order that waits; the pursuit and its end are reasons of their own. Each reason is handed in with the notice that rests on it, as a decision to give no order. A refusal and a leave by answer are words in the reason of the day. [0214, rules 6, 10 and 16]
40. **Where no one commands.** A side with no commander has the leave at `go`, so its forces pursue a find that is too far. In a dry run the side tried carries the settings of its orders, `set_pursuit`, and not its answers: a made-up world's contacts are numbered anew, so an answer to a numbered contact, its pursuits by answer and its refusals are not carried into it (chapter 9). The made-up enemy has no commander and goes. A player who runs time and does not answer gives no leave. [0214, "Who answers where no one is there"]
41. **A side sees:** its own asking, its pursuit and its end as notices, whole, and their reasons; its own `CommandTaken` of an answer and of a setting; its own interrupt firing. The enemy sees none of it. [0214, rule 16]

A side sees: its own orders, their acceptance or refusal, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none of it. The staff reads only the side's own view: the contacts it holds, its own forces, the map and the engine's forecasts of its own passages. [0125, 0130]

## Carriers serving an order for an end

Decision 0202. A commander's "deny an area" or "neutralise a base" uses a free carrier force of his side, and the staff chooses where it works from by the risk, the enemy and the fuel. It runs at the area's level, in one pass right after the island doctrine (The day's order, below), and forms, keeps or withdraws carrier tasks; the force's morning is then the carrier doctrine's (Operating a carrier force, above). It adds no state and no rule of the engine, and draws no random number. The doctrine version is `supply-50`; since decision 0203 it is `supply-52` (The way a force takes, below). [0202]

**Which orders, which forces** (rules 1 to 5).

1. **The orders.** Each deny and neutralise the commander stated, in force, whose plan is released. One whose forces limit binds no ship (names no ship or task force, nor lists `ships` in `none_of`) takes the free forces. One whose limit says `in_place` is passed over: its named forces work from where they lie (ruling I). One whose limit binds them otherwise uses only the carrier forces inside every such limit, each stationed as a free force is, where it has no carrier task in force and the island's test of a force the staff may take passes it over the intents of every other tree; its reason says it "is named in your order's forces limit", not that it is free (decision 0222, rule 2 and ruling D). A force the commander gave its own task keeps it (A task force as an addressee, above). A deny or a neutralise the staff formed under an island keeps the island's rule for its carriers (Controlling an island). Nothing is formed before the plan is released: with leave to launch withheld the force stays where it is.
2. **A free force** is on the carrier mission, has a carrier afloat with an air group aboard, has no carrier task in force, no intent in force is addressed to it, no forces limit in force names it, it is not held, and it may be used today: the island's test of a force the staff may take, read over every intent in force.
3. **One pass.** The orders are taken in priority order, then by number. Each first keeps the carrier tasks the staff chose for it earlier: a task stands, with its station, unless its force is held (it is let go: the staff withdraws the task) or an intent of another tree higher in priority is addressed to the force or names it in a forces limit (the task is withdrawn). Then each free force not yet given to an order this morning is tried for a station; where one is found the task is formed and the force is the order's. A force for which an order finds no station is left for the next. A force given to an order higher in priority this morning is named in a reason under each order after it: "the one carrier force task_force:N is taken by intent:M".
4. **A hold, and the commander's word.** A force held is never taken, and one taken and then held is let go the next morning; lifted, the force is free again and its station chosen afresh. A direct order to a force the staff has tasked holds it by the override (Holds and the override, above); a direct order to a free force does not.

**Where** (rules 6 to 17).

5. **The candidates** are the hexes the force can reach by sea (the map's sea distance from the hex it lies in, worked once) from which the task can be done: for a neutralise, within the least normal strike radius of the bomber groups aboard of the base, in a straight line by the engine's own test of an air distance; for a deny, within that radius less 40 nm for each hex of the waters' distance, of the centre of the waters, or, where that leaves less than one hex (40 nm), within the radius itself. Where the centre is a hex ships cannot enter, the nearest sea hex of the waters stands for it. The hex the force lies in is a candidate like any other. A force with no bomber group aboard has none.
6. **Circles of danger.** Each enemy base on the side's map casts a circle of 15 hexes (the distance at which a supply run is timed by night, decision 0145), whatever it holds; each contact of the area's picture reported as a carrier and no more than 3 pulses old casts one of 8 hexes (the distance at which a lift is turned back, decision 0191). A contact in the deny's own waters casts none against it; the base to be neutralised casts its own. A hex at exactly the radius is inside.
7. **The margin** of a candidate is counted in hex steps: the least, over the circles, of its distance from the centre less the radius. Above 0 it is outside every circle, by that much; at 0 or below it is inside, the depth in the circle that holds it deepest.
8. **The choice.** The passage to a candidate is its hex steps by sea from the force. At **preserve the force**, only candidates outside every circle: the least passage, then the greater margin, then hex order; with none, no task. At **balanced**, as at preserve where one outside every circle is in fuel; with none, the candidate least deep in danger, then the least passage, then hex order. At **press**, the least passage, in danger or out, then the greater margin, then hex order. The candidates are tried for fuel in that order, and the first in fuel is the station. So a neutralise is never served by carriers at preserve, and at balanced they stand where the base's circle is shallowest within reach.
9. **Fuel.** A candidate is in fuel when every ship has the fuel for the passage out at the speed the carrier doctrine sails at (item 11 of Operating a carrier force: cruise at preserve, and at balanced and press cruise where it arrives within the night's 12 hours, else full speed), by the engine's measure of the miles and the speed, and home from it to the force's home port at cruise (the engine's charged miles), at the class's cruise burn, together times the risk's margin (1.5, 1.2, 1). A force stopped at a base of the side that holds fuel above its reserve and fuels a ship is weighed with its ships full, since it fuels before it sails. A force with no home port is not weighed, and the reason says its fuel could not be measured against a passage home.
10. **Kept.** The station is chosen on the morning the task is formed and kept: it is not chosen again when a contact comes or goes, only when the task is formed again, after a hold is lifted or a higher order has had the force.

**The regard for submarine waters** (decision 0218, rule 55; doctrine `supply-55`). Among the candidates in fuel, one inside submarine waters the area's picture holds is passed over for the next in the risk's order that lies outside them; the reason names each one passed over and why, and where none outside serves the station is the first in fuel, the reason says so and the reason carries the caution `station_in_submarine_waters`. The way, the circles and the choice of a way are as they were (Patrolling waters with submarines, rule 15). [0218, rule 55]

**What the force does.** The station is the hex of a task "operate a carrier force" under the order (the pair under a neutralise is allowed since this decision). The rest is the carrier doctrine as it stands: its patrol, its strikes on ships, its closing on a fresh contact by its own rule (the station is where it waits, not a fence), going home for fuel. Under a deny it reads the order's waters and targets limit through the chain, as a force named in the order does (Operating a carrier force, items 2 and 3). Under a neutralise, on a morning with no fresh contact of ships within a bomber group's reach, the groups the raid's rules send are set to strike the target, by the routine of the strike on the place (Operating a carrier force, item 22), given the target for the place; ships come first. While the force goes to its station, its search takes in the hexes of its way for the coming day (the sea path, as many hexes as a day at its speed covers) and the station itself; at the station the arc is as before. [0202 items 18 to 23]

**The way a force takes** (decision 0203, stage 1). Every ship takes the engine's shortest way by sea between the points of its route; the staff has chosen where a force goes and when, and, for the force below, now chooses how. It adds no state and no rule of the engine, draws no random number and changes no hash. The doctrine version is `supply-52`. [0203]

**Whose way is chosen** (items 1 to 6, 23 to 26).

1. **A carrier task of the staff's making.** The way is chosen only for a carrier task the staff formed under a commander's deny or neutralise whose forces limit names no task force (the station of decision 0202), at balanced or preserve. At press nothing is chosen: the force is routed as it was. The way home for fuel, and closing on a contact, are the shortest way, as before, and so is every route of every other task (a station of the commander's, a stand, a supply run, a lift, a take, a bombardment). [0203 items 1 to 5]
2. **When it is chosen.** Only where the force is stopped, or its route ends elsewhere than its station or is done: on the morning the task is formed, on the morning after fuelling at its home port (where the doctrine's own test sends it back to its station), and on the morning after closing on a contact. Where the engine's forecast of the shortest way does not arrive (the order refused, no ship able to move, a ship run dry), nothing is chosen. [0203 items 1 and 6]
3. **A force under way on the one point of its station** is routed each morning as before, and no way is chosen for it. [0203 item 23]
4. **A kept way.** A force under way on a route of more than one point whose last is its station is on a kept way: it is given no route and no speed, the engine's route holds the way, and the reason says so. A force that is stopped, or whose route ends elsewhere, is routed afresh and its way chosen afresh. A kept way arrives when its forecast said; the shortest way, routed day by day, may arrive later than its forecast, because the doctrine drops a force from full speed to cruise when twelve hours or less remain, so the lateness limit below is a little stricter for it. [0203 items 24 and 26]
5. **The search on the way** (decision 0202, item 23, above) reads the engine's path through the waypoints that remain: those of the route given that morning, or those that remain of a kept way, or the station alone. The doctrine knows a force is bound for its station by the last waypoint of its route. [0203 item 25]
6. **No circle, no search.** With no circle of danger at all, the shortest way is taken and nothing is searched, since the search would give the engine's own way alone. [0203 item 17]

**The speed.** It is the one the carrier doctrine's rule gives the shortest way on the morning the route is given (item 11, above: cruise at preserve; at balanced cruise where the shortest way is twelve hours or less at cruise, else full), and every way is weighed at that speed. A longer way does not change it. [0203 item 7]

**What is weighed** (items 7 to 12).

7. **The circles** are decision 0202's, made by the same routine from the area's picture on the morning the route is given. [0203 item 10]
8. **The danger of a hex** is how many hexes inside a circle it lies, and one more: 1 at the rim, 16 on an enemy base, 9 on a carrier contact. In more than one circle it is the greatest. Outside every circle it is nothing. [0203 item 11]
9. **The danger by day of a way** is the sum of the danger of the hexes the engine's forecast has the force enter in the morning or the afternoon, but for the last. A hex entered by night adds nothing, and the station's own danger, the same for every way, is left out. The engine's forecast of a route reports, for each hex it enters, the day and pulse it enters it in (chapter 2, The path); the danger is read from that report. [0203 items 8 and 12]
10. **The limit on lateness.** A way is within the limit when the engine forecasts its arrival no later than the pulse after the one the shortest way arrives in at balanced, and no later than the same pulse of the day after at preserve. [0203 item 13]
11. **The limit of fuel.** A way is in fuel by decision 0202's test for a station (decision 0202, item 9, above), made on the way's own charged miles at the speed above, with the ships full for a force stopped at a base that can fuel it. With no home port it is in fuel, as in decision 0202. The doctrine's own test of whether a fuelled force goes back to its station at all comes first. [0203 items 14 and 15]

**The choice** (items 13 to 17).

12. **The staff's clock.** To propose ways only, and never to decide, the staff takes the force to leave at the start of the night of the day the route is given, at the knots the engine's forecast of the shortest way gives. The latest it enters the hex of step n is after 40n less 20 nautical miles. The earliest is after the straight line from the start to the hex, less 20. A hex counts as entered by night only where both hours fall in the same night pulse. A moment on the line between two pulses belongs to the earlier. The most steps searched is the most whose latest hour falls by the end of the last pulse the limit on lateness allows. [0203 item 9]
13. **The search.** For each count of steps from the shortest way's to that most, the staff finds the way of that many steps or fewer with the least danger by its clock, then the fewest steps. It runs on the steps ships can take, as the engine tests a step (`SideView::can_step`, which reads the map, which is common knowledge), from the hex the engine's forecast starts the force from. It is worked backward from the station; the way is built forward from the start, taking at each step the first direction in the engine's order that keeps the least. The station costs nothing. A proposal may pass a hex twice or double back. [0203 item 17]
14. **Routes.** Each proposal is made a route by the routine that keeps a route inside an area (a supply run's route, "Its route", above; decision 0107), the hexes of the proposal and the start being the area. Routes with the same waypoints are one. The shortest way, the one point of the station, is always among the routes. [0203 items 16 and 18]
15. **Each route is forecast by the engine** at the speed above. Its danger is read from that forecast, so what is weighed is the path the engine would steam, which need not be the proposal hex for hex; its arrival must be within the limit on lateness, and its fuel is read on its charged miles. [0203 item 19]
16. **Of the routes within both limits, the least danger is taken; then the fewest miles charged; then the fewest waypoints; then the proposal of fewest steps.** A way that is longer so that it comes to a circle after dark is a way like any other: the staff does not stop or slow the force to wait for the night. [0203 items 20 and 22]
17. **Where none is in fuel,** the shortest way is taken, as before, and the reason says that no way is in fuel. [0203 item 21]

**What the staff says** (items 27 to 29).

18. **A bent way.** The reason of the route joins the route's reason and gives the route and its miles, the hexes the engine steams through, the pulse it arrives in and the pulse the shortest way would, and, for each circle, the hexes inside it entered by day (and the nearest of them to the circle's centre) and by night, the limit on lateness, the fuel, and, where a clearer way that arrives in time is out of fuel, that it is. [0203 item 27]
19. **The shortest way taken in danger.** Where the shortest way is taken and it enters a hex inside a circle by day, the reason says so and that no way that arrives in time is clearer, or that no clearer way in fuel arrives in time. Where it is taken with nothing inside by day, nothing is said of a way. [0203 item 28]
20. **No figure of danger is shown;** the reason speaks in hexes, pulses and distances. [0203 item 29]

A side sees these reasons as any reason of the staff, its own and whole. The staff reads only its own view: the picture of the area and the map. [0203]

**Reasons.** The reason of a station gives the force and that it is free, the candidates and the reach they rest on, the rule of the risk, the station with its distance and compass point from the target or the centre of the waters, the passage there, each circle it lies outside or inside with the base's name or the contact's number and age and by how much, a nearer station passed over and why, and the fuel out and home against the least aboard; with command levels on, the force as of when its state was known; and what would change it: another risk, the contact growing old, more fuel. Where no station is found it says which circles held every candidate (and for a neutralise that a raid is flown from inside the base's own circle), or that no candidate was in fuel and which ships were short, or that none could be reached by sea. "Cannot meet" is said only when no base and no carrier force can serve the order, and its reason names both. [0202 items 26 to 29]

**A deny a carrier task serves** (decision 0217, rules 1 to 3; doctrine `supply-54`). Where no base the deny may use can strike or search its waters, and a task "operate a carrier force" is in force under the deny (one kept, or formed that morning by the carrier pass, by the island doctrine or for a force its forces limit names), the staff hands in no notice that it cannot meet the order. Its reason for giving the bases no task names each carrier task first, with its force and its hex, then says of each base what "cannot meet" says (its distance from the waters, the reach and range of each type it has, the groups coming), and does not say "cannot meet". This holds for every deny, whoever made it: the deny the staff forms under "control an island" included, whose carrier tasks the carrier pass does not read. Where no carrier task serves it, the notice and the words of "cannot meet" are as above; where a stand task alone serves it, nothing is said of the bases, as before. Nothing else of the deny changes: what it forms, the orders it gives and the days it gives them. A neutralise the island forms is not changed by this, and says it cannot be met while the carrier's bombers raid its target (`differences.md`). [0217 rules 1 to 3]

**Cautions** (decision 0217, rules 4 and 5). A reason may carry cautions: short statements of what the commander should weigh before he approves, each with its kind, the unit or place it is of, and its words. The reason of a carrier station the staff chose (Reasons, above) carries one for each circle of danger that holds the station, `station_in_danger`, of the enemy base whose circle it is (or, for a contact's circle, of the contact's hex), and one where the force has no home port the staff knows, `fuel_not_measured`, of the force, and, since decision 0218, one where the station lies in submarine waters the side holds and no place outside them serves, `station_in_submarine_waters`, of the station's hex (Patrolling waters with submarines, The staff's regard for submarine waters). The working keeps its sentences as they are. A side is shown a reason's cautions wherever it is shown the reason; `propose` gives each sub-intent's cautions as a list, and the table shows them under the list of what a plan would do. No other reason carries cautions yet. They are recorded with the reason and change no state and no hash. [0217 rules 4 and 5; 0218, rule 55]

**At the table.** The two orders' words say what may be used: aircraft from the side's bases and a carrier force that is free. The preview of a deny or a neutralise whose plan waits for a leave carries, beside the staff's decisions, the proposal as if released, which names the force and its station; the order's draft and the plan paper read it, and a plan that would form nothing, or cannot meet the order, says so where it would be called ready. A carrier force's card offers "keep this force out of the staff's plans", a hold. [0202 items 30 to 33]

A side sees: its own `IntentFormed`, `StaffReason`, the `CommandTaken` of each command and the orders, whole. The enemy sees none of it. The staff reads only its own view: no reason names what an enemy base holds. [0202]
