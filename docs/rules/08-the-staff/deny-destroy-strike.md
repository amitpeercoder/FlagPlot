# The staff: Denying an area, destroying a force, striking ships

Part of chapter 8, [The staff](README.md). Denying an area; destroying a force; striking ships from a base (deny.rs, strike.rs).

## Denying an area

The end "deny an area" and the doctrine at the area's level that serves it. It is run after the neutralise doctrine and before the cover doctrine, on the same morning (The day's order, below). It forms sub-intents and gives no order to a unit. It draws no random number. [0123 items 1 to 3]

**The end.** A deny intent names a hex and a distance in whole hexes. It names no base. It has no date: it stands until withdrawn. Its code is 6. It is given to the area. It is a root, stated by the commander with `set_intent` and the kind `deny`, and its plan is drafted, made ready and released by the leaves as a hold intent's plan is (Holding a place, above). `set_intent` refuses a hex that is not on the map as "Not on the map"; so does `form_intent`. Under it the staff may form the tasks "strike ships from a base" and "search waters from a base" and, since decisions 0124 and 0125, a stand task or a carrier task for each task force its forces limit names (A task force as an addressee, below), and, since decision 0202, where its forces limit names no task force, a carrier task for a free carrier force at a station the staff chooses (Carriers serving an order for an end, below), and nothing else. [0123 items 1 and 11; 0124 item 8; 0202 item 1]

**The waters** of a deny intent are the hexes on the map within the distance of its hex, by hex distance, in hex order. A hex off the map is no part of them. With a distance of 0 the waters are the hex alone. [0123 item 1]

**What it may use.** As the neutralise intent: with no forces limit on it or above it that binds the air groups, every base of the side and the air groups at each; a forces limit that names air groups narrows it to the bases where a group it names is, and only the groups named count there. Since decision 0222, a forces limit that names task forces and no air group leaves the air groups to the staff, and every base serves it beside the named forces; a commander who means only the forces lists `air_groups` in `none_of` (A forces limit binds the kinds it names, above). The oddity decision 0202 kept, that such a limit left the deny no base at all, is gone (differences.md row 75). [0123 item 3; 0202; 0222, rule 2]

**Each morning, for each deny intent in force whose plan is released,** in priority order, and for each base of the side that the intent may use, in base number order, the staff reads the bomber groups and the groups that can search that the intent may use at the base:

- A **bomber** is a group that is not a fighter and whose type carries a weapon. Its **reach** is its type's strike radius, on normal reach.
- A group that can **search** is a group that is not a fighter and whose type has a search range above 0. Its **range** is that search range.
- A reach or a range **touches** the waters when, from the base's hex, a hex of the waters is within it by the engine's own test of an air distance (chapter 5, Air distance).

Then, for the base:

1. **Striking ships.** Where a bomber whose reach touches the waters is there, and no strike-ships task from that base is in force under the intent, whoever made it, the staff forms one: a `form_intent` command (parent the deny intent, kind strike ships, the base), with a reason that gives the base's distance from the nearest hex of the waters and the bombers that touch them. Where one stands, the staff forms no other and hands in a decision to give no order that names it.
2. **Searching the waters.** Where a group that can search whose range touches the waters is there, and no cover of that base is in force (under any intent), and no search-waters task from that base is in force under the intent, the staff forms one in the same way. Where a cover of the base is in force the staff forms none: the cover's arc takes the waters as threats (Covering a base from the air, above), and the staff hands in a decision to give no order that says so. Where a search task stands it forms no other.
3. **No base reaches.** Where, over all the bases, no bomber's reach and no group's range touches the waters, and no carrier force serves it (below), the staff forms nothing and hands in a decision to give no order whose reason gives, for each base, its distance from the nearest hex of the waters and the reach of each bomber type and the range of each group that can search (or that it has none the intent may use), and, where the side has a carrier force, what each was, with a `cannot_meet` notice resting on it. It does this every morning on which no base reaches. The standing interrupt `cannot_meet` fires on it (Interrupts, above).

After the bases, the staff forms the tasks of the task forces the intent's forces limit names, at the centre of the waters (A task force as an addressee, below). A task formed for a force, or one that stands, counts as serving the intent, and so does the carrier task of a free carrier force (Carriers serving an order for an end, below), so the notice of case 3 is not handed in on a morning when one does. The new sub-intent's orders are handed in on the same morning, as for a raid (Neutralising a base, above). A task the staff has formed stands until it is withdrawn with the intent above it: the staff does not withdraw it. [0123 items 2 and 3; 0124 item 8]

**The waters first** (decision 0190). A commander may set a targets limit with `waters_first` true on a deny intent or an intent beneath one (The limits, above). The staff then orders the bombers of the deny's tasks to strike a contact in the waters before any outside them, as Striking ships from a base, What to strike first, below, says; a carrier force that the deny's forces limit names has the waters added to its search arc and closes on a contact in them (Operating a carrier force, below). A deny with no such limit does none of this: its waters are as they were. [0190 items 14, 27 to 29]

**Its measure.** A deny intent is met on a day that ends with no contact of the enemy held in its waters. The measure is worked by the agent interface from the side's own view and nothing else, stored nowhere (`sim_agent::deny_measure`), as a neutralise intent's is. It gives:

| Part | What |
| --- | --- |
| Days in force | The days from the day the intent was set to the last day resolved, both counted; or, for an intent withdrawn, to the day before it was withdrawn |
| Contacts held | The distinct contacts the side held in the waters (hexes within the distance of the hex, by hex distance) at the end of a day in force. A contact is held from the report that made or moved it to the one that dropped it |
| Strikes launched | The `StrikeLaunched` events of the side's own groups in the days in force that are now at a base which was given a strike-ships task under this intent, on a day that task stood, or now aboard a ship of a task force given a carrier task under it, on a day that task stood (decision 0202, 22). They count wherever the strike went, not only at the waters: a base given the task strikes what its groups reach. A group rebased since counts for the base it is at now |
| Claimed sunk, claimed hits | The ships the side claims to have sunk, and the hits it claims, from the air (`ClaimReceived`, from the air) at a hex of the waters in the days in force. A claim is what the side's people believe: never below what happened and never corrected |
| Days met | Of the days in force, those that ended with no contact held in the waters |

It is shown in `command_state` (as `measure` on the deny intent) and in `report` (as `denials`), with words. It changes nothing and no rule reads it. [0123 item 4]

**A proposal.** `propose` for a deny intent runs the air doctrine for the tree of its root as if its plan were released (Holding a place, A proposal). [0123]

A side sees: its own `StaffReason`, `StaffNotice`, `IntentFormed`, the `CommandTaken` of each staff command and the measure, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0123]

## Destroying a force

The end "destroy a force" and the doctrine at the area's level that serves it: an order against an enemy force the side holds a contact of (decision 0220). It is a deny whose centre is read, each dusk, from the force it follows, and it is served by the deny doctrine's own tasks (Denying an area, above) with the differences below. The staff's part is `follow_forces` in `sim-staff`: it runs at dusk, before every other pass of the day (The day's order, below), and works each order to destroy a force by priority and then by number. It forms no sub-intent of its own and gives no order to a unit; it withdraws the tasks it names below, and hands in notices. It draws no random number. [0220]

**The order.** An intent of the kind `destroy_force` (code 17) names a contact of the commander's picture, a distance in whole hexes (0 or more) and whether it is search only, false where the commander leaves it out. It names no hex and no base. It has no date: it stands until it ends or is withdrawn. It is given to the area, is a root and only a commander states it; `set_intent` and a scenario's `[[command]]` give it with `intent_kind = "destroy_force"`, `contact`, `distance` and `search_only`, and a hex or a base with them is refused when a scenario is read. Its plan is drafted, made ready and released by the leaves as a deny's is. Under it the staff may form the tasks "strike ships from a base", "search waters from a base" and "operate a carrier force", and no other: **no stand task stands under it**, the pair "stand at a hex" under it is not built. A `set_intent` that names a contact the commander's picture does not hold, or none, is refused as "Contact not held" (Every reason a command is refused); a contact that is held and no longer fresh may be named. Setting the order again, with the same contact, changes its distance and whether it is search only and keeps what it keeps; naming another contact keeps the report of that one. [0220 rule 1, R12]

**The commander's picture.** The contact's number is the number it has in the commander's picture. With command levels off that is the side's belief; with them on it is the area command's. A contact is later than another by its last report, which a picture holds as an age; nothing is read from events. [0220 rule 12]

**What it keeps.** The order keeps, in the side's command state and by order, the contact it follows (its number), the hex and the day and pulse of that contact's last report, the count of ships and the kind that report says (written with the hex, at the giving and at every notice of following; the sort is kept as it was and changes only at a taking), whether it was taken for a carrier, and whether it is lost-contact. Where the contact is no longer held, the notices of lost and of ended say the count and kind the order kept, never a count or a kind made up. These are state of the side's command, hashed in their place and named, and are not part of the order's kind, so keeping or changing them is no amendment of a plan. The order keeps them when it is given and the engine writes them when it records a notice of following (below); they go when the order is withdrawn. The pulse of a contact's last report is read from its age (R2): the pulse `age + 1` pulses before the pulse next to be played. A contact a side starts with at age 0 is read as one reported in the afternoon of day 0. A command given inside a day is taken in at the next dusk, so what the order keeps is read then. [0220 rules 5 and 13, R2, R3]

**Following, at dusk.** For each order in force, by priority then by number, the staff works in this order, the first that holds being done and the rest not (R5):

1. **The contact followed is held and fresh.** Fresh is the picture's own figure, `fresh_contact_max_age_pulses` (R1), so that at dusk a contact last reported in the day just played is fresh. The order follows it: its centre is the contact's hex. Where the report is not the one the order kept, the staff hands in a notice **follows**, with the contact and the report and no number it was taken from, and the engine writes the report on the order. A report that only refreshed the contact keeps the number, and is no taking. The notice is handed in at the dusk the followed contact's last report is not the one the order kept, whether or not it was refreshed that day (R3); the order is given its report at the giving, from the picture, and a command given inside a day keeps it as the picture holds it when the command is taken in at dusk (differences.md row 79).
2. **Else it takes another contact.** A contact of the picture other than the one followed qualifies where all hold: its last report is later than the report the order reads (the followed contact's own where it is held, else what the order kept); its hex is within the distance a force at `followed_force_knots` steams between the two reports, counted in whole hexes by hex distance from the hex of the earlier report (R4); and no other order to destroy a force follows it, as the orders stand and as those worked earlier this dusk took. The distance of R4 is the hours from the start of the pulse of the earlier report to the end of the pulse of the later one, at the figure, cut to whole hexes of 40 nautical miles; equal is within. Of those that qualify the order takes first one taken for a carrier where the contact followed was, and one not where it was not, before another; then the nearest by hex distance; then the lowest number. The order keeps the new number, hex, report and sort and is no longer lost-contact; the staff hands in a **follows** notice that says it is a taking, names the number it took from, and whether the contact taken is of the other sort. A count and a kind are not compared: a report is not compared with a contact. A taking is told on the order's paper; only a taking of the other sort may stop the clock (Stops, below).
3. **Else, at the dusk that begins day `D + lost_contact_days + 1`,** D being the day of the report the order reads, **the order ends.** The staff hands in a notice **force_not_found**. The engine, recording it, withdraws the order and everything beneath it, each withdrawal an `IntentWithdrawn` caused by the notice's event (R8), and takes what the order kept. The staff may not withdraw an intent the commander made; this is the engine's own act on its notice. With `lost_contact_days` at 2 an order is lost for one dusk and ends at the next, and a contact that qualifies at the dusk it would end is taken. It never ends because the force is believed sunk: a side does not know that, and a force destroyed is no longer reported.
4. **Else the order is lost-contact.** Its centre stays the hex of the report it reads, and it is served by the tasks to search waters alone. The staff hands in a notice **contact_lost**, once, and the engine sets the order lost. The staff withdraws the strike tasks and carrier tasks it formed under the order that dusk (R6): its search tasks stand. A group whose task is withdrawn keeps its settings, the waters to strike first among them, as for a group the staff may no longer order. At each dusk after, until a contact is taken or the order ends, it stays lost and the staff hands in nothing more. Where a notice names the report, its count and kind are those of the contact where it is still held, and where it is not, 0 ships and a kind taken from the sort the order kept: a carrier or a cruiser (differences.md row 82).

Each notice rests on a reason that gives the contact, the report the order reads, the figures and, for a taking, each contact that qualified and why the others did not. The order may be given to a contact not fresh and an order lost takes a contact again by rule 2: nothing else changes the contact an order follows. The commander may withdraw the order at any time, and may refuse a contact taken by withdrawing it. [0220 rules 6 to 10, R1, R3 to R6, R8]

**What it does with the staff's tasks.** The pass fixes the order's centre for the day and every pass that serves a deny reads the waters of the order from it, in place of a deny's hex (`Day::waters_of`); the doctrines of Denying an area, Striking ships from a base, Searching waters and Operating a carrier force are otherwise as they are. With these differences from a deny:

- **Its waters are struck first.** The order puts its own waters first, as a targets limit with `waters_first` does (Striking ships from a base, What to strike first), with no such limit set: a base's bombers take a contact in the waters before one outside, and a carrier force that serves the order closes on a contact in them of any kind, at balanced risk and at press, which at balanced it would not do for a kind it does not close on. A reason says "in the waters the order to destroy a force, intent:N, puts first".
- **Its leave to pursue is go,** unless the commander sets another on it or above it (The three leaves). The bounds of decision 0214 are lifted for it as a leave go lifts them. A hard limit still binds: an area limit, a hold, the forces limit.
- **No stand task is formed under it.** A surface force its forces limit names is not sent after a force on the move: the staff says so as a decision to give no order and forms nothing for it.
- **A free carrier force's station is chosen again** at a dusk at which the station lies more than `station_rechosen_hexes` hexes beyond the farthest a station may be from the waters' centre as it is now (R10). The farthest a station may be is the candidates' air distance of decision 0202 (Carriers serving an order for an end), in whole hexes cut: 4 for a reach of 250 nm and a distance of 2. The staff withdraws the force's carrier task and forms a new one, under a new number, in the same hand-in, and only where a station can be chosen; it never leaves a force with no task by this rule, and says nothing of "let go" (differences.md row 80). A free carrier force is stationed for it as for a deny, and one under an order that is search only or lost is not. Where the station lies too far and no other can be chosen, the station is kept and the task's reason of the day says that the station lies too far and that none can be chosen (ruling 4 after the build).
- **A base's task out of reach is withdrawn.** A strike task or a search task the staff formed at a base is withdrawn at a dusk at which nothing the task may use at the base touches the waters as they are at this dusk, nor the waters round the hex the order kept before this dusk (R7), by the deny's own test of a reach or a range: a bomber's normal reach for a strike task, a searcher's range for a search task. That is "two dusks running" read with no new state. The task is formed again by the deny's rule on the first dusk a reach touches. The order is worked at dusk whatever the state of its plan, a draft included, so a notice of following may be handed in before leave to launch is given (differences.md row 81).

**Search only** (`search_only`, the shadow). The order forms the tasks to search waters and no other. It forms no strike task, no carrier task and no stand task, sets no waters to be struck first and gives no leave to pursue: its leave reads as stay (R9). Its search tasks fly in both daylight pulses at any risk, in place of a deny's once a day at balanced and at preserve (Searching waters, below). Nothing stops a group from striking the contact under another order or its own mission. Where no base can search the waters it says it cannot be met, as a deny does. [0220 rule 3, R9]

**Where nothing can serve it** the order says so as a deny does: the staff hands in the decision to give no order and the notice `cannot_meet`, on each morning no base reaches and no carrier force serves it. [0220 rule 4]

**Stops.** The three notices of following that may stop the clock are a taking of the other sort (a carrier force for a force that was not one, or the other way), a loss and an end. They are no interrupt: nothing is added to the engine's interrupts, and `cannot_meet` and the rest read none of them. They are keys of `run` and `advance`, `follows_other_sort`, `contact_lost` and `force_not_found`, off unless asked for, and they stop after a day and never before one: `advance` at the end of the first pulse of the day the notice is handed in, and `run` at the dusk after that day (R11). A taking of the same sort, and a report that refreshed the contact, stop nothing. A seat that asks for none of them is stopped by none. [0220 rule 13, R11]

**At a seat below the area.** `set_intent` is not offered to a seat at a base or a task force (decision 0138, rule 5), so a seat below the area cannot give the order (differences.md row 78). [0220 statement 42]

**The figures.** `followed_force_knots` 20, `lost_contact_days` 2 and `station_rechosen_hexes` 3 are constants of the doctrine, in `sim-staff/src/follow.rs`, in no data file, and they are placeholders and judgements. They change the doctrine's version. [0220]

**Dry runs.** A trial and a preview work the order on a copy: the followed contact is found again by its place in the order of the picture's contacts, which a world made for the dry run keeps (R14). The saved game records the order with the commands that gave it, and the notices with an optional `follow` (the order, the contact and the report).

**A side sees:** its own `StaffNotice`, `StaffReason` and the withdrawals the notice causes, and the order's paper in `command_state`. The enemy sees none of it. The waters, the kind taken and the contact taken are read from the commander's own picture and from nothing else: the order learns nothing of the enemy that the side did not hold. [0220]

## Striking ships from a base

The task "strike ships from a base" and the doctrine at the base's level that serves it. It is run after the raid doctrine, on the same morning (The day's order, below). It gives orders to air groups and draws no random number. A strike is launched, sized, met and resolved by chapter 5 as it stands: this doctrine only sets the standing orders of the groups. [0123 items 5 to 9]

**The task.** A strike-ships intent names a base of the side. Its code is 7. It has no date. It is given to the base. The staff forms it under a deny intent (the engine also lets one stand under a hold intent for the same base, but no doctrine forms it there); a commander may state it himself by `set_intent` with the kind `strike_ships`, when it is a root with a plan of its own, made ready and released by the leaves. A task the staff formed belongs to its root's plan. Its limits narrow as the tree says (The intent tree, above). [0123 items 5 and 11]

**The groups it serves.** Each morning, for each strike-ships intent in force whose plan is released, in priority order, the staff reads the groups that the task may use (The six limits, above), that are at its base and that are not held as a whole (Holds and the override, above), in group number order. A group is left out that a raid served earlier the same morning has, or a strike-ships task served earlier. Each group is one of:

- a **bomber**: not a fighter, and its type carries a weapon. It is the task's, unless a raid task in force may use it as a bomber and ranks above this task, in which case the raid has it (Raiding a base, above). A cover leaves a strike-ships task's bomber out as it leaves a raid's;
- a **fighter group**: it is the base's cover's where a cover of the base is in force, and the task gives it no order; with no cover it is the task's;
- **any other group**: no order.

A task ranks above another by the rank read up the tree, 1 first, then the unranked, each by number. [0123 items 7 and 8]

**The risk level** is the task's risk limit read up the tree, balanced where none is set. It gives, for a bomber:

| Set by the risk level | Preserve the force | Balanced | Press |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A bomber rests above a fatigue of | 30 | 45 | 60 |
| Oldest contact it launches on, in pulses | The figure `strike_contact_max_age_pulses` | The figure | The figure and two more, to 4 at most |
| Its target list | Carrier, battleship, cruiser | Every kind | Every kind (where a targets limit binds the task, its kinds at every risk: What to strike first, below) |
| Its reach | Normal | Normal | Extended |
| Share it holds back | A third | None | None |

The rows are decision 0113's table, as the raid doctrine takes its fatigue levels and held share. [0123 item 7]

**The orders.** A bomber is set to the naval strike mission, or to rest when its fatigue in the morning is above the level its risk gives (equal goes). Its oldest contact, target list (the targets limit's kinds where one binds, What to strike first, below), waters to strike first (those of the denies that bind it, and none where none does), reach and held share are set to what the risk gives, and its strike sizing is set on (Strike sizing, below). A fighter group the task has, with no cover at the base, is set to the combat air patrol with a patrol share of a half, since a base that strikes will be struck. A fighter of the base's cover and any other group are given no order. An order is given only where the group's mission or setting differs from what is wanted, so a morning on which nothing changed has no order. Orders are handed in in group number order, a mission before the settings in the order above. A decision to give no order is handed in on a morning on which a group got none, or the task has no group the staff may order, and lists those groups. [0123 items 7 and 8]

**What to strike first** (decision 0190). Two things the commander's targets limit gives a bomber, in place of what the risk gives or beside it, both set by orders handed in with the others and neither holding the group.

1. **The list.** Where a targets limit binds the task, read up its chain (the nearest set), each bomber's target list is set to the limit's kinds in the limit's order, at every risk, in place of the list the risk gives. A kind the limit leaves out is not launched at. This task and a carrier task (Operating a carrier force, below) are the two that a targets limit binds. For a carrier force with no targets limit on its own chain, the lowest-numbered deny in force whose plan is released, whose nearest forces limit names the force and whose chain carries a targets limit, gives it. [0190 items 3 and 28]
2. **The waters.** A deny binds the bombers of a task when its plan is released and the targets limit read for it has `waters_first` true: a deny above the task (the nearest targets limit on the task's chain decides), and, for a carrier force, a deny whose forces limit names the force, under whatever task the force has. Each morning, for every bomber the staff may order under the task, it works the waters of all the denies that bind the group, their hexes together, and orders the group's waters to strike first (chapter 5, Air group orders) where they differ from what the group has. The empty set is among them: a deny withdrawn, or its limit changed or cleared, clears the waters the next morning. The order comes after the target list and before the reach. A group the staff may no longer order keeps the waters it has, as it keeps a target list: that is a known limit. [0190 items 14, 27, 28 and 33]

The reason of an order that sets the list names the targets limit and the intent it is set on; the reason of an order that sets the waters names the denies and the limit, and says a contact in them is struck before every one outside. The waters order is handed in under the task the group serves, which the engine's forces check reads, and its reason serves the lowest-numbered deny that binds the group, with that deny's plan; an order that clears the waters serves the task (the architect's ruling on decision 0190, rule 33; `docs/rules/differences.md`). With no targets limit the staff sets the list the risk gives and no waters, and no order or reason differs from before. [0190 items 6, 33; the architect's ruling]

**Strike sizing (rule 9).** Every bomber of the task has its strike sizing set on, whatever the risk. A group under a task to strike what comes within reach has more to hit than one force, so its strikes are sized by doctrine (chapter 5, A strike sized to its target), which sends everything at a kind it gives no figure for. The reason of this order names the air group as the level that decided it (A doctrine belongs to a level, below). The task never sets sizing off. [0123 item 9]

**Reasons.** An order's reason gives the group, its kind, its fatigue against the level, what it has and what is wanted. [0123]

A side sees: its own orders, their acceptance or refusal, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0123]
