# The staff: Escorting, holding a place and covering a base

Part of chapter 8, [The staff](README.md). Escorting and covering a run; holding a place; covering a base from the air (escort.rs, holding.rs, cover.rs).

## Escorting and covering a run

The doctrine of decision 0126, at the area's level where "keep supplied" is, and the one that uses decision 0121's escort rule (chapter 2, Keeping station) and decision 0124's stand task (A task force as an addressee and Standing at a hex by night, below). It adds no rule of the engine and no state. It draws no random number. [0126]

**What the commander names.** The forces limit of a keep-supplied intent may name task forces. A named force on the escort mission with a ship afloat is an escort the staff may give to a run. A named force on the surface combat mission with a ship afloat is a covering force. A force on any other mission is given nothing here. With no task force named, the threat is not read, no reason speaks of it and runs sail exactly as `supply-11` sent them. [0126 items 1 and 2]

**The threat.** It is read on the morning the run is routed, in `dispatch` (Each day, for each intent, step 3): a contact of ships the side holds, within 15 hexes of the intent's destination (decision 0124's threat, taken at the destination), by the contact's estimated hex. Every contact counts at every risk level, whatever its number of ships: a run is no force that could outnumber one. The nearest is taken, the lowest number on a tie, and the reason names it, its ships and its distance. [0126 item 3]

**Whether the run is escorted.** By the risk read up the tree (The six limits, above):

| | Preserve the force | Balanced | Press |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A run is escorted | Always, where an escort is free | On a threat, where an escort is free | Never |
| A threat and no escort free | The run is held: it does not sail this morning, the reason says why, and the cannot-meet notice is handed in | The run sails alone, and its reason says so | The run sails |

A run that preserve holds is not given up: it is loaded and ready, its dispatch is tried again on each later morning, and it is routed on the first morning on which an escort is free (or, at preserve, the morning on which no threat is held). The reason of the day for the intent says "The loaded run cannot sail" with the cause, and the notice, cannot meet (Notices, above), rests on that reason; while the run waits, no new run is weighed, as for any loaded run that has not sailed. At preserve with no threat held and no escort free the run sails alone, and says so. [0126 item 4]

**A free escort.** A named force on the escort mission, with a ship afloat, that:

- is stopped at the run's source hex;
- has no charge;
- is not held by the commander, as a force or by a ship of it (Holds and the override, above);
- is not short of ammunition at the run's risk (Ammunition as policy, below); and
- has in each of its ships the fuel for the passage out and back by the route the run will take, by the engine's forecast at the run's speed, times the margin the risk gives: 1.5 at preserve the force, 1.2 at balanced, 1 at press (decision 0124's table). The fuel is each ship's as the staff sees it that morning.

The lowest numbered free escort is taken. One escort force goes with one run. The reason of the escort's order says which escort was taken and the fuel of each ship aboard against what it needs; where none is free it gives, for each named force, why it is not (not stopped at the source, already a charge, held, short of ammunition, short of fuel, or no route by the engine's measure). [0126 items 5 and 8]

**The orders.** On the morning the run is routed, the order that gives the escort the run's force as its charge (the escort order, chapter 2, Keeping station) is handed in before the run's route order. The escort then keeps station by that rule and needs no route. On a later morning on which a named escort's charge is stopped at the source, with ships, and the escort is not held, the staff hands in an escort order that clears the charge, with a reason that says the run is home; this is done on each morning the intent's plan is released, before the runs are carried on, and for each such escort at most once. The escort is free again on the morning after it has fuelled (chapter 2, Refuelling in port). A held escort is left alone: no order clears its charge, and none is given it. [0126 item 6]

**The covering force.** For each task force named in the forces limit of a keep-supplied intent on the surface combat mission with a ship afloat, in task force number order, a stand task (Standing at a hex by night, below) is formed under the keep-supplied intent, by the forming rule of decision 0124 (A task force as an addressee, above), with the hex of the intent's destination base as its station, where no stand task for that force stands under it. A stand task may stand under a keep-supplied intent only at the hex of the base the intent names, which command intake checks (the stand task is refused where its hex is not the base's, or its parent's plan is not released, chapter 8, Every reason a command is refused). A carrier force named in the limit is given no task under keep supplied. The stand task's own doctrine decides the nights it sorties; this doctrine gives no order to a covering force.

The air doctrine runs where a keep-supplied intent's forces limit names task forces, and not only where a hold, cover, neutralise, raid, deny or stand intent is in force; with none named, a side with only keep-supplied intents hands in nothing from it, as before. The covering forces are formed in the air doctrine's pass, after the search doctrine and before the stand doctrine (The day's order, below), and the air doctrine takes the keep-supplied plan as released on a morning on which the supply doctrine releases it (leave to prepare and leave to launch given, as the leaves of the supply doctrine say), so that the task formed that morning is served that morning. [0126 item 7]

**Reasons.** The reason of a run's route order, when task forces are named, says the threat (the contact, its ships and its distance from the destination, or that none is held within 15 hexes) and the escort taken or why the run sails alone. The reason of the escort order, with its fuel figures, names the forces limit that names the escort and what would change it (no threat at balanced risk, press risk, the escort held, short of fuel or away from the source). The reason of a cleared charge names the run's force as back at the source. [0126 item 8]

**What it leaves out.** How many escorts (one named force goes or none does); timing a run to the dark; escorts for supply under "hold a place"; air cover of a run. [0126, What it leaves out]

A side sees: its own orders, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0126]

## Holding a place

The end "hold a place" and the doctrine that serves it. The staff runs it after the supply doctrine, on the same morning, at the step where a staff is asked (The staff, above). It hands in nothing for a side with no hold or cover intent in force, so every game without one is as it was. It draws no random number. [0117 items 12 to 15]

**The end.** A hold intent names a base of the side, or a base on the side's map that is not the side's (decision 0144, 1; below). It has no date: it stands until withdrawn. It is given to the base it names (The intent tree, above). It is a root: a commander states it by `set_intent` with the kind `hold`, and nothing may be formed above it. Under it the staff may form cover from the air (Covering a base from the air, below), strike ships from the base, "keep supplied" for the base (decision 0144, below), and, since decisions 0124 and 0125, a stand task or a carrier task for each task force its forces limit names (A task force as an addressee, below), and nothing else. Its limits are read as any intent's are: with no forces limit anywhere it may use the side's air groups based at the base, and it needs no source. Under "control an island" the staff forms it, of the side's lowest-numbered base at a named place, else of the enemy's, and sets its forces limit of the source it chose (Controlling an island, below). [0117 items 2, 8 and 12; 0151 items 6 and 8]

**Its plan and the leaves.** A hold intent's plan, like that of a cover intent stated as a root, is drafted when the intent is first set and is made ready and released by the same leaves as a supply plan, by the staff's `approve` command. For each such root in priority order (the rank read up the tree, ranked first, then by number):

- A plan that has ended: one reason for giving no order ("the staff forms nothing and gives no order under" the intent), and no notice.
- A draft: with leave to prepare the staff approves it, to preparing, with a reason; without it, a reason that the plan waits for the commander and a `waits_for_leave_to_prepare` notice resting on it.
- A plan that is preparing (one the staff has just prepared this morning included): with leave to launch the staff releases it the same morning, with a reason; without it, a reason and a `waits_for_leave_to_launch` notice.

The staff forms nothing and orders nothing under a root whose plan is not released, and the doctrine below reads a plan it has just released as released the same morning. [0117 item 9; 0107 items 21 and 22]

**Each morning, for each hold intent in force whose plan is released,** in priority order, the staff first forms the tasks of the task forces its forces limit names, at the base's hex (A task force as an addressee, below); it does this before the cover, and what it forms is no part of the cover's three cases. Then it does the first of these that applies, and gives the reason:

1. **A cover stands.** Where a cover intent for the same base is in force, whoever made it and wherever it stands in the tree, the staff forms none. It hands in a decision to give no order whose reason names the cover that stands and who made it. This is the reason of every morning after the first on which the staff has formed cover.
2. **Nothing at the base to cover it with.** Where the intent may use no air group at the base that is a fighter group or a group that can search (Covering a base from the air, below; a group that is held counts here), the staff forms none. Its reason says there is nothing at the base to cover it with, and gives what would change that: a fighter group or a group that can search at the base, or in the forces limit.
3. **Otherwise** the staff forms cover from the air under it: it hands in a `form_intent` staff command (parent the hold intent, kind cover, the same base) with a reason that says the base is held, its plan is released and no cover stands. The command passes intake as any staff command does (The intent tree, above). The cover doctrine runs for the new sub-intent on the same morning, on the state as the formation will leave it, so its orders are handed in the same morning and name the new intent.

[0117 item 13]

**Its measure.** A hold intent is met on a day the base is the side's at the end of the day and its airfield is open. The measure of the intent is worked by the agent interface from the side's own view and nothing else, stored nowhere, so the same view gives the same measure after a save and a resume (`sim_agent::hold_measure`). It gives:

| Part | What |
| --- | --- |
| Days in force | The days from the day the intent was set to the last day resolved, both counted; or, for an intent withdrawn, to the day before it was withdrawn. 0 where the intent was set after the last day resolved |
| Days met | Of those days, the days on which the side's own events show the base held at the end of the day (`BaseAtDayEnd`) and its airfield open. A base has its airfield open on a day if it has an airfield now (and, where the side's events show it built, that it was built by then) and the damage to the field at the end of that day is below `ground.toml: airfield_landing_refused_at` (the last `AirfieldDamaged` or `AirfieldMended` event of the day or before it; where the side's events show none, the base's damage as it stands). A side's events of a base are its own only while the base is its own, so a base that was lost shows none of the days it was held before |
| Air raids | The daylight pulses in the days in force in which the base was bombed, from the bombs the side's own events show falling on it |
| Raids fought | Of those, the pulses in which a patrol of the side's, flown by a group now based at the base, fired |
| Days of supply | The base's days of supply today (Days of supply, above), or none where the base is not the side's or the hex has no figure |

It is shown in `command_state` (as `measure` on the hold intent) and in `report` (as `holds`), with the words "held on N of M days in force (the base the side's at the end of the day with its airfield open); N air raids, N fought by a patrol of the side's; N days of supply at the base today". It changes nothing and no rule reads it. [0117 items 14, 0116 item 2]

**A place not yet the side's.** A hold may name a base on the side's map that is not the side's: intake takes it in, and its plan is drafted, prepared and released by the leaves as any hold's. On each morning its plan is released and the base is not the side's (not yet, or no longer: a base the side held and lost is in the same case), the staff forms nothing and orders nothing under it, hands in no notice and marks nothing failed; its one reason says that it waits for the base to be the side's. [0144 items 1 to 3]

**Men fed before it is taken.** Where the base is on the side's map and the side has, in its hex, a base of its own and men of any unit ashore (fighting or working, whatever operation they serve), the waiting hold forms one thing: the supply of those men. The base they are fed through is the side's lowest-numbered base in the hex, as a rule a base formed by a landing (chapter 7). The hold forms "keep supplied" for that base under itself exactly as for a base that is the side's (The supply that follows, below): on a morning no "keep supplied" for a base in the hex is in force, with the floor of the staff's supply (the margin of its risk, never less than 1 day: The supply that follows, below) and no interval, its forces limit the hold's, and with no source named none, with the reason that says so. It gives no order to the men and forms no task of a force and no cover. The hold's reason says the base is not the side's, that the staff waits for it, and through which base the men are fed. When the place becomes the side's the "keep supplied" stays on the base it names: men in a hex draw on every base of their side there, and no second one is formed for the hex while it is in force. The staff learns the hex of the base it holds not from the truth but from its own map. With no man ashore in the hex, or no base of its own there, the hold plays as above. Under "control an island" the men of the side at a base of the side beside a place, outside every named place's hex, are fed by the island (Controlling an island, Men beside a place). [0159, A; 0231]

**With men.** On each morning a hold's plan is released and its base is the side's, before the tasks of its forces and the cover, the staff reads **the men of the hold**: the fighting units with men ashore in the base's hex that the side may use and the commander does not hold; those every forces limit on its chain names, or, where no forces limit on its chain names a land unit, all of them. A unit named in the forces limit of a "take a place" whose plan has not ended and whose place is not the side's, or of a task under it, is that operation's and is left to it. Each man of the hold on attack is set to defend (`set_posture`, with no instruction for when it is spent), with a reason; nothing else is ordered of them: on the defence they rest and dig in by the rules as they are (chapter 7). A unit on attack that is held gets a reason naming the hold. A task force on the transport mission named in the hold's forces limit is given no task of its own: the tasks of a task force are formed for the surface combat and carrier missions only (A task force as an addressee, below). [0144 items 4 to 6]

**The supply that follows.** (For a side whose scenario states that its staff chooses its sources of supply, the staff forms it and sets its limit by The staff chooses a source of supply, below, and what follows here is for a side without that key.) On a morning the hold has men at the base, where no "keep supplied" for a base in the base's hex is in force: where the forces limit on the hold's chain names a source, the staff forms one under the hold, for the base (`form_intent`), with a floor of the margin of the hold's risk in days and never less than 1 day (10 at preserve the force, 5 at balanced, 1 at press: the staff's figures, placeholders; one function gives it, and every supply the staff forms has it, under a hold, under a hold that waits for its base and under an island for a base beside a place; the margin of the check that the place can be held stays 0 at press, so the men land with the supply for the days until a run can follow and nothing over) and no interval, so the staff uses 15 days. The reason says "with a floor of 1 day (the least floor, at press)" at press and "(the margin at balanced)" or "(the margin at preserve)" elsewhere. One day is the least floor at which, with a ship free, the supply served the morning before and the run's days the same on both mornings, no night is short: the doctrine checks the reading of the day after a run would arrive, and a reading is never below 0 t, so at a floor of 0 no run is ever ordered. A supply first served late, or a run whose days moved, may still leave a night short, as at any floor. A floor a commander sets is his. (decision 0230) Its forces limit is the hold's, read up the tree: its ships, task forces and source. The supply doctrine serves it as any (The doctrine: keep a base supplied, above) from the next morning; nothing of that doctrine changes, but that a plan's runs are its supply intent's, which in the plan of a hold is the "keep supplied" formed under it. With no source named, the staff forms none and its reason says why, and what could change it (decision 0222, rule 1). It works out, from the side's own view, the bases of the side that could be a source by the island's test (Controlling an island, The source of supply): a base of the side not in the place's hex (under "control an island", in no named place's hex), that the staff may take, with supply above its reserve. Where there is one or more, the reason names them, each with its supply above its reserve, says a source may be named among them in the forces limit, and would change with a source named among them. Where there is none, it says that no other base of the side holds supply above its reserve, so the place lives on what it has, with its days of supply today (whole days, cut down; with no planned use, its tons), and would change with a base of the side with supply above its reserve; it offers nothing to name. Nothing the staff does changes with these words. A hold with no men at the base forms none and says nothing of supply: it plays as it did. Under "control an island" the hold's forces limit is the staff's, set on the morning it forms the hold, of the island's source of supply and the transport forces stopped there (Controlling an island, The source of supply), and the "keep supplied" it forms has a plan of its own, made ready and released by the supply doctrine on the leaves as a root supply intent's is (Controlling an island, Plans of their own). With no forces limit of its own it reads the hold's, up the tree, for its forces and its source, and so do `add_run` and `commit_ships`. A hold of an island with no source forms no supply, and says so. [0144 items 7 to 9; 0151 items 8 and 9]

**Forces with men aboard.** On each morning a hold's plan is released and its base is the side's, after its men and its supply, each lift as it stands (Taking a place, Forces with men aboard) that no "take a place" can use, because none is in force for a place not yet the side's with a plan that has not ended or its men are all of working units, is sent to the base: in force order, where the staff may take it (decision 0149, item 5), it is free, it is no take's lift and no hold higher in priority sent it that morning, it is given a route from where it lies to the base's hex and on to its source (or on to the source of the hold of the base, Taking a place, Forces with men aboard, item 5), marked to act there, and the unload order, with nothing to load; everyone aboard lands. It goes by night as a lift does (A run by night). A hold the commander made sends only a force inside the forces limits on its chain; a hold the staff made has the force added to its forces limit by `set_limit` that morning, so that its orders are inside it. Under "control an island" the holds are in the island's order, so the first place held has them. Where the side has a force with men aboard and none is sent, one reason gives each and why. [0152 items 8 and 9; the architect's ruling of Nov 18, 2026]

**A proposal.** `propose` for a hold or a cover intent runs the air doctrine for the tree of its root as if its plan were released, and keeps what the doctrine hands in for that tree: the sub-intents it would form, each air group it would order with its order, the arc with its threats, the decisions to give no order, and one reason. It has no projection and no runs. [0117 item 14]

A side sees: its own `StaffReason`, `StaffNotice`, `IntentFormed`, the `CommandTaken` of each staff command and the measure, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0117]

## Covering a base from the air

The task under the end "hold a place", and an intent a commander may state himself, as a root. The doctrine below is the whole of it. [0115]

**The intent.** A cover intent names a base of the side. It has no date. It is given to the base. The staff forms it under a hold intent (Holding a place, above), and a commander may state it himself by `set_intent` with the kind `cover`, when it is a root with a plan of its own, made ready and released by the leaves. Where it is the staff's, it belongs to its root's plan. [0115 item 1; 0117 items 4, 9 and 12]

**The groups it serves.** Each morning, for each cover intent in force whose plan is released, in priority order (the rank read up the tree, ranked first, then by number), the staff serves the air groups that:

- the intent may use (The six limits, above): named in every forces limit on its chain, or, with none, the side's groups at its base;
- are at the intent's base (a group named in a forces limit that is elsewhere is given no order);
- are not held, neither as a whole nor by their search arc (Holds and the override, above); and
- are not taken by a cover intent served earlier the same morning: a group serves one intent, and where two may use it the higher in priority has it.

They are taken in group number order. The doctrine gives an order only where what a group has differs from what the doctrine wants, so a morning on which nothing changed has no order for a group. Each order is a staff order for an air group, serves the cover intent, and passes intake as any staff order does (The forces limit on a staff's orders, above). [0115 items 2 and 3, 5 to 11]

A group is a **fighter group** if its aircraft type's role is fighter; a **search group** if it is not and its type has a search range above 0 (`search_range_nm`); otherwise it is neither. A group's **fatigue** and the number of its aircraft **able to fly** are the side's own view's: the engine's count of the aircraft that are ready, crewed and on a field and deck that let them fly, and the group's fatigue now (chapter 5, What a group can fly in a pulse).

**The risk level** is the cover intent's risk limit read up the tree, balanced where none is set. It gives two figures:

| Risk | Share of its aircraft a fighter group keeps over the base | Fatigue above which a group rests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve the force | 3/4 | 30 |
| Balanced | 1/2 | 45 |
| Press | 1/4 | 60 |

[0115 items 4, 5 and 8]

1. **Fighters fly the patrol.** A fighter group that does not rest (rule 4) is set to the combat air patrol mission, with the patrol share the risk gives (chapter 5, Missions and orders and Escorts and patrol), and the rest of its aircraft are its escorts. The staff orders the mission if it is not combat air patrol and the share if it is not the share wanted.
2. **The share is raised.** On a morning when the base was bombed on any of the three days before the day being resolved (as the side's own events show: the `BaseAttackedAgainst` events for the base), the share is raised by 1/4, and not above 1. The reason names the day.
3. **Groups that can search, search.** A search group that does not rest, and that no raid task in force may use as a bomber (**A raid's bomber**, below), is set to the search mission and to a search arc from the arc of rule 6, at its type's search range, and to search once a day at preserve the force and at balanced, in both daylight pulses at press (chapter 5, Air group orders). The staff orders the mission if it is not search, the setting if the group's is not the one the risk wants, and the arc if the group's arc (from, to and range) is not the one wanted, in that order. The reason of the setting gives the group's fatigue against the level and the risk. [0115; 0131 item 6]
4. **Rest.** A group rests for a day, by the rest mission, when its fatigue in the morning is above the level its risk gives (a fatigue equal to it does not rest). The order is given only where the group's mission is not rest already. Fighter groups are taken most tired first, then by number. A fighter group above the level rests if it has no aircraft able to fly, or if another fighter group that can fly is still not rested; **the last fighter group that can fly is never rested**, so the base is not left without a patrol while a fighter group can fly. A search group above the level always rests: it has no such exception. The engine's own rest threshold is not set by the staff. [0115 item 8]

5. **A group that is neither** is given no order, and the reason says it is left for a strike intent.
6. **The search arc.** It points at what the side knows. The threats are the hexes of the enemy's bases on the side's map and the estimated hexes of the contacts it holds. With none the arc is the whole circle (from and to both 0). Otherwise it is the narrowest arc, on a grid of ten degrees, that holds every threat as seen from the base's hex by the engine's own test of an arc (`bearing::in_arc`): widths of 10 to 350 degrees in steps of 10, and for a width every start from 0 to 350, the lowest start first, the first that holds every threat taken. It is ordered widened by 30 degrees at each end (from the start less 30, to the end plus 30, each modulo 360), and as the whole circle where the width and the 60 degrees come to 360 or more, or where no arc under 360 holds them. [0115 item 9]
7. **When it cannot.** On a morning when none of the fighter groups the doctrine serves for the intent has an aircraft able to fly (which includes the case that it serves none at all), the staff hands in a decision to give no order whose reason says it cannot meet the intent, with why, and a `cannot_meet` notice that rests on it. The standing interrupt `cannot_meet` of the commander fires on that notice (Interrupts, above). [0115 item 10]
8. **Reasons.** An order's reason says the group, its kind, its fatigue against the level of the risk, what the group has and what is wanted, and, for the share, the share and what raised it; for an arc, the threats and the arc they give, and the contacts the group's arc was set against that the side has since dropped. A decision to give no order is handed in on a morning on which a group of the intent got no order or the intent cannot be met: it lists each such group with its fatigue, mission and setting, the share, the level and the arc, and, where it cannot, the sentence that it cannot. [0115 item 11]

**A raid's bomber.** Where a raid task is in force at the cover's base (Raiding a base, below), a group that the raid may use and that can bomb the raid's target on normal reach is the raid's, though it can also search. The cover gives it no order: it is left out of the groups the cover serves, and the cover's reason says that the group is the raid's. The cover therefore searches only with the groups no raid in force may use as bombers. A fighter is not a bomber and stays the cover's: where a cover of its base that may use it is in force, the raid does not order it (Raiding a base, rule 16). The cover's order of the morning is worked first, so that the raid reads, for each fighter the cover has, the patrol share the cover wants this morning, or that the cover rests it. [0120, 16, amending 0115, 7]

**A strike-ships task's bomber, and the waters to deny** (decision 0123). A group that a strike-ships task in force at the cover's base may use as a bomber is the task's, as a raid's bomber is: the cover gives it no order (Striking ships from a base, below). The search arc of rule 6 has more threats where the base's search groups reach the waters of a deny intent in force with a released plan: every hex of those waters is added, beside the hexes of the enemy's bases and the contacts the side holds, where a range of a search group the cover serves touches them (Searching waters, below). A deny intent whose waters none of them touches adds none. One arc serves the base's search groups. [0123 item 13]

A cover intent's limits narrow as the tree says (The intent tree, above): a forces limit on the staff's cover intent names only groups its hold intent may use, and a risk limit is no bolder than its hold intent's.

**A bomber that takes a turn at the search** (decision 0131, 8). Where a raid task or a strike-ships task is in force at the cover's base and the cover has no group that can only search (a group that can search and cannot attack a ship), the task that has the bombers lends the cover its search, where it has two or more bomber groups that can search, can fly and are not held: the least tired of them, then the lowest numbered, chosen each morning. That group is the cover's that day, whatever the cover's forces limit says: it flies the cover's arc by rules 3 and 4 (it rests above the level, as any search group does) and the raid or the strike task gives it no order and says it searches today. With one such group, it holds the strike or the raid and the cover has no searcher. Where both a raid and a strike task have such bombers, the first in priority lends. Who lends is worked out once each morning, before the cover is served, and the same choice is read by the raid and the strike task. A group that searched yesterday and strikes today is given the missions in turn by orders. The search task's groups are read the same way (Searching waters, below). [0131 items 8 and 9]

**The cost of search.** The engine sends every aircraft of a search group that can fly, in the morning alone when it is set to search once a day and in both daylight pulses otherwise. A search tires a group by its own figure for distance where the fatigue figures give one (chapter 5, Fatigue gained and lost): with the live figure a flying boat group searching to 600 nm once a day is rested by the afternoon. The doctrine does not send part of a group. [0115, What it leaves for later steps; 0131]

A side sees: its own orders, their acceptance or refusal, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0115]
