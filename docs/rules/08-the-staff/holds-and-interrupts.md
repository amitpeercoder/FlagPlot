# The staff: Holds, the override and interrupts

Part of chapter 8, [The staff](README.md). Holds and the override of a commander's order inside a forces limit; interrupts and their acknowledgement.

## Holds and the override

A **hold** keeps one thing from the staff: the commander says "this is mine, not yours". It is a record in the side's command state, hashed with it, and it has no date of its own: it stands until a `release`. [0107, rulings 17 and 18]

**What can be held.** A ship (the side's own and afloat), a task force of the side, an air group of the side as a whole, and one setting of an air group, which today is its search arc (a group's search arc and the mission that sets it flying). A group held as a whole is given no order of any kind by the staff; a group whose search arc is held is given no order for its arc or its mission. A group and its search arc are two targets and each may have a hold. A land unit of the side may be held (decision 0141, 14): the staff gives it no order. So may a submarine of the side's own, not sunk (decision 0218, 34): the staff gives her no order, and a patrol passes her over (Patrolling waters with submarines, below). A hold on a base waits for a stage whose doctrine acts on a base. A target has one hold: a second `hold` of a target already held is refused, whoever made the first. A hold records the day it was made and what made it: a `hold` command, or an order of the commander's (the override, below). A `release` lifts the hold on a target whatever made it, and a later order of the commander's to the unit holds it again (below). A hold moves nothing and stops nothing: a held unit keeps its standing orders and goes on by them. [0107, rulings 17 and 19]

**What a hold takes from the staff.** One rule says which ships are held, and every rule below uses it: a ship is held if it is held itself, if the task force it is in is held, or if any ship of that force is held. A held ship is not free to the staff, no ship of a held force is free, and no ship of a force that has a held ship in it is free: an order to that force would move the held ship. A held setting keeps the staff from changing it, and a held air group is one the staff gives no order to: the air doctrine leaves a group out of its work altogether if the group or its search arc is held (Covering a base from the air, below). Nothing else of the side is touched. The staff gives no order to what is held. [0107, ruling 18]

**The guard behind it.** The engine refuses what the staff would do to a held thing, so the doctrine's own care is not the only check:

- A staff command that names ships, `commit_ships` and `add_run`, is refused as held for a ship that is held (Every reason a command is refused).
- A staff's order is refused **held** ("the unit is held by its commander", with the target) if it is for a task force that is held or has a held ship in it; if it is an order to form a task force that names a held ship, or a ship in a held force; if it is any order for an air group that is held as a whole; or if it is a search-arc order or an air mission order for an air group whose search arc is held (a patrol-share order for such a group is not refused as held); or if it is any order for a land unit that is held (decision 0141, 14), or any order for a submarine that is held (decision 0218, 34). It is tried before the check of the forces limit (The forces limit on a staff's orders, below), so a held unit is refused as held whether or not it lies in the limit. A base order other than forming is never refused as held.
- The guard binds orders handed in by a staff. It does not bind the commander's own orders, and it does not bind an order a scenario's script gives with the issuer `staff`, which is a scenario's statement (below).

A refused order is numbered, kept and recorded as refused, as any is. [0107, rulings 18 and 30]

**The override.** The commander's own order to a unit the staff works with holds that unit, from that morning. It is step 2 of the day (chapter 1, Before the pulses). In the code it runs after the returns from the yard and before the staff is asked. For each side with at least one intent in force:

1. The side's pending orders are walked in the order intake sorts them: orders to form a task force first, then the rest by the unit they are for. An order of the issuer staff is skipped, so a scenario's scripted staff order never overrides.
2. An order **overrides** if it is for a unit inside the forces limit of an intent of the side in force: a task force the limit lists, or one with at least one ship whose every ship the limit lists, or one that an intent of the side in force is addressed to (decision 0124, 6); or an order at any base to form a task force that names at least one ship of the pool of such a limit (a ship the limit lists or one in a task force it lists). Or it is an order of any kind for an air group that some intent of the side in force may use (The six limits, above: named in the forces limit read up the tree, or, with none, at the base the intent is addressed to; the intent's plan is not looked at). Or it is an order of any kind for a land unit that some intent of the side in force may use (Readying a landing, and building up a base, below), which holds the unit (decision 0141, 14). Or it is an order for a submarine that a patrol in force may use or that an intent in force names in its forces limit (decision 0218, 12 and 34), which holds the boat; an order for a boat no intent may use or names does not override. An order for a land unit no intent may use, a base order other than forming, a group no intent may use, or a unit in no forces limit and in no intent's addressing does not override: it stays pending, in the order handed in, for step 5, as it was.
3. An order that overrides is taken in at once: numbered then, ahead of every other order of the day, and checked as any order is. If it is accepted, it holds what it was for, a task force, an air group as a whole, a land unit, a submarine, or each ship of the pool that a forming order names, and a `HeldByOrder` event is recorded for each, citing the order and its acceptance. An order that is refused holds nothing. A target that is held already gets no second hold, and the order is taken in all the same.

A side with no intent in force is not touched, and the day is as it was. `World::at_staff_step` takes the override too, so the view a staff is asked on shows the holds of the morning. A hold made by an order is lifted by a `release` like any other, and a later order of the commander's to the unit holds it again at the next step 2. [0107, rulings 23 and 30]

**The override inside a day.** In a scenario that turns orders inside a day on (chapter 1, Orders inside a day), the override acts at 0600 and 1200 as well. An order the commander hands in there is taken in at the start of the pulse that follows, with the pulse on its events; if it is accepted and its unit lies inside the forces limit of an intent in force, by the same test as step 2 above (the code is the same), it holds the unit with a `HeldByOrder` event in that pulse, citing the order and its acceptance. A unit already held is not held again. An order that is refused, as given at dusk or for any other reason, holds nothing. The staff is not asked inside a day: the hold binds it from its next asking, at dusk, when it sets aside a run of the held unit that has not sailed (The doctrine: keep a base supplied, Each morning, before any intent) and gives the unit no order. A trial asked at 0600 or 1200 (chapter 9, A seat's trial, rule 14) does not try such an order, nor the hold it makes. [0156, rule 5; 0204, 5]

A side sees: its own holds, whole: each with its target, the day, whether a command or an order made it, and the order's number, in the agent's `command_state` (`holds`); its own `HeldByOrder`; the acceptance or refusal of each hold and release command and of each order, with the reason, including a staff order refused as held. The enemy sees none of it. A hold on an enemy unit, or on one that does not exist, is refused as not the side's, so a refusal never shows that an enemy unit exists. [0107, rulings 17 and 23]

## Interrupts

An interrupt is something the commander wants to be told of: when it fires, a run of days stops (The tools, `run`) and the command report lists it. It changes nothing else: a firing moves no unit, gives no order and changes no stock. It is command state: the interrupts set, whether each is armed, the day each last fired and whether that firing is acknowledged. It is hashed, saved through the commands that made it and rebuilt by a replay. [0107, rulings 31 and 32]

**The kinds.** One is set by the commander, and six stand without being set. [0107, ruling 33; 0159 item 7; 0191 item 21; 0214 item 7]

| Kind | Holds on a morning when | Armed apart for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set: a base's days of supply below a number | The days of supply of the hex of the base (Days of supply, below), read when the staff is asked that morning, has a figure and the figure is below the number of days. 35 is not below 35. It does not hold where the hex has no figure (no supply is planned to be used there), or where the base is no longer the side's | Each numbered interrupt |
| Standing `cannot_meet` | The staff handed in a cannot-meet notice for the plan that morning (Notices, below) | Each plan |
| Standing `waits_for_leave` | The staff handed in a notice for the plan that it waits for a leave, to prepare or to launch | Each plan |
| Standing `cannot_bring_enough` | The staff handed in a notice for the plan that it cannot bring the men its take wants (Notices, below), and where men ashore wait with nothing more coming (Taking a place, Ashore, item 10: it fires on the first such morning, not on later notices). Added at the end, code 4 (decision 0159, 7) | Each plan |
| Standing `lift_turned_back` | The staff handed in a notice for the plan that it turned its take's lift back (Notices, below; Taking a place, A lift turned back). Added at the end, code 5 (decision 0191, 21) | Each plan |
| Standing `ship_lost` | A ship of the side was lost the day before: a ship sunk in a fight (`ShipSunk`) or lost in port with its base (`ShipLostWithBase`) on the day before, by the side the ship belongs to. Tested only for a side with at least one intent in force, so a game with no command gets no event of it. On day 1 there is no day before | Each ship |
| Standing `asks_leave_to_pursue` | The staff handed in a notice that a force asks leave to pursue (Notices, below) that morning. Its subject is a task force, the force the notice names. Added at the end, code 6 (decision 0214, rule 7) | Each task force |

A set interrupt names a base of the side and a number of days, at least 1. It has its own number, a whole number from 1, that the commander gives; a number is not withdrawn and no command withdraws one. A standing kind is on until the commander switches it off, and on again when he switches it on. A kind that is off is not tested; the records of its subjects stay as they were. The kinds in the code are `SupplyBelow` for the set one and `cannot_meet`, `waits_for_leave`, `ship_lost`, `cannot_bring_enough`, `lift_turned_back` and `asks_leave_to_pursue` for the standing ones; they are tested in that order, the order of their codes. [0107, rulings 31 and 33; 0159 item 7; 0191 item 21; 0214 item 7]

**Armed, fired, acknowledged.** A set interrupt is armed when it is set, never fired and not acknowledged. A standing interrupt for a subject that has no record is armed and never fired, and gets a record the first morning it fires. On a morning when the condition holds and the interrupt is armed, it fires: it is no longer armed, it last fired today and it is not acknowledged. On a morning when the condition holds and it is not armed, nothing happens. On a morning when the condition does not hold, it is armed again, which for a standing kind is every record of the kind whose subject does not hold. So an interrupt already true when it is set fires that morning, once, and then fires again only after a morning on which it did not hold. Acknowledging marks the last firing seen and arms nothing; the next firing is not acknowledged. [0107, ruling 32]

**When they are tested.** Each morning, at step 5: after the staff's commands are taken in and its notices recorded, and before any order is taken in. The figure a set interrupt is tested on is the one read at step 4, on the view the staff was asked on (after the commands and the override, before the staff's commands), so a staff's order or command of that morning changes no reading it is tested on. The sides are tested in side order, Allied first; for a side the set interrupts by number, then the standing kinds in the order of their codes (`cannot_meet`, `waits_for_leave`, `ship_lost`, `cannot_bring_enough`, `lift_turned_back`, `asks_leave_to_pursue`), and for a kind its subjects in plan, then ship, then task force number order. A side with a set interrupt and no staff is tested all the same. [0107, ruling 31]

**What a firing records.** An `InterruptFired` event, with no pulse, naming the side and the interrupt. A set one carries its condition and the figure it was tested on; a standing one carries neither. It cites the cause of the firing:

| Interrupt | Cites |
| --- | --- |
| A set one | The latest `CommandTaken` of the command that set that number. A set interrupt of a scenario cites its scripted command |
| A standing one for a plan | The `StaffNotice` events of the morning for the plan that make it hold |
| `asks_leave_to_pursue` | The `StaffNotice` events of the morning that the force asks leave to pursue |
| `ship_lost` | The event that sank the ship, or lost it with its base |

When breakdowns are on, a set interrupt's firing records the stock in tons, the planned use in tons, the days of supply and the number it fell below. Recording it changes nothing. A firing draws no random number. [0107, rulings 31 and 33]

**The commands.**

- `set_interrupt` with a number, a base and a number of days sets that interrupt, or sets it again: the condition is replaced, the day set is today and its status is as a new one's, armed and never fired. With a standing kind and `on` it switches the kind off or on, from that morning.
- `acknowledge` names a set interrupt by its number, or a standing one by its kind and the plan or the ship. It marks the interrupt's last firing seen.

Both are the commander's and are taken in at the first step of the day, so a set interrupt is tested that same morning and an acknowledgement made that day stands for the firing of an earlier day. The refusals are in the table above (Every reason a command is refused). An acknowledgement of a ship that is not the side's is refused as not the side's, whether the ship exists or not, so a refusal never shows that an enemy unit exists. [0107, rulings 31 to 33]

**The interrupt of a force's asking** (`asks_leave_to_pursue`, decision 0214, rules 7 and 9). It is the one interrupt that stops the clock before a day and not after it:

- **It fires at the morning's test,** as the others do, on the notice that the force asks leave to pursue. It is armed apart for each task force, and armed again on a morning on which the staff hands in no such notice for that force. A force that asks on a run of mornings fires once, on the first.
- **The seat is stopped before that.** A seat that runs time (`run` or `advance`) is stopped in the evening, at the dusk before the day the staff would ask in, with the day not yet resolved; chapter 1, The stop in the evening, has it. The stop is worked on a copy of the coming day with the side's queued commands taken in, and it is made where the standing interrupt is on and the force's record is absent or armed. So a force that was asked about the dusk before and not answered does not stop the clock again while its asking goes on unbroken. `end_day` does not stop.
- **A firing on a played day does not stop `run` again** after that day. The stop for that asking was the evening before; a second stop for it would be a second one. `run` stops after a day for every other interrupt as before. The firing is still listed in the command report and in `command_state`.
- **Switching it off does not give leave.** The standing kind off leaves the staff asking in its notices, and the force where it is: no stop, no answer, no leave. It is the setting of the leave to pursue that gives or withholds leave (The three leaves, above), and not the switch.
- **Its subject is a task force,** so `acknowledge` of it names the kind and the task force, in the side's number. The refusals of `acknowledge` are as for the other standing kinds (a force that is not the side's is refused as not the side's, whether it exists or not).

A side sees: its own set interrupts, standing records and the kinds it has switched off, with whether each is armed, when it last fired and whether that is acknowledged, in the agent's `command_state` (`interrupts`) and `report`; its own `InterruptFired`, whole; and the acceptance or refusal of each command. The enemy sees none of it. [0107, rulings 31 and 37]
