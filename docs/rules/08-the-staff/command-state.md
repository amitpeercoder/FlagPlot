# The staff: Command state and the intent tree

Part of chapter 8, [The staff](README.md). What a commander says as state: intents with parent, maker and addressee, the six limits, the two leaves, plans with their states, baselines, amendments and runs, commitments of ships; and the intent tree.

## Command state

Each side has one command state, held by the world beside its belief and hashed after the beliefs, Allied side first (chapter 1, The state hash). It holds the side's intents with their limits, its two leaves, its plans, its commitments, its holds and its interrupts. It changes only by a command, by the test of its interrupts each morning (Interrupts, below), or by the override of a hold, which an order of the commander's makes (Holds and the override, below). The enemy has none of it, and a side is never told whether the other has a staff. [0107 items 1 and 6]

A side sees: its own command state, whole, through its view. It sees none of the other side's. [0107 item 6]

### Intents

An intent is what the commander wants. Twelve kinds are built. Three name a base of the side, the two of decision 0120 name an enemy base as their target (Neutralising a base and Raiding a base, below), and the three of decision 0123 are Denying an area, Striking ships from a base and Searching waters (below). **Keep a base supplied** names a base, a floor in days of supply (at least 1) and, optionally, an interval in days (at least 1) between shipments. The floor is the days of supply the base's hex is never to fall below. The interval is the days of use each run is to bring above the floor. Where the commander gives none, the interval is not set and the staff proposes one. **Hold a place** and **cover a base from the air** name a base and nothing else (Holding a place and Covering a base from the air, below). **Neutralise a base** names a target, a base on the side's map that is not the side's, and no base of the side's own. **Raid a base** names a base of the side and a target (Neutralising a base and Raiding a base, below). **Deny an area** (code 6) names a hex and a distance in whole hexes and no base; **strike ships from a base** (code 7) and **search waters from a base** (code 8) name a base of the side (Denying an area, Striking ships from a base and Searching waters, below). The two of decisions 0124 and 0125 name a task force of the side and a hex and no base: **stand at a hex** (code 9) is the task of keeping the force at that hex for the night (Standing at a hex by night, below), and **operate a carrier force** (code 10) the task of the groups and the place of a force with a carrier in it (Operating a carrier force, below). The two of decision 0141 order land units (Readying a landing, and building up a base, below): **ready a landing** (code 11) names a task force of the side, a coast hex on the map and, optionally, a date, the morning by which it is to be ready (a whole number from 1); **build up a base** (code 12) names a base of the side and what is wanted there, in order: one to five of an airfield, its damage mended, defences to a number of points from 1 to 100, the port worked and aircraft repair, each at most once. A "ready a landing" whose hex is not a coast hex on the map is refused as "Not a coast hex". Every other intent has no date: it stands until it is withdrawn, and so does a "ready a landing", whose date says when it is to be ready and not when it ends. **Control an island** (code 15, decision 0151) names an island of the scenario and, optionally, a date that the takes formed under it are given; it names no base. It is an end given to the area and a root, and a commander who names an island the scenario does not have is refused as "No such island" (Controlling an island, below). **Patrol the waters with submarines** (code 16, decision 0218) names a hex and a distance and no base; it is an end given to the area, a root, which only a commander states, and it is the only intent that may use a submarine (Patrolling waters with submarines, below). **Destroy a force** (code 17, decision 0220) names a contact of the commander's picture, a distance in whole hexes and whether it is search only, and no hex and no base; it is an end given to the area, a root, which only a commander states, and the staff serves it as a deny whose centre is the force it follows (Destroying a force, below). A kind of intent is added to the twelve and the others: the count above is of the kinds of the first build. [0107 items 17 and 22, question 5; 0115; 0117; 0120, 5 and 9; 0124, 7; 0125, 1; 0141, 1 and 8; 0151, 3; 0218, 11; 0220, 1]

The commander names the intent's number when he sets it. It is a whole number from 1. A withdrawn intent keeps its number in the state and the number is never used again. An intent records the day it was first set, the plan that serves it, the day it was withdrawn, if it was, and, since the tree (The intent tree, below), its parent, its maker and its addressee. [0107, 0117]

An intent in force carries its limits as they stand. Setting an intent that is in force again may change its figures and not its kind: it replaces its base (and a supply intent's floor and interval) together, and leaves its limits, its parent and its maker as they are. A `set_intent` that names another kind of intent than the one in force is refused as "Kind cannot change"; the commander withdraws the intent and states another. A `set_intent` that names a parent other than the one the intent has is refused as "Parent cannot change"; a parent left out for an intent in force is read as the one it has, and for a root as none, so a scenario's command and a seat's are one case (Every reason a command is refused, below). [0107, 0117, "As built", the architect's ruling after the build; 0232, rule 3]

### The eleven limits

A limit says what the staff may use, where it may go, when it may go, what it must know first and what its aircraft are to strike first. Ten belong to an intent and one to a base. A limit is read up the tree: every limit set on the intent or above it binds it, the narrowest binding (The intent tree, below). A limit set nowhere above it does not bind, with one exception that follows from what it is: an intent with no forces limit anywhere above it may use what its addressee commands, which for an intent given to the area is nothing, so it gives the staff no ship and no order for it, with the one exception of a neutralise intent (Neutralising a base, below); for one given to a base it is the side's air groups based there. With no area limit the sea is open. [0107; the architect's ruling, Nov 15, 2026; 0117 item 8]

| Limit | What it holds | What reads it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forces | The pool: a set of ships, a set of task forces, a set of air groups, a source base, and a set of land units (decision 0141; a land unit not the side's is refused as "Not your land unit"), and a set of submarines (decision 0218; a boat not the side's, one that does not exist or one that is sunk is refused as "Not your submarine"), and `none_of`, the kinds of which nothing may be used, and `in_place`, that the task forces named work from where they lie (decision 0222). A supply intent's forces limit must name exactly one source (a `set_limit` of forces without one is refused as "No source"); the forces limit of another kind of intent need not | The check of a staff's order and of a staff command that names ships (below); the air doctrine, for the groups it may order; the two tasks of decision 0141, for the land units they may order; the patrol order, for the boats it may use |
| Area | A set of hexes the intent's forces may enter. It is not empty, and every hex is on the map. An area not set on it or above it does not bind: the sea is open | A supply's route (its own area, not one above it); a take's lifts, its way and its turn back; a bombardment's route; a carrier force's closing on a find. Nothing else reads it (decision 0222, As built, which lists where it is taken and not read) |
| Priority | A rank, 1 first, or none. An intent with none, on it and above it, comes after every ranked intent, then by number | The order in which every doctrine serves intents, the rank read up the tree (for the supply doctrine since decision 0222, rule 6) |
| Protected | A set of ships the staff may not take from the intent | The doctrine: no other intent's plan takes a protected ship, free or not, whatever the ranks (The doctrine, below) |
| Risk | Preserve, balanced or press. Balanced where none is set on the intent or above it | The cover doctrine: the share of the patrol and the fatigue at which a group rests (Covering a base from the air, below) |
| Reserve | For a base, a supply reserve and a fuel reserve, in tons, neither below 0 | Refuelling, and a load the staff orders (chapter 2, Refuelling in port and Cargo orders) |
| Not before | A day, or none. Set only on a "take a place" or a "control an island" of the commander's | The release of a take (Taking a place, Told to wait, below) |
| Wait for | A task force or a land unit of the side, or none. Set only as not before is | The release of a take, as not before |
| After | Another "take a place" or "control an island" of the commander's, or none. Set only as not before is | The release of a take, as not before |
| Targets | One to six kinds of ship, each once, in the order they are to be struck, and whether the waters of a deny come first (`waters_first`, false where left out); or not set. The waters may come first only on a "deny an area" or an intent beneath one | The staff's order of a bomber's target list and of its waters to strike first, under a task that strikes ships (Striking ships from a base, What to strike first, below); a carrier force's search arc and its closing (Operating a carrier force, below); the raid on a place of a carrier force |
| Knowledge | None, a count, or a recent count of a number of days, at least 1 (3 where the commander gives none); or not set. Set only as not before is | The need to know of a take (Taking a place, The need to know, below), in place of the risk where it is set |

[0107 items 18 to 20, 0111, 0115 items 2 and 4, 0117 items 7 and 8, 0159 items 9 and 12, 0190]

**A forces limit binds the kinds it names** (decision 0222, rule 2, and the architect's ruling A). A forces limit holds four kinds and a source: ships and task forces, which are one kind (the pool); air groups; land units; submarines; and the source. A limit binds a kind where it names one of it, or lists the kind in `none_of` (then nothing of that kind may be used), and leaves every kind it neither names nor lists to the staff, read up the tree as though it were not set; a limit that names nothing and lists nothing binds every kind: "use nothing". It binds the source where it names one. So a deny whose limit names two carrier forces uses the bases' air groups too, unless it lists `air_groups` in `none_of`. Naming a unit of a kind and listing that kind is refused as "Named and none of". Under a "take a place" or a "control an island" on the chain the limits are read as those orders read them (Taking a place, With no forces named; Controlling an island, Forces limits under an island), unchanged; a kind listed in `none_of` there is bound to nothing and is not filled from beneath. The groups aboard a task force go with the force: where no limit on the chain binds ships, a force the staff takes flies its own groups. A limit is the only change the staff reads: what each doctrine uses of a kind left to it is what it uses with no limit (Denying an area, What it may use; A task force as an addressee; Carriers serving an order for an end; Holding a place). [0222, rule 2, rulings A and D]

**Task forces from where they lie** (decision 0222, the architect's ruling I). A forces limit's `in_place`, true or false (false where not given), says how the task forces it names are used under a deny or a neutralise of the commander's. True: each carrier or surface force it names is used from the hex where it lies, by the forming rule of decisions 0124 and 0125 (A task force as an addressee, below): the staff forms its carrier or stand task under the order at that hex, and the carrier pass stations none of it. False: rule 2 stands, and the pass stations a named carrier force as a free one. It is read from the nearest limit on the chain that binds the ships. `in_place` true on a limit that names no task force is refused as "In place with no task force". The limit's words say "from where they lie". [0222, ruling I]

**Clearing a forces limit or an area** (decision 0222, rule 3). `set_limit` of `forces` or of `area` with `cleared` true and nothing but the intent clears the limit set on that intent: what it bound is read up the tree again, as though it had not been set. It is a command like the setting: refused for an intent not the side's, an amendment of a plan approved (even where nothing was set), never refused as "Wider than its parent", and not refused where nothing is set. A forces limit that names nothing is not a cleared one. [0222, rule 3]

**The three limits that tell a plan to wait** (decision 0159, rule C). Each is set by `set_limit` with its value and cleared by `set_limit` with none, by a seat and in a scenario's commands, like the others. Each is read up the tree, and every one on the chain binds: of the "not before" days the latest, and every "wait for" and every "after". Only the release of a "take a place" reads them, the commander's own and those the staff forms under an island; nothing else the staff does waits for them. No ring of waits is taken in: an "after" that would have two intents wait for each other, directly or through a third, is refused (Every reason a command is refused, below; the ring is looked for as No wait that waits for itself, in The intent tree, below, says). [0159 item 9; the architect's ruling on step 3; 0232, rule 7]

**The knowledge limit** (decision 0159, rule D, item 12) says how much must be known of the enemy at the place before a landing goes: **none** (on any count or on none), **a count** (seen from the air or by men in contact, of any day), or **a recent count** (such a count no more than so many days old on the morning it goes; 3 where the commander gives no number, and a recent count of no days is refused). It is set and cleared by `set_limit` as the three above are, only on a "take a place" or a "control an island" of the commander's, and read up the tree: the nearest on the chain binds. Where it is not set the risk sets it as before (press none, balanced a count, preserve a recent count of 3 days); where it is set the risk no longer says how much must be known, and still says the men wanted for each of the enemy's and everything else it says. [0159 item 12]

**The targets limit** (decision 0190) says which kinds of ship the aircraft of a task are to strike, and in what order, and whether the waters of a deny come first. It is set by `set_limit` with `limit` `targets`, the intent, `kinds` (one to six kinds of ship, each once, most wanted first) and `waters_first` (true or false; false where left out). It is set on any intent of the commander's in force, as a limit is. `set_limit` with no kinds clears it. It is read up the tree: the nearest set on the chain binds. A child's targets limit replaces its parent's and is never refused as wider than it. Set on an intent whose plan is approved it is an amendment of the plan, as every limit is. Only the commander sets it: the staff sets limits only on intents it formed, and its doctrine sets none. `waters_first` may be true only on a "deny an area" or on an intent beneath one. The limit is not an order to a unit and holds no group: the staff goes on ordering the groups it binds. It gives the groups under a task that strikes ships their target list, and their waters to strike first where `waters_first` is true (Striking ships from a base, What to strike first, below); it adds to the search of a carrier force and moves what the force closes on (Operating a carrier force, below). With no targets limit anywhere, no order, ranking, closing or search arc differs from what it would have been without the rule. [0190 items 1 to 8, 19, 27]

A ship is in the pool of a forces limit if the limit lists it, or if it lies in a task force the limit lists, afloat in it or away from it at the yard. A task force is inside the limit if the limit lists it, or if it has at least one ship and every ship of it is a ship the limit lists. An air group is one an intent **may use** if it is one of the side's own and either the intent's forces limit, its own or the nearest above it, lists it, or no forces limit is set on it or above it and the group is at the base the intent is addressed to (an intent given to the area may use no group without a forces limit; a group aboard ship or in no base is not at a base). An intent given to a task force may use that force's ships and nothing else, and the air groups aboard them where every forces limit on its chain names the group or has the force inside it (A task force as an addressee, below). Under "control an island" a forces limit that names no air group does not bind the air groups: the groups based at a base are used as with no limit (Controlling an island, Forces limits under an island). Elsewhere, since decision 0222, a forces limit binds the air groups only where it names one, lists them in `none_of` or names nothing at all (A forces limit binds the kinds it names, above). A submarine is one an intent may use only if the intent is a patrol in force and the boat is one of the side's own and not sunk and is named in every forces limit on the patrol's chain that names at least one boat; with no limit on the chain naming a boat, every boat of the side's. A limit that names no boat does not bind the boats (Patrolling waters with submarines, below), but one that names nothing at all and lists nothing binds them: "use nothing" (decision 0222, ruling D; differences.md row 74). [0107, 0115 item 2, 0117 item 8, 0124 item 6, 0125 item 1; 0151 item 4; 0218, rule 12]

The reserve limit names a base and no intent. It writes the base's own two reserves (chapter 2, Bases on hexes): one store, not a second. A scenario's base entry gives the same two figures (`supply_reserve_t` and `fuel_reserve_t`). It is taken in at the first step of the day, so it binds that morning's refuelling. [0111, 0107]

### The three leaves

A side's commander holds two leaves for his staff. Leave to prepare lets the staff itself move a draft plan to preparing. Leave to launch lets it move a plan that is preparing to released. The defaults are the spec's for a player: prepare given, launch withheld. Only the commander sets a leave, and he may set either on any day. [0107 item 21]

**The leave to pursue** is a third, and unlike the other two it is an order's and not the side's. It says whether a carrier force of that order may leave the order to go after what it has found (Operating a carrier force, items 28 to 41). [0214, rule 4]

- **Three values.** `ask`: the staff asks the commander's leave for each find. `go`: the staff takes the leave as given and tells him. `stay`: nothing changes from before; the staff looks for no find. [0214, rule 4]
- **Set on an intent, read up the tree** as a limit is: the nearest setting on the chain from the intent up is the one that binds, and the answer says which intent it is set on. The commander sets it with `set_pursuit` on any intent in force, his own or one the staff formed, and clears it by giving none, so that it is read up the tree again. It is not an amendment of a plan. [0214, rule 4]
- **The default,** where none is set on the chain: `go` where the side's leave to launch is given, and `ask` where it is withheld. A side with no commander is given both leaves, so it goes. A player's side has leave to launch withheld until he gives it, so it asks. [0214, rule 4]
- **Only the commander sets it.** The staff has no command that sets it, and it never changes it. A commander's rating does not move it (Not built). [0214, rule 4]
- **It is state:** the settings by intent are hashed after the rest of the side's command state (state layout `flagplot-state-v55`) and saved through the commands that set them. [0214, rule 15]

### Plans

A plan is what the staff does to serve one intent and the intents beneath it: one plan for a tree, with two exceptions that decision 0151 makes (below). It is drafted when a root intent's number is first set, and numbered in the side's own series, 1, 2, 3 and on, so a side's plan numbers depend on its own history only. A sub-intent has no plan of its own: it belongs to the plan of the nearest intent on its chain that owns one, which is its root's, and forming it drafts nothing and is no amendment (The intent tree, below). The three exceptions are a "take a place" under "control an island", whoever made it (formed by the staff or, since decision 0232, stated by the commander), a "keep supplied" formed under a "hold a place" that stands under "control an island" and, since decision 0231, a "keep supplied" formed directly under "control an island" for a base beside a place: each has a plan of its own, drafted when it is formed, so the plan of the island is not theirs and what is beneath each of them belongs to it (Controlling an island, Plans of their own). [0151 item 9; 0232, rule 4] A plan has one of six states:

| State | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Draft | Drafted, not yet approved |
| Preparing | Approved for preparation. It has a baseline |
| Released | Approved to run: it may be carried out |
| Complete | Ended: its intent was withdrawn while it was released, or the staff marked it complete |
| Failed | Ended: the staff marked it failed |
| Cancelled | Ended: the commander cancelled it, or its intent was withdrawn before it was released |

Preparing and released are together **approved**. Complete, failed and cancelled are **ended**: nothing moves an ended plan again. The moves are these, and no other:

- Draft to preparing, and preparing to released, by an `approve` command (below).
- A plan that is not ended to cancelled, by a `cancel_plan` command.
- An approved plan to complete or to failed, by a `mark_plan` command, which only the staff gives.
- A plan that is not ended, when the intent that owns it is withdrawn, its root or an intent beneath the island that has a plan of its own (above): a released plan becomes complete and a draft or preparing one becomes cancelled. A take under an island whose plan its own doctrine has marked complete is ended already. [0107 items 22 to 24; 0151 item 9]

Setting again an intent that is in force whose plan has ended drafts a new plan for it, with the next number, and the intent serves from then on. A plan that has ended with its intent still in force does not withdraw the intent. [0107]

Limit: decision 0107, item 23, says a plan's phase begins when it is released and ends when the intent is withdrawn. The code has no phase state. A plan's runs are tasks held in the plan, and its state is the one of the six above (differences.md row 42). [0107 item 23]

### The baseline and amendments

A plan's **baseline** is taken the first time it is approved: the day, the intent's kind and the intent's limits as they stand that day. It never changes. A plan that is not yet approved has none.

An **amendment** is a numbered change to a plan that is approved. While a plan is approved, a `set_intent` for its intent, and a `set_limit` of any limit of its intent (or of any intent beneath it, which shares its plan), apply to the intent as they stand and are also kept in the plan as its next amendment, numbered 1 and up, with the day and the change. A reserve is no amendment, because it names a base and no intent. Withdrawing the intent is none either: it ends the plan. A change made while the plan is a draft, or after it has ended, changes the intent and is no amendment: a draft's change enters its baseline when it is approved. [0107 items 22 and 25; 0190 item 21]

What the staff may change alone in a plan is its runs and commitments, inside the limits. A change to the days, the base or a limit, and cancelling, are the commander's. [0107, question 3]

### Runs

A run is one convoy of a supply plan: a task inside the plan's one phase. (The forces limit a run of a hold's plan is checked against is the hold's, or, for a side whose staff chooses its sources, the staff's supply under it: The staff chooses a source of supply.) It has a number in its plan (1 and up), its ships, its load in tons of supply (above 0), its source, which is the source base of its intent's forces limit on the day the run is added, and four days:

| Day | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Load | The day loading begins |
| Sail | The day the force sails |
| Arrive | The day it is due at the destination |
| Free | The day its ships are free: the morning after the day they have finished fuelling at the source on their return (by the hours of the fuelling, below, Its days) |

The days are in this order or equal. A run added has its load day no earlier than the day the command is taken in, and a run timed again has its free day no earlier than that day. A run does nothing by itself. The orders that carry it out, form the force, load it to a weight, send it and unload it, are the staff's to give on the days it names. [0107 items 26 and 27, rulings 7 and 8]

A run also records **the day it ended**, none while it has not: the day an `end_run` command was taken in (below). An ended run is ended for good: nothing takes it up again. It holds no ship, is pending no load and is counted by no reading, and so is a run of a plan that has ended: the staff reads both as finished. What is aboard its ships stays aboard, and ending a run frees no commitment: that is a command of its own. [0107, rulings 20 and 29]

### Commitments

A commitment gives one ship to one plan, and to one of its runs if named, from one day to another, both days included. A ship holds at most one commitment on any day: a commitment that overlaps another of the same ship, whatever plan it is for, is refused. The staff commits ships and frees them. Freeing a ship ends every commitment it has to the plan. No rule of the day reads a commitment: it is the staff's record, and the check of the next commitment reads it. A commitment to a plan that has ended holds no ship from the staff's reading (The doctrine, below).

A **commitment of stock** is no record of its own: it is the run. A ton at a run's source is committed to its plan from the day the run is added until it is aboard: the run's load less the supply aboard its ships while it loads there. The stock at a source that a new run may draw on is the stock, less the reserve, less those tons for the runs of every plan of the side. [0107 item 27, ruling 12]

## The intent tree

Intents form a tree. An intent is an end, a state of the world the commander wants of one of the side's bases; the staff breaks an end into sub-intents, and orders are given at the leaves. The tree is state: it is hashed, changed only by commands, and rebuilt by a replay. The engine records and checks it and never reads a reason's words about it. [0116, 0117]

**What an intent gains.** Each intent has three more fields beside its kind and base:

| Field | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| Parent | The intent it stands under, or none for a **root** |
| Maker | The commander (by `set_intent`) or the staff (by `form_intent`) |
| Addressee | The level the intent is given to. It is set by the kind and chosen by nobody: keeping a base supplied, neutralising a base, denying an area, destroying a force, patrolling waters with submarines and controlling an island are given to the area; holding a place, covering a base, raiding a base, striking ships from a base and searching waters from a base are given to the base they name (for a raid, the base it raids from). A task force is an addressee for the two tasks that name one: stand at a hex and operate a carrier force are given to the force they name (A task force as an addressee, below). An air group is a level too, but no intent is given to it (A doctrine belongs to a level, below). A land formation is not built as an addressee |

The level that owns an intent is the kind of its addressee. [0117 items 1 and 2; 0124 items 6 and 7]

**Roots and sub-intents.** A commander's `set_intent` makes a root, whatever its kind, unless it names a parent (A sub-intent the commander states, below): a commander states a task such as cover himself by stating it as a root (every kind but search waters, which is refused as "Not a root"; stand at a hex and operate a carrier force he may state, naming the force and the hex). The staff's `form_intent` makes a sub-intent: it names the parent and the kind, and the new intent takes the number one above the highest the side has ever used (a withdrawn number is never reused). Its maker is the staff, its addressee is its kind's, its plan is its parent's (so its root's: one plan for a tree, but for the three intents of decisions 0151 and 0231 that have a plan of their own, Plans, above), it has no limits of its own, and it is set on the day the command is taken in. Intake refuses a `form_intent` as in Every reason a command is refused: the parent must be in force, its plan released (or preparing, for "ready a landing" and "operate a carrier force" under "take a place"), and the kind able to stand under the parent's kind. Ten pairs are built, and the four that stand under control an island (below): cover from the air under hold a place for the same base, a raid under neutralise a base for the same target, strike ships and search waters under deny an area, strike ships under hold a place for the same base, stand at a hex under deny an area (the hex is the centre of the waters) and under hold a place (the hex is the base's, which intake checks), and operate a carrier force under deny an area and under hold a place, and, since decision 0202, under neutralise a base (Carriers serving an order for an end, below); and, since, "ready a landing" under "take a place" at the place's hex (decision 0142) and "keep supplied" under "hold a place" for the same base, or for a base of the side in the held base's hex, which intake checks (decisions 0144, 7, and 0159, A: its floor may be 0, the margin at press, and with no forces limit of its own the supply doctrine reads the nearest one up the tree, the hold's); the commander names a parent only for the one pair of decision 0232 (below), and every other pair is the staff's alone. Under "control an island" the staff forms a take a place, a hold a place, a deny an area and a neutralise a base, and under the denial an operate a carrier force (decision 0151, 6). `form_intent` records `IntentFormed`, citing the command's `CommandTaken`; it drafts no plan, but for a take or a supply intent under an island (Plans, above), which drafts its own and records `PlanDrafted` after `IntentFormed`, and is no amendment. [0117 items 3, 4 and 9; 0151 items 6 and 9]

**A sub-intent the commander states** (decision 0232, step 1). The commander may write part of a plan himself: a sub-intent under an intent of his own, of a kind the staff forms there when he does not, with himself as its maker. The staff fills what he leaves blank and carries what he wrote out as it does its own (Taking a place, Under "control an island"; Controlling an island, A node the commander wrote). No state is added: the parent and the maker of an intent are state already. [0232]

1. **`set_intent` may name a parent.** The command has `parent`, an intent number or none. With none it is the command as it was: a root. With one, and a number not yet used that is above the parent's (the tree's check holds a parent's number below its child's, and the tools' next free number is always above), it makes a sub-intent under that parent whose maker is the commander. The intent is otherwise what a `form_intent` of the same kind under the same parent would make: its addressee is its kind's, it is set on the day the command is taken in, and it has no limits until he sets them. [0232, rule 1]
2. **One pair is open to him: "take a place" under "control an island",** where the place is the hex of a named place of that island. It is one of the pairs the staff may form; the pairs the commander may write are a second, narrower table of the same shape, and a pair is added to it only when its doctrine has been read for it. [0232, rule 2]
3. **It is refused** as the reasons of Every reason a command is refused say: "Parent cannot change", "No such intent" for the parent, "Intent number not usable" for a number not above the parent's, "Cannot stand under", "Wrong plan state" and "Already planned". The parent's plan may be a draft, preparing or released. A root take stated for a place he has written under an island is not refused, as one stated beside a staff's take is not; the island's rule of what stands is unchanged for roots. [0232, rule 3]
4. **Its plan.** A take under an island has a plan of its own, whoever made it (Plans, above): it is drafted when the command is taken in, with the side's next plan number, and `PlanDrafted` is recorded after the command's `CommandTaken`, citing it. No `IntentFormed` is recorded. Stating it is no amendment of the island's plan; a change to it while its own plan is approved is an amendment of its own plan, as for any intent. [0232, rule 4]
5. **Set again, and withdrawn.** `set_intent` on its number, with its parent given or left out, changes its figures (its date) and not its kind, its parent or its maker, as for a root. He withdraws it by `withdraw_intent`, which withdraws what the staff formed beneath it and ends its plan, as for a take under an island formed by the staff. Withdrawing the island withdraws it with the rest of the tree, with its `IntentWithdrawn`. [0232, rule 5]
6. **Limits on it** are set as on any intent with a parent: a forces limit, a risk or an area must narrow, by the check of A limit must narrow (below, and Controlling an island, Forces limits under an island). The limits that tell a plan to wait and the knowledge limit may be set on it: it is a "take a place" of the commander's, which is all intake asks. An "after" may name another take he wrote under the same island, or any other take or island of his. [0232, rule 6]
7. **No wait that waits for itself.** The ring check of decision 0159 walked the "after" limits from the intent named, on the state before the limit was set, and so missed every ring that runs through a limit read up the tree by a node beneath: a take that waits for its own island, an island that waits for a take beneath it, and a ring through a third intent (R a root take, T his take under island I: "R after T", then "I after R", which T reads). The check is made instead **on the command state with the new limit in place, from every intent in force that would read it:** the intent the limit is set on and each intent beneath it. From each, the walk follows every "after" read up the tree and, for an island named, goes down to each take in force beneath it, since an island is met when its places are. The limit is refused as "Not an end to wait for" where any walk comes back to the intent it started from. With no sub-intent of the commander's in the state the walk is the one made before. [0232, rule 7]
8. **The tree's check.** A root is the commander's; a sub-intent is the staff's, or is the commander's where its parent is the commander's and the pair is open to him; a parent's number is below its child's. [0232, rule 8]

**A staff-made intent is the staff's.** `set_intent` and, from the commander, `withdraw_intent` on the number of an intent the staff formed are refused as "Made by the staff". The commander shapes it by its limits (below), by holds, and by withdrawing the intent it stands under. The staff may withdraw an intent it made, and only that (decision 0151, item 8b; Controlling an island, below): one it has not made is refused as "Not yours". [0117 item 5; 0151 item 8b]

**Withdrawing a tree.** A `withdraw_intent` withdraws the intent and every intent in force beneath it, depth first, each branch by number, all with the day of the command. Each intent beneath records an `IntentWithdrawn` event naming the intent it stood under, citing the command's `CommandTaken` (the intent named in the command has no event of its own: the command is its record). A withdrawn intent's plan ends as for any intent (Plans, above) when it is the root, or owns a plan of its own; withdrawing the island ends the plan of each take and supply intent beneath it that has one. [0117 item 6; 0151 item 9]

**Limits read up the tree.** The commander may set a limit on any intent in force, a staff-made one too (`set_limit` names the intent). Every limit set on the intent and above it binds it, so the narrowest binds: the risk is the least bold of those set on the chain, balanced where none is set; an air group may be used only where every forces limit on the chain names it, and a ship, a task force or a forming base is checked against every forces limit on the chain; the area is the hexes in every area set on the chain. The rank is the nearest set on the chain, and the protected limit is the intent's own. A limit set on a parent after one on its child binds the child from then on. (As first built, a child's own limit shadowed its parent's; the architect ruled after the build that the narrowest binds, decision 0117, "As built".) With no forces limit on it or above it, an intent may use what its addressee commands (The six limits, above). A limit set on an intent that is in force while its plan is approved is an amendment of the plan, whichever intent of the tree it is on. Under "control an island" the limits above an intent are read as Controlling an island, Forces limits under an island, says. For a "keep supplied" under a hold under an island whose plan is its own and that has no forces limit of its own, intake reads the forces limit and the source of a run (`add_run`, `commit_ships`) up the tree: the hold's, set by the staff. [0117 items 7 and 8; 0151 items 4 and 9]

**A limit must narrow.** On an intent that has a parent, `set_limit` of forces, risk or area is refused as "Wider than its parent" unless it narrows what the parent may use, read up the tree from the parent (for a side whose staff chooses its sources, a forces limit on a "keep supplied" under a "hold a place" is read as under a take: The staff chooses a source of supply):

- **Forces:** each ship is in the parent's pool, each task force is inside the parent's limit, each air group is one the parent may use, and a source is named only where it is the parent's source. Each kind is checked against the limits above that bind it (decision 0222, rule 2); where none binds it, the parent may use of it what its addressee commands: the limit may name air groups the parent may use and no ship, task force or source.
- **Risk:** no bolder than the parent's, from preserve the force (the most careful) through balanced to press.
- **Area:** a subset of the parent's area. Where the parent has none, any area.

A priority, a protected set and a reserve are never refused for this. A limit on a root is not narrowed against anything. This reason is tried after every other reason of the limit. [0117 item 7. The rule for an area is in the code and decision 0117 does not state it]

**A reason names the chain above it.** A reason's serves part gains, for the intent it serves, the intents above it, nearest first: each with its number, its maker and its statement in words as it stood that morning. The top of every chain is an intent the commander stated. A reason for a root has none. The words follow the statement, for example "intent 2: cover base:1 from the air; plan 1; under intent 1 (the commander's): hold base:1". A game saved before the chain reads each reason with none. The engine records the chain and never reads it. [0117 item 10]

**Order among intents.** The staff serves intents of a kind in priority order: a rank, 1 first, read up the tree, then the intents with none, each by number. The supply doctrine serves keep-supplied intents (roots, and those the staff forms under a hold, decision 0144) in the same order, the rank read up the tree since decision 0222, rule 6: a supply under a ranked order is ranked with it, and a rank set on the supply itself is read first; the air doctrine serves hold and cover intents (The doctrine, covering a base from the air, below). [0107 ruling 10; 0117 item 11]

**The tools** show the tree: `command_state` lists each intent under its parent with its maker, addressee, children and depth, and its limits as set and as read up the tree with the intent each is read from; `propose` shows the tree the staff would form, with the commander's own nodes beneath the intent proposed set in it as his (The tools, below); `why` and `staff_orders` give the chain (The tools, below). A node's maker is the mark of whose it is: nothing else is stored. [0117; 0232, rules 17 and 18]

A side sees: its own intents, whole, with `IntentFormed` and `IntentWithdrawn`, and the acceptance or refusal of each command. The enemy sees none of it. [0117]
