# The staff: The tools, a scenario's commands and the day's order

Part of chapter 8, [The staff](README.md). The tools a seat commands with; the commands a scenario gives; the day's order.

## The tools

A seat whose side has a staff commands through these tools beside the others, in `sim-agent` and through the MCP server and `play`. Every seat of a game those open has one; the staff does nothing for a side until an intent is set, and for a supply intent until it has a forces limit. A session made with no staff has none of them. The agent interface is version 24 since decision 0151 (below), and was version 23 from decision 0149, 22 from decision 0147, 21 from decision 0146 and 20 from decisions 0143 and 0144. It was version 19 since decision 0142, which adds the kind `take_place` (`hex`, or `base` for an enemy base on the side's map, and `by_day`) and an intent's `phase` in `command_state`. It was version 18 from decision 0141, which adds its two kinds (`ready_landing` with `task_force`, `hex` and `by_day`; `build_up` with `base`, `wants` and `defences`), a forces limit's `land_units`, a hold's `land_unit` and the notices `ready`, `late` and `met`. It was version 11 (version 7 had the four tools of step 4, and `interrupts` in `command_state`; version 8 added the intent tree, the kinds `cover` and `hold`, air groups in a forces limit, a hold on a whole air group, a reason's chain and the measure of a hold intent; version 9 added the kinds `neutralise` and `raid`, the `target` of an intent, an intent's `base` that is null for a neutralise, a reason's `level` and the measure of a neutralise intent; version 10 added the kinds `deny`, `strike_ships` and `search_waters`, a deny intent's `hex`, `distance` and `measure`, and the air group order `set_strike_sizing`; version 11 adds the kinds `stand_at` and `operate_carriers`, with a `task_force` and a `hex` and a `base` that is null, a route order's `stand_at` and a force line's, a reason's `level` that may be a task force's, and the `pulse` a halt or steam-on choice is asked for). The staff's `withdraw_intent` is in a hand-in, on an intent it made. [0151]

Since decision 0230 (doctrine `supply-62`; agent interface version 55, state layout `flagplot-state-v60`, saved game version 28 and rules label 34 stay): the floor of a "keep supplied" the staff forms is the margin of its risk and never less than 1 day (10, 5, 1), at every site that forms one; the reason says "with a floor of 1 day (the least floor, at press)" at press. A saved game of an earlier doctrine is refused by `replay --check-staff` as for any change of doctrine; no field is added.

Since decision 0214 (agent interface version 43): `set_pursuit` takes `intent` and `leave` (`ask`, `go` or `stay`; left out or null clears it) and `answer_pursuit` takes `task_force`, `contact` (the commander's number of the contact the asking named) and `allow` (true for yes, false for no); each queues one command for the coming day, taken in at its first step and answered queued or refused with the engine's reason (an intent not in force; a force that is not the side's). `set_interrupt` takes the standing kind `asks_leave_to_pursue`, and `acknowledge` takes it with `task_force`. `command_state` gives each intent's `pursue` (the leave as read up the tree, `from`: the intent it is set on or `"default"`, and whether one is `set`), a `pursue_meaning` in words, the side's `pursuits` (each force, whether `answer` or `taken`, the contact and the day it began) and its `pursuit_refusals` (force and contact). The notices `asks_leave_to_pursue`, `pursues` and `pursuit_ended` are in `report` and the briefing, each force's a matter of its own, with the force, the order and the find. `run` and `advance` stop in the evening with `stopped` of `asks_leave` and an `asking` that lists each asking: the `task_force`, the `intent` it would leave, the `contact`, its `kind` and `age_pulses`, where it was `reported_at`, where it would go (`to`), the `hours` there and the `interrupt`; chapter 1, The stop in the evening. `run` does not stop after a day for the firing of that interrupt. A trial carries the settings and not the answers (item 40). The tools are the interface's, not rules. [0214, rule 18]

Since decision 0232, step 1 (agent interface version 56; state layout `flagplot-state-v60` stays, saved game version 29, rules label 35, doctrine `supply-63`): `set_intent` takes `parent`, an intent number; left out it makes a root, or for an intent in force is read as the parent it has, so no tool fills it in (A sub-intent the commander states). `command_state` gives each intent's `maker`, `parent` and `children` as before; the note it writes for each intent the staff formed stays; an island gains nothing. `propose` of an island gives a `tree` that gains an entry in `sub_intents` for each intent in force beneath the intent proposed that the commander made, before the staff's own entries: its own number, its parent, `maker` "commander" and `standing` true, with its kind's fields and no reason; the staff's entries are as before, each with `maker` "staff", numbered from the side's next free number; the reason of rule 9 for each of his takes is among the tree's `no_orders`, as every reason for forming nothing is. `order` of an island lists every intent in force beneath it and each task gains `maker`; its text says "Yours:" before his takes and "The staff's:" before the rest. `why` and `staff_orders` give a reason's chain, which already names the maker of each intent above ("under intent 2 (the commander's)"); a reason that serves his node says so in its first line, and a reason that turns on his node says which node and that it is his, and lists the limit it speaks of where one binds. [0232, rules 17 to 19]

Since decision 0232, step 2 (agent interface version 57; state layout `flagplot-state-v60` stays, saved game version 29, rules label 35, doctrine `supply-63`): `preview` of a `set_intent` takes `under`, the written part of an order, and its answer and `approve`'s gain what is said below; `trial` reads the staff's own plan as the second trial for a written part, and refuses `dates` for one (chapter 9, What-ifs from a seat). No rule of the game moves. [0232, rules 20 to 26]

- `command_state`, `plans`: what stands, as the coming day will find it, with each intent's figure and first day below, and yesterday's staff decisions with their numbers for `why`. `command_state` lists every intent, each root by number with the intents beneath it after it, and gives each its kind and base, its `parent`, `maker`, `addressee`, `children` and `depth`, its `limits` as set on it and its `limits_read_up` (each of forces, risk, area and priority as the staff reads it, with the intent it is read from and, where none is set, what then holds), for a hold intent its `measure` (Holding a place, above), for a neutralise intent its `measure` (Neutralising a base, below), for a deny intent its `measure` (Denying an area, above), its `hex` and `distance` for a deny intent (`base` is null for a deny too), its `target` for a neutralise or a raid intent (`base` is null for a neutralise), its `task_force` (the side's number; null for the other kinds) and its `hex` (the station) for a stand-at or an operate-carriers intent (`base` is null for these too), and notes for a supply intent given to the area with no forces limit and for each intent the staff formed. It gives each intent in force its place in the priority order (`served`, by the rank read up the tree, as the doctrine serves it) and what displaces it today (`displaced_by`), and the side's `holds`: each with its target, the day, whether a `hold` command or an order made it, and that order's number. It gives too the side's `interrupts`: each interrupt set with its base, its number of days, the day it was set, whether it is armed, the day it last fired, whether that is acknowledged and whether it needs acknowledging; each standing record that has fired, by its kind and its plan or ship; and the kinds switched off. Those fired and not acknowledged come first.
- `set_intent` (the side's next free number when none is given; `intent_kind` is `keep_supplied`, the default, `hold`, `cover`, `neutralise`, `raid`, `deny`, `strike_ships`, `search_waters`, `stand_at` or `operate_carriers`; `floor_days` and `interval_days` belong to `keep_supplied` alone; `neutralise` takes a `target`, the number of an enemy base on the side's map, and no `base`; `raid` takes a `base` of the side's and a `target`; no other kind takes a `target`; `deny` takes `hex` as `[q, r]` and `distance` in whole hexes and no `base`; `strike_ships` takes a `base` of the side's; `search_waters` is refused as "Not a root" and is the staff's alone; `stand_at` and `operate_carriers` take a `task_force`, one of the side's own forces in its numbers (an enemy force and one that does not exist are refused alike), and a `hex` as `[q, r]` and no `base`; no other kind takes a `task_force`, and no other kind but these and `deny` takes a `hex`; only `deny` takes a `distance`), `withdraw_intent`, `set_limit`, `set_leave`, `hold`, `release`, `approve`, `cancel_plan`, `set_interrupt` and `acknowledge`: each queues one command for the coming day, taken in at its first step. It is checked at once, by taking it in on a copy of the world after the commands already queued, and answered queued or refused with the engine's reason. `set_limit` forces takes `ships`, `task_forces`, `air_groups` and `source`, each optional, and `set_limit` works on any intent. `hold` and `release` name exactly one of a ship, a task force, or an air group, which is held as a whole with no setting and by its search arc alone with the setting `search_arc`, in the side's own numbers, and a number that is not the side's is refused as the engine refuses it. `set_interrupt` takes `interrupt` (the side's next free number, one past the highest ever set or queued, when none is given), `base` and `below_days`; or `standing` (`cannot_meet`, `waits_for_leave` or `ship_lost`) and `on`; a table that mixes the two is refused. `acknowledge` takes `interrupt`, or `standing` with one of `plan` and `ship`, in the side's own numbers.
- `preview`: what a changed intent or limit would do, for `set_intent` and `set_limit` only, worked on two copies of the world as the coming day will find it (the side's queued commands taken in, and the same with the changed command last). It stores nothing: no state, no event, no hash, nothing queued. It sets what the staff would do on the coming day for the intent as things stand beside what it would do with the change (its orders, commands and decisions to give no order, the next run and the runs over 30 days, the first day below the floor and what binds), and says which ships are held and so not free, what each run still in hand would do (a run that has sailed goes on to the base it was sent to and lands there; a run still loading is sent to the new base; a change that is not of the base leaves every run as ordered), what is lost that was made ready (nothing: stock aboard a ship stays aboard and is carried by its next run, and stock landed stays where it lies), and what other plans give up. For a deny or a neutralise whose plan waits for a leave, the decisions to give no order carry one more, the proposal's: what the staff would do were the plan released, a carrier force and its station among it (decision 0202, 24). A command the tool would refuse is answered as the refusal and is not numbered. **Its written part** (agent interface version 57): a `set_intent` takes `under`, a list of sub-intents, each a `set_intent` body without `parent`, with its own number where he gives one and its own `limits`. The order, its limits, and each sub-intent followed by its own limits are taken in on the copy in that order and worked as one, so the staff's reading is of the whole draft: the order with each landing in force under it and the island's plan read as released. A sub-intent given no number takes one past the highest number the side has ever set, has queued, or that stands before it in the batch; an "after" in the batch may name an earlier sub-intent of it by its number. The answer gains `under`: for each sub-intent its number, its command, its words and its limits. Where the engine would refuse any command of the batch, the answer is that refusal with its place in the batch and the batch's size (`of_batch`), the order being place 1, then its own limits, then each sub-intent followed by its own limits; nothing is numbered and nothing is queued. A fault of the call itself (`under` given with a `set_limit`, `under` that is not a list or an entry that is not an object, an entry that carries `parent`, a limit of an entry that carries `intent` or is a reserve) is an error of the call that names what is wrong, as a bad `limits` is, and not a refusal of the engine's. A sub-intent under an order that stands (in force or queued) is previewed as one `set_intent` with `parent` and its `limits`, as above. [0232, rules 20 and 22]
- `approve` with a `preview` number in place of a plan queues exactly the command that preview showed, and the limits given with it (decision 0222), answered as any queued command is. A preview is **void** once the side has queued an order or a command, or emptied its queue, or a day has been resolved, since it was made; approving a void preview is refused and says why (what was queued after it, or that a day was resolved). Of a preview with a written part it queues all of it or none, in the batch's order: the order, its limits, then each sub-intent with its own limits; its answer gains `under_queued`, for each sub-intent its number and the count of its limits, and `limits_queued` counts the order's own. A batch the engine would refuse was never numbered, so it cannot be approved; one that was numbered is queued whole or, void, not at all. It is the session's, not the world's. [0107, ruling 26; 0232, rule 21]
- `propose`: the staff's proposal for an intent, with what it is displaced by and what it displaces (`displaced_by`, `displaced_intents`, and in words `displaces`). For a hold, a cover, a neutralise, a raid or a deny intent it also gives the `tree` the staff would form (Holding a place, A proposal): the sub-intents, each with its number as the side's next free numbers give it, parent, maker and reason (and, first, for an island, an entry for each intent of the commander's beneath it, with its own number: Since decision 0232, above); the air groups each intent would order with the order and its working; the search arcs with their threats; and the decisions to give no order. It is null for a supply intent.
- `staff_orders`: what the staff would hand in at the start of the coming day, each with its reason, asked on a copy of the world with the day's first three steps taken (`World::at_staff_step`, which takes the override too). Only the session holds that copy; the staff and the tools are given its side's view.
- `run`: resolves up to `days` days (a whole number, at least 1), each exactly as `end_day` does, with the orders and commands queued going in on the first day. It stops after a day on which any interrupt of the side fired, or at the end of the game, or at its number of days, whichever comes first. The clock stops at the end of the day of a firing, not before it: nothing is ordered inside a day and the commander acts the next morning. The one exception is a force's asking leave to pursue (Interrupts, above): `run` stops in the evening before the day the staff would ask in, with `stopped` of `asks_leave` and the asking in its answer (chapter 1, The stop in the evening), and does not stop after that day for the same firing. It gives the staff's orders for the first day, worked before that day is resolved as `staff_orders` works them; the days resolved; why it stopped (`interrupt`, `game_over` or `days`); and everything that fired on the last day, each with its day, its event number, whether it is acknowledged and the `why` numbers of the reasons its notices rest on, with the result when the game is over. It takes an optional `reason`, which must be text and changes nothing. It is offered only where exactly one seat of the game is held by a player; with none or more it is not among the tools, and a call is refused, saying to use `end_day`. [0107 item 29, ruling 36]
- `report`: the command report, with no argument. It is worked from the side's own events and stored nowhere, so it is the same after a save and a resume. Its matters: a **matter** is a plan, a run where there is one and a kind of notice (the two leaves are two kinds), as the staff handed it in. It is open on the day last resolved if its notice was handed in that day, and closed otherwise, from the day after its last notice; handed in again later it is the same matter, with a gap in its history. Each matter gives its plan and intent, run, kind, whether it is open, the day it was first handed in and the day it closed, and its history: one entry for each day a notice of it was handed in, with the `why` number of the reason that day's notice rests on, so that `why` reads each day's reason. Open matters come first, then closed ones, each by the day first handed in, then plan, run and kind. The report also gives the interrupts as `command_state` does, the firings of the day last resolved, `holds` (the measure of each hold intent in force, with its intent, its base and its words), `neutralisations` (the measure of each neutralise intent in force, with its intent, its target and its words), `denials` (the measure of each deny intent in force, with its intent and its words) and a summary in words, which ends with a sentence for each hold intent and for each neutralise intent. [0107 item 30, ruling 37; 0117 item 14]
- `order`: the plan order of one intent of the commander's, or of a "take a place" the staff formed, by its number (The plan order, above), worked from the view the coming day finds (the queued commands taken in) and stored nowhere. Any other intent of the staff's, or one the side does not have, is refused as `propose` refuses an intent not in force. A take the commander wrote is his intent and has an order of its own (decision 0232). Since decision 0159, rule E (agent interface version 26; the answer kind `plan_order`); a take the staff formed since decision 0164, with the answer's shape and the interface version unchanged.
- `why`: one `StaffReason` by its event number, whole, with the order or command it is for, or as a decision to give no order; its `serves` is the statement in words, then "decided at the level of" the area or the base (its `level`, null for a reason of a game saved before it was handed in), and its `chain` lists the intents above the one served, nearest first, each as "under intent N (the commander's)" with its statement, the last the commander's own. `staff_orders` gives the same chain in each reason's `serves`.
- `queued` and `take_back` (decision 0221, rules 15 to 18; agent interface version 46) are tools of every seat, with or without a staff, and are the session's. `queued` lists what the seat has queued, the orders for the next pulse, the orders for dusk and the commands, each with its place, its unit and order or its tool and command in the side's numbers (a ship, task force, air group, land unit or submarine by the number every other tool gives it, never the engine's), when it acts, what is taken back with it and what a hold rests on, and an `as_of`. `take_back` withdraws the entry at a place, given the latest `as_of`, with what goes with it: the holds queued on a unit when the last queued order to it goes, and the commands queued for a new intent when the `set_intent` that makes it goes: for an order, its limits and the sub-intents queued under it with their limits (decision 0232, rule 21); for a sub-intent queued alone, its own limits. A sub-intent that another queued command rests on (an "after" that names it) is not taken back before that command, by this rule as it is. It is refused, and nothing changes, where another queued entry would fail without it, naming that entry and its place. A preview made before a take back is void after it. Neither is in the state, the hash or a saved game.

A tool is not a rule: what it shows is worked from the side's view, and the agent's schemas say each answer's shape (`crates/sim-agent/schemas/`).

## A scenario's commands

A scenario may state a side's commands by day, as it states orders, so that a test can begin with an intent in force. A `[[command]]` table has `day`, `side`, `kind` and the keys of its kind, and no other key. Only the commander's kinds may be written; the staff's are the staff's to give, `form_intent` among them: a scenario states a commander's end, as a root, and the staff forms what serves it. A scenario with no `[[command]]` plays as it did. [0107 item 16]

| --- | --- |

| `set_leave` | `leave` (`"prepare"` or `"launch"`) and `given` |
| `approve`, `cancel_plan` | `plan` |
| `hold`, `release` | Exactly one of `ship`, `task_force`, `air_group`, `land_unit` and `submarine`; with `air_group`, `setting` (`"search_arc"`) holds that setting alone and no `setting` holds the whole group; no other target takes a `setting` |
| `set_interrupt` | Either `interrupt`, `base` and `below_days` (a numbered interrupt set), or `standing` (`"cannot_meet"`, `"waits_for_leave"`, `"ship_lost"`, `"cannot_bring_enough"`, `"lift_turned_back"` or `"asks_leave_to_pursue"`) and `on` (`true` or `false`) |
| `acknowledge` | Either `interrupt`, or `standing` with exactly one of `plan`, `ship` and `task_force` (a standing interrupt for a task force, `asks_leave_to_pursue`) |
| `set_pursuit` | `intent` and `leave` (`"ask"`, `"go"` or `"stay"`; left out clears the setting so that it is read up the tree again) (decision 0214) |
| `answer_pursuit` | `task_force`, `contact` (the commander's number of the contact) and `allow` (a bool) (decision 0214) |

A `set_intent` may take `parent`, an intent number: the intent is then a sub-intent of the commander's under that parent, and the engine's refusals of A sub-intent the commander states apply when the command is taken in. Left out for an intent in force it is read as the parent it has. (Decision 0232.)

The limits: `"forces"` takes `intent` and, each optional (a list not given is empty), `ships`, `task_forces`, `air_groups`, `land_units` and `submarines`, and `source` (a supply intent's forces limit must name one, another kind's need not); `"area"` takes `intent` and `hexes`, as `[[q, r], ...]`; `"priority"` takes `intent` and `rank`; `"protected"` takes `intent` and `ships`; `"reserve"` takes `base`, `supply_t` and `fuel_t`; `"risk"` takes `intent` and `risk`, `"preserve"`, `"balanced"` or `"press"`; `"not_before"` takes `intent` and `from_day`; `"wait_for"` takes `intent` and one of `task_force` and `land_unit` (both is refused); `"after"` takes `intent` and `other_intent` (decision 0159, 9). `"targets"` takes `intent` and, each optional, `kinds` (a list of ship kinds by name, each once, most wanted first; left out clears the limit) and `waters_first` (`true` or `false`, `false` where left out; decision 0190). Each of the last three before it with its other key left out clears the limit. A quantity is a whole number or a decimal string, never a float.

The loader refuses, when the scenario is read: a day that is 0 or after the scenario's last day; a key the kind does not take, or that is no key of any command; a missing key; a TOML float; a kind that is the staff's (`end_run` among them); an unknown limit, leave, risk or setting (a `set_pursuit` leave that is none of ask, go and stay among them), a `set_pursuit` with no `intent`, an `answer_pursuit` with no `task_force`, `contact` or `allow`; a hold or release that names none or more than one target, or a `setting` with a ship, a task force, a land unit or a submarine; a `patrol` without `hex` or `distance`, or with a `base`; an `intent_kind` that is none of the ten, a `deny` without `hex` or `distance`, a `deny` with a `base`, a `stand_at` or `operate_carriers` without `task_force` or `hex` or with a `base`, a `distance` on a kind but deny, a `hex` on a kind but deny, stand_at and operate_carriers, a `task_force` on another kind, or a `cover` or `hold` with a key only a supply intent takes; a `set_interrupt` or `acknowledge` that mixes the keys of its two forms; a standing kind that is none of the six; a `set_interrupt` with `standing` and no `on`; an `acknowledge` with `standing` and not exactly one of `plan`, `ship` and `task_force`. Whether a base, ship, task force, intent, plan or interrupt exists, whether a number of days is 0 and whether an interrupt has fired are not the loader's to say: the engine checks it when the command is taken in, and refuses it as above. A scenario's commands are kept by day in the order written, kept when a seed is changed, and handed in before the day is resolved, as the commander's, so the engine takes them in at its first step. Two commands of one day for one side are taken in in the order written, and the Allied side's before the Japanese side's. [0107 item 16]

A scenario's `[[island]]` tables name the islands a `control_island` may name (Controlling an island, above): a `number`, a `name` and `places`, each `{ base = N }` or `{ hex = [q, r] }`. The loader refuses what Controlling an island says it refuses. Whether the island a `control_island` names exists is the engine's to say when the command is taken in, and it is refused as "No such island". [0151 item 1]

A scenario's `[[order]]` may give the issuer `staff` and an `intent`. Such an order is not checked against a forces limit or against the holds, and the override skips it (above, The forces limit on a staff's orders; Holds and the override). [0107, As built]

## The day's order

Chapter 1 owns the sequence. Where this chapter's rules run, in the numbering of decision 0107's table (which chapter 1 now numbers the same: the staff is asked at its step 4, and its commands, notices, interrupts and the orders intake are its step 5):

| Step | What happens | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The commander's commands are taken in, `hold` and `release` among them | This chapter, Commands |
| 2 | The override: the commander's orders to units in an intent's forces limit (an air group an intent may use, and a boat a patrol may use or a limit names, among them) are taken in and hold them. In the code it runs after step 3 | This chapter, Holds and the override |
| 3 | Forces back from the yard are home. Ships in port are not refuelled here: they take fuel in the loading phase of each pulse, each base down to its reserve | Chapter 4, Repair; chapter 2, Refuelling in port |
| 4 | Each side's staff, if it has one, is asked on its view as it now stands. Then the days of supply of the base of each interrupt the commander set are read on that same view | This chapter, The staff; Interrupts |
| 5 | The staff's commands are taken in (the runs set aside first; the air doctrine's after the supply doctrine's, with `form_intent` among them), then its decisions to give no order are recorded, then its notices; then each side's interrupts are tested, on those readings and those notices; then all orders not already taken in, the commander's and the staff's | This chapter, The staff; Notices; Interrupts; chapter 1, Orders |
| 6 | The rest of the day, as it was | Chapters 2 to 7 |

**The staff's morning, in its order** (what each of these does, in the sections named; one pass over the side's view as the staff step leaves it, the sub-intents formed in the pass read by the doctrines after them in the same pass):

1. The supply doctrine, for the keep-supplied intents.
2. The air doctrine's leaves, for each hold or cover root and, since decision 0120, each neutralise or raid root, and, since decision 0123, each deny or strike-ships root, and, since decisions 0124 and 0125, each stand-at or operate-carriers root, and, since decision 0151, each control-an-island root. **The island doctrine runs right after these leaves and before every other air doctrine:** it forms the island's takes, holds, denial, carrier tasks and neutralisings (Controlling an island, above), so that what it forms is served by the doctrines after it the same morning. It gives no order. **The carrier pass** runs right after it (decision 0202): the free carrier forces handed out to the commander's denies and neutralises, with their tasks formed, kept or withdrawn (Carriers serving an order for an end, below).
3. The hold doctrine: the stand and carrier tasks of the forces named in its forces limit, then cover under each held base (A task force as an addressee, above).
4. The neutralise doctrine: raid tasks under each neutralise intent (Neutralising a base, below). It forms sub-intents and gives no order.
5. The deny doctrine: strike-ships and search-waters tasks under each deny intent, then the stand and carrier tasks of the forces named in its forces limit (Denying an area and A task force as an addressee, above). It forms sub-intents and gives no order.
6. The cover doctrine, over its groups, leaving a raid's or a strike-ships task's bomber out; it reads which fighters it keeps and at what share, and takes the waters of a released deny intent its searchers reach as threats beside its own (Covering a base from the air, A raid's bomber; Searching waters, above).
7. The raid doctrine, over its groups, which reads what the cover wants of the fighters it has, and leaves out a bomber a strike-ships task of higher rank may use (Raiding a base, below).
8. The strike doctrine, over its groups (Striking ships from a base, above).
9. The search doctrine, over its groups (Searching waters, above).
10. The stand doctrine, over the stand tasks: each force's orders and a stand's reasons (Standing at a hex by night, above).
11. The carrier doctrine, over the carrier tasks: each force's orders and its groups' (Operating a carrier force, above), with the raid of a carrier task under a neutralise (Carriers serving an order for an end, below), except those standing under a "take a place" or under the denial of an island, which step 14 serves.
12. "Take a place" forms its landing, the tasks that ready units are served, and "take a place" launches it (Readying a landing, and building up a base; Taking a place, above).
13. The bombard doctrine: bombard tasks formed under each released neutralise and take-a-place end, then each bombard task's orders (Bombarding a base, above).
14. The carrier tasks under "take a place": formed under each end whose plan is released, or, to look, under each end whose plan is preparing (Operating a carrier force, item 25), then each served by the carrier doctrine with the strike on the place, or, on the first morning the end is met, sent home (Operating a carrier force, Under take a place, above). The carrier tasks under the denial of an island are served here too, with the takes' carrier tasks, after the landing's morning, and are never sent home (Operating a carrier force, Under "control an island"). [0148; 0151]
15. The patrol doctrine, last: for each patrol intent in force whose plan is released, in priority order, the stations of the boats it may use and their orders (Patrolling waters with submarines, above). It forms nothing, reads no other doctrine's work and is read by none, so no other doctrine's hand-in changes for a game with no patrol. A boat held by the commander that morning is passed over, since the hold was made at step 2. [0218, rule 54]

Orders are handed in in that order and are taken in after the commander's, as above. [0107, The order of a day; rulings 23, 30 and 31; 0120; 0123; 0124; 0125; 0218]
