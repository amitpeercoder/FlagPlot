# The staff

This chapter holds what a side's commander says as state, the commands that change it, the staff that a side may have, the reasons the staff gives, and days of supply. It is written by step 1 of stage 1 of step 3: the engine's half (command state, commands, the staff passed in, days of supply) and the staff's half (the crate `sim-staff`, the doctrine for keeping a base supplied, the reasons of staff commands, the staff's projection of days of supply, and the tools a seat commands with), step 2 adds priority between intents, commitments of stock and displaced demand, and step 3 adds holds, the override of a hold by the commander's own order, ending a run, the supply a free ship has aboard, the fallback of a cancelled plan, previews and the tools `hold`, `release` and `preview`, and step 4 adds interrupts and their acknowledgement, the staff's notices, a run of days that stops for an interrupt, the command report and the tools `set_interrupt`, `acknowledge`, `run` and `report`. Step 2 of stage 2 adds the intent tree (an intent has a parent, a maker and an addressee, and the staff forms sub-intents), the end "hold a place", the task "cover a base from the air" with the air doctrine that serves them, air groups in a forces limit, a hold on a whole air group, limits read up the tree and the measure of a hold intent. Step 3 of stage 2 adds the level a doctrine belongs to, the end "neutralise a base" with its doctrine at the area's level, the task "raid a base" with its doctrine at the base's level, and the cover's give of a raid's bomber and its escorts. Step 4 adds the end "deny an area" with its doctrine at the area's level, the tasks "strike ships from a base" and "search waters from a base" with their doctrines at the base's level, the size of a strike decided at the group's level, and the cover's arc over the waters to deny. The second and third parts of step 4 add the task force as a level that an intent is addressed to, the task "stand at a hex" with its doctrine at the task force's level and the rule of the engine it needs (the night stand, chapter 2), and the task "operate a carrier force" with its doctrine. Every side of every game has the staff; a side with no intent in force has a staff that does nothing, so a game with no command is played exactly as it was before this chapter. [0107, 0111, 0115, 0116, 0117, 0120, 0123, 0124, 0125]

No rule here draws a random number. Every quantity is a fixed-point number (chapter 1, Numbers and chance).

## Command state

Each side has one command state, held by the world beside its belief and hashed after the beliefs, Allied side first (chapter 1, The state hash). It holds the side's intents with their limits, its two leaves, its plans, its commitments, its holds and its interrupts. It changes only by a command, by the test of its interrupts each morning (Interrupts, below), or by the override of a hold, which an order of the commander's makes (Holds and the override, below). The enemy has none of it, and a side is never told whether the other has a staff. [0107 items 1 and 6]

A side sees: its own command state, whole, through its view. It sees none of the other side's. [0107 item 6]

### Intents

An intent is what the commander wants. Twelve kinds are built. Three name a base of the side, the two of decision 0120 name an enemy base as their target (Neutralising a base and Raiding a base, below), and the three of decision 0123 are Denying an area, Striking ships from a base and Searching waters (below). **Keep a base supplied** names a base, a floor in days of supply (at least 1) and, optionally, an interval in days (at least 1) between shipments. The floor is the days of supply the base's hex is never to fall below. The interval is the days of use each run is to bring above the floor. Where the commander gives none, the interval is not set and the staff proposes one. **Hold a place** and **cover a base from the air** name a base and nothing else (Holding a place and Covering a base from the air, below). **Neutralise a base** names a target, a base on the side's map that is not the side's, and no base of the side's own. **Raid a base** names a base of the side and a target (Neutralising a base and Raiding a base, below). **Deny an area** (code 6) names a hex and a distance in whole hexes and no base; **strike ships from a base** (code 7) and **search waters from a base** (code 8) name a base of the side (Denying an area, Striking ships from a base and Searching waters, below). The two of decisions 0124 and 0125 name a task force of the side and a hex and no base: **stand at a hex** (code 9) is the task of keeping the force at that hex for the night (Standing at a hex by night, below), and **operate a carrier force** (code 10) the task of the groups and the place of a force with a carrier in it (Operating a carrier force, below). The two of decision 0141 order land units (Readying a landing, and building up a base, below): **ready a landing** (code 11) names a task force of the side, a coast hex on the map and, optionally, a date, the morning by which it is to be ready (a whole number from 1); **build up a base** (code 12) names a base of the side and what is wanted there, in order: one to five of an airfield, its damage mended, defences to a number of points from 1 to 100, the port worked and aircraft repair, each at most once. A "ready a landing" whose hex is not a coast hex on the map is refused as "Not a coast hex". Every other intent has no date: it stands until it is withdrawn, and so does a "ready a landing", whose date says when it is to be ready and not when it ends. [0107 items 17 and 22, question 5; 0115; 0117; 0120, 5 and 9; 0124, 7; 0125, 1; 0141, 1 and 8]

The commander names the intent's number when he sets it. It is a whole number from 1. A withdrawn intent keeps its number in the state and the number is never used again. An intent records the day it was first set, the plan that serves it, the day it was withdrawn, if it was, and, since the tree (The intent tree, below), its parent, its maker and its addressee. [0107, 0117]

An intent in force carries its limits as they stand. Setting an intent that is in force again may change its figures and not its kind: it replaces its base (and a supply intent's floor and interval) together, and leaves its limits, its parent and its maker as they are. A `set_intent` that names another kind of intent than the one in force is refused as "Kind cannot change"; the commander withdraws the intent and states another. [0107, 0117, "As built", the architect's ruling after the build]

### The six limits

A limit says what the staff may use and where it may go. Five belong to an intent and one to a base. A limit is read up the tree: every limit set on the intent or above it binds it, the narrowest binding (The intent tree, below). A limit set nowhere above it does not bind, with one exception that follows from what it is: an intent with no forces limit anywhere above it may use what its addressee commands, which for an intent given to the area is nothing, so it gives the staff no ship and no order for it, with the one exception of a neutralise intent (Neutralising a base, below); for one given to a base it is the side's air groups based there. With no area limit the sea is open. [0107; the architect's ruling, Nov 15, 2026; 0117 item 8]

| Limit | What it holds | What reads it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forces | The pool: a set of ships, a set of task forces, a set of air groups, a source base, and a set of land units (decision 0141; a land unit not the side's is refused as "Not your land unit"). A supply intent's forces limit must name exactly one source (a `set_limit` of forces without one is refused as "No source"); the forces limit of another kind of intent need not | The check of a staff's order and of a staff command that names ships (below); the air doctrine, for the groups it may order; the two tasks of decision 0141, for the land units they may order |
| Area | A set of hexes the intent's forces may enter. It is not empty, and every hex is on the map. An area not set on it or above it does not bind: the sea is open | The doctrine's route |
| Priority | A rank, 1 first, or none. An intent with none, on it and above it, comes after every ranked intent, then by number | The order in which the doctrine serves intents (the rank read up the tree, for the air doctrine) |
| Protected | A set of ships the staff may not take from the intent | The doctrine: no other intent's plan takes a protected ship, free or not, whatever the ranks (The doctrine, below) |
| Risk | Preserve, balanced or press. Balanced where none is set on the intent or above it | The cover doctrine: the share of the patrol and the fatigue at which a group rests (Covering a base from the air, below) |
| Reserve | For a base, a supply reserve and a fuel reserve, in tons, neither below 0 | Refuelling, and a load the staff orders (chapter 2, Refuelling in port and Cargo orders) |

[0107 items 18 to 20, 0111, 0115 items 2 and 4, 0117 items 7 and 8]

A ship is in the pool of a forces limit if the limit lists it, or if it lies in a task force the limit lists, afloat in it or away from it at the yard. A task force is inside the limit if the limit lists it, or if it has at least one ship and every ship of it is a ship the limit lists. An air group is one an intent **may use** if it is one of the side's own and either the intent's forces limit, its own or the nearest above it, lists it, or no forces limit is set on it or above it and the group is at the base the intent is addressed to (an intent given to the area may use no group without a forces limit; a group aboard ship or in no base is not at a base). An intent given to a task force may use that force's ships and nothing else, and the air groups aboard them where every forces limit on its chain names the group or has the force inside it (A task force as an addressee, below). [0107, 0115 item 2, 0117 item 8, 0124 item 6, 0125 item 1]

The reserve limit names a base and no intent. It writes the base's own two reserves (chapter 2, Bases on hexes): one store, not a second. A scenario's base entry gives the same two figures (`supply_reserve_t` and `fuel_reserve_t`). It is taken in at the first step of the day, so it binds that morning's refuelling. [0111, 0107]

### The two leaves

A side's commander holds two leaves for his staff. Leave to prepare lets the staff itself move a draft plan to preparing. Leave to launch lets it move a plan that is preparing to released. The defaults are the spec's for a player: prepare given, launch withheld. Only the commander sets a leave, and he may set either on any day. [0107 item 21]

### Plans

A plan is what the staff does to serve one intent and the intents beneath it: one plan for a tree. It is drafted when a root intent's number is first set, and numbered in the side's own series, 1, 2, 3 and on, so a side's plan numbers depend on its own history only. A sub-intent has no plan of its own: it belongs to its root's, and forming it drafts nothing and is no amendment (The intent tree, below). A plan has one of six states:

| State | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Draft | Drafted, not yet approved |
| Preparing | Approved for preparation. It has a baseline |
| Released | Approved to run: it may be carried out |
| Complete | Ended: its intent was withdrawn while it was released, or the staff marked it complete |
| Failed | Ended: the staff marked it failed |
| Cancelled | Ended: the commander cancelled it, or its intent was withdrawn before it was released |

Preparing and released are together **approved**. Complete, failed and cancelled are **ended**: nothing moves an ended plan again. The moves are these, and no other:

- Draft to preparing, and preparing to released, by an `approve` command (below).
- A plan that is not ended to cancelled, by a `cancel_plan` command.
- An approved plan to complete or to failed, by a `mark_plan` command, which only the staff gives.
- A plan that is not ended, when its root intent is withdrawn: a released plan becomes complete and a draft or preparing one becomes cancelled. [0107 items 22 to 24]

Setting again an intent that is in force whose plan has ended drafts a new plan for it, with the next number, and the intent serves from then on. A plan that has ended with its intent still in force does not withdraw the intent. [0107]

Limit: decision 0107, item 23, says a plan's phase begins when it is released and ends when the intent is withdrawn. The code has no phase state. A plan's runs are tasks held in the plan, and its state is the one of the six above (differences.md row 42). [0107 item 23]

### The baseline and amendments

A plan's **baseline** is taken the first time it is approved: the day, the intent's kind and the intent's limits as they stand that day. It never changes. A plan that is not yet approved has none.

An **amendment** is a numbered change to a plan that is approved. While a plan is approved, a `set_intent` for its intent, and a `set_limit` of a forces, area, priority, protected or risk limit of its intent (or of any intent beneath it, which shares its plan), apply to the intent as they stand and are also kept in the plan as its next amendment, numbered 1 and up, with the day and the change. A reserve is no amendment, because it names a base and no intent. Withdrawing the intent is none either: it ends the plan. A change made while the plan is a draft, or after it has ended, changes the intent and is no amendment: a draft's change enters its baseline when it is approved. [0107 items 22 and 25]

What the staff may change alone in a plan is its runs and commitments, inside the limits. A change to the days, the base or a limit, and cancelling, are the commander's. [0107, question 3]

### Runs

A run is one convoy of a supply plan: a task inside the plan's one phase. It has a number in its plan (1 and up), its ships, its load in tons of supply (above 0), its source, which is the source base of its intent's forces limit on the day the run is added, and four days:

| Day | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Load | The day loading begins |
| Sail | The day the force sails |
| Arrive | The day it is due at the destination |
| Free | The day its ships are free: the morning after the day they have finished fuelling at the source on their return (by the hours of the fuelling, below, Its days) |

The days are in this order or equal. A run added has its load day no earlier than the day the command is taken in, and a run timed again has its free day no earlier than that day. A run does nothing by itself. The orders that carry it out, form the force, load it to a weight, send it and unload it, are the staff's to give on the days it names. [0107 items 26 and 27, rulings 7 and 8]

A run also records **the day it ended**, none while it has not: the day an `end_run` command was taken in (below). An ended run is ended for good: nothing takes it up again. It holds no ship, is pending no load and is counted by no reading, and so is a run of a plan that has ended: the staff reads both as finished. What is aboard its ships stays aboard, and ending a run frees no commitment: that is a command of its own. [0107, rulings 20 and 29]

### Commitments

A commitment gives one ship to one plan, and to one of its runs if named, from one day to another, both days included. A ship holds at most one commitment on any day: a commitment that overlaps another of the same ship, whatever plan it is for, is refused. The staff commits ships and frees them. Freeing a ship ends every commitment it has to the plan. No rule of the day reads a commitment: it is the staff's record, and the check of the next commitment reads it. A commitment to a plan that has ended holds no ship from the staff's reading (The doctrine, below).

A **commitment of stock** is no record of its own: it is the run. A ton at a run's source is committed to its plan from the day the run is added until it is aboard: the run's load less the supply aboard its ships while it loads there. The stock at a source that a new run may draw on is the stock, less the reserve, less those tons for the runs of every plan of the side. [0107 item 27, ruling 12]

## Commands

A command is a change to the side's command state, handed in beside orders. It is checked against the side's own units and its command state, and it is accepted or refused with a reason, as an order is. A refused command changes nothing but the log. [0107 items 1 and 2]

### Who may give each command

| Command | Given by | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `set_intent` | Commander | Sets an intent (a root), or changes the figures of one in force that the commander made, never its kind |
| `withdraw_intent` | Commander | Withdraws an intent in force that the commander made, everything beneath it, and ends its plan |
| `set_limit` | Commander, or the staff for a forces limit | Sets one limit, on any intent; the staff sets a forces limit only, and only on an intent it formed (decision 0142, 12) |
| `set_leave` | Commander | Gives or withholds leave to prepare, or leave to launch |
| `approve` | Commander, or the staff with leave | Moves a draft to preparing, or a preparing plan to released |
| `cancel_plan` | Commander | Cancels a plan that has not ended |
| `hold` | Commander | Holds a ship, a task force, a whole air group or one setting of an air group (its search arc) from the staff |
| `release` | Commander | Lifts a hold, whatever made it |
| `set_interrupt` | Commander | Sets a numbered interrupt, or sets it again, or switches a standing kind off or on (Interrupts, below) |
| `acknowledge` | Commander | Marks the last firing of an interrupt seen |
| `commit_ships` | Staff | Commits ships of the intent's pool to an approved plan from one day to another |
| `free_ships` | Staff | Ends the commitments of ships to a plan |
| `add_run` | Staff | Adds a run to an approved plan, with the plan's next run number |
| `time_run` | Staff | Gives a run new days |
| `mark_plan` | Staff | Marks an approved plan complete or failed |
| `end_run` | Staff | Ends a run of an approved plan for good (Holds and the override, below; The doctrine) |
| `form_intent` | Staff | Forms a sub-intent under an intent in force whose plan is released, or, for a "ready a landing" under "take a place", preparing or released (The intent tree, below; decision 0142, 12) |

A command from the other issuer is refused as not yours. A staff may give `approve` only where the commander has given it the leave for the move: leave to prepare for a draft, leave to launch for a plan that is preparing. The commander's `approve` needs no leave. [0107 items 4 and 21]

The commander's commands are handed in with the orders for a day and taken in at its first step, the Allied side's first, each side's in the order handed in, so a hold or a release given for a day binds from that morning, before the staff is asked. The staff's are taken in after the staff is asked, in the order handed in, before any order. [0107, The order of a day; rulings 17 and 23]

| `commit_ships` | Not your ship, not in the pool, held, already committed | For each ship in ship order, in that order: it is not the side's; it is not in the pool of the forces limit; it is held (itself, its force, or any ship of its force); it has a commitment that overlaps the days asked for |
| `free_ships` | No such plan | No plan of that number. A plan of any state is found |
| `free_ships` | Not committed | A ship has no commitment to the plan |
| `add_run` | No such plan, wrong plan state, no forces limit | As for `commit_ships` |
| `add_run` | No ships | The run names no ship |
| `add_run` | Not your ship, not in the pool, held | For each ship in ship order, in that order, as for `commit_ships` (a ship that is held is refused before the load and the days are looked at) |
| `add_run` | Bad weight | The load is not above 0 |
| `add_run` | Bad days | The days are not in order, or the load day is before the day of the command |
| `time_run` | No such plan, wrong plan state | As for `commit_ships` |
| `time_run` | No such run | The plan has no run of that number |
| `time_run` | Bad days | The days are not in order, or the free day is before the day of the command |
| `mark_plan` | No such plan, wrong plan state | As for `commit_ships` |
| `end_run` | No such plan, wrong plan state | As for `commit_ships`: the plan is not approved |
| `end_run` | No such run | The plan has no run of that number |
| `end_run` | Run ended | The run has ended already |

`set_leave` is refused only when the staff gives it. [0107 items 1, 4, 22 and 27, rulings 17, 20, 31 and 33]

The same intent number set twice. Two `set_intent` commands of one day that name the same new number are taken in in the order handed in: the first sets the intent and drafts its plan, and the second changes the intent that now exists. The plan is a draft, so the second is no amendment and drafts nothing. The intent is as the second left it. [0107]

### What a command does and records

A command that passes is applied, then `CommandTaken` is recorded with the whole command and its issuer. It has no cause: it is the root record of the change. A command that fails records `CommandRefused` with the command, its issuer and the reason, and has no cause. What an accepted command sets off is recorded after `CommandTaken` as an event that cites it:

| Event | Recorded when |
| --- | --- |
| `PlanDrafted` | An intent's number is first set, or an intent in force whose plan has ended is set again. Names the plan and the intent |
| `PlanStateChanged` | A plan changes state, by `approve`, `cancel_plan`, `mark_plan` or the withdrawal of its intent. Names the plan and both states |
| `PlanAmended` | A change is kept as an amendment. Names the plan and the amendment's number |
| `IntentFormed` | A `form_intent` is taken in. Names the side, the new intent's number and its parent |
| `IntentWithdrawn` | A `withdraw_intent` is taken in: one for each intent beneath the one withdrawn, in the order the tree gives (The intent tree, below). Names the side, the intent and the one it stood under |

A `set_leave`, a `hold`, a `release`, a `set_interrupt`, an `acknowledge`, a `commit_ships`, a `free_ships`, an `add_run`, a `time_run` and an `end_run` set off no other event: the command's own record, with the whole command, is the record of the change. There is no command number. Withdrawing an intent whose plan has already ended, and setting an intent that is a draft again, set off nothing. [0107]

One more event is recorded for a hold, and it is not a command's:

| Event | Recorded when |
| --- | --- |
| `HeldByOrder` | The override (below) holds a unit by an order of the commander's. Names the side, what is held and the order. It cites the order and the event that accepted it, and is recorded after them, at the step of the override, with no pulse |

A side sees: its own `CommandTaken`, `CommandRefused`, `PlanDrafted`, `PlanStateChanged`, `PlanAmended`, `HeldByOrder`, `IntentFormed` and `IntentWithdrawn`, whole, and the enemy sees none of them. Each of these kinds is decided in `side_events.rs`. The two events of step 5, `StaffNotice` and `InterruptFired`, are the same (Interrupts and Notices, below). [0107, ruling 23; 0117]

## The intent tree

Intents form a tree. An intent is an end, a state of the world the commander wants of one of the side's bases; the staff breaks an end into sub-intents, and orders are given at the leaves. The tree is state: it is hashed, changed only by commands, and rebuilt by a replay. The engine records and checks it and never reads a reason's words about it. [0116, 0117]

**What an intent gains.** Each intent has three more fields beside its kind and base:

| Field | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| Parent | The intent it stands under, or none for a **root** |
| Maker | The commander (by `set_intent`) or the staff (by `form_intent`) |
| Addressee | The level the intent is given to. It is set by the kind and chosen by nobody: keeping a base supplied, neutralising a base and denying an area are given to the area; holding a place, covering a base, raiding a base, striking ships from a base and searching waters from a base are given to the base they name (for a raid, the base it raids from). A task force is an addressee for the two tasks that name one: stand at a hex and operate a carrier force are given to the force they name (A task force as an addressee, below). An air group is a level too, but no intent is given to it (A doctrine belongs to a level, below). A land formation is not built as an addressee |

The level that owns an intent is the kind of its addressee. [0117 items 1 and 2; 0124 items 6 and 7]

**Roots and sub-intents.** A commander's `set_intent` makes a root, whatever its kind: a commander states a task such as cover himself by stating it as a root (every kind but search waters, which is refused as "Not a root"; stand at a hex and operate a carrier force he may state, naming the force and the hex). The staff's `form_intent` makes a sub-intent: it names the parent and the kind, and the new intent takes the number one above the highest the side has ever used (a withdrawn number is never reused). Its maker is the staff, its addressee is its kind's, its plan is its parent's (so its root's: one plan for a tree), it has no limits of its own, and it is set on the day the command is taken in. Intake refuses a `form_intent` as in Every reason a command is refused: the parent must be in force, its plan released, and the kind able to stand under the parent's kind. Nine pairs are built: cover from the air under hold a place for the same base, a raid under neutralise a base for the same target, strike ships and search waters under deny an area, strike ships under hold a place for the same base, stand at a hex under deny an area (the hex is the centre of the waters) and under hold a place (the hex is the base's, which intake checks), and operate a carrier force under deny an area and under hold a place; and, since, "ready a landing" under "take a place" at the place's hex (decision 0142) and "keep supplied" under "hold a place" for the same base (decision 0144, 7: its floor may be 0, the margin at press, and with no forces limit of its own the supply doctrine reads the nearest one up the tree, the hold's); a commander cannot name a parent. `form_intent` records `IntentFormed`, citing the command's `CommandTaken`; it drafts no plan and is no amendment. [0117 items 3, 4 and 9]

**A staff-made intent is the staff's.** `set_intent` and `withdraw_intent` on the number of an intent the staff formed are refused as "Made by the staff". The commander shapes it by its limits (below), by holds, and by withdrawing the intent it stands under. [0117 item 5]

**Withdrawing a tree.** A `withdraw_intent` withdraws the intent and every intent in force beneath it, depth first, each branch by number, all with the day of the command. Each intent beneath records an `IntentWithdrawn` event naming the intent it stood under, citing the command's `CommandTaken` (the intent named in the command has no event of its own: the command is its record). A withdrawn intent's plan ends as for any intent (Plans, above) when it is the root. [0117 item 6]

**Limits read up the tree.** The commander may set a limit on any intent in force, a staff-made one too (`set_limit` names the intent). Every limit set on the intent and above it binds it, so the narrowest binds: the risk is the least bold of those set on the chain, balanced where none is set; an air group may be used only where every forces limit on the chain names it, and a ship, a task force or a forming base is checked against every forces limit on the chain; the area is the hexes in every area set on the chain. The rank is the nearest set on the chain, and the protected limit is the intent's own. A limit set on a parent after one on its child binds the child from then on. (As first built, a child's own limit shadowed its parent's; the architect ruled after the build that the narrowest binds, decision 0117, "As built".) With no forces limit on it or above it, an intent may use what its addressee commands (The six limits, above). A limit set on an intent that is in force while its plan is approved is an amendment of the plan, whichever intent of the tree it is on. [0117 items 7 and 8]

**A limit must narrow.** On an intent that has a parent, `set_limit` of forces, risk or area is refused as "Wider than its parent" unless it narrows what the parent may use, read up the tree from the parent:

- **Forces:** each ship is in the parent's pool, each task force is inside the parent's limit, each air group is one the parent may use, and a source is named only where it is the parent's source. Where the parent has no forces limit on it or above it, the limit may name air groups the parent may use and no ship, task force or source.
- **Risk:** no bolder than the parent's, from preserve the force (the most careful) through balanced to press.
- **Area:** a subset of the parent's area. Where the parent has none, any area.

A priority, a protected set and a reserve are never refused for this. A limit on a root is not narrowed against anything. This reason is tried after every other reason of the limit. [0117 item 7. The rule for an area is in the code and decision 0117 does not state it]

**A reason names the chain above it.** A reason's serves part gains, for the intent it serves, the intents above it, nearest first: each with its number, its maker and its statement in words as it stood that morning. The top of every chain is an intent the commander stated. A reason for a root has none. The words follow the statement, for example "intent 2: cover base:1 from the air; plan 1; under intent 1 (the commander's): hold base:1". A game saved before the chain reads each reason with none. The engine records the chain and never reads it. [0117 item 10]

**Order among intents.** The staff serves intents of a kind in priority order: a rank, 1 first, read up the tree, then the intents with none, each by number. The supply doctrine serves keep-supplied intents (roots, and those the staff forms under a hold, decision 0144) as it always has; the air doctrine serves hold and cover intents (The doctrine, covering a base from the air, below). [0107 ruling 10; 0117 item 11]

**The tools** show the tree: `command_state` lists each intent under its parent with its maker, addressee, children and depth, and its limits as set and as read up the tree with the intent each is read from; `propose` shows the tree the staff would form; `why` and `staff_orders` give the chain (The tools, below). [0117]

A side sees: its own intents, whole, with `IntentFormed` and `IntentWithdrawn`, and the acceptance or refusal of each command. The enemy sees none of it. [0117]

## Holds and the override

A **hold** keeps one thing from the staff: the commander says "this is mine, not yours". It is a record in the side's command state, hashed with it, and it has no date of its own: it stands until a `release`. [0107, rulings 17 and 18]

**What can be held.** A ship (the side's own and afloat), a task force of the side, an air group of the side as a whole, and one setting of an air group, which today is its search arc (a group's search arc and the mission that sets it flying). A group held as a whole is given no order of any kind by the staff; a group whose search arc is held is given no order for its arc or its mission. A group and its search arc are two targets and each may have a hold. A land unit of the side may be held (decision 0141, 14): the staff gives it no order. A hold on a base waits for a stage whose doctrine acts on a base. A target has one hold: a second `hold` of a target already held is refused, whoever made the first. A hold records the day it was made and what made it: a `hold` command, or an order of the commander's (the override, below). A `release` lifts the hold on a target whatever made it, and a later order of the commander's to the unit holds it again (below). A hold moves nothing and stops nothing: a held unit keeps its standing orders and goes on by them. [0107, rulings 17 and 19]

**What a hold takes from the staff.** One rule says which ships are held, and every rule below uses it: a ship is held if it is held itself, if the task force it is in is held, or if any ship of that force is held. A held ship is not free to the staff, no ship of a held force is free, and no ship of a force that has a held ship in it is free: an order to that force would move the held ship. A held setting keeps the staff from changing it, and a held air group is one the staff gives no order to: the air doctrine leaves a group out of its work altogether if the group or its search arc is held (Covering a base from the air, below). Nothing else of the side is touched. The staff gives no order to what is held. [0107, ruling 18]

**The guard behind it.** The engine refuses what the staff would do to a held thing, so the doctrine's own care is not the only check:

- A staff command that names ships, `commit_ships` and `add_run`, is refused as held for a ship that is held (Every reason a command is refused).
- A staff's order is refused **held** ("the unit is held by its commander", with the target) if it is for a task force that is held or has a held ship in it; if it is an order to form a task force that names a held ship, or a ship in a held force; if it is any order for an air group that is held as a whole; or if it is a search-arc order or an air mission order for an air group whose search arc is held (a patrol-share order for such a group is not refused as held); or if it is any order for a land unit that is held (decision 0141, 14). It is tried before the check of the forces limit (The forces limit on a staff's orders, below), so a held unit is refused as held whether or not it lies in the limit. A base order other than forming is never refused as held.
- The guard binds orders handed in by a staff. It does not bind the commander's own orders, and it does not bind an order a scenario's script gives with the issuer `staff`, which is a scenario's statement (below).

A refused order is numbered, kept and recorded as refused, as any is. [0107, rulings 18 and 30]

**The override.** The commander's own order to a unit the staff works with holds that unit, from that morning. It is step 2 of the day (chapter 1, Before the pulses). In the code it runs after the returns from the yard and before the staff is asked. For each side with at least one intent in force:

1. The side's pending orders are walked in the order intake sorts them: orders to form a task force first, then the rest by the unit they are for. An order of the issuer staff is skipped, so a scenario's scripted staff order never overrides.
2. An order **overrides** if it is for a unit inside the forces limit of an intent of the side in force: a task force the limit lists, or one with at least one ship whose every ship the limit lists, or one that an intent of the side in force is addressed to (decision 0124, 6); or an order at any base to form a task force that names at least one ship of the pool of such a limit (a ship the limit lists or one in a task force it lists). Or it is an order of any kind for an air group that some intent of the side in force may use (The six limits, above: named in the forces limit read up the tree, or, with none, at the base the intent is addressed to; the intent's plan is not looked at). Or it is an order of any kind for a land unit that some intent of the side in force may use (Readying a landing, and building up a base, below), which holds the unit (decision 0141, 14). An order for a land unit no intent may use, a base order other than forming, a group no intent may use, or a unit in no forces limit and in no intent's addressing does not override: it stays pending, in the order handed in, for step 5, as it was.
3. An order that overrides is taken in at once: numbered then, ahead of every other order of the day, and checked as any order is. If it is accepted, it holds what it was for, a task force, an air group as a whole, a land unit, or each ship of the pool that a forming order names, and a `HeldByOrder` event is recorded for each, citing the order and its acceptance. An order that is refused holds nothing. A target that is held already gets no second hold, and the order is taken in all the same.

A side with no intent in force is not touched, and the day is as it was. `World::at_staff_step` takes the override too, so the view a staff is asked on shows the holds of the morning. A hold made by an order is lifted by a `release` like any other, and a later order of the commander's to the unit holds it again at the next step 2. [0107, rulings 23 and 30]

A side sees: its own holds, whole: each with its target, the day, whether a command or an order made it, and the order's number, in the agent's `command_state` (`holds`); its own `HeldByOrder`; the acceptance or refusal of each hold and release command and of each order, with the reason, including a staff order refused as held. The enemy sees none of it. A hold on an enemy unit, or on one that does not exist, is refused as not the side's, so a refusal never shows that an enemy unit exists. [0107, rulings 17 and 23]

## Interrupts

An interrupt is something the commander wants to be told of: when it fires, a run of days stops (The tools, `run`) and the command report lists it. It changes nothing else: a firing moves no unit, gives no order and changes no stock. It is command state: the interrupts set, whether each is armed, the day each last fired and whether that firing is acknowledged. It is hashed, saved through the commands that made it and rebuilt by a replay. [0107, rulings 31 and 32]

**The kinds.** One is set by the commander, and three stand without being set. [0107, ruling 33]

| Kind | Holds on a morning when | Armed apart for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set: a base's days of supply below a number | The days of supply of the hex of the base (Days of supply, below), read when the staff is asked that morning, has a figure and the figure is below the number of days. 35 is not below 35. It does not hold where the hex has no figure (no supply is planned to be used there), or where the base is no longer the side's | Each numbered interrupt |
| Standing `cannot_meet` | The staff handed in a cannot-meet notice for the plan that morning (Notices, below) | Each plan |
| Standing `waits_for_leave` | The staff handed in a notice for the plan that it waits for a leave, to prepare or to launch | Each plan |
| Standing `ship_lost` | A ship of the side was lost the day before: a ship sunk in a fight (`ShipSunk`) or lost in port with its base (`ShipLostWithBase`) on the day before, by the side the ship belongs to. Tested only for a side with at least one intent in force, so a game with no command gets no event of it. On day 1 there is no day before | Each ship |

A set interrupt names a base of the side and a number of days, at least 1. It has its own number, a whole number from 1, that the commander gives; a number is not withdrawn and no command withdraws one. A standing kind is on until the commander switches it off, and on again when he switches it on. A kind that is off is not tested; the records of its subjects stay as they were. The kinds in the code are `SupplyBelow` for the set one and `cannot_meet`, `waits_for_leave` and `ship_lost` for the standing ones. [0107, rulings 31 and 33]

**Armed, fired, acknowledged.** A set interrupt is armed when it is set, never fired and not acknowledged. A standing interrupt for a subject that has no record is armed and never fired, and gets a record the first morning it fires. On a morning when the condition holds and the interrupt is armed, it fires: it is no longer armed, it last fired today and it is not acknowledged. On a morning when the condition holds and it is not armed, nothing happens. On a morning when the condition does not hold, it is armed again, which for a standing kind is every record of the kind whose subject does not hold. So an interrupt already true when it is set fires that morning, once, and then fires again only after a morning on which it did not hold. Acknowledging marks the last firing seen and arms nothing; the next firing is not acknowledged. [0107, ruling 32]

**When they are tested.** Each morning, at step 5: after the staff's commands are taken in and its notices recorded, and before any order is taken in. The figure a set interrupt is tested on is the one read at step 4, on the view the staff was asked on (after the commands and the override, before the staff's commands), so a staff's order or command of that morning changes no reading it is tested on. The sides are tested in side order, Allied first; for a side the set interrupts by number, then the standing kinds in the order above, and for a kind its subjects in plan, then ship number order. A side with a set interrupt and no staff is tested all the same. [0107, ruling 31]

**What a firing records.** An `InterruptFired` event, with no pulse, naming the side and the interrupt. A set one carries its condition and the figure it was tested on; a standing one carries neither. It cites the cause of the firing:

| Interrupt | Cites |
| --- | --- |
| A set one | The latest `CommandTaken` of the command that set that number. A set interrupt of a scenario cites its scripted command |
| A standing one for a plan | The `StaffNotice` events of the morning for the plan that make it hold |
| `ship_lost` | The event that sank the ship, or lost it with its base |

When breakdowns are on, a set interrupt's firing records the stock in tons, the planned use in tons, the days of supply and the number it fell below. Recording it changes nothing. A firing draws no random number. [0107, rulings 31 and 33]

**The commands.**

- `set_interrupt` with a number, a base and a number of days sets that interrupt, or sets it again: the condition is replaced, the day set is today and its status is as a new one's, armed and never fired. With a standing kind and `on` it switches the kind off or on, from that morning.
- `acknowledge` names a set interrupt by its number, or a standing one by its kind and the plan or the ship. It marks the interrupt's last firing seen.

Both are the commander's and are taken in at the first step of the day, so a set interrupt is tested that same morning and an acknowledgement made that day stands for the firing of an earlier day. The refusals are in the table above (Every reason a command is refused). An acknowledgement of a ship that is not the side's is refused as not the side's, whether the ship exists or not, so a refusal never shows that an enemy unit exists. [0107, rulings 31 to 33]

A side sees: its own set interrupts, standing records and the kinds it has switched off, with whether each is armed, when it last fired and whether that is acknowledged, in the agent's `command_state` (`interrupts`) and `report`; its own `InterruptFired`, whole; and the acceptance or refusal of each command. The enemy sees none of it. [0107, rulings 31 and 37]

## The staff

### What a staff is, and when it is asked

A staff is code outside `sim-core`. `sim-core` defines what it is: given a side's view, it decides the day and hands in a `StaffDecisions`:

- **Orders**, each with the intent it serves and its reason.
- **Staff commands**: the commands of the staff's column above, each with its reason.
- **Decisions to give no order**, each a reason.
- **Notices**: structured statements beside these, each resting on one of the reasons above (Notices, below).

A staff is a function of the view: it is asked with a shared reference, so that a staff asked twice on the same view gives the same answer. That is what `replay --check-staff` tests. The function that resolves a day takes each side's staff or none. A side with none is not asked, and a day with no staff and no command is resolved as it was. [0107 items 4, 7 and 8]

A staff is asked once a day, at its place in the day's order (chapter 1, Before the pulses): after the commander's commands are taken in, after forces back from the yard are home, and after the override has taken in the commander's orders to units in a forces limit and held them, and before any other order is taken in. It gets the side's view as it then stands, so it sees the reserves, the leaves and the holds set that day, the orders that held a unit that morning, and the fuel ships and bases held at the end of the day before: nothing is refuelled before the staff is asked (chapter 2, Refuelling in port). It never gets the truth (chapter 1, Truth and belief). [0107, The order of a day]

What a replay hands in, in place of asking, is taken in at the same step as if a staff had given it. If a side has a live staff and something handed in for it, both are taken in, what was handed in first.

The staff's commands are taken in first, each side's in the order handed in, and after each side's commands its decisions to give no order are recorded, and then its notices (Notices, below). When every side's are done, the interrupts are tested (Interrupts, above). Then every order of the day that the override has not already taken in is taken in: the commander's and the staff's together (chapter 1, How orders are numbered and sorted). A staff's order comes after the commander's to the same unit, so the staff's replaces the commander's in a standing slot. A staff's order is marked with the issuer staff and the intent it serves (chapter 1, Who can issue), and is refused as held if it is for something the commander holds (Holds and the override, above). An air group's order serves the cover intent, and not the hold intent above it. A cargo order the staff gives heeds the reserve of a base (chapter 2, Cargo orders). [0107, 0111]

Every runner gives both sides the staff of `sim-staff` (`sim_staff::staffs()`): the doctrine for keeping a base supplied and, after it, the air doctrine for holding a place and covering a base from the air (below). A staff whose side has no intent in force and no commitment to free hands in nothing: nothing is taken in and nothing is saved for that day. [0107, 0115, 0117]

### The forces limit on a staff's orders

A staff's order that is not refused as held (Holds and the override, above) is refused as **outside the forces limit** ("the order is for a unit outside the forces limit of the intent it serves") unless the intent the order serves is in force and:

- for an order to an air group, the group is one the intent may use (The six limits, above): named in every forces limit on its chain, or, with no forces limit on it or above it, at the base it is addressed to, or, for an intent addressed to a task force, aboard that force's ships with every forces limit naming it or having the force inside it; or
- for an order to a land unit, the unit is one the intent may use (decision 0141; Readying a landing, and building up a base, below): named in every forces limit on its chain, or, with no forces limit on it or above it, for a "build up a base", a working unit of the side with men ashore in its base's hex; or, for a "hold a place" with no land unit named in any forces limit on its chain, a fighting unit of the side with men ashore in its base's hex (decision 0144, 4); or
- for an order to a task force that the intent is addressed to (decision 0124, 6), the order is for that force, whatever forces limit stands on its chain or above; or
- for any other order, the intent has a forces limit on it or above it, and against every forces limit on its chain the order is for a task force that the limit lists, or that has at least one ship and every ship of it is a ship the limit lists, or it is the limit's source base's order to form a task force from ships that are in the pool.

A base order other than forming is inside no forces limit, so a staff's order for one is refused. The check does not look at the plan's state or at the area. A refused order is numbered and kept, and recorded as refused with this reason, and nothing else about it is checked. An order that passes is checked as any order is (chapter 1, Every reason an order is refused). [0107, As built]

The check binds the orders a staff hands in. It does not bind an order that a scenario's script gives with the issuer `staff`: that order is a scenario's statement, set down by the scenario's author as a starting condition, and not a decision of a staff made on a view. Scenario 39 is such a scenario. Such an order is checked as any scripted order is. [0107, As built]

A staff command that names ships is checked against the pool and the holds too (`commit_ships`, `add_run`; above). [0107]

### A unit before its day

Before the day from which its side may use it (chapter 1, A day from which a unit may be used), the staff leaves a unit out: it is in no pool, it is no force a task may use, and no doctrine gives it an order. Every doctrine reads the day through one test, the side view's `may_use`, for the day the staff gives orders for. The places that read it: the pool of a forces limit (a ship before its day is in no pool, so no run commits it and nothing is loaded on it), the air groups an intent may use (so the cover, the raid, the strike, the search of waters, the neutralising and the denial of an area use none before its day), the groups aboard a carrier force, a free escort force, a force a surface force that stands by night is formed from, and an intent addressed to a task force. The staff still reads a unit before its day where it reads its own side for anything but choosing what to order. [0140 item 3]

A reason that would have used a unit names its day, in one wording, "U may be used from day N". An intent addressed to a task force that may not be used yet gets no order to the force or to the groups aboard, and one reason: that it is to be served by the force, which may be used from day N. A pool whose every ship waits for its day is not a failed plan: one reason names each ship with its day, and nothing is sent. Where a reason lists what a task may use, a unit left out for its day is named with its day. A forces limit is the commander's, and may name a unit before its day; intake still refuses an order to it with the day, from a staff as from the commander. [0140 items 2 and 3]

A side sees: its own acceptance or refusal of the order whole, with the reason. [0107, 0124 item 6]

### Reasons

Every order a staff gives and every decision it makes to give none has a **reason**. A reason has five parts:

| Part | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| Serves | The intent, and the plan and the amendment the decision was made under, with the intent's statement: the intent in words as the commander set it and as it stood that day ("keep base:2 (Placeholder base T) at 30 days of supply, a run every 15 days"; "no interval set (the staff takes 15 days)" where he set none; "withdrawn on day N" where it was). The amendment is none for the baseline. Then the **level** that decided (A doctrine belongs to a level, below). Then, for an intent with a parent, the chain: the intents above it, nearest first, each with its number, its maker and its statement as it stood (The intent tree, above) |
| Evidence | Each thing the side held that mattered, in words, with its day |
| Binding | The limit, stock, distance or commitment that shaped the decision, in words |
| Worked | The calculation, in words |
| Would change | What, if different, would give a different decision |

A reason is handed in with its order, with its staff command, or with the decision to give no order, and is recorded as a `StaffReason` event. For an order, the event cites the order and the event that accepted or refused it, and is recorded after them. For a staff command it has no order, holds the command as handed in, and cites the command's `CommandTaken` or `CommandRefused`, and is recorded after it and its follow-ups. For a decision to give no order it has no order and no cause. [0107; differences.md row 43] **A reason is kept, not worked out again**: it stays in the log whatever the intent has since become, so it still resolves after its intent is replaced or withdrawn. Its number is the number of its event: a reason has no number of its own. [0107 items 4 and 12]

The staff writes the intent's statement into every reason it hands in, from the side's command state that morning, so the words travel with the reason: every place a reason is shown (the tools `why`, `staff_orders`, `propose`, `preview` and `report`, the replay export and viewer, `run --events` and `sim-cli events`) gives the same words, and a reason made before its intent was changed or withdrawn still says what the intent was then. Where its worked part speaks of a plan with no base in it (a leave given or withheld, a plan ended, a run ended or set aside, a commitment made again), it names the plan with what it is for: "plan:1, the plan to keep base:2 (Placeholder base T) at 30 days of supply,". The statement of a hold intent reads "hold base:1 (name)" and of a cover intent "cover base:1 (name) from the air", of a neutralise intent "neutralise base:9 (name)" and of a raid "raid base:9 (name) from base:1 (name)", of a deny intent "deny the waters within 4 hexes of (q, r)", of a strike-ships task "strike ships from base:1 (name)" and of a search-waters task "search the waters from base:1 (name)", with ", withdrawn on day N" where it was. The statement of every intent above the one served is in the reason's chain, with its maker (The intent tree, above). A game saved before the statement was handed in reads its reasons with none. In the log's own line a reason reads "staff's reason for order N", "staff's reason for the command to ..." or "staff's reason for giving no order", then the serves part in brackets, then the worked part; a count of one there and in a command's own line is "1 ship", not "1 ships". [0107, "As built (step 4)", the mend]

The engine stores what the staff writes in a reason and checks none of it. Evidence names a side's own ships freely and the enemy only as a contact, a claim or an estimate (chapter 3, Belief): that is the staff's to keep, and a staff is given nothing else to name. [0107, truth and belief]

A side sees: its own `StaffReason` whole. The enemy sees none. [0107]

### Notices

A staff hands in notices beside its orders, commands and reasons: that it cannot meet a plan's intent, that a plan waits for a leave, that it has ended a run. A notice is structured, so that a standing interrupt can read it without reading words. It has a kind, the plan, the run where the kind is about one (a run ended; else none) and the reason it rests on, which is one of the same hand-in's staff commands or decisions to give no order. [0107, ruling 34]

| Kind | Means | The doctrine hands it in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cannot_meet` | The staff cannot meet the plan's intent | On a morning it weighs a new run for the plan and either can have none, or the run it can have leaves the reading it checks below the floor (The doctrine, Cannot meet). It rests on the day's reason for giving no order about the next run, or, for a cut run, on the reason of the `add_run`. A load cut that still leaves the reading at or above the floor is no notice. For a cover intent, on a morning no fighter group it may order can fly (Covering a base from the air, When it cannot); it rests on that morning's reason for giving no order. For a neutralise intent, on a morning no base qualifies (Neutralising a base, above); for a raid, on a morning it cannot be met (Raiding a base, above); each rests on that morning's reason for giving no order. For a take-a-place intent at preserve the force, on the first morning the ships can carry fewer men than are wanted, or on the first morning its landing is ready and the check that the place can be held fails, or on the first morning its landing is ready and nothing it may use can look at the place for the count the risk asks for (The need to know), once for the plan (Taking a place, below) |
| `waits_for_leave_to_prepare` | The plan is a draft and waits for the commander's leave to prepare | On a morning the plan is a draft and leave to prepare is withheld, for a supply plan and for the plan of a hold, cover, neutralise or raid root. It rests on the reason that says so |
| `waits_for_leave_to_launch` | A run that is loaded waits in a plan that is preparing, for the leave to launch or the commander's approval | On a morning a loaded run waits for that; for the plan of a hold, cover, neutralise or raid root, on a morning the plan is preparing and leave to launch is withheld; for the plan of a take-a-place root, on each morning its landing is ready, the plan is preparing and leave to launch is withheld (Taking a place, below). It rests on the day's reason for giving no order |
| `run_ended` | The staff ended a run | On a morning it sets a run aside for a held ship (The doctrine, Each morning, before any intent). It names the run and rests on the reason of the `end_run` |
| `ready` | A "ready a landing" task is ready | On the first morning it is ready (Readying a landing, below), once for the plan. It rests on a reason that gives the loading and each unit's rehearsal and experience |
| `late` | A "ready a landing" task cannot be ready by its date | On the first morning the staff can see it, once for the plan. It rests on a reason that gives the days late and what binds |
| `met` | Every want of a "build up a base" task is met, or a "take a place" end is met | On the first morning every want is met, once for the plan; it rests on the day's reason for giving no order. For a take-a-place end, on the first morning the place is the side's, once for the plan; it rests on the reason of the `mark_plan` that marks the plan complete (Taking a place, below). The event's words are a build-up's, "every want of plan:N is met", and a take-a-place's the intent's, "intent:N is met" (the ruling on decision 0142's reading 4) |

[0107, ruling 35]

The engine records each notice as a `StaffNotice` event, naming the side, the kind, the plan and the run, with no pulse; a `met` notice of a plan whose intent is a "take a place" names that intent too, and says its words. It is recorded after the staff's commands and the decisions to give no order of the side, in the order handed in, and cites the `StaffReason` it rests on, so a notice leads to its reason and the reason to the evidence. A notice that rests on nothing the hand-in holds is recorded with no cause. The engine tests the standing interrupts on the notices and reads nothing of a reason's words. A notice changes no state. [0107, ruling 34]

A side sees: its own `StaffNotice`, whole. The enemy sees none. [0107]

## Days of supply

Days of supply is a side's supply stock in a hex over its planned use of supply there for a day. It is worked by `SideView::days_of_supply(hex)` from the side's view only: its own bases, land units, forces, ships and air groups, the map and the figures. It is nothing of the enemy's, changes nothing and draws no random number. [0107 items 31 to 36]

**Where.** It is for the side in a hex, not for a base. A hex where the side holds no base has none. Every base of the side in a hex carries the same figure, because the men of a side are fed from every base it has there. An intent that names a base means its hex. [0107 item 31]

**Stock.** The supply of all the side's bases in the hex, in tons. [0107 item 32]

**Planned use.** The tons the side is planned to spend there on the coming day, in five parts. A part whose figures the scenario does not load is 0, as the rule that charges it charges nothing:

| Part | What | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Men ashore | Each of the side's land units with men ashore in the hex, at its daily charge for its men | Chapter 7, The men's supply |
| Men due | The same charge for men aboard the side's own forces that are ordered to unload in the hex and are due on or before the day, or were due earlier and have not landed | Chapter 7, Landing men. A force is due on the day its passage to the hex ends, forecast from the view as the measuring tool forecasts it. A force already stopped in the hex counts when its unload can work there and men are still aboard |
| Aircraft upkeep | The daily supply for each ready and each damaged aircraft of the groups at the side's bases in the hex | Chapter 5, Daily supply; `air_supply.toml: daily_supply_t_per_aircraft` |
| Repairs | For each base, the smaller of its repairs a day (its figure, and the repairs the work done on aircraft the day before gives it) and the damaged aircraft at it, times the supply of a repair. Counted only where the air upkeep figures are loaded | Chapter 5, Repair; `air_supply.toml: repair_supply_t` |
| Sorties | The sorties of the groups at its bases there that are on a mission that flies whatever is sighted, at each of the day's two daylight pulses: a search group with an arc set, at the cost of a sortie of twice its arc's range, for the aircraft able to fly; a group on combat air patrol, at the cost of a sortie of no distance, for the fighters that fly its patrol share of the aircraft able to fly | Chapter 5, Supply for sorties and What a group can fly |

**A strike mission counts nothing.** Whether a strike group flies depends on what is sighted, and a figure that moved with enemy reports would move for no change in supply. A group at rest counts nothing, and a group aboard a ship is counted at no base. Each part is worked by the engine's own rule for it and by no second formula. [0107 items 32 to 34, rulings 1 and 2]

**The figure.** The stock over the planned use, a fixed-point quotient cut toward zero at six decimals. **No planned use, no figure**: where the planned use is 0 there is no figure, and the answer says so in words ("No supply is planned to be used in this hex today, so there is no days-of-supply figure"). The stock is still given. [0107 item 36]

**When it is read.** At the step where a staff is asked: at the start of a day, after the commands, the returns and the override and before the day's orders are taken in. Read between days it gives the same figure, since nothing between the end of one day and that step changes a stock or a use. An order given today shows in the figure of tomorrow: a group set to patrol today raises the planned use read the next day. [0107 item 31, ruling 3]

**The projection** of the figure over the coming days, and the first day it falls below an intent's floor, are the doctrine's (below, The doctrine). The engine gives it what it counts by its own rule: `SideView::men_due_by_day(hex)`, each force due in the hex with its due day and its men's charge, the rule the men-due part above uses. [0107 items 34 and 35]

A side sees: its own figure, with its stock, its planned use and the five parts, in the agent's `list_bases` and `get_entity` (as `days_of_supply`) and in the briefing's entry for each of its bases (as `planned_days_of_supply`). The briefing's entry for a base also keeps its older `days_of_supply`, which is a different figure: the stock over yesterday's spending, cut to one decimal (glossary, Days of supply). The enemy sees none. [0107 item 47]

## The doctrine: keep a base supplied

The staff (`sim_staff::SupplyStaff`) decides from its side's view alone, at the step of the day where a staff is asked. It holds nothing of its own: what it remembers is the side's command state (its intents, plans, runs and commitments) and what the view shows of its ships and air groups. Asked twice on the same view it gives the same answer. It draws no random number and uses no floating point. Its version is `supply-12`. It runs two doctrines in this order: this one, for the keep-supplied intents, which serves no other kind; then the air doctrine (Holding a place, Covering a base from the air, Neutralising a base and Raiding a base, below), for the hold, cover, neutralise and raid intents. It hands in notices (Notices, above) at the places named below. [0107 items 3 and 37 to 45, rulings 4 to 9, 17 to 30, 34 and 35; 0126]

"Today" below is the day being resolved. A ship, a force and a base are named in a reason as the engine writes them (`ship:1`, `task_force:1000001`, `base:2`); the agent's tools show them in the side's own numbers.

### The projection

For the hex of the intent's base the staff projects a reading for each day from today. Today's reading is the view's (Days of supply, above). The reading of the next day is that day's reading, less its planned use, plus what lands that day, and never below 0. The planned use of a coming day is today's, with the men due counted from the day each force is due (`men_due_by_day`). What lands: for each run of an approved plan of the side that has not finished, its load while it has not sailed, else the supply aboard its ships, on the day after its arrival day, or today if that day is past, and only at the hex where the run will land it (below). A run ended, set aside this morning or of a plan that has ended has finished and lands nothing. A reading's figure is its stock over its use; with no use it has none.

**Where a run lands.** A run still loading lands at the base its intent names as it stands now, so a changed base moves it. A run that has sailed lands at the first hex of its force's route still ahead that is marked to act at, and nowhere once that has been passed; where its route has no mark at all, or its ships are in no force, it lands at the intent's base. So a run that sailed for a base the intent no longer names is counted at the hex it will reach, and not at the new base. [0107, rulings 19 and 28] The report of it is today's figure and the first day whose figure is below the floor. [0107 item 35, ruling 1]

### Each morning, before any intent

The staff first **sets aside** each run of an approved plan that has not sailed and has a held ship (the ships of a run in a force, held through the force, are held; Holds and the override, above). A run has not sailed while its ships are all at the source, stopped; a run that has sailed is under way and is not set aside. For each run set aside the staff hands in an `end_run` and, where its ships hold a commitment to the plan, a `free_ships` for them, each with a reason that says which hold ("task_force:1000001 is held by the commander's hold of day 8", or "... by the commander's order of day 2") and what is aboard ("the 100 t aboard stay aboard", or "nothing is aboard"). These are the first staff commands of the morning. For each run set aside it also hands in a notice, run ended, naming the plan and the run and resting on the reason of the `end_run`. For everything else the staff reads that morning, the run is finished the same morning and the ships' commitment to the plan is gone, so they are free to the next run of whichever intent needs one, in the held ship's place, and the projection no longer counts the run's landing. The run is ended for good: a release does not take it up again, and the staff sizes a new run. A run of a plan that has ended is set aside by nothing, since it holds nothing. [0107, rulings 20 and 21]

A run that has sailed goes on by the orders it has when one of its ships is held: it lands its load and comes home, and the projection counts what it will land. The staff gives it no further order, since a held force is not free to it. [0107, ruling 19]

### Each day, for each intent

The keep-supplied intents in force are served in priority order: ranked ones by rank, 1 first, then those with none, each at equal rank by the lower number. A supply intent has no date, so the spec's tie-break by date does not arise. **What the staff has given an intent served earlier the same morning is committed for every intent served after it:** the ships of a run it has just added (until the run's free day) and the tons that run has still to load (all of its load). So two plans never count the same ship or the same ton on the day both are planned. A free ship goes to the first intent, in this order, that needs a run that day; the staff keeps no free ship idle for a higher intent that will need one later, and recalls no run under way. A changed priority is a new allocation from the next morning the staff is asked, and moves nothing that is committed. [0107 items 37 and 43, rulings 10, 11, 13 and 15]

For each intent:

1. **What stops it.** With no forces limit, no order and a reason. A destination or source base that is not the side's: an approved plan is marked failed (`mark_plan`), and no order. A plan that has ended: no order, except the fallback of a cancelled plan (Cancelling, below). A pool with no ship afloat: an approved plan is marked failed, and no order. A pool whose every ship afloat is protected for another intent in force (that intent's protected limit): no order, and the reason says that no ship of the pool may be taken, naming each ship and the intent it is protected for; the plan is not marked failed. The staff never marks a plan complete: a supply intent ends when it is withdrawn. [ruling 16]
2. **The leaves.** A draft is approved to preparing when leave to prepare is given; else no order, and the reason says the plan waits for the commander; a notice, waits for leave to prepare, rests on that reason. A plan that is preparing is released the same day when leave to launch is given. A plan that is preparing forms and loads its first run and does not sail it.
3. **The runs under way.** A run whose ships are all at the source, stopped, is loading, unless its sail day is past and nothing is aboard, when it has finished; a run with a ship away from the source is under way; a run whose ships a later run has taken, or whose ships are all sunk, has finished; so has a run that has ended and a run of a plan that has ended. A run loading whose load is complete (the supply aboard is its load, or the source has nothing more above its reserve, or its ships are full), after its load day, sails if the plan is released: a route to the destination and back to the source, marked to act at the destination, and an unload order. If its sail day has passed, it is timed again first (`time_run`) and its commitment renewed (`free_ships`, `commit_ships`). A run loaded in a plan that is preparing waits for leave to launch.
4. **A new run.** When no run of the plan is loading, a run is needed if, without it, the reading of the day after a run ordered today would arrive is below the floor's stock: the floor in days times the planned use of that day. Then:
   - **Its ships** are the pool's free ships in number order, taken until their room covers the load. A ship is free when no unfinished run of any plan holds it, no run added earlier the same morning holds it, no commitment of a plan that has not ended covers today, no other intent in force protects it, the commander holds neither it nor its force nor any ship of its force, and it is stopped at the source, in port in no force or in a force there with no route. A held ship gives no day on which it will be free: it is not counted in the day the staff expects the next ship. A ship in a force brings its whole force. A ship's room, for taking more, is its class's cargo less the supply aboard it. The supply aboard the free ships counts toward the load (below), so ships are taken in number order until what is aboard them and the room they have to take more cover the load, or the source's tons above its reserve if those are less.
   - **Its load** is the floor's stock on the day it arrives, less the reading of that day, and not below 0, plus the planned use of the interval's days from the day it arrives. The interval is the intent's, or 15 days where the commander set none. It is cut to the ships' room, and to the source's stock above its reserve and above what runs still loading there have yet to take, those added earlier the same morning included. It is fixed when it is ordered. **Supply already aboard** (a free ship that was set aside, or one that came back with its cargo): the run carries the larger of that load and what is aboard, and takes only the difference. The tons to take are the load less what is aboard, not below 0, cut to the source's tons above its reserve and to the ships' room; the run's load is what is aboard plus those tons. Nothing is put back ashore. A load order is given for the difference only, to a weight that is what is aboard plus those tons, and none is given when there is nothing to take: the supply aboard is the load, and the reason says so ("the run carries what is aboard and no load order is needed") and, where the sum's load is less than what is aboard, says that too. [0107, ruling 22]
   - **Its days**, in whole days: loading takes the hours of the load at the source's berth rate (the lighter rate for a ship with no berth), rounded up to days and at least 1; the passage each way takes the hours of the charged sea distance at the class's cruise speed, rounded up to days; unloading as loading, at the destination's rate. It loads today, sails after its loading days and arrives on its sail day plus the outward passage less one. Its ships are free on the morning after the day they have finished fuelling at the source (decision 0112): the hours are counted from the first hour of the sail day, the passage out and the unloading in whole days, the passage back at cruise, then the fuelling, of what each ship then lacks (its capacity less the fuel it holds now and less the fuel the round trip burns, as in Its fuel), at the source's fuel berth rate where the source has a berth and its fuel lighter rate where it has none, the longest ship's hours. The free day is the day that hour falls in, plus one, and never earlier than the sail day plus the passages and the unloading in whole days. Where the source gives no fuelling rate (no limit) the fuelling takes no hours and the ships are free on the day after they are back at the source, as before. The hour a force steams on from the destination does not move these days. [rulings 6, 8 and 9]
   - **Its route.** The engine's sea path out and back. Where an area limit is set and the path leaves it, waypoints are added along the shortest sea path inside the area until the engine's path between them stays inside; if there is no such path, there is no route. With no area limit the sea is open.
   - **Its fuel.** Each ship needs, in the fuel it holds when the staff is asked (the fuel as it stood at the end of the day before: nothing is refuelled before the staff is asked), the fuel of the round trip at cruise.
   - **On the day,** the staff hands in `add_run` and `commit_ships` (from today to the day before the ships are free), and orders: a forming order at the source where its ships lie in port in no force (the new force's number is the view's `next_task_force_id`, in the order intake forms forces), a join where they lie in more than one force, and, where there is anything to take, a load to a weight at the source, the weight being what is aboard and the tons to take. The `add_run` records the run's whole load. [ruling 7, ruling 22]
5. **When it cannot.** It says which binds: no ship free until a day, the source's stock, no route in the area, a ship without the fuel, or the use more than the pool can carry. It gives the shortfall in days at the reading it checked: the floor less the figure of the day after the run would arrive, with what the run sends counted as landed, and not below 0. The source binds when there is nothing aboard and nothing to take. It gives the day the source falls to its reserve at the present rate (its stock above its reserve over the planned use of the destination and its own). With less stock than the load it sends what it can and says how many days it brings: it slips a run before it accepts fewer days, and accepts fewer days before it stands a plan down. [0107 items 25 and 44, ruling 14]

   **Displaced demand.** An intent that needs a run and has no ship free, while a ship of its pool is held by another plan, or whose load is cut by the ships' room while one is, or by the source's stock while tons there are still to be taken by another plan's run, is displaced. Its reason, and the reason of a run it sends cut, names its shortfall and each commitment that holds a ship of its pool or the tons at its source, its own plan's among them: for ships, the ships, the plan, its run and the days from the commitment's first to its last (`ship:1 committed to run 1 of plan:1 (intent:1) from day 1 to day 4`); for tons, the tons, the source, the plan and its run, from the run's load day to the day before it sails. It is said whichever of the two intents ranks higher: the earlier commitment stands. Where every commitment that holds them is the intent's own plan's, nothing is displaced and none is named. [ruling 14]

   **What the hold took.** Where the choice of ships for a run, made again with no hold, would take a ship the real choice did not because it is held, the staff says so: the reason of the run it gives, or of the day it gives none for want of a ship, says "Without the hold, ship:1 would have been sent.", names the hold in its evidence and in what bound it, and gives as what would change it that the target is released, when it would then be free. [0107, ruling 21]
   **Cannot meet.** The staff hands in a cannot-meet notice for the plan on a morning it weighs a new run (no run of the plan is loading and the intent needs one) and: (a) it can have none, because something binds (step 5 above); the notice rests on the day's reason for giving no order (step 6), and a day on which a loaded run is also dispatched gives that reason too, with its working beginning "No new run:", so that the notice has a reason about the new run and not about the dispatch; or (b) it orders a run whose load is cut and the reading it checks, with what the run sends counted as landed, is still below the floor (the shortfall above is more than 0 days): the notice rests on the reason of the `add_run`. A load cut that leaves the reading at or above the floor gives none. The staff weighs no new run, and so hands in no cannot-meet notice, for an intent with no forces limit, a base that is not the side's, a pool with no ship afloat, a pool whose every ship afloat is protected for another intent, a plan that has ended, or a day on which a run of the plan is still loading or waits to sail. [0107, ruling 35]

6. **A day it gives the intent no order** has one reason: what each run is doing, and the reading the next run waits for, with the day it is expected to be ordered, and the hold's words where a hold is what kept a ship from a run. A loaded run that waits in a plan that is preparing says that what would change it is leave to launch, or the commander's approval of the plan; a notice, waits for leave to launch, rests on that reason. A day the staff dispatches a loaded run and a new run is needed and can be had by none, the reason is given as well, and its working begins "No new run:" (Cannot meet, above).

First, before any intent is served, a plan that has ended whose ships are committed from today on has them freed (`free_ships`), so that an intent served that morning may commit them: the staff reads them as free, and the engine refuses a commitment that meets one still standing. (Until doctrine `supply-7` this was done last, after the intents; the soak ashore found the refusal it made, decision 0093, item 10.)

Every order, staff command and decision to give no order has a reason of five parts (above, Reasons). Its "worked" part is the arithmetic: the stocks, uses, days, loads and hours.

A side sees: its own staff's decisions, reasons and commands whole; the enemy sees none (above).

### A run by night

Where an enemy base on the side's map lies within 15 hexes of the place a run goes to and not in that place's own hex, a supply run of this doctrine and a lift of "take a place" (Taking a place, below) are timed to arrive in the night, at preserve the force and at balanced; at press a run goes as before. The 15 hexes are the stand doctrine's placeholder. The risk is the intent's, read up the tree. A base in the place's own hex does not count. **A place the side's own fighters are based at is run to by day:** where a base of the side's in the place's hex, or in a hex next to it, has a fighter group of the side's based at it, whatever its state, the run goes as before, at every risk; a side knows its own groups exactly, so this reads nothing of the enemy (decision 0145, item 2a, added by the architect after the build, and ruling 6, the hex next to it). Where more than one base counts, the reason names the nearest, the lowest number on a tie. [0145 items 1 to 3 and 2a]

1. **The hour it would arrive** is the engine's forecast of the passage from the source to the place (`SideView::forecast_route`), sailing at the first hour of a day, at cruising speed and then at full speed. A passage arrives in the night when its hours, less the whole days in them, are fewer than 12. The night's hours are 12 less that. Where a run's ships lie in no one force on the morning it is ordered, the passage is the route's miles over the slowest ship's speed and the fuel the burn rule of chapter 2 (Fuel burn), which is what the forecast gives on an open route. [0145 items 4 and 6]
2. **The speed** is the first of the two that arrives in the night with room for the load (3) and for which every ship has the fuel of the route out and home at that speed, by the engine's measure: above cruising speed a ship burns its cruising rate times the square of the speed over its cruising speed. Where a ship would run dry on the route the need given is the burn of the whole route at that speed. A speed a ship has not the fuel for is passed over, and the reason gives the two figures. Where no speed that arrives in the night with the fuel has room for the whole load, the one with the longest night is taken and the load is cut to it. On the morning the run sails, where the speed differs from the force's, the staff orders it before the route; it is left as it is afterwards. [0145 item 5]
3. **The night's limit.** No ship carries more than it can land in the night's hours: the place's rate for the ship (the berth rate for the first ships up to its berths, else the lighter rate) times those hours, the men by their weight and the supply together, at the rate the force's loading gives it where men are landed (chapter 2, Working cargo). A load to a weight fills ships in ship order, so on the morning a run is ordered its load is cut until the first ship filled carries no more than its limit, and each ship after it likewise; the cut is one of the run's cuts, and so counts toward its shortfall. For a lift, the supply is cut so, the men aboard counted with it, and a unit goes aboard only where every ship it would fill could land its part in the hours; a unit that cannot is left for a later run and the reason says so. The days of a run are reckoned as before, at cruising speed. [0145 items 7 to 10]
4. **Where no speed arrives in the dark.** At balanced the run goes by day at cruising speed (a speed order to cruise where the force is at another), and the reason says that neither speed brings it there in the night, with the two hours. At preserve the force a supply run is loaded and does not sail: on each morning it would have sailed the staff gives it no order and hands in the notice that it cannot meet the intent, resting on the day's reason. A lift of "take a place" is not routed and hands in that notice once (Taking a place, below). [0145 items 11 and 12]
5. **Gone by light.** Nothing is added: a force that has landed its load in the night steams on at the start of the morning pulse (chapter 2, Halting at a marked waypoint and steaming on). [0145 item 13]
6. **Reasons.** The reason of the run's load, its speed order and its route give the enemy base and its distance, the two hours of arrival, the speed chosen, the night's hours, each ship's load against its limit, and what was cut or left. [0145 item 14]

### Leaves

The two leaves are as they were: leave to prepare is given and leave to launch withheld unless the commander sets them. A plan that is preparing forms and loads its first run and sails nothing. It is released by the commander's approval, or by the staff the same morning where leave to launch is given. A run that waits is not sized again when it sails: its load was fixed when it was ordered, and it is timed again, and its ships' commitment renewed, on the day it sails. [0107, ruling 24]

### Cancelling

`cancel_plan` ends the plan (Plans, above): its commitments are freed from that day, and the intent stays in force with no plan until the commander sets it again or withdraws it. The staff reads every run of the cancelled plan as finished and gives the plan no more runs. A force of the plan that is still on its way out is given a way home, **once**: for each run of the plan, not ended, that has sailed (its ships are away from the source) and whose ships afloat are all in one force, if the force's remaining route still holds the intent's destination and the force is not stopped there, and the commander does not hold it, the staff gives it a route to the source, by the engine's sea path inside the area (the source's hex alone where there is none), with no mark to act at, and a reason that says the plan is cancelled and the force is sent home. It gives no new cargo order: the cargo stays aboard and the force's standing unload order stands, so the cargo is put back ashore at the source. A force that has begun to unload at the destination, a force still loading at the source and a force whose remaining route no longer holds the destination are given nothing, and one that has been sent home is not sent again, since its new route does not hold the destination. Where it gives such an order it gives no other decision for the intent that day. [0107, rulings 24 and 29]

### A proposal

What the staff would do for one intent, worked out and stored nowhere: what it would hand in on the coming day as if the plan were released, the runs the same rules give over the next 30 days on the projection, the projection itself, what binds if it cannot meet the intent, and a reason. It is worked in the day's priority order: the intents before it are served first as the staff would serve them that morning, so what they would commit is committed for it; then it is served; then the intents after it, with what it would commit. So it says both what it is displaced by (the commitments of the plans served before it that hold its ships or its tons) and what it displaces (each intent after it that it would displace, with that intent's shortfall and the commitments of this plan that cause it). It begins as the day does, with the runs set aside for a held ship (above) ended and their ships freed, so it shows the morning the staff will have, and the load of each of its runs counts the supply aboard its ships. The runs of its 30 days count as free only the ships free today: a ship held, under way or given to a higher intent that morning is not offered to its later runs. [0107 items 5 and 46, rulings 14 and 22]

## Escorting and covering a run

The doctrine of decision 0126, at the area's level where "keep supplied" is, and the one that uses decision 0121's escort rule (chapter 2, Keeping station) and decision 0124's stand task (A task force as an addressee and Standing at a hex by night, below). It adds no rule of the engine and no state. It draws no random number. [0126]

**What the commander names.** The forces limit of a keep-supplied intent may name task forces. A named force on the escort mission with a ship afloat is an escort the staff may give to a run. A named force on the surface combat mission with a ship afloat is a covering force. A force on any other mission is given nothing here. With no task force named, the threat is not read, no reason speaks of it and runs sail exactly as `supply-11` sent them. [0126 items 1 and 2]

**The threat.** It is read on the morning the run is routed, in `dispatch` (Each day, for each intent, step 3): a contact of ships the side holds, within 15 hexes of the intent's destination (decision 0124's threat, taken at the destination), by the contact's estimated hex. Every contact counts at every risk level, whatever its number of ships: a run is no force that could outnumber one. The nearest is taken, the lowest number on a tie, and the reason names it, its ships and its distance. [0126 item 3]

**Whether the run is escorted.** By the risk read up the tree (The six limits, above):

| | Preserve the force | Balanced | Press |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A run is escorted | Always, where an escort is free | On a threat, where an escort is free | Never |
| A threat and no escort free | The run is held: it does not sail this morning, the reason says why, and the cannot-meet notice is handed in | The run sails alone, and its reason says so | The run sails |

A run that preserve holds is not given up: it is loaded and ready, its dispatch is tried again on each later morning, and it is routed on the first morning on which an escort is free (or, at preserve, the morning on which no threat is held). The reason of the day for the intent says "The loaded run cannot sail" with the cause, and the notice, cannot meet (Notices, above), rests on that reason; while the run waits, no new run is weighed, as for any loaded run that has not sailed. At preserve with no threat held and no escort free the run sails alone, and says so. [0126 item 4]

**A free escort.** A named force on the escort mission, with a ship afloat, that:

- is stopped at the run's source hex;
- has no charge;
- is not held by the commander, as a force or by a ship of it (Holds and the override, above);
- is not short of ammunition at the run's risk (Ammunition as policy, below); and
- has in each of its ships the fuel for the passage out and back by the route the run will take, by the engine's forecast at the run's speed, times the margin the risk gives: 1.5 at preserve the force, 1.2 at balanced, 1 at press (decision 0124's table). The fuel is each ship's as the staff sees it that morning.

The lowest numbered free escort is taken. One escort force goes with one run. The reason of the escort's order says which escort was taken and the fuel of each ship aboard against what it needs; where none is free it gives, for each named force, why it is not (not stopped at the source, already a charge, held, short of ammunition, short of fuel, or no route by the engine's measure). [0126 items 5 and 8]

**The orders.** On the morning the run is routed, the order that gives the escort the run's force as its charge (the escort order, chapter 2, Keeping station) is handed in before the run's route order. The escort then keeps station by that rule and needs no route. On a later morning on which a named escort's charge is stopped at the source, with ships, and the escort is not held, the staff hands in an escort order that clears the charge, with a reason that says the run is home; this is done on each morning the intent's plan is released, before the runs are carried on, and for each such escort at most once. The escort is free again on the morning after it has fuelled (chapter 2, Refuelling in port). A held escort is left alone: no order clears its charge, and none is given it. [0126 item 6]

**The covering force.** For each task force named in the forces limit of a keep-supplied intent on the surface combat mission with a ship afloat, in task force number order, a stand task (Standing at a hex by night, below) is formed under the keep-supplied intent, by the forming rule of decision 0124 (A task force as an addressee, above), with the hex of the intent's destination base as its station, where no stand task for that force stands under it. A stand task may stand under a keep-supplied intent only at the hex of the base the intent names, which command intake checks (the stand task is refused where its hex is not the base's, or its parent's plan is not released, chapter 8, Every reason a command is refused). A carrier force named in the limit is given no task under keep supplied. The stand task's own doctrine decides the nights it sorties; this doctrine gives no order to a covering force.

The air doctrine runs where a keep-supplied intent's forces limit names task forces, and not only where a hold, cover, neutralise, raid, deny or stand intent is in force; with none named, a side with only keep-supplied intents hands in nothing from it, as before. The covering forces are formed in the air doctrine's pass, after the search doctrine and before the stand doctrine (The day's order, below), and the air doctrine takes the keep-supplied plan as released on a morning on which the supply doctrine releases it (leave to prepare and leave to launch given, as the leaves of the supply doctrine say), so that the task formed that morning is served that morning. [0126 item 7]

**Reasons.** The reason of a run's route order, when task forces are named, says the threat (the contact, its ships and its distance from the destination, or that none is held within 15 hexes) and the escort taken or why the run sails alone. The reason of the escort order, with its fuel figures, names the forces limit that names the escort and what would change it (no threat at balanced risk, press risk, the escort held, short of fuel or away from the source). The reason of a cleared charge names the run's force as back at the source. [0126 item 8]

**What it leaves out.** How many escorts (one named force goes or none does); timing a run to the dark; escorts for supply under "hold a place"; air cover of a run. [0126, What it leaves out]

A side sees: its own orders, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0126]

## A doctrine belongs to a level

With command levels on, a doctrine reads the picture of the command its intent is addressed to: the area command, a base or a task force (chapter 1, Commands and their pictures). Without report figures every picture is the side's, so this changes nothing a staff does; with them a picture holds what its command held first or was passed, and a staff reason that names a contact names the command whose picture it is (chapter 1). [0134, 0135]

Each doctrine of the staff belongs to one level, the addressee of the intents it serves (The intent tree, above). A doctrine of a higher level forms sub-intents for the level below and gives no order to a unit and sets no setting of one; a doctrine of the lowest level it reads gives the orders. [0120 items 1 to 4]

1. **The level of each doctrine.** Keeping a base supplied is the area's, and gives orders itself (stage 1 built it so, and nothing here changes it). The neutralise doctrine (Neutralising a base, below) is the area's and forms raid tasks, which it gives no order for. Holding a place, covering a base from the air and raiding a base are the base's. Denying an area is the area's and forms strike-ships and search-waters tasks, which it gives no order for, and, for each task force its forces limit names, a stand task or a carrier task; striking ships from a base and searching waters from a base are the base's. Holding a place, at the base's level, forms the same tasks for the forces its forces limit names. Standing at a hex and operating a carrier force are the task force's: its doctrine gives the force and the air groups aboard it their orders, and is the lowest level to give any order to a ship. [0120 item 4; 0123; 0124 items 6 to 8; 0125]
2. **A reason says which level decided.** A reason's serves part holds the level: the addressee of the intent it serves, written "decided at the level of the area" or "decided at the level of base:1", after the intent, plan and amendment and before the chain. It is filled for every reason a staff hands in, from the intent served, so a reason of stage 1 says "decided at the level of the area" too. It is text: no order is changed by it, and the engine never reads it. A game saved before it was handed in reads each reason with none. [0120 item 2]
3. **An air group is a level too.** Below the base is the air group. No intent is given to a group: its level is `Addressee::AirGroup`, and the only reason that names it is the reason of the strike sizing order a strike-ships task gives (Striking ships from a base, above). That reason says "decided at the level of air_group:N" in place of the level of the base, because the group decides the size of its own strikes from the task it is under. A hash and a replay carry it as the other levels. It is text: no order is changed by it. [0123 item 9]
4. **A task force is a level too,** and the one a reason names in "decided at the level of task_force:N" for a stand task or a carrier task. A hash and a replay carry it as the other levels. [0124 item 6]
5. **The picture is still the side's.** A side has one picture and every level reads it. No doctrine reads less of it for being at a lower level. [0120 item 3]
6. **The state of a unit is read at its level.** With report figures, a doctrine addressed to the area (keeping a base supplied, neutralise, deny, and the forming of a force's stand task or carrier task) reads the side's task forces, bases and what they hold through the area's view: as last reported to the area command (chapter 1, Commands and their pictures, State reports). Its forecasts of passages and fuel start from what is held, and a reason says "(as of the morning of day 1)" where the state it rests on is older than the pulse just ended. A doctrine of a base or a task force reads its own units exactly and the units of other commands as the side's view gives them. An order of the area's doctrine that the truth refuses where the held state would have accepted it is refused with "; the area command held ..." and counted apart from the staff's faults. [0136]

## Holding a place

The end "hold a place" and the doctrine that serves it. The staff runs it after the supply doctrine, on the same morning, at the step where a staff is asked (The staff, above). It hands in nothing for a side with no hold or cover intent in force, so every game without one is as it was. It draws no random number. [0117 items 12 to 15]

**The end.** A hold intent names a base of the side, or a base on the side's map that is not the side's (decision 0144, 1; below). It has no date: it stands until withdrawn. It is given to the base it names (The intent tree, above). It is a root: a commander states it by `set_intent` with the kind `hold`, and nothing may be formed above it. Under it the staff may form cover from the air (Covering a base from the air, below), strike ships from the base, "keep supplied" for the base (decision 0144, below), and, since decisions 0124 and 0125, a stand task or a carrier task for each task force its forces limit names (A task force as an addressee, below), and nothing else. Its limits are read as any intent's are: with no forces limit anywhere it may use the side's air groups based at the base, and it needs no source. [0117 items 2, 8 and 12]

**Its plan and the leaves.** A hold intent's plan, like that of a cover intent stated as a root, is drafted when the intent is first set and is made ready and released by the same leaves as a supply plan, by the staff's `approve` command. For each such root in priority order (the rank read up the tree, ranked first, then by number):

- A plan that has ended: one reason for giving no order ("the staff forms nothing and gives no order under" the intent), and no notice.
- A draft: with leave to prepare the staff approves it, to preparing, with a reason; without it, a reason that the plan waits for the commander and a `waits_for_leave_to_prepare` notice resting on it.
- A plan that is preparing (one the staff has just prepared this morning included): with leave to launch the staff releases it the same morning, with a reason; without it, a reason and a `waits_for_leave_to_launch` notice.

The staff forms nothing and orders nothing under a root whose plan is not released, and the doctrine below reads a plan it has just released as released the same morning. [0117 item 9; 0107 items 21 and 22]

**Each morning, for each hold intent in force whose plan is released,** in priority order, the staff first forms the tasks of the task forces its forces limit names, at the base's hex (A task force as an addressee, below); it does this before the cover, and what it forms is no part of the cover's three cases. Then it does the first of these that applies, and gives the reason:

1. **A cover stands.** Where a cover intent for the same base is in force, whoever made it and wherever it stands in the tree, the staff forms none. It hands in a decision to give no order whose reason names the cover that stands and who made it. This is the reason of every morning after the first on which the staff has formed cover.
2. **Nothing at the base to cover it with.** Where the intent may use no air group at the base that is a fighter group or a group that can search (Covering a base from the air, below; a group that is held counts here), the staff forms none. Its reason says there is nothing at the base to cover it with, and gives what would change that: a fighter group or a group that can search at the base, or in the forces limit.
3. **Otherwise** the staff forms cover from the air under it: it hands in a `form_intent` staff command (parent the hold intent, kind cover, the same base) with a reason that says the base is held, its plan is released and no cover stands. The command passes intake as any staff command does (The intent tree, above). The cover doctrine runs for the new sub-intent on the same morning, on the state as the formation will leave it, so its orders are handed in the same morning and name the new intent.

[0117 item 13]

**Its measure.** A hold intent is met on a day the base is the side's at the end of the day and its airfield is open. The measure of the intent is worked by the agent interface from the side's own view and nothing else, stored nowhere, so the same view gives the same measure after a save and a resume (`sim_agent::hold_measure`). It gives:

| Part | What |
| --- | --- |
| Days in force | The days from the day the intent was set to the last day resolved, both counted; or, for an intent withdrawn, to the day before it was withdrawn. 0 where the intent was set after the last day resolved |
| Days met | Of those days, the days on which the side's own events show the base held at the end of the day (`BaseAtDayEnd`) and its airfield open. A base has its airfield open on a day if it has an airfield now (and, where the side's events show it built, that it was built by then) and the damage to the field at the end of that day is below `ground.toml: airfield_landing_refused_at` (the last `AirfieldDamaged` or `AirfieldMended` event of the day or before it; where the side's events show none, the base's damage as it stands). A side's events of a base are its own only while the base is its own, so a base that was lost shows none of the days it was held before |
| Air raids | The daylight pulses in the days in force in which the base was bombed, from the bombs the side's own events show falling on it |
| Raids fought | Of those, the pulses in which a patrol of the side's, flown by a group now based at the base, fired |
| Days of supply | The base's days of supply today (Days of supply, above), or none where the base is not the side's or the hex has no figure |

It is shown in `command_state` (as `measure` on the hold intent) and in `report` (as `holds`), with the words "held on N of M days in force (the base the side's at the end of the day with its airfield open); N air raids, N fought by a patrol of the side's; N days of supply at the base today". It changes nothing and no rule reads it. [0117 items 14, 0116 item 2]

**A place not yet the side's.** A hold may name a base on the side's map that is not the side's: intake takes it in, and its plan is drafted, prepared and released by the leaves as any hold's. On each morning its plan is released and the base is not the side's (not yet, or no longer: a base the side held and lost is in the same case), the staff forms nothing and orders nothing under it, hands in no notice and marks nothing failed; its one reason says that it waits for the base to be the side's. [0144 items 1 to 3]

**With men.** On each morning a hold's plan is released and its base is the side's, before the tasks of its forces and the cover, the staff reads **the men of the hold**: the fighting units with men ashore in the base's hex that the side may use and the commander does not hold; those every forces limit on its chain names, or, where no forces limit on its chain names a land unit, all of them. A unit named in the forces limit of a "take a place" whose plan has not ended and whose place is not the side's, or of a task under it, is that operation's and is left to it. Each man of the hold on attack is set to defend (`set_posture`, with no instruction for when it is spent), with a reason; nothing else is ordered of them: on the defence they rest and dig in by the rules as they are (chapter 7). A unit on attack that is held gets a reason naming the hold. A task force on the transport mission named in the hold's forces limit is given no task of its own: the tasks of a task force are formed for the surface combat and carrier missions only (A task force as an addressee, below). [0144 items 4 to 6]

**The supply that follows.** On a morning the hold has men at the base, where no "keep supplied" for a base in the base's hex is in force: where the forces limit on the hold's chain names a source, the staff forms one under the hold, for the base (`form_intent`), with a floor of the margin of the hold's risk in days (10 at preserve the force, 5 at balanced, 0 at press: the staff's figures, placeholders) and no interval, so the staff uses 15 days. Its forces limit is the hold's, read up the tree: its ships, task forces and source. The supply doctrine serves it as any (The doctrine: keep a base supplied, above) from the next morning; nothing of that doctrine changes, but that a plan's runs are its supply intent's, which in the plan of a hold is the "keep supplied" formed under it. With no source named, the staff forms none and its reason says that the commander may name a source in the forces limit, or state "keep supplied" himself. A hold with no men at the base forms none and says nothing of supply: it plays as it did. [0144 items 7 to 9]

**A proposal.** `propose` for a hold or a cover intent runs the air doctrine for the tree of its root as if its plan were released, and keeps what the doctrine hands in for that tree: the sub-intents it would form, each air group it would order with its order, the arc with its threats, the decisions to give no order, and one reason. It has no projection and no runs. [0117 item 14]

A side sees: its own `StaffReason`, `StaffNotice`, `IntentFormed`, the `CommandTaken` of each staff command and the measure, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0117]

## Covering a base from the air

The task under the end "hold a place", and an intent a commander may state himself, as a root. The doctrine below is the whole of it. [0115]

**The intent.** A cover intent names a base of the side. It has no date. It is given to the base. The staff forms it under a hold intent (Holding a place, above), and a commander may state it himself by `set_intent` with the kind `cover`, when it is a root with a plan of its own, made ready and released by the leaves. Where it is the staff's, it belongs to its root's plan. [0115 item 1; 0117 items 4, 9 and 12]

**The groups it serves.** Each morning, for each cover intent in force whose plan is released, in priority order (the rank read up the tree, ranked first, then by number), the staff serves the air groups that:

- the intent may use (The six limits, above): named in every forces limit on its chain, or, with none, the side's groups at its base;
- are at the intent's base (a group named in a forces limit that is elsewhere is given no order);
- are not held, neither as a whole nor by their search arc (Holds and the override, above); and
- are not taken by a cover intent served earlier the same morning: a group serves one intent, and where two may use it the higher in priority has it.

They are taken in group number order. The doctrine gives an order only where what a group has differs from what the doctrine wants, so a morning on which nothing changed has no order for a group. Each order is a staff order for an air group, serves the cover intent, and passes intake as any staff order does (The forces limit on a staff's orders, above). [0115 items 2 and 3, 5 to 11]

A group is a **fighter group** if its aircraft type's role is fighter; a **search group** if it is not and its type has a search range above 0 (`search_range_nm`); otherwise it is neither. A group's **fatigue** and the number of its aircraft **able to fly** are the side's own view's: the engine's count of the aircraft that are ready, crewed and on a field and deck that let them fly, and the group's fatigue now (chapter 5, What a group can fly in a pulse).

**The risk level** is the cover intent's risk limit read up the tree, balanced where none is set. It gives two figures:

| Risk | Share of its aircraft a fighter group keeps over the base | Fatigue above which a group rests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve the force | 3/4 | 30 |
| Balanced | 1/2 | 45 |
| Press | 1/4 | 60 |

[0115 items 4, 5 and 8]

1. **Fighters fly the patrol.** A fighter group that does not rest (rule 4) is set to the combat air patrol mission, with the patrol share the risk gives (chapter 5, Missions and orders and Escorts and patrol), and the rest of its aircraft are its escorts. The staff orders the mission if it is not combat air patrol and the share if it is not the share wanted.
2. **The share is raised.** On a morning when the base was bombed on any of the three days before the day being resolved (as the side's own events show: the `BaseAttackedAgainst` events for the base), the share is raised by 1/4, and not above 1. The reason names the day.
3. **Groups that can search, search.** A search group that does not rest, and that no raid task in force may use as a bomber (**A raid's bomber**, below), is set to the search mission and to a search arc from the arc of rule 6, at its type's search range, and to search once a day at preserve the force and at balanced, in both daylight pulses at press (chapter 5, Air group orders). The staff orders the mission if it is not search, the setting if the group's is not the one the risk wants, and the arc if the group's arc (from, to and range) is not the one wanted, in that order. The reason of the setting gives the group's fatigue against the level and the risk. [0115; 0131 item 6]
4. **Rest.** A group rests for a day, by the rest mission, when its fatigue in the morning is above the level its risk gives (a fatigue equal to it does not rest). The order is given only where the group's mission is not rest already. Fighter groups are taken most tired first, then by number. A fighter group above the level rests if it has no aircraft able to fly, or if another fighter group that can fly is still not rested; **the last fighter group that can fly is never rested**, so the base is not left without a patrol while a fighter group can fly. A search group above the level always rests: it has no such exception. The engine's own rest threshold is not set by the staff. [0115 item 8]

5. **A group that is neither** is given no order, and the reason says it is left for a strike intent.
6. **The search arc.** It points at what the side knows. The threats are the hexes of the enemy's bases on the side's map and the estimated hexes of the contacts it holds. With none the arc is the whole circle (from and to both 0). Otherwise it is the narrowest arc, on a grid of ten degrees, that holds every threat as seen from the base's hex by the engine's own test of an arc (`bearing::in_arc`): widths of 10 to 350 degrees in steps of 10, and for a width every start from 0 to 350, the lowest start first, the first that holds every threat taken. It is ordered widened by 30 degrees at each end (from the start less 30, to the end plus 30, each modulo 360), and as the whole circle where the width and the 60 degrees come to 360 or more, or where no arc under 360 holds them. [0115 item 9]
7. **When it cannot.** On a morning when none of the fighter groups the doctrine serves for the intent has an aircraft able to fly (which includes the case that it serves none at all), the staff hands in a decision to give no order whose reason says it cannot meet the intent, with why, and a `cannot_meet` notice that rests on it. The standing interrupt `cannot_meet` of the commander fires on that notice (Interrupts, above). [0115 item 10]
8. **Reasons.** An order's reason says the group, its kind, its fatigue against the level of the risk, what the group has and what is wanted, and, for the share, the share and what raised it; for an arc, the threats and the arc they give, and the contacts the group's arc was set against that the side has since dropped. A decision to give no order is handed in on a morning on which a group of the intent got no order or the intent cannot be met: it lists each such group with its fatigue, mission and setting, the share, the level and the arc, and, where it cannot, the sentence that it cannot. [0115 item 11]

**A raid's bomber.** Where a raid task is in force at the cover's base (Raiding a base, below), a group that the raid may use and that can bomb the raid's target on normal reach is the raid's, though it can also search. The cover gives it no order: it is left out of the groups the cover serves, and the cover's reason says that the group is the raid's. The cover therefore searches only with the groups no raid in force may use as bombers. A fighter is not a bomber and stays the cover's: where a cover of its base that may use it is in force, the raid does not order it (Raiding a base, rule 16). The cover's order of the morning is worked first, so that the raid reads, for each fighter the cover has, the patrol share the cover wants this morning, or that the cover rests it. [0120, 16, amending 0115, 7]

**A strike-ships task's bomber, and the waters to deny** (decision 0123). A group that a strike-ships task in force at the cover's base may use as a bomber is the task's, as a raid's bomber is: the cover gives it no order (Striking ships from a base, below). The search arc of rule 6 has more threats where the base's search groups reach the waters of a deny intent in force with a released plan: every hex of those waters is added, beside the hexes of the enemy's bases and the contacts the side holds, where a range of a search group the cover serves touches them (Searching waters, below). A deny intent whose waters none of them touches adds none. One arc serves the base's search groups. [0123 item 13]

A cover intent's limits narrow as the tree says (The intent tree, above): a forces limit on the staff's cover intent names only groups its hold intent may use, and a risk limit is no bolder than its hold intent's.

**A bomber that takes a turn at the search** (decision 0131, 8). Where a raid task or a strike-ships task is in force at the cover's base and the cover has no group that can only search (a group that can search and cannot attack a ship), the task that has the bombers lends the cover its search, where it has two or more bomber groups that can search, can fly and are not held: the least tired of them, then the lowest numbered, chosen each morning. That group is the cover's that day, whatever the cover's forces limit says: it flies the cover's arc by rules 3 and 4 (it rests above the level, as any search group does) and the raid or the strike task gives it no order and says it searches today. With one such group, it holds the strike or the raid and the cover has no searcher. Where both a raid and a strike task have such bombers, the first in priority lends. Who lends is worked out once each morning, before the cover is served, and the same choice is read by the raid and the strike task. A group that searched yesterday and strikes today is given the missions in turn by orders. The search task's groups are read the same way (Searching waters, below). [0131 items 8 and 9]

**The cost of search.** The engine sends every aircraft of a search group that can fly, in the morning alone when it is set to search once a day and in both daylight pulses otherwise. A search tires a group by its own figure for distance where the fatigue figures give one (chapter 5, Fatigue gained and lost): with the live figure a flying boat group searching to 600 nm once a day is rested by the afternoon. The doctrine does not send part of a group. [0115, What it leaves for later steps; 0131]

A side sees: its own orders, their acceptance or refusal, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0115]

## Neutralising a base

The end "neutralise a base" and the doctrine at the area's level that serves it. It is run after the hold doctrine and before the cover doctrine, on the same morning (The day's order, below). It forms sub-intents and gives no order. It draws no random number. [0120 items 5 to 8]

**The end.** A neutralise intent names a **target**: a base on the side's map that is not the side's. It names no base of the side's own. It has no date: it stands until withdrawn. It is given to the area. It is a root, stated by the commander with `set_intent` and the kind `neutralise`, and its plan is drafted, made ready and released by the leaves as a hold intent's plan is (Holding a place, above). `set_intent` refuses a target that is not an enemy base on the side's map as "Not an enemy base"; so does `form_intent`. Under it the staff may form a raid task for the same target (Raiding a base, below) and nothing else. [0120 items 5 and 9]

**What it may use.** An intent given to the area may use no air group without a forces limit (The six limits, above); a neutralise intent is the one exception. With no forces limit on it or above it, it may use every base of the side for the forming of raid tasks, and each raid task may then use the air groups at its base. A forces limit that names air groups narrows it, as any does: a base qualifies (below) only where a group the limit names is, and only the groups it names count there. [0120 item 7]

**Each morning, for each neutralise intent in force whose plan is released,** in priority order, the staff reads each base of the side, in base number order, that the intent may use, with the distance from the base's hex to the target's, in nautical miles by air, and the groups the intent may use at it. A group counts whether it is held or not, as the hold doctrine counts one. A **bomber** here is a group that is not a fighter and whose type carries a weapon; its **reach** is its type's strike radius, the distance it can fly on normal reach. A base **qualifies** if it has a bomber whose reach is at least the distance. Then:

1. **No base qualifies.** The staff forms nothing and hands in a decision to give no order whose reason gives, for each base, its distance and the reach of each bomber type it has (or that it has no bomber group it may use), with a `cannot_meet` notice resting on it. It does this every morning on which no base qualifies. The standing interrupt `cannot_meet` fires on it (Interrupts, above).
2. **A base qualifies where a raid stands.** Where a raid task from that base at that target is in force under the intent, whoever made it, the staff forms no other. It hands in a decision to give no order whose reason names the raid that stands. It does this every morning after the first on which the raid has been formed.
3. **A base qualifies and no raid stands.** The staff forms one: a `form_intent` command (parent the neutralise intent, kind raid, the base, the target), with a reason that gives the distance and the bombers that reach. It passes intake as any staff command does. The raid doctrine runs for the new sub-intent on the same morning, on the state as the formation will leave it, so its orders are handed in the same morning and name the new intent.

Where the target is not an enemy base on the side's map when the staff looks (it has been taken, or the picture no longer shows it), the staff forms nothing, hands in a decision to give no order that says so, and no notice. A raid task the staff has formed stands until it is withdrawn with the intent above it: the staff does not withdraw it. [0120 items 6 and 7]

**Its measure.** A neutralise intent is met on a day on which no base of the side was raided from the air. The measure is worked by the agent interface from the side's own view and nothing else, stored nowhere (`sim_agent::neutralise_measure`). It gives:

| Part | What |
| --- | --- |
| Days in force | The days from the day the intent was set to the last day resolved, both counted; or, for an intent withdrawn, to the day before it was withdrawn. 0 where the intent was set after the last day resolved |
| Raids flown | The distinct pairs of a day and a pulse, in the days in force, in which a group of the side launched a base strike at the target (from the side's own `BaseStrikeLaunched` events). Two groups launching in one pulse are one raid |
| Claimed on the ground | The aircraft the side claims to have destroyed on the ground, from the air, at the target's hex, in the days in force (the side's `ClaimReceived` events). It is a claim, as the briefing's are: never below what happened and never corrected. Where the target has changed hands, the hex is the side's own base's |
| Raids on its own bases | The air raids on the side's bases in the days in force, summed over every base of the side (`air_raids_on`). A side cannot tell where a raid on it came from, so they all count |
| Days met | Of the days in force, those on which none of the side's bases was raided from the air |

It is shown in `command_state` (as `measure` on the neutralise intent) and in `report` (as `neutralisations`), with words. It changes nothing and no rule reads it. [0120 item 8]

**A proposal.** `propose` for a neutralise intent runs the air doctrine for the tree of its root as if its plan were released (Holding a place, A proposal). [0120]

A side sees: its own `StaffReason`, `StaffNotice`, `IntentFormed`, the `CommandTaken` of each staff command and the measure, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0120]

## Raiding a base

The task "raid a base" and the doctrine at the base's level that serves it. It is run last in the staff's morning, after the cover doctrine (The day's order, below). It draws no random number. A raid is launched, escorted, met and resolved by chapter 5 as it stands: this doctrine only sets the orders of the groups. [0120 items 9 to 16]

**The task.** A raid intent names a base of the side and a target (a base on the side's map that is not the side's). It has no date. It is given to the base. The staff forms it under a neutralise intent naming the same target, and a commander may state it himself by `set_intent` with the kind `raid`, when it is a root with a plan of its own, made ready and released by the leaves. Where it is the staff's, it belongs to its root's plan. A raid task is a sub-intent, so its limits narrow as the tree says (The intent tree, above). [0120 item 9]

**The groups it serves.** Each morning, for each raid intent in force whose plan is released, in priority order, the staff reads the groups that:

- the raid may use (The six limits, above): named in every forces limit on its chain, or, with none, the side's groups at its base;
- are at the raid's base;
- are not held as a whole (Holds and the override, above);
- are not taken by a raid served earlier the same morning; and
- are the raid's by rule 16, below.

They are taken in group number order. A group is a **bomber** if it is not a fighter group, its type carries a weapon, and it can reach the target on normal reach (its type's strike radius at least the distance from the base). A group is a **fighter group** and has a **fatigue** and a number of aircraft **able to fly**, as the cover doctrine reads them (Covering a base from the air, above). [0120 item 10]

**The risk level** is the raid's risk limit read up the tree, balanced where none is set. It gives five figures:

| Set by the risk level | Preserve the force | Balanced | Press |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A group rests above a fatigue of | 30 | 45 | 60 |
| Share of its bombers a group holds back | A third | None | None |
| The least a raid sends | 12 | 6 | 1 |
| A raid without an escort | Does not go | Goes | Goes |
| A group stands down after losing | A tenth of what it sent | A quarter | Never |

The fatigue levels are the cover doctrine's. The first two rows are decision 0113's; the last three are placeholders that rest on no row. [0120 item 14 and the table]

1. **Which bombers go.** A bomber group goes unless its fatigue in the morning is above the level its risk gives (a fatigue equal to it goes), or it stands down by rule 3. [0120 item 11]
2. **Whether the raid goes.** What a group can send is the aircraft it is able to fly less its held share, cut to a whole aircraft: the held share is the one the risk gives this morning, whatever the group has set now. The raid goes on a morning when the bombers that go can send, between them, at least the least weight the risk asks, and more than none, and, at preserve the force, when the escorts the fighters at the base can give this morning are above 0. A fighter the raid has gives every aircraft it is able to fly; a fighter of the base's cover gives what the patrol share the cover wants this morning leaves of the aircraft it is able to fly, cut to a whole aircraft, and none where the cover rests it. At balanced and press the raid goes without escort. Where the raid does not go, every bomber rests. [0120 item 12]
3. **What the last raid cost.** A bomber group stands down for a morning when its last base strike launch was on the day before, and the aircraft it lost in the pulse of that launch, shot down or ditched (the side's own `AircraftLost` and `AircraftDitched` events of that pulse), come to at least the share of the aircraft it sent that the risk gives (a tenth, a quarter). A group that lost less, or that flew its last raid on any earlier day, does not stand down; at press none does. A group stands down on the morning right after that raid only. [0120 item 13]
4. **The orders.** A bomber is set to the strike mission at the target (`BaseStrike`) if it goes and the raid goes, and to rest otherwise. Its held share is set to the share the risk gives, if the group has another. A fighter group the raid has is set to the combat air patrol mission with a patrol share of 0, so that every aircraft it can fly is an escort; it is given no rest order and flies only on a morning a raid goes. A fighter group of the base's cover is given no order. Any other group is given no order, and the reason says so. An order is given only where the group's mission or setting differs from what is wanted, so a morning on which nothing changed has no order for a group. Orders are handed in in group number order, a mission before a setting. [0120 item 10; 0114]
5. **When it cannot.** On a morning when no bomber group the raid may use and is not held can reach the target, or, at preserve the force, the base has no fighter group it may use or draw escorts from (a fighter of the cover counts, though the cover rests it), the staff hands in a decision to give no order whose reason says it cannot meet the intent and why, with a `cannot_meet` notice resting on it. A raid that is not sent for want of weight, fatigue or escort is no failure: its bombers rest and the reason says why. [0120 item 15]
6. **Reasons.** An order's reason says the group, its kind, its fatigue against the level, what the group has and what is wanted, the weight the bombers that go can send against what the risk asks, and the escorts the fighters can give. A decision to give no order is handed in on a morning on which a group got no order, or the raid cannot be met, or the raid has no group at the base that the staff may order: it lists those groups and says whether the raid goes. [0120]

**Whose a group is (rule 16).** A cover and a raid task may both be in force at one base and both may use a group. A group has one owner, decided by its kind and not by the rank of the intents, so that a base both defended and raiding does both:

- a fighter group is the cover's where a cover of its base that may use it is in force, and the raid draws the escorts the cover's patrol share leaves (rule 2); with no such cover it is the raid's and the raid sets it (rule 4);
- a group that can bomb the target is the raid's, though it can also search: the cover leaves it out (Covering a base from the air, A raid's bomber). A bomber a strike-ships task in force may use is that task's where the task ranks above the raid, and the raid leaves it out (Striking ships from a base, above);
- any other group is the cover's if a cover that may use it is in force (it may search), and otherwise no one's;
- rank decides only between two tasks of the same kind that may use one group: a group serves one cover, and one raid, the first in priority order.

[0120 item 16, amending 0115, 7]

A side sees: its own orders, their acceptance or refusal, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0120]

## Denying an area

The end "deny an area" and the doctrine at the area's level that serves it. It is run after the neutralise doctrine and before the cover doctrine, on the same morning (The day's order, below). It forms sub-intents and gives no order to a unit. It draws no random number. [0123 items 1 to 3]

**The end.** A deny intent names a hex and a distance in whole hexes. It names no base. It has no date: it stands until withdrawn. Its code is 6. It is given to the area. It is a root, stated by the commander with `set_intent` and the kind `deny`, and its plan is drafted, made ready and released by the leaves as a hold intent's plan is (Holding a place, above). `set_intent` refuses a hex that is not on the map as "Not on the map"; so does `form_intent`. Under it the staff may form the tasks "strike ships from a base" and "search waters from a base" and, since decisions 0124 and 0125, a stand task or a carrier task for each task force its forces limit names (A task force as an addressee, below), and nothing else. [0123 items 1 and 11; 0124 item 8]

**The waters** of a deny intent are the hexes on the map within the distance of its hex, by hex distance, in hex order. A hex off the map is no part of them. With a distance of 0 the waters are the hex alone. [0123 item 1]

**What it may use.** As the neutralise intent: with no forces limit on it or above it, every base of the side and the air groups at each; a forces limit that names air groups narrows it to the bases where a group it names is, and only the groups named count there. [0123 item 3]

**Each morning, for each deny intent in force whose plan is released,** in priority order, and for each base of the side that the intent may use, in base number order, the staff reads the bomber groups and the groups that can search that the intent may use at the base:

- A **bomber** is a group that is not a fighter and whose type carries a weapon. Its **reach** is its type's strike radius, on normal reach.
- A group that can **search** is a group that is not a fighter and whose type has a search range above 0. Its **range** is that search range.
- A reach or a range **touches** the waters when, from the base's hex, a hex of the waters is within it by the engine's own test of an air distance (chapter 5, Air distance).

Then, for the base:

1. **Striking ships.** Where a bomber whose reach touches the waters is there, and no strike-ships task from that base is in force under the intent, whoever made it, the staff forms one: a `form_intent` command (parent the deny intent, kind strike ships, the base), with a reason that gives the base's distance from the nearest hex of the waters and the bombers that touch them. Where one stands, the staff forms no other and hands in a decision to give no order that names it.
2. **Searching the waters.** Where a group that can search whose range touches the waters is there, and no cover of that base is in force (under any intent), and no search-waters task from that base is in force under the intent, the staff forms one in the same way. Where a cover of the base is in force the staff forms none: the cover's arc takes the waters as threats (Covering a base from the air, above), and the staff hands in a decision to give no order that says so. Where a search task stands it forms no other.
3. **No base reaches.** Where, over all the bases, no bomber's reach and no group's range touches the waters, the staff forms nothing and hands in a decision to give no order whose reason gives, for each base, its distance from the nearest hex of the waters and the reach of each bomber type and the range of each group that can search (or that it has none the intent may use), with a `cannot_meet` notice resting on it. It does this every morning on which no base reaches. The standing interrupt `cannot_meet` fires on it (Interrupts, above).

After the bases, the staff forms the tasks of the task forces the intent's forces limit names, at the centre of the waters (A task force as an addressee, below). A task formed for a force, or one that stands, counts as serving the intent, so the notice of case 3 is not handed in on a morning when one does. The new sub-intent's orders are handed in on the same morning, as for a raid (Neutralising a base, above). A task the staff has formed stands until it is withdrawn with the intent above it: the staff does not withdraw it. [0123 items 2 and 3; 0124 item 8]

**Its measure.** A deny intent is met on a day that ends with no contact of the enemy held in its waters. The measure is worked by the agent interface from the side's own view and nothing else, stored nowhere (`sim_agent::deny_measure`), as a neutralise intent's is. It gives:

| Part | What |
| --- | --- |
| Days in force | The days from the day the intent was set to the last day resolved, both counted; or, for an intent withdrawn, to the day before it was withdrawn |
| Contacts held | The distinct contacts the side held in the waters (hexes within the distance of the hex, by hex distance) at the end of a day in force. A contact is held from the report that made or moved it to the one that dropped it |
| Strikes launched | The `StrikeLaunched` events of the side's own groups in the days in force that are now at a base which was given a strike-ships task under this intent, on a day that task stood. They count wherever the strike went, not only at the waters: a base given the task strikes what its groups reach. A group rebased since counts for the base it is at now |
| Claimed sunk, claimed hits | The ships the side claims to have sunk, and the hits it claims, from the air (`ClaimReceived`, from the air) at a hex of the waters in the days in force. A claim is what the side's people believe: never below what happened and never corrected |
| Days met | Of the days in force, those that ended with no contact held in the waters |

It is shown in `command_state` (as `measure` on the deny intent) and in `report` (as `denials`), with words. It changes nothing and no rule reads it. [0123 item 4]

**A proposal.** `propose` for a deny intent runs the air doctrine for the tree of its root as if its plan were released (Holding a place, A proposal). [0123]

A side sees: its own `StaffReason`, `StaffNotice`, `IntentFormed`, the `CommandTaken` of each staff command and the measure, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0123]

The task "strike ships from a base" and the doctrine at the base's level that serves it. It is run after the raid doctrine, on the same morning (The day's order, below). It gives orders to air groups and draws no random number. A strike is launched, sized, met and resolved by chapter 5 as it stands: this doctrine only sets the standing orders of the groups. [0123 items 5 to 9]

**The task.** A strike-ships intent names a base of the side. Its code is 7. It has no date. It is given to the base. | Its target list | Carrier, battleship, cruiser | Every kind | Every kind |
| Its reach | Normal | Normal | Extended |
| Share it holds back | A third | None | None |

The rows are decision 0113's table, as the raid doctrine takes its fatigue levels and held share. [0123 item 7]

Orders are handed in in group number order, a mission before the settings in the order above. The reason of this order names the air group as the level that decided it (A doctrine belongs to a level, below). The task never sets sizing off. [0123 item 9]

**Reasons.** An order's reason gives the group, its kind, its fatigue against the level, what it has and what is wanted. [0123]

A side sees: its own orders, their acceptance or refusal, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0123]

The task "search waters from a base" and the doctrine at the base's level that serves it. It is run last in the staff's morning (The day's order, below). It gives orders to air groups and draws no random number. [0123 items 11 to 13]

**The task.** A search-waters intent names a base of the side. Its code is 8. It has no date. It is given to the base. A deny intent whose waters none of those groups reaches adds nothing. The base's searchers have one arc, the cover's (Covering a base from the air, above). [0123 item 13]

A side sees: its own orders, their acceptance or refusal, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0123]

## A task force as an addressee

The task force is a level that an intent is addressed to, and three kinds of task are given to it: standing at a hex by night, operating a carrier force, and bombarding a base (below; the third is formed under neutralise and take a place, Bombarding a base). The staff forms them, at the level above, from the forces limit. This section is the rules of being addressed and of forming; each task's own doctrine follows. It draws no random number. [0124 items 6 to 8; 0125 items 1 and 2]

**What it commands.** An intent addressed to a task force of the side may use that force's ships and nothing else, and the air groups aboard those ships where every forces limit on its chain names the group or has the force inside it. The addressee has code 4 and holds the force's number. Two things follow from it:

- A staff's order to the force is inside the forces limit of the intent it serves, whatever limits stand above the intent (The forces limit on a staff's orders, above).
- A commander's order to a force an intent in force is addressed to holds it, as an order to a force in a forces limit does (Holds and the override, above).

**Forming.** Under a deny or a hold intent in force whose plan is released, the doctrine of that level forms a task for each task force named in the nearest forces limit on the intent's chain (its own, for a root), in task force number order, where no task of that kind for that force is in force anywhere on the side, whoever made it and wherever it stands in the side's trees (a commander's own task stated as a root counts):

- a **stand task** for a force on the surface combat mission with a ship afloat, at the end's hex: the centre of the waters under a deny intent, the base's hex under a hold intent;
- a **carrier task** for a force on the carrier mission with a carrier afloat that has an air group aboard, at the hex the force is in that morning. The staff does not choose where a carrier force stands. A commander who wants it elsewhere states the task himself, as a root, or moves the force.

With no task force named by the commander in a forces limit, no such task is formed. A force on any other mission is given no task. The staff hands in a `form_intent` command and a reason for each task it forms; where a task stands it hands in a decision to give no order that names the task, who made it and where it stands, and forms no other. So a force has one task of each kind, and no group is ordered twice for one force. Under a deny intent this runs after the bases' tasks, and a task formed or standing counts as serving the end; under a hold intent it runs first. A task formed is a sub-intent as any is (The intent tree, above), and a commander may state either kind himself as a root: it then needs a plan of its own, made ready and released by the leaves. [0124 items 6 to 8; 0125 items 1 and 2; the architect's ruling of Nov 16, 2026, made on the loading check of the second proof (decision 0128)]

A side sees: its own `IntentFormed`, `StaffReason` and the `CommandTaken` of the command, whole. The enemy sees none of it. [0124]

## Standing at a hex by night

The task "stand at a hex" and the doctrine at the task force's level that serves it. It is run after the search doctrine, before the carrier doctrine (The day's order, below). It gives orders to a task force and draws no random number. The engine's half is one rule of movement, the night stand (chapter 2, Halting at a marked waypoint and steaming on). [0124 items 1 to 5 and 9 to 15]

**The task.** A stand-at intent names a task force of the side and a hex, the **station**. Its code is 9. It has no date. It is given to the force (A task force as an addressee, above). It may stand under a deny intent where the station is the centre of the waters, and under a hold intent where the station is the base's hex; a commander may state it himself as a root. [0124 item 7]

**The force's morning.** Each morning, for each stand task in force whose plan is released, in priority order, and whose force is not held (a force held, or with a ship held, is given no order), the staff decides for the force, at its level, from the side's view:

1. **The threat.** There is a threat when the side holds a contact of ships within 15 hexes of the station, counted in whole hexes from the contact's estimated hex. At preserve the force, only a contact that reports no more ships than the force has afloat counts. The nearest that counts is named, the lowest number where two tie; at preserve the force a nearer contact that does not count is named with its ships against the force's.
2. **Whether it sorties tonight.** At press, every night. At balanced and at preserve the force, on a day with a threat.
3. **The speed and the route.** The force must be stopped at its **home port**: not under way, not halted and on no leg, at its own home port where it has one, else at a base of the side, which an order that morning sets as its home port. A force that is not stopped at a home port is **away from home**: it is given no order, and the reason says where it is and when it is expected. A force stopped at its home port is routed to the station and home again, the station marked to stand at. The engine's own measure of the passage out (the passage forecast of chapter 2) is worked at cruising speed and then at full speed, and the first that brings the force to the station within the night's 12 hours is the speed ordered. Where neither does, there is no sortie, and the notice that the intent cannot be met is handed in. Where the station is the force's own hex, it is given no route.
4. **Fuel comes first.** The fuel the sortie needs is, for each ship, the engine's measure of the route to the station and home with no mark at that speed, times the margin the risk gives (table). A force with a ship that has less aboard, or that the engine's measure has run dry short of the route (the measure gives no more burnt than is aboard), does not sortie [0130 item 4]. Its reason gives the two figures and the day from which the force may be used: the day after it will have fuelled where it lies (the hours fuelling takes there by chapter 2, Refuelling in port: the longest ship's lack of a full load at the berth rate where the port has a berth, else its lighter rate), the day counted from the start of the day and never today, or no day where the base has less fuel stock than the ships lack of their need or gives no rate, and then the notice that the intent cannot be met. The notice is handed in on each morning that holds. Ammunition is read in the same pass (Ammunition as policy, below): a force short of it does not sortie either, and the day it may be used is the later of the two.
5. **Readiness and rules of engagement.** On a day it sorties, or stands at its own hex on a day it would sortie, the force is set to battle readiness; on a day it does not, to normal. Its rules of engagement are set once, from the risk: the stance engage and the break-off level of the table; the straggler instruction and the keep share are left as they are.
6. **The order of the orders** is home port, rules of engagement, readiness, speed, route, each only where it differs from what the force has. A reason with no order is handed in on each morning the force is not routed; a morning with an order has its reasons on the orders.
7. **Reasons** carry the contact and its distance, the fuel aboard against the fuel needed, the hours to the station and the level, written "decided at the level of task_force:N".

The risk is the task's risk limit read up the tree, balanced where none is set.

| Set by the risk level | Preserve the force | Balanced | Press |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| It sorties | On a threat it is not outnumbered by | On a threat | Every night |
| Fuel needed, of the passage out and home | One and a half times | One and a fifth | Once |
| Break-off level | A quarter | A half | Three quarters |

The fuel and break-off rows are decision 0113's. The 15 hexes are a placeholder: a day's steaming at 25 knots. The force steams for home at the morning and is at sea, within reach of the enemy's aircraft, for as long as the passage takes. [0124 items 9 to 15]

**Radio silence.** At preserve the force, and where report figures are loaded, the doctrine of the task force sets the force to radio silence, by the order `set_radio_silence`, on a morning it is not set already (chapter 1, Commands and their pictures, Radio silence). At balanced and at press it leaves the setting as it finds it, and it never turns silence off; a commander does. The reason says the force is not on silence and that reports travel, and that silence buys nothing yet because nothing listens to radio traffic (decision 0136, rule 19). The order is not given where the force is held. [0136 items 18 to 20]

A side sees: its own orders, their acceptance or refusal, reasons and notices, whole, with the force's `TaskForceHalted` and `TaskForceSteamedOn`. The enemy sees none of it. [0124]

## Ammunition as policy

The staff's use of rearming (chapter 2, Rearming in port; decision 0122). It adds no rule of the engine and no intent: ammunition is policy, as fuel is. It is read by the stand doctrine and by the choice of a free escort, and it gives no order of its own; it gives orders no more than a force that is short of fuel does. It draws no random number. [0127]

**Short of ammunition.** A ship is short when its rounds aboard are below the share of its outfit its risk gives, or its fired tubes are more than the share of its tubes its risk gives. The risk is the task's, read up the tree, balanced where none is set. A ship whose class carries the marker for ammunition that is not limiting (chapter 2) is never short of rounds. A force is short when any ship of it is. [0127 item 1]

| Set by the risk level | Preserve the force | Balanced | Press |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A ship is short of rounds below | Three quarters of its outfit | A half | A quarter |
| A ship is short of torpedoes with more fired than | None | A half of its tubes (whole tubes, cut) | Never |

The shares are placeholders that rest on no row. A ship is short of rounds only when its rounds are below the level, not at it. [0127]

**What a short force does not do.** A force that is short does not sortie (Standing at a hex by night, rule 4) and is not a free escort (Escorting and covering a run, A free escort). Its reason gives each short ship, its rounds against the level and her tubes fired against the tubes allowed. A force whose station is its own hex is given no route and is not asked: the check is made for a force that would sortie. [0127 item 2]

**The day it may be used.** A short force stopped at a base of its side that holds ammunition and gives a rate for it is left there, and rearms by chapter 2. Its reason gives the day from which it may be used: the day after the last of its short ships will be no longer short, counted from the start of the day and never today, by the engine's own measure of the hours rearming takes there (the force line's `ammunition_lacked`, chapter 2, What the tools say): the berths as the queue would give them, a ship's fuel first, then the tons it must take to be short no longer over its ammunition rate, the longest ship's. The tons it must take are the rounds to the level, rounded up to a whole round, and for a ship short of torpedoes the whole of the rounds she lacks, because rounds are taken first, plus the torpedoes over the number allowed. Cargo is not counted, though a ship's cargo uses hours before its fuel and its ammunition: the measure is made as the tools make it. [0127 item 3]

**No day.** Where the base the force lies at holds no ammunition, holds less than its short ships need together (tons to be short no longer), or gives no rate for ammunition to a ship that needs some, the reason gives no day, and the staff hands in that it cannot meet the intent (the cannot-meet notice, on each morning that holds). It does not send the force to another base: moving a force's home is the commander's. [0127 item 4]

**Read with fuel.** Fuel and ammunition are read in one pass. A force sorties when it is neither short of fuel (Standing at a hex by night, rule 4) nor short of ammunition. A force short of both gives one reason with both, and the day it may be used is the later of the two days; if either has no day, it has none, and the notice is handed in. A force short of ammunition only gives a reason that says so and takes no order for the night. [0127 item 5]

The doctrine's version, `supply-13`, is since this section. Shortness reads the ships alone: it does not ask whether the base holds ammunition or the scenario gives weights and rates. A force whose ships are short, at a base that cannot rearm them, therefore does not sortie and has no day, and the staff hands in that it cannot meet the intent, as a force short of fuel at a base with none does. A force whose ships are full of ammunition is never short, so a game in which no ship has fired and none starts short is the game it was. [0127]

A side sees: its own orders, reasons and notices, whole, as for any doctrine. The enemy sees none of it. [0127]

## Operating a carrier force

The task "operate a carrier force from a hex" and the doctrine at the task force's level that serves it. It is run last in the staff's morning (The day's order, below). It gives orders to a task force and to the air groups aboard its ships, reuses the cover, strike and search pieces of the base's doctrines, and draws no random number. It adds no rule of the engine. [0125 items 1 to 11]

**The task.** An operate-carriers intent names a task force of the side and a hex. Its code is 10. It has no date. It is given to the force. It may use the force's ships and the air groups aboard them. It may stand under a deny intent and under a hold intent, and a commander may state it himself as a root. [0125 item 1]

**The groups.** Each morning, for each operate-carriers task in force whose plan is released, in priority order, and whose force is not held, the staff decides for the air groups aboard the force's ships that are not held, in group number order. The risk is the task's risk limit read up the tree, balanced where none is set.

1. **Fighters fly the patrol,** with the patrol share the risk gives (three quarters, a half, a quarter of the aircraft), raised by a quarter and not above 1 on a morning when the force was attacked from the air on any of the three days before (read from the side's own events: an air attack on a ship of the force). The rest of a fighter group are its escorts.
2. **Who searches.** A group that can search (not a fighter, its type has a search range) and cannot attack a ship (its type carries no weapon) searches. Where the force has none, the least tired of the groups that can do both and can fly, then the lowest numbered, searches, chosen each morning, and the others strike; where none of them can fly, the lowest numbered of them (decision 0131, 7, amending decision 0125, 4). A group that searched yesterday and strikes today is given the missions in turn by orders. The reason names each such group's fatigue and the aircraft it can fly. A searching group is set to search once a day at preserve the force alone, and in both daylight pulses at balanced and at press (decision 0131, 6, as amended on Nov 16, 2026: a carrier force searched in the afternoon as well as the morning), the setting ordered after the mission and before the arc, and only where the group's setting differs; the reason says which applied ("at balanced risk a carrier force searches in both daylight pulses"). A search is flown at the type's search range over the arc of the cover's rule (Covering a base from the air, below), taken from the force's hex, with these threats: the enemy's bases on the side's map, the contacts the side holds, and, under a deny intent, the hexes of its waters.
3. **The rest strike ships,** by the strike table (Striking ships from a base, above), with strike sizing on: a carrier has more to hit than one force.
4. **Rest.** A group rests for a day when its fatigue in the morning is above the level the risk gives (30, 45, 60). The last fighter group that can fly is never rested, and neither is the last group that searches; a striking group rests above the level with no exception.
5. **Who is left.** A group that is none of these is given no order.

**The force.** The force's orders come before its groups', in the order readiness, rules of engagement, speed, route, then each group's mission first.

6. **Its place.** Where the force is not at the task's hex and not on its way home, it is routed there, with a speed order first where the speed it has is not the speed of item 11, and given no readiness order. At the hex it is given no route and is set to battle readiness. A force **on its way home** is under way, or halted, with its home port's hex as its last waypoint; it is given no route. [0125 item 7; 0130 item 1]
7. **Fuel.** The fuel the force needs is, for each ship, the engine's measure of the passage to the home port at cruise, times the margin the risk gives (the table of Standing at a hex by night). A ship the engine's measure has run dry short of a passage is short of the fuel for it, here, for the passage back and in the sortie of a stand task (Standing at a hex by night): the measure gives no more burnt than is aboard. On a morning a ship is short, the force is routed home at cruise at every risk, with a speed order first where it has another speed and no readiness order, and the reason gives the figures. At home and not at its hex, it fuels (chapter 2, Refuelling in port), and on the first morning every ship has what the passage to the hex and home again needs, at the speed of item 11, with the margin, it is routed back; else it stays. Its home port is the force's own; else the base it is stopped at; with none, the fuel is not weighed and the reason says so. [0125 item 8; 0130 items 1 and 4]
8. **Rules of engagement.** The stance is avoid, since a carrier force does not seek a gun action, and the break-off level is the risk table's.
9. **When it cannot.** On a morning the force has no carrier afloat, or no air group aboard able to fly, the staff hands in that it cannot meet the intent, and gives no order that morning. On a morning a ship of the force has no fuel aboard, or the engine's forecast refuses the passage to the task's hex as out of fuel, the force cannot move: the staff gives it no route and no speed order and does not weigh closing, hands in that it cannot meet the intent with a reason that names the ship out of fuel and the hex where the force lies, and gives the air groups their orders as on any morning (the architect's ruling of Nov 16, 2026, recorded in decision 0131). The stand doctrine has no such hole: it routes a force only on a sortie whose fuel it has measured, and a ship that runs dry is short. [0125 item 10; 0131]
10. **Reasons** carry each group's fatigue against the level, the share and what raised it, the threats and the arc, the fuel aboard against the fuel needed, and the level, "decided at the level of task_force:N".

**Speed and closing the range.** Two rules of the force's commander, after the second proof of stage 2. [0130]

11. **Speed.** A force routed to its hex, to a hex it closes to, or back from its home port to its hex steams at cruise where the engine's measure of the passage at cruise brings it there within the 12 hours of the night, and otherwise at full speed; at preserve the force always at cruise. A speed order is given only where the force has another speed. The fuel for the passage back from home, and for closing (item 14), is measured at the speed chosen. The passage home for fuel is at cruise (item 7). [0130 item 1]
12. **A contact to close on.** On a morning the force is not held, not on its way home and not fuelling at home, and a bomber group aboard (not a fighter, its type carrying a weapon) has an aircraft able to fly, the staff weighs the contacts of ships the side holds. The **reach** is the least strike radius (normal reach) among those bomber groups. A contact is **fresh** when it is no more than 3 pulses old. The kinds closed on are: nothing at preserve the force, a carrier at balanced, any kind at press. Of the fresh contacts of a kind closed on that are within a night at full speed (the force's full speed by the engine's measure, times 12 hours) and the reach, by air distance from the force's hex, the staff takes the first: the kind first in the order of importance, then the nearest, then the lowest number. Where that one is within the reach the force does not close; where it is beyond, the force closes on it. With fresh contacts of a kind closed on and none within a night and the reach, it is too far. [0130 item 2]
13. **Where it goes.** The first hex along the shortest sea route from the force to the contact's hex that is within the reach of the contact: it goes no nearer than its bombers need. It is routed there at the speed of item 11. [0130 item 3]
14. **Fuel first.** It does not close where a ship is short (item 7) of the engine's measure of the passage to that hex and from there to its home port, at the speed chosen, times the risk's margin. With no home port the fuel is not weighed. [0130 item 4]
15. **It stays while something is in reach.** A force that has left its task's hex to close (one not at its hex whose last route ends at a hex that is neither the task's nor its home port's) is given no route on a morning the first contact of item 12 is within the reach, and stays; where that contact is beyond the reach it closes again. On a morning it holds none, in reach or to close on, it is routed back to its task's hex by item 6. [0130 item 5]
16. **Its arc, readiness and reasons.** Its search arc is taken from the hex the force is in that morning. It is set to battle readiness on a morning it closes or stays, as at its hex. The reason of a closing route gives the contact, its kind and age, its distance against the reach and against a night at full speed and the reach, the hex chosen, its distance and the hours to it, and the fuel; on a morning it does not close the reason says why: preserve the force closes on nothing, nothing fresh, nothing of a kind it closes on, already in reach, too far, no sea route, not the fuel, or no bomber group that can fly. These words join the reason it stays on its task where there is one. [0130 items 6 to 8]

**Initiative.** One rule reads a commander's rating (chapter 1, Commanders), and it is this one. [0137]

17. **A contact of his own.** In item 12 each fresh contact is weighed at a level of risk: one that is the force's own (chapter 1, A contact of its own) at the level the table gives, one it was told of at the task's risk, whatever the rating. The kinds closed on are read at that level, contact by contact, and the first contact is taken from those of a kind closed on at their own level. At preserve the force the force closes on nothing unless a fresh contact is weighed at a bolder level. With command levels off, no commander or a steady one, every contact is weighed at the task's risk, and the rule is items 11 to 16 as they were. [0137 items 7 and 8]

| The commander | A contact of his own is weighed at |
| --- | --- |
| Cautious | One level more careful than the task's risk: press as balanced, balanced as preserve the force (which closes on nothing), preserve the force as itself |
| Steady, or none named | The task's risk |
| Bold | One level bolder: preserve the force as balanced, balanced as press, press as itself |

18. **Nothing else moves with it.** The speed (item 11), the fuel and its margin (items 7 and 14), where it goes (item 13), its search, its patrol share, its strike settings and its break-off level are the task's risk's, as before; a bold commander at preserve the force who closes goes at cruise and measures his fuel with preserve the force's margin. He stays inside his task's forces. Nothing here is a refusal. [0137 item 9]
19. **The reason says so.** Where a rating changed how a fresh contact was weighed, the reason for closing or for not closing names the commander, the command, his rating, that the contact is the force's own, the level it was weighed at against the task's risk and the kinds closed on at that level; the binding of a closing route says that a commander of that rating weighs a contact of his own at that level. Where no rating changed how a contact was weighed, no reason's words change. [0137 item 10]

A task already standing is not withdrawn when the force's carrier is lost: item 9 hands in the notice. [0125 items 3 to 11]

**Radio silence.** The force's morning, at preserve the force and where report figures are loaded, begins with the order `set_radio_silence` on for a force not on silence, as Standing at a hex by night, above, has it; at balanced and at press the setting is left as found. [0136 items 18 to 20]

A side sees: its own orders, their acceptance or refusal, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none of it. The staff reads only the side's own view: the contacts it holds, its own forces, the map and the engine's forecasts of its own passages. [0125, 0130]

## Readying a landing, and building up a base

Decision 0141, step 2 of stage 3. Two tasks a commander may state as roots, the first doctrines that order land units. Each is served each morning in the air doctrine's run, after the carrier forces, by the level it is addressed to: a "ready a landing" by its task force, a "build up a base" by its base. Its numbers are the ground figures (chapter 7); with none loaded it gives no order and says so. The staff reads only the side's own view.

**Leaves.** A plan of either kind takes leave to prepare as a hold's does (a draft waits for it, with a reason and the notice `waits_for_leave_to_prepare`), and works while it is preparing or released. It never asks leave to launch and the staff never releases it: it launches nothing. Nothing is ordered without leave to prepare. [0141 items 5 and 15]

**The units a task may use.** Those named in every forces limit on its chain (The six limits, above). With none on the chain, a "build up a base" may use the working units of the side with men ashore in its base's hex, and a "ready a landing" none. The same test is intake's (The forces limit on a staff's orders) and the override's. Each morning the tasks of both kinds are served in priority order (ranked first, then by number), and each leaves out, with words in its reasons: a unit before its day (A unit before its day, above); a unit that is held, "the staff plans without it"; a unit a higher task took this morning, "serves intent:N, which is higher in priority"; a unit with no man left; and, for a build-up, a unit not ashore in the base's hex. A unit serves one task. [0141 items 8, 13, 14 and 16]

**Ready a landing.** Each morning:

1. **The force's order.** Where the force's preparation is not for the task's hex with exactly the units kept as its roster, the staff gives the force `prepare_landing` for the hex with those units (chapter 7, Preparing a landing). A force that is held is given none, and a force before its day is given none and the task gets one reason. A working unit kept is on the roster and is given no order. [0141 items 2 and 4]
2. **Each fighting unit on the roster** is set to train or to stand ready (which on a roster is to rehearse), where its activity differs. Below the standard of experience, 2, it trains at preserve the force until it is at the standard; at balanced until it is at the standard, or, with a date, until the days left before the date (the date less today) are no more than the days its rehearsal still needs; at press it does not train. A unit at or above the standard rehearses, and so does one that cannot train: it has men aboard, its hex holds no base of its side, or its experience is at the training ceiling. The days a rehearsal needs are what is left to 100 of its rehearsal for the task's hex over its day's gain (`preparation_per_day` times its assault factor), rounded up. [0141 item 3]
3. **Ready.** The task is ready on a morning the force's preparation is already for the hex with that roster, the roster is not empty, and the force's loading and each fighting unit's rehearsal for the hex are at least 100 (50 at press), and at preserve the force each fighting unit is at the standard. On the first such morning the staff gives no order and hands in the notice `ready`, once. [0141 item 5]
4. **Late.** With a date, on a morning the task is not ready, the staff projects the morning it would be: today plus the longest of the days the loading needs (from the loading the force will have after today's order, at `preparation_per_day`) and, for each fighting unit, its days of training by the rule of item 2 (the days left to the standard, `training_days_per_level` a level less those counted) and then of rehearsal to the risk's mark. If that morning is after the date, or a unit can never be ready, it hands in the notice `late`, once, with the days late and what binds: the loading, a unit's training (where its days include training) or a unit's rehearsal; at a tie the loading, then the lower unit. [0141 item 6]
5. **Reasons** give each unit's experience against the standard, its days of training counted against the days a level takes, its rehearsal for the hex and the days it still needs at its gain, the force's loading, the risk and the date. A morning with no order for the task has one reason that says so. [0141 item 7]

**Build up a base.** Each morning every unit the task may use, at the base, is set to work at the job of the first want not yet met, where its activity differs: an airfield and its damage mended at `airfield`, defences at `defences`, the port at `port`, aircraft repair at `aircraft`. An airfield is met when the base has one; its damage mended when its airfield has no damage (a base with no airfield has none); defences when the base's defences are at the number or above. The port and aircraft repair are never met: they are worked while they are the first want left. With every want met the staff gives no order and says so, and on the first such morning hands in the notice `met`, once; a want that stops being met is worked again. The reasons give the work still to do on the want (for an airfield `airfield_work` less the work done; for mending the damage times `work_per_airfield_damage_point`; for defences the work at each grade's cost from the defences to the number), the work the units make in a day (chapter 7, The day's work, with the fed share of the last night), and the days that comes to, rounded up. A fighting unit is put to work only where a forces limit names it. [0141 items 8 to 12]

**Once.** A notice is handed in once for its plan: the staff reads its own side's events and hands in no notice of a kind it handed in for the plan before. [0141 items 5, 6 and 11]

A side sees: its own orders, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none. [0141]

## Taking a place

Decision 0142, step 3 of stage 3. An end a commander states, served by the area each morning in the air doctrine's run: its preparing before the tasks that ready units are served, and its launching after them. It carries a landing as far as the day the place is the side's; what the men do ashore when the enemy has men there is step 4. Its numbers are the ground figures (chapter 7); with none loaded it gives no order and says so. The staff reads only the side's own view: the enemy as its public bases and the side's estimate of its men.

**The end.** "Take a place" (`take_place`, code 13) names a hex of land, coast or inland, and may have a date: the day by the end of which the place is to be the side's. A tool may name the place by a base on the side's map that is not the side's, which means that base's hex. It is a root, addressed to the area. Its forces limit names the task forces, the land units and a source, a base of the side; a forces limit with no source is refused, as for keeping a base supplied. It is met when the side holds a base in the hex and the enemy holds none there. [0142 items 1 to 3]

**Its phase** is read, not stored: prepare while the plan is preparing; move from its release until a man of the side is ashore at the place; land from then until the intent is met. `command_state` shows it. [0142 item 5]

**Leaves.** A draft waits for leave to prepare as a hold's does. A plan that is preparing is not released by the leaves: the end's own rule asks leave to launch, below. Nothing is ordered without leave to prepare, and nothing embarks or sails before the plan is released. [0142 items 15 and 25]

**Met.** On each morning, first: where the place is the side's, the staff marks the plan complete (`mark_plan`) and hands in the notice `met`, once (its words the intent's, "intent:N is met"), and orders nothing else. Stated of a place that is the side's already, that is the first morning. [0142 items 3 and 23]

**Prepare.** On the first morning the plan is approved and no "ready a landing" stands under it, the staff:

1. **Lands at the place itself.** For a place that is not a coast hex it gives no order and says it has no way there. [0142 item 4]
2. **Finds the transport force:** the lowest-numbered task force of the forces limit on the transport mission, with a ship afloat of a class that carries men, that the side may use, that the commander does not hold, and that lies stopped at the source or has men of the limit's units aboard. With none it forms nothing and says why for each. [0142 items 6 and 26]
3. **Finds the units that may land:** the fighting units of the forces limit that the side may use, that the commander does not hold, and whose men are all ashore in the source's hex or all aboard the transport force. A working unit is left out. Their order: the higher assault skill first, then the higher experience, then the lower number. [0142 items 7, 8 and 26]
4. **Gives them ships.** A ship carries men of one unit. A unit aboard keeps the ships it is in. In that order, each unit ashore takes the force's ships that carry men and are not yet taken, in number order, until their berths hold its men; a unit for which the ships left are too few is left out, and the next is tried. Which ship each unit's men go into is the load order's (chapter 7). [0142 item 9]
5. **Sizes them.** Where the side holds an estimate of the enemy's men at the place, it stops taking units once the men chosen are at least the estimate times 3 at preserve the force, 2 at balanced, 1 at press (the staff's figures, placeholders). With no estimate it takes every unit the ships can carry, and says that it goes with no count of the enemy. Where the units the ships can carry are fewer than the men wanted, at balanced and at press it goes with what it has and says so; at preserve the force it forms nothing, asks no leave, and hands in the notice `cannot_meet`, once. [0142 item 10]
6. **Forms the task** "ready a landing" under the end, for the transport force at the place's hex (`form_intent`), and sets its forces limit to the units chosen (`set_limit`). The choice is made once and kept as that limit, which the commander may change as he may any. With a date, the task's date is the morning of the end's day, less the days to embark and the days of the passage, plus one, and not before day 1. The task is served that morning (Readying a landing, above). [0142 items 11 to 13]

**Ready.** A "ready a landing" whose force does not lie stopped at a base of its side can gain no loading and its units no rehearsal: the task is ready as it stands, whatever its loading and rehearsals, and its reason says what it goes without. [0142 item 14]

**The need to know.** A take-a-place whose plan is preparing needs a count of the enemy at the place, by its risk, read up the tree (decision 0147, items 13 to 20, `crates/sim-staff/src/look.rs`). The count asked for: at preserve the force an estimate of the place *seen from the air* or *by men in contact* (a *told* opening estimate never counts), dated no more than 3 days before the morning (the 3 days are a placeholder); at balanced such an estimate of any day; at press anything or nothing, and none is ever sent for.

1. **Who is sent.** On each morning the need is unmet and the side has no man of a fighting unit ashore in the place's hex, the staff sets one air group to the reconnaissance mission for the place (an order to the group, serving the end), where none the end may use is set so already: the lowest-numbered of the air groups the end's forces limit names, read up the tree, that the side may use, that the commander does not hold, that no cover, raid or strike task served earlier in the morning has taken, that can search (not a fighter, a search range above 0) and whose search range reaches the place from where it is based. Only the groups the limits name are read, never the groups aboard task forces they name. The order is given after the landing task is served, so on the forming morning the landing order comes first. The staff does this too where it could not form the landing for want of ships to carry the men wanted (below), so that the count can come. [0147 items 14 and 15]
2. **When the need is met** each group the end may use that is on reconnaissance of the place and that the commander does not hold is set to rest, by one order, and is the staff's to use again. [0147 item 15]
3. **The choice made again.** On the first morning the staff holds a count seen from the air or by men in contact (dated the day before), while the plan is preparing and no man of a unit of the task is aboard, the units are chosen again as in Prepare, items 3 to 5, on that count. Where the choice differs the staff sets the forces limit of the "ready a landing" to it (`set_limit`), and that task orders the landing again with the new roster the same morning, at the cost the rules give a change of roster (chapter 7; the loading keeps half where a unit is added). An estimate of 0 asks for one unit, the first in the order. At preserve the force a choice that is short of the men wanted is not made: the limit is left as it is and the launch is held back (below). [0147 items 16 and 17]
4. **Not launched without it.** The landing is ready for leave and the release only when the need is met as well. Where a group is sent or flying, the day's reason says the landing waits for the count. Where nothing can look (no group the end names that the side may use and the commander does not hold can search and reach the place, or the side's own men ashore there have given no count): at preserve the force the plan is not released, the staff hands in the notice `cannot_meet` on the first morning the landing is otherwise ready, once for the plan, and gives the reason each morning after, which says that nothing it may use can look and why; at balanced the plan goes, and the reason of its release says that it goes on no count, or on what it was told, or on the count it holds, with its grade and day. [0147 items 18 and 19]
5. **Reasons** give the estimate with its grade and its day, the count the risk asks for, the group sent with its range and its distance from the place, and what a new count changed in the choice. [0147 item 20]

**Leave to launch.** On each morning its landing is ready, the need to know is met (above) and the plan is preparing, the staff releases the plan (`approve`) where leave to launch is given; where it is withheld it gives no order and hands in the notice `waits_for_leave_to_launch`, with a reason that says the landing is ready and when, with leave on a morning, it would sail and land. Before the landing is ready the day's reason gives the loading, each unit's rehearsal and the morning it expects the landing to be ready, and no such notice is handed in. [0142 item 15]

**The check that it can be held.** Where a hold of a base in the place's hex is in force with a source in the forces limit on its chain (Holding a place, above), the staff works the check on each morning the landing is ready and the plan is preparing, before leave is asked or the plan released. The daily need is the daily charge of the men chosen. The days until supply follows are, in whole days: the days to land the men (the men's weight, `handling_t_per_man` a man, in the fullest ship, at the place's rate for a ship, the berth rate where its port has a berth and the lighter rate where it has none, rounded up to days and one at least), the passage back to the hold's source, one day to load, and the passage out, each passage at the cruise speed of the transport force's slowest ship, rounded up to days and one at least. The margin is the hold's, in days (10 at preserve the force, 5 at balanced, 0 at press). The supply that lands with the men is the daily need times those days and the margin, in place of the 15 days of the move below. The first run is taken to carry the daily need for 15 days. It can be held if the source's stock above its reserve is at least the supply that lands with the men and the first run together, and the room of the transport force (its ships' cargo capacity less any fuel carried as cargo) is at least each. The reason of the release, or of the morning it waits for leave, gives the check: the need, the days, the margin, the supply, the source's stock and the first run. Where it cannot be held the reason says what is short and by how much; at preserve the force (the end's risk) the staff does not ask leave and does not release: it hands in the notice `cannot_meet` once and gives the reason each morning the check fails; at balanced and at press it goes and says so. With no such hold nothing follows the landing, no check is made and the supply is the 15 days. [0144 items 10 to 17; the ruling on decision 0142's reading 8]

**Move.** Each morning the plan is released and no man of the side is ashore at the place, the staff takes one step, the first not yet done:

1. **The supply.** The force is to have aboard the daily charge of the men chosen (their men, times `daily_supply_t_per_man`, times each unit's weight factor) for 15 days, the interval of decision 0107, or for the days of the check that the place can be held where it is made (above); cut to what is aboard plus the source's stock above its reserve (none where the force does not lie at the source) and to what is aboard plus the ships' room. What is aboard counts toward it. Where more is to be taken the force is ordered to load supply to that weight (`load_to`); while that order stands and the load is not done it gives no order. With nothing to take it goes on to the next step the same morning. [0142 item 16]
2. **The men.** The lowest-numbered unit chosen with men ashore is set to attack, where it is not on attack, and the force is ordered to load it with no cargo; while that order stands and the unit still has men ashore it gives no order. A unit aboard already needs neither. [0142 item 17]
3. **The passage.** With every unit aboard and nothing more to take, the force is given a route from where it lies to the place and on to the source, marked to act at the place, and the unload order. The route and the area are as for a run: inside the intent's area read up the tree, where one is set. Each ship must have the fuel for the miles out and back at its class's rate (its fuel capacity over its endurance), as for a run. An escort is given before the route as for a run (Escorting and covering a run, above), with the place as the destination. A ship without the fuel, no route, or a run held for want of an escort stops it, and the reason says which. [0142 items 18 and 19]

So a force released on the morning of day R sails on day R plus the days to embark (one for the supply where there is any to take, one for each unit ashore) and is due on that day plus the days of the passage (its miles out at the cruise speed of its slowest ship, rounded up to whole days, at least one) less one. Once the force's route acts at the place, or it is landing there, it is given nothing more, and the "ready a landing" under the end is given nothing either, nor from the first morning a unit chosen has men ashore at the place: the landing spends what it made ready. [0142 items 18 and 20]

**Land.** The landing is the unload order's (chapter 7). The phase begins when a man of the side is ashore at the place. [0142 item 21]

**Ashore.** On each morning the plan is released, the place is not taken and a unit chosen (one in the forces limit of the "ready a landing" under the end, a later lift's included) has men ashore at the place, the staff judges:

1. **The units of the operation** are the units chosen with men ashore at the place that the side may use and the commander does not hold. A unit held, by a hold or by an order of the commander's, is named in the reason and not counted.
2. **A unit can attack** if it is on attack already, or its fatigue is at or below the mark for the risk: a third of `attack_fatigue_limit` at preserve the force, two thirds at balanced, the limit itself at press, each cut to a whole number (the staff's shares, placeholders).
3. **Its fit men** are its men ashore times 100 less its fatigue over 100, cut to a whole man.
4. **The men wanted** are the side's estimate of the enemy's men at the place times 3, 2 or 1 by the risk, as for the choice.
5. **Attack.** Where no estimate is held, or the fit men of the units that can attack are at least the men wanted, each unit that can attack and is not on attack is set to attack, with no instruction for when it is spent. With no estimate the reason says it attacks with no count of the enemy. With nothing to order, one reason says so.
6. **Wait.** Otherwise each unit of the operation on attack is set to defend, and nothing else is ordered of the men; with none on attack, one reason. A unit the rules sent back to the defence is under the same judgement, and a unit that lands is under it from its first morning ashore.

The reason gives the estimate and its day, the men wanted, each unit with its men, posture, fatigue against the mark and fit men, the fit men of those that can attack and how many are short, and what is coming. [0143 items 1 to 8 and 15]

**A second lift.** The units left behind are the fighting units of the end's forces limit not chosen, that the side may use, that the commander does not hold, and whose men are all ashore at the source. On a morning of the judgement where the place is not taken, the transport force lies stopped at the source with nothing aboard and no estimate is held or the fit men of the units that can attack are fewer than the men wanted, the staff takes the units left behind in the order of the choice (the higher assault skill, the higher experience, the lower number), each by the force's ships in number order until their berths hold its men, until the fit men that can attack and the men taken are at least the men wanted or no ship is left; with no estimate, every unit the ships can carry. It adds them to the forces limit of the "ready a landing" by `set_limit` that morning, so the choice is made once and the commander may change it. The lift then goes one step a morning as the first did (Move, above): supply for its men for 15 days, a unit a morning by a load order, then the route and the unload order with an escort as for a run. A unit of a second lift is not set to attack before it sails, and the force goes as it stands, with no landing made ready, and the reason says so. Nothing more is sent once the place is taken or no unit is left behind; where the men are short and none is left, the reason says so and the staff waits. [0143 items 9 to 14]

**Reasons** carry the numbers in the order a staff works in: the estimate and the men wanted; each unit in its order with its assault skill, its experience and its men; the berths and the ships; each unit left out and why; the supply in tons; the days to embark and of the passage. **A proposal** shows the task formed, its units, ships, supply and days, and goes no further. [0142 items 27 and 28]

A side sees: its own orders, reasons and notices, whole. The enemy sees none. [0142]

## Bombarding a base

The task "bombard a base" and the doctrine at the task force's level that serves it. It runs last in the staff's morning (The day's order, below), after "take a place", whose landing force's due day it reads. It gives orders to a task force and draws no random number. The engine's half is the shore bombardment (chapter 4, Shore bombardment) and the halt at a marked waypoint (chapter 2). [0146]

**The task** (`bombard`, code 14) names a task force of the side and a base on the side's map that is not the side's, the target; intake refuses a base that is not (`not_an_enemy_base`) and a force not the side's. It has no date. It is addressed to the force. A commander may state it as a root; it then needs a plan of its own, made ready and released by the leaves. [0146 item 1]

**Forming.** Under an end whose plan is released, for each task force on the bombardment mission with a ship afloat that the nearest forces limit on the end's chain names, in task force order, where no bombard task for that force is in force anywhere on the side (a force before its day is left out): under **neutralise a base**, at that base; under **take a place**, at the enemy's lowest-numbered base on the side's map in the place's hex, and none where there is none (intake refuses one whose target is not in the place's hex, `cannot_stand_under`). Where one stands, a decision to give no order names it. A force on the bombardment mission is given no stand task. [0146 items 2 and 3]

**The force's morning.** Each morning, for each bombard task in force whose plan is released, in priority order, the staff decides at the force's level from the side's view, as the stand doctrine does (Standing at a hex by night, above), with these differences:

1. **The force** gone, before its day, held by the commander, or with no ship afloat: one reason and no order, and for a force gone or with no ship afloat the notice that the intent cannot be met.
2. **The target.** Where the target is not a base of the enemy's on the side's map (it is the side's own now, or gone from the map), the force is given no route, is set to normal readiness where it is not, and the reason says so. No notice is handed in. [0146 item 4]
3. **Radio silence, the home port, away from home and the rules of engagement** are the stand's: silence at preserve the force where reports travel; the base it lies at stopped as its home port where it has none; no order away from its home port; the stance engage and the break-off level of the risk, set once. [0146 items 8 and 9]
4. **Whether it sorties tonight.** As a root or under neutralise: at press and at balanced every night; at preserve the force every night on which the force's own picture holds no contact of ships within 15 hexes of the target that reports more ships than the force has afloat. Under take a place: on the morning of the day the landing force is due at the place, and on no other. That day is, on the morning the landing force is routed (an order of the same morning), that day plus the days of the passage at the cruise speed of its slowest class less one (decision 0142, item 18); while it is under way to the place, the day the engine's forecast brings it there; before it is routed it is not known and the force waits; once it has come to the place there is none. [0146 item 5]
5. **The speed and the route.** From its home port, routed to the target's hex and home, the target's hex marked to act at; at cruise, then full speed, the first that brings it there within the night's 12 hours, ordered where it differs from the force's. Where neither does, there is no sortie and the notice that the intent cannot be met is handed in. [0146 item 6]
6. **Fuel and ammunition first,** by the stand doctrine's rule (the passage out and home, by the engine's measure, times the risk's margin) and decision 0127 (Ammunition as policy, below): a force short of either does not sortie, and its reason gives the day from which it may be used, or none and the notice that the intent cannot be met, on each morning it holds. [0146 item 7]
7. **Readiness:** battle on a morning it sorties, normal on one it does not. The orders are in the stand's order: silence, home port, rules of engagement, readiness, speed, route. [0146 items 8 and 9]
8. **It fires and comes home by the rules as they are:** the force halts in the target's hex, fires in the night's bombardment phase, and steams on at the start of the morning pulse. [0146 item 10]
9. **Reasons** give the target and its distance, the hours to it, the fuel aboard against the fuel needed, each ship's rounds against the level of the risk, and, at preserve the force, the contact that stopped it. [0146 item 11]

A side sees: its own `IntentFormed`, staff reasons, orders and notices, whole. The enemy sees none of it.

## The tools

A seat whose side has a staff commands through these tools beside the others, in `sim-agent` and through the MCP server and `play`. Every seat of a game those open has one; the staff does nothing for a side until an intent is set, and for a supply intent until it has a forces limit. A session made with no staff has none of them. The agent interface is version 19 since decision 0142, which adds the kind `take_place` (`hex`, or `base` for an enemy base on the side's map, and `by_day`) and an intent's `phase` in `command_state`. It was version 18 from decision 0141, which adds its two kinds (`ready_landing` with `task_force`, `hex` and `by_day`; `build_up` with `base`, `wants` and `defences`), a forces limit's `land_units`, a hold's `land_unit` and the notices `ready`, `late` and `met`. It was version 11 (version 7 had the four tools of step 4, and `interrupts` in `command_state`; version 8 added the intent tree, the kinds `cover` and `hold`, air groups in a forces limit, a hold on a whole air group, a reason's chain and the measure of a hold intent; version 9 added the kinds `neutralise` and `raid`, the `target` of an intent, an intent's `base` that is null for a neutralise, a reason's `level` and the measure of a neutralise intent; version 10 added the kinds `deny`, `strike_ships` and `search_waters`, a deny intent's `hex`, `distance` and `measure`, and the air group order `set_strike_sizing`; version 11 adds the kinds `stand_at` and `operate_carriers`, with a `task_force` and a `hex` and a `base` that is null, a route order's `stand_at` and a force line's, a reason's `level` that may be a task force's, and the `pulse` a halt or steam-on choice is asked for). [0107 items 46, rulings 25 and 36 to 37; 0117; 0120; 0123; 0124; 0125]

Since decisions 0143 and 0144 (agent interface version 20): `set_intent` of a `hold` may name an enemy base on the side's map, which the staff waits for; the staff's `form_intent` may be `keep_supplied` under a hold, written and read with `floor_days` (0 allowed) and `interval_days` where set; the command report and the export's `staff_notice` event name the intent of a take-a-place's `met` notice, whose words are "intent:N is met". [0143; 0144; the ruling on decision 0142's reading 4]

- `command_state`, `plans`: what stands, as the coming day will find it, with each intent's figure and first day below, and yesterday's staff decisions with their numbers for `why`. `command_state` lists every intent, each root by number with the intents beneath it after it, and gives each its kind and base, its `parent`, `maker`, `addressee`, `children` and `depth`, its `limits` as set on it and its `limits_read_up` (each of forces, risk, area and priority as the staff reads it, with the intent it is read from and, where none is set, what then holds), for a hold intent its `measure` (Holding a place, above), for a neutralise intent its `measure` (Neutralising a base, below), for a deny intent its `measure` (Denying an area, above), its `hex` and `distance` for a deny intent (`base` is null for a deny too), its `target` for a neutralise or a raid intent (`base` is null for a neutralise), its `task_force` (the side's number; null for the other kinds) and its `hex` (the station) for a stand-at or an operate-carriers intent (`base` is null for these too), and notes for a supply intent given to the area with no forces limit and for each intent the staff formed. It gives each intent in force its place in the priority order (`served`, by the rank read up the tree, as the doctrine serves it) and what displaces it today (`displaced_by`), and the side's `holds`: each with its target, the day, whether a `hold` command or an order made it, and that order's number. It gives too the side's `interrupts`: each interrupt set with its base, its number of days, the day it was set, whether it is armed, the day it last fired, whether that is acknowledged and whether it needs acknowledging; each standing record that has fired, by its kind and its plan or ship; and the kinds switched off. Those fired and not acknowledged come first.
- `set_intent` (the side's next free number when none is given; `intent_kind` is `keep_supplied`, the default, `hold`, `cover`, `neutralise`, `raid`, `deny`, `strike_ships`, `search_waters`, `stand_at` or `operate_carriers`; `floor_days` and `interval_days` belong to `keep_supplied` alone; `neutralise` takes a `target`, the number of an enemy base on the side's map, and no `base`; `raid` takes a `base` of the side's and a `target`; no other kind takes a `target`; `deny` takes `hex` as `[q, r]` and `distance` in whole hexes and no `base`; `strike_ships` takes a `base` of the side's; `search_waters` is refused as "Not a root" and is the staff's alone; `stand_at` and `operate_carriers` take a `task_force`, one of the side's own forces in its numbers (an enemy force and one that does not exist are refused alike), and a `hex` as `[q, r]` and no `base`; no other kind takes a `task_force`, and no other kind but these and `deny` takes a `hex`; only `deny` takes a `distance`), `withdraw_intent`, `set_limit`, `set_leave`, `hold`, `release`, `approve`, `cancel_plan`, `set_interrupt` and `acknowledge`: each queues one command for the coming day, taken in at its first step. It is checked at once, by taking it in on a copy of the world after the commands already queued, and answered queued or refused with the engine's reason. `set_limit` forces takes `ships`, `task_forces`, `air_groups` and `source`, each optional, and `set_limit` works on any intent. `hold` and `release` name exactly one of a ship, a task force, or an air group, which is held as a whole with no setting and by its search arc alone with the setting `search_arc`, in the side's own numbers, and a number that is not the side's is refused as the engine refuses it. `set_interrupt` takes `interrupt` (the side's next free number, one past the highest ever set or queued, when none is given), `base` and `below_days`; or `standing` (`cannot_meet`, `waits_for_leave` or `ship_lost`) and `on`; a table that mixes the two is refused. `acknowledge` takes `interrupt`, or `standing` with one of `plan` and `ship`, in the side's own numbers.
- `preview`: what a changed intent or limit would do, for `set_intent` and `set_limit` only, worked on two copies of the world as the coming day will find it (the side's queued commands taken in, and the same with the changed command last). It stores nothing: no state, no event, no hash, nothing queued. It sets what the staff would do on the coming day for the intent as things stand beside what it would do with the change (its orders, commands and decisions to give no order, the next run and the runs over 30 days, the first day below the floor and what binds), and says which ships are held and so not free, what each run still in hand would do (a run that has sailed goes on to the base it was sent to and lands there; a run still loading is sent to the new base; a change that is not of the base leaves every run as ordered), what is lost that was made ready (nothing: stock aboard a ship stays aboard and is carried by its next run, and stock landed stays where it lies), and what other plans give up. A command the tool would refuse is answered as the refusal and is not numbered. [0107, rulings 25, 27 and 28]
- `approve` with a `preview` number in place of a plan queues exactly the command that preview showed, answered as any queued command is. A preview is **void** once the side has queued an order or a command, or emptied its queue, or a day has been resolved, since it was made; approving a void preview is refused and says why (what was queued after it, or that a day was resolved). It is the session's, not the world's. [0107, ruling 26]
- `propose`: the staff's proposal for an intent, with what it is displaced by and what it displaces (`displaced_by`, `displaced_intents`, and in words `displaces`). For a hold, a cover, a neutralise, a raid or a deny intent it also gives the `tree` the staff would form (Holding a place, A proposal): the sub-intents, each with its number as the side's next free numbers give it, parent, maker and reason; the air groups each intent would order with the order and its working; the search arcs with their threats; and the decisions to give no order. It is null for a supply intent.
- `staff_orders`: what the staff would hand in at the start of the coming day, each with its reason, asked on a copy of the world with the day's first three steps taken (`World::at_staff_step`, which takes the override too). Only the session holds that copy; the staff and the tools are given its side's view.
- `run`: resolves up to `days` days (a whole number, at least 1), each exactly as `end_day` does, with the orders and commands queued going in on the first day. It stops after a day on which any interrupt of the side fired, or at the end of the game, or at its number of days, whichever comes first. The clock stops at the end of the day of a firing, not before it: nothing is ordered inside a day and the commander acts the next morning. It gives the staff's orders for the first day, worked before that day is resolved as `staff_orders` works them; the days resolved; why it stopped (`interrupt`, `game_over` or `days`); and everything that fired on the last day, each with its day, its event number, whether it is acknowledged and the `why` numbers of the reasons its notices rest on, with the result when the game is over. It takes an optional `reason`, which must be text and changes nothing. It is offered only where exactly one seat of the game is held by a player; with none or more it is not among the tools, and a call is refused, saying to use `end_day`. [0107 item 29, ruling 36]
- `report`: the command report, with no argument. It is worked from the side's own events and stored nowhere, so it is the same after a save and a resume. Its matters: a **matter** is a plan, a run where there is one and a kind of notice (the two leaves are two kinds), as the staff handed it in. It is open on the day last resolved if its notice was handed in that day, and closed otherwise, from the day after its last notice; handed in again later it is the same matter, with a gap in its history. Each matter gives its plan and intent, run, kind, whether it is open, the day it was first handed in and the day it closed, and its history: one entry for each day a notice of it was handed in, with the `why` number of the reason that day's notice rests on, so that `why` reads each day's reason. Open matters come first, then closed ones, each by the day first handed in, then plan, run and kind. The report also gives the interrupts as `command_state` does, the firings of the day last resolved, `holds` (the measure of each hold intent in force, with its intent, its base and its words), `neutralisations` (the measure of each neutralise intent in force, with its intent, its target and its words), `denials` (the measure of each deny intent in force, with its intent and its words) and a summary in words, which ends with a sentence for each hold intent and for each neutralise intent. [0107 item 30, ruling 37; 0117 item 14]
- `why`: one `StaffReason` by its event number, whole, with the order or command it is for, or as a decision to give no order; its `serves` is the statement in words, then "decided at the level of" the area or the base (its `level`, null for a reason of a game saved before it was handed in), and its `chain` lists the intents above the one served, nearest first, each as "under intent N (the commander's)" with its statement, the last the commander's own. `staff_orders` gives the same chain in each reason's `serves`.

A tool is not a rule: what it shows is worked from the side's view, and the agent's schemas say each answer's shape (`crates/sim-agent/schemas/`).

## What is saved

A game is saved as it has always been, as what was handed in, and from version 3 of the saved game it holds what step 1 of stage 1 adds (chapter 1, What a saved game holds); version 4, since step 2 of stage 2, adds the intent tree to the commands and the hand-ins (below): the commander's commands by day, and each side's staff hand-in by day, whole. A hand-in is the orders with their intent and reason, the staff's commands and the decisions to give no order with their reasons, and, since step 4, the notices (each its kind's name, its plan, its run or none, and the reason it rests on, as the number of a command or of a decision to give no order of the same hand-in). A hand-in with no notice is written with no `notices` key, as it always was, and a hand-in saved without the key reads as having none; the saved game is still version 3. Only the days and sides that handed in something are kept. The saved game also records the doctrine version beside the rules label. Version 4 writes in a command `set_intent` with `intent_kind` (`cover` or `hold`; left out for keeping a base supplied), the staff's `form_intent` (`parent`, `intent_kind`, `base`), a forces limit with `air_groups` and a `source` that may be left out, and a hold or release of a whole air group (an `air_group` and no `setting`), and in a reason's `serves` the `chain`. A saved game of version 3 or before is refused by the reader, not converted. It holds no command state: the state is rebuilt by taking the commands in again, the commander's `hold` and `release` among them, and the staff's `end_run` among its hand-in, and a commander's `set_interrupt` and `acknowledge` among the commands. It holds no interrupt's status: it is rebuilt by taking the commands in and testing each morning again, and a replay hands the saved notices in as the staff gave them. It holds no override either: the commander's orders are saved as handed in, and the override is taken again when a replay takes them in at step 2. [0107 items 9, 11 and 12]

**Replay.** A replay hands the saved orders, the saved commands and, before each day, the saved hand-in of each side into the engine (`World::submit_staff`), and asks no staff. So a replay of a staffed game works when the doctrine has since changed, and gives every daily hash that was saved. [0107 item 9]

**`replay --check-staff`.** It asks the doctrine's staff as well. On each saved day, each side that the doctrine gives a staff is asked on the view the engine then holds, and what it gives is compared part by part, orders, then commands, then decisions to give no order, then notices, with what was saved. The saved hand-in is what is handed in, whatever the staff gave, so the replay is the saved game and its hashes are still compared. The run stops at the first day and side where they differ and says what differs. A saved hand-in for a side the doctrine gives no staff is a difference too. A difference in the staff is reported before a hash that differs on the same day. If there is none, it says so, naming the doctrine now and the one the game was saved under. It is the test that a staff is a function of the view. [0107 item 10]

## A scenario's commands

A scenario may state a side's commands by day, as it states orders, so that a test can begin with an intent in force. A `[[command]]` table has `day`, `side`, `kind` and the keys of its kind, and no other key. Only the commander's kinds may be written; the staff's are the staff's to give, `form_intent` among them: a scenario states a commander's end, as a root, and the staff forms what serves it. A scenario with no `[[command]]` plays as it did. [0107 item 16]

| `kind` | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| `set_intent` | `intent`, `base`, and `intent_kind`: `"keep_supplied"` (the default where the key is absent, so a scenario written before the kinds reads as it did), which also takes `floor_days` and `interval_days` (optional); `"cover"` (cover `base` from the air) or `"hold"` (hold `base`), which take neither; `"neutralise"` (neutralise the enemy base `target`), which takes `intent` and `target` and no `base`; `"raid"` (raid `target` from `base`), which takes both; `"deny"` (deny the waters within `distance` whole hexes of `hex`, given as `[q, r]`), which takes `intent`, `hex` and `distance` and no `base`; `"strike_ships"` (strike ships from `base`), which takes `base`; `"search_waters"` takes `base` too, and is read, but the engine refuses it as "Not a root" when the command is taken in; `"stand_at"` (stand the task force `task_force` at `hex`, given as `[q, r]`) or `"operate_carriers"` (operate the carrier force `task_force` from `hex`), each of which takes `intent`, `task_force` and `hex` and no `base` (decisions 0124 and 0125); `"take_place"` (take the hex of land `hex`, given as `[q, r]`, with an optional `by_day`, a whole number from 1), which takes `intent`, `hex` and `by_day` and no `base` (decision 0142) |
| `withdraw_intent` | `intent` |
| `set_limit` | `limit`, and the keys of that limit, below |
| `set_leave` | `leave` (`"prepare"` or `"launch"`) and `given` |
| `approve`, `cancel_plan` | `plan` |
| `hold`, `release` | Exactly one of `ship`, `task_force` and `air_group`; with `air_group`, `setting` (`"search_arc"`) holds that setting alone and no `setting` holds the whole group; no other target takes a `setting` |
| `set_interrupt` | Either `interrupt`, `base` and `below_days` (a numbered interrupt set), or `standing` (`"cannot_meet"`, `"waits_for_leave"` or `"ship_lost"`) and `on` (`true` or `false`) |
| `acknowledge` | Either `interrupt`, or `standing` with exactly one of `plan` and `ship` |

The limits: `"forces"` takes `intent` and, each optional (a list not given is empty), `ships`, `task_forces` and `air_groups`, and `source` (a supply intent's forces limit must name one, another kind's need not); `"area"` takes `intent` and `hexes`, as `[[q, r], ...]`; `"priority"` takes `intent` and `rank`; `"protected"` takes `intent` and `ships`; `"reserve"` takes `base`, `supply_t` and `fuel_t`; `"risk"` takes `intent` and `risk`, `"preserve"`, `"balanced"` or `"press"`. A quantity is a whole number or a decimal string, never a float.

The loader refuses, when the scenario is read: a day that is 0 or after the scenario's last day; a key the kind does not take, or that is no key of any command; a missing key; a TOML float; a kind that is the staff's (`end_run` among them); an unknown limit, leave, risk or setting; a hold or release that names none or more than one target, or a `setting` with a ship or a task force; an `intent_kind` that is none of the ten, a `deny` without `hex` or `distance`, a `deny` with a `base`, a `stand_at` or `operate_carriers` without `task_force` or `hex` or with a `base`, a `distance` on a kind but deny, a `hex` on a kind but deny, stand_at and operate_carriers, a `task_force` on another kind, or a `cover` or `hold` with a key only a supply intent takes; a `set_interrupt` or `acknowledge` that mixes the keys of its two forms; a standing kind that is none of the three; a `set_interrupt` with `standing` and no `on`; an `acknowledge` with `standing` and both or neither of `plan` and `ship`. Whether a base, ship, task force, intent, plan or interrupt exists, whether a number of days is 0 and whether an interrupt has fired are not the loader's to say: the engine checks it when the command is taken in, and refuses it as above. A scenario's commands are kept by day in the order written, kept when a seed is changed, and handed in before the day is resolved, as the commander's, so the engine takes them in at its first step. Two commands of one day for one side are taken in in the order written, and the Allied side's before the Japanese side's. [0107 item 16]

A scenario's `[[order]]` may give the issuer `staff` and an `intent`. Such an order is not checked against a forces limit or against the holds, and the override skips it (above, The forces limit on a staff's orders; Holds and the override). [0107, As built]

## The day's order

Chapter 1 owns the sequence. Where this chapter's rules run, in the numbering of decision 0107's table (which chapter 1 now numbers the same: the staff is asked at its step 4, and its commands, notices, interrupts and the orders intake are its step 5):

| Step | What happens | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The commander's commands are taken in, `hold` and `release` among them | This chapter, Commands |
| 2 | The override: the commander's orders to units in an intent's forces limit (an air group an intent may use, among them) are taken in and hold them. In the code it runs after step 3 | This chapter, Holds and the override |
| 3 | Forces back from the yard are home. Ships in port are not refuelled here: they take fuel in the loading phase of each pulse, each base down to its reserve | Chapter 4, Repair; chapter 2, Refuelling in port |
| 4 | Each side's staff, if it has one, is asked on its view as it now stands. Then the days of supply of the base of each interrupt the commander set are read on that same view | This chapter, The staff; Interrupts |
| 5 | The staff's commands are taken in (the runs set aside first; the air doctrine's after the supply doctrine's, with `form_intent` among them), then its decisions to give no order are recorded, then its notices; then each side's interrupts are tested, on those readings and those notices; then all orders not already taken in, the commander's and the staff's | This chapter, The staff; Notices; Interrupts; chapter 1, Orders |
| 6 | The rest of the day, as it was | Chapters 2 to 7 |

**The staff's morning, in its order** (what each of these does, in the sections named; one pass over the side's view as the staff step leaves it, the sub-intents formed in the pass read by the doctrines after them in the same pass):

1. The supply doctrine, for the keep-supplied intents.
2. The air doctrine's leaves, for each hold or cover root and, since decision 0120, each neutralise or raid root, and, since decision 0123, each deny or strike-ships root, and, since decisions 0124 and 0125, each stand-at or operate-carriers root.
3. The hold doctrine: the stand and carrier tasks of the forces named in its forces limit, then cover under each held base (A task force as an addressee, above).
4. The neutralise doctrine: raid tasks under each neutralise intent (Neutralising a base, below). It forms sub-intents and gives no order.
5. The deny doctrine: strike-ships and search-waters tasks under each deny intent, then the stand and carrier tasks of the forces named in its forces limit (Denying an area and A task force as an addressee, above). It forms sub-intents and gives no order.
6. The cover doctrine, over its groups, leaving a raid's or a strike-ships task's bomber out; it reads which fighters it keeps and at what share, and takes the waters of a released deny intent its searchers reach as threats beside its own (Covering a base from the air, A raid's bomber; Searching waters, above).
7. The raid doctrine, over its groups, which reads what the cover wants of the fighters it has, and leaves out a bomber a strike-ships task of higher rank may use (Raiding a base, below).
8. The strike doctrine, over its groups (Striking ships from a base, above).
9. The search doctrine, over its groups (Searching waters, above).
10. The stand doctrine, over the stand tasks: each force's orders and a stand's reasons (Standing at a hex by night, above).
11. The carrier doctrine, over the carrier tasks: each force's orders and its groups' (Operating a carrier force, above).
12. "Take a place" forms its landing, the tasks that ready units are served, and "take a place" launches it (Readying a landing, and building up a base; Taking a place, above).
13. The bombard doctrine: bombard tasks formed under each released neutralise and take-a-place end, then each bombard task's orders (Bombarding a base, above).

Orders are handed in in that order and are taken in after the commander's, as above. [0107, The order of a day; rulings 23, 30 and 31; 0120; 0123; 0124; 0125]

## Not built

- **A hold of the commander on a base,** and holds on a setting other than a search arc: they wait for a stage whose doctrine acts on a base or changes a setting. (A hold intent is an intent, and is built.) [0107, rulings 17 and 30]
- **Interrupts of other kinds, and the withdrawal of a numbered one.** The one set kind is a base's days of supply below a number. A numbered interrupt cannot be withdrawn, only set again with another base and number. [0107, ruling 33]
- **A notice on every day the staff weighs no new run.** A cannot-meet notice is handed in only when the staff weighs a new run for the plan: none is handed in for an intent with no forces limit, a base that is not the side's, a pool with no ship afloat or one whose every ship afloat is protected for another intent, a plan that has ended, or a day a run of the plan is still loading or waits to sail. Those days give a reason, or a plan marked failed, and no notice. A preview carries no notice. A run-ended notice is handed in only for a run set aside for a hold: a run of a cancelled plan, which the staff reads as finished, has none. [0107, ruling 35]
- **A second source base** for an intent. [0107 item 19]
- **The recall of a run, an intent for fuel and a phase of a plan**: stage 2. Escort of a run is built (Escorting and covering a run); how many escorts a run is given, an escort sized to the threat, timing a run to the dark and air cover of a run are not. [0107; 0126, What it leaves out]
- **Bombers and fighters sent as escort on purpose** (the strike intent), and a hex of sea as the subject of an intent other than a station. The cover doctrine orders air groups at a base and no ship; the ships that stand at a base's hex or in the waters to deny are the stand task's (Standing at a hex by night, below).
