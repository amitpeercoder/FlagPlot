# The trial

A trial tries a side's course in many worlds that could be the one it is in, each built from a picture of a world (chapter 1, A world built from a picture). The side's own half of each picture is what its view gives it (chapter 1, A side's own half of a picture). The other half is what it does not know, and a trial must still have an enemy to play against. This chapter says how that enemy is made, by written rules each named in the list of what was assumed, and how a trial is played: one course, in a number of worlds, for a number of days, both sides on doctrine. The estimate a trial gives is not built yet. [0161, 0174, 0175]

Nothing here is a rule of play. No rule of a game reads what this chapter makes, and making it or playing a trial changes no game: no state, no draw of a live game, no hash. The live game's hash after a trial is its hash before. Every quantity is a fixed-point number (chapter 1, Numbers and chance).

## What is built and what is not

Built: the crate `sim-trial`; the pictures of a trial, made from a side's view, what the scenario says that side believed (chapter 1, A game from a scenario) and a trial's figures (`trial.toml`); the enemy's half of each, by the rules below, with the list of what was assumed; and the world of a picture, built with what the enemy is taken to want handed in for him. The playing of a trial: a course, in each world from 1, for the days of its horizon, with both sides' staffs; its three sizes; and its time, measured by `sim-cli time`. [0174, 0175]

Not built, and so not rules: the estimate (step 6 of decision 0161); dates and what-ifs, which give a trial other courses (steps 7 and 8); worlds played side by side; a trial inside a day; an enemy's land air groups, which a side knows only by the raids it suffers; a kind that is wrong, but where a claim raises it; an enemy's make-up by his own practice, in place of one ship and destroyers; what the enemy knows of us; the works and men a side believes at an enemy base (decision 0160's belief of a base's works is not built). [0174 and 0175, "What it leaves for later"]

## The pictures of a trial

1. **What a trial reads.** A side's view; that side's believed forces and intents; the trial's figures, from the file a scenario names with its `trial` key; and the trial's settings: a seed (in a trial played, the trial's figures' own, rule 2 of Playing a trial), a trial's number and a count of worlds. Never the world, the umpire's view or the full log. The crate depends on `sim-core` and `sim-staff` only, and a test reads its source: it may name a world only to build one from a picture and to keep the world it built, and no function of it takes a world; what it does to a world is hand in commands and resolve a day with the staffs, and read the world's day, a day's hash and events (for the commands of the course refused) and what the staffs handed in; it may not name the umpire, a whole picture, a rebuild on a live seed or a view taken from a world (`side_view`), nor hand in orders or staff decisions, work a day in its parts, or read a world's order book. [0161, 6; 0174, 1; 0175, amended 12]
2. **One call makes the pictures** (`sim_trial::pictures`): one for each world, numbered from 1, each with the seed and the domain its world will draw in (the trial and the world) and the list of what was assumed in making it. A second entry (`sim_trial::pictures_from_half`) takes the side's own half beside its view, of the view's side and day, and reads the half's units and belief in place of the view's. [0174, 2]
3. **Refused:** a view that gives no half (a day open; a view cut to a command); a believed force or intent that is not the tried side's; a view with no search figures; and a side whose own units reach the numbers the made units take: a ship, task force, air group or land unit numbered from 900,001 to 1,000,000. A unit the side made in play is numbered from its side's series, above 1,000,000, and cannot meet them. [0174, 20]
4. **Every draw is the trial's own.** A draw of this chapter is keyed on the trial's seed, the picture's day, no pulse, the trial subsystem (code 9), the thing drawn for by its place in the side's own list (the entity `Place`, code 9: a contact by its place among the side's contacts, a believed force by its place among the side's believed forces), for a pair the second contact's place as the second entity, the rule's own `Draw` value, and the domain of the trial and the world. The values are `TrialContactHex` (14), `TrialPair` (15), `TrialBelievedHex` (16) and `TrialBelievedBase` (17) (chapter 1, Every draw is keyed). The same view, beliefs, seed, trial and world give the same picture, byte for byte. [0174, 3]
5. **The side's own half** is its view's, unchanged. [0174, 4]
6. **No peek.** Two live games that differ only in what the side cannot see and has not been told give the same pictures, byte for byte, for the same seed, trial and worlds. [0174, 22]

## How a picture's enemy is made

The rules are worked in this order, and each thing made or left out is put in the list of what was assumed, in that order, with its rule and figures. The ships, task forces, air groups and land units made are numbered from 900,001 in the order made, each kind by itself; the enemy's counters of units made in play stand at their first numbers. [0174, 20 and 21]

### The enemy's bases and men

1. **A base for each base of the enemy's on the side's map,** with the number, name, hex and side the map gives it, in the order of their numbers. It is given `trial.toml: enemy_base_supply_t` of supply and `trial.toml: enemy_base_fuel_t` of fuel, no anti-aircraft guns, no ammunition, no repairs of aircraft or ships, no yard, no defences, no reserves, and an airfield, undamaged, with nothing at it. [0174, 5]
2. **A land unit for each estimate of the enemy's men ashore,** in the order of their hexes: one fighting unit of the estimate's men, in its hex, on defend, of the standard experience and every skill at the standard, fed, rested, with no fieldworks, no rehearsal, no march, no formation and no standing order. An estimate's age and grade change nothing. [0174, 6]

### A contact becomes a force

3. **A claim raises a kind and never lowers a count.** A claim of the side's own, made from the air, naming a kind, whose hex is within the merging distance (`search.toml: merge_distance_hexes`, by hex distance) of a contact's place, raises the contact to that kind where it is more important than the contact's; of several such claims, the most important kind, and of equal kinds the first claim held. A claim of ships sunk takes no ship from any contact. [0174, 11]
4. **Two reports that may be one force.** Two contacts may be one where the older (the one of more pulses; of equal ages, the first) could have steamed to the newer: the steps by sea between their reported hexes are no more than the merging distance and the steps of item 7 below for the pulses between their ages, at the cruising speed of the slowest class the older's force would have. The pairs are taken in the order of the side's own numbers, the first contact with the first after it that may be one with it, each contact in one pair at most. For each pair one draw in each world (`TrialPair`), at an even chance: one force, made at the first contact's turn, with the newer's place and age, the more important kind and the larger count; or two. The list says which. No number supports the chance. [0174, 12]
5. **The ships.** A force of `n` ships taken for a kind is one ship of that kind and `n - 1` destroyers; for a transport or an auxiliary, `n` ships of that kind; for a destroyer, `n` destroyers. Each ship is of the lowest-numbered class of its kind that the file of sides gives to the enemy, alone or with the side. Where the enemy has no class of a kind (or the scenario names no file of sides), no ship of that kind is built, and the list says how many were left out. A force none of whose ships can be built is not made. [0174, 7 and 8]
6. **Their state.** Each ship is undamaged, every system working, with the fuel her class holds, her guns' full rounds (guns times rounds a gun), her torpedoes ready where her class has tubes, no supply, no cargo, no men aboard and the standard experience. A carrier has two air groups aboard: fighters, a third of her aircraft capacity, rounded down, and bombers, the rest; each of the lowest-numbered type of its role the file of sides gives to the enemy; all ready, none damaged, at rest, with no fatigue, no pilots counted and no standing order. A group of no aircraft, or of a role the enemy has no type of, is not made, and the list says so. [0174, 9]
7. **Where a force is.** A contact of age 0 is at its reported hex. Older, it is at a hex ships can be in, drawn evenly (`TrialContactHex`, keyed on the contact's place; for a pair that is one, on the newer's) from those within its reach by sea of the reported hex, taken in their own order: the hours of its age, counted back from the picture's evening by the pulses' own lengths (the afternoon 6, the morning 6, the night 12, and so round), times the cruising speed of its slowest class, in miles; and the miles to steps at 40 a step, rounded down. Where no hex is within reach (the reported hex is not one ships can be in), the force is not made. The list gives the hours, the speed, the miles and the steps. [0174, 13]
8. **The force.** One task force for a contact or a pair: of the enemy's side, at its place, stopped, at cruise, at normal readiness, with the standard rules of engagement, no route, no home port, no order, no fatigue. Its mission is by the contact's kind: carrier for a carrier; surface combat for a battleship, cruiser or destroyer; transport for a transport or an auxiliary, whether or not the ship of that kind was built. [0174, 10]

### The forces a side believes in and has not seen

9. **By its standing,** each believed force of the side's list in its order. Believed present: in every world. Believed absent: in none; the list says the side ruled it out. Unknown: the `k`-th unknown force of the list, counted from 0, is in the worlds whose number less one has binary digit `k` set, and in no other, with no draw: the first in the even-numbered worlds, the second in worlds 3, 4, 7 and 8 of every eight, and so on. Over a count of worlds that is a power of two, every combination of the unknown forces is tried equally often. [0174, 14]
10. **A contact may stand for it.** A believed force in a world is not made where the side holds a contact of its kind (after item 3) not already taken for another believed force, which it could be: the first such contact in the side's list where the force's place is not known; else the first whose reported hex is within the reach of item 7 by sea of where it was last known, for the hours since. The list says the contact was taken for it. [0174, 15]
11. **Built as a contact is,** from its kind and its count (items 5, 6 and 8). [0174, 16]
12. **Placed** by item 7 from where it was last known (`TrialBelievedHex`, keyed on its place in the list), with the hours since the evening of the day it was known there, 24 for each day to the picture's (a day of 0, before the first day, counts from the evening before day 1; a day after the picture's counts 0 hours). Where its place is not known: at a base of the enemy's on the side's map, one drawn evenly for each world (`TrialBelievedBase`) from those bases in the order of their numbers; where the map has none, it is not made, and the list says so. [0174, 17]

### What the enemy holds and wants

13. **The enemy is given no belief:** no contact, no claim and no estimate. He finds what he finds in the days played. The list says so. [0174, 18]
14. **What the enemy wants is what the side believes he wants.** The world of a picture is built (`sim_trial::build_world`) from the reference data and the map it is handed, on the picture's seed and domain; then, where the side believes the enemy holds intents, his commands for the first morning are handed in: his leave to prepare and his leave to launch, then each believed intent as a commander's `set_intent`, numbered from 1 in the order given, at the risk a commander has by default. They are taken in at the first step of the world's first day (chapter 8, Commands). With none stated he is given no intent and no leave, holds what he holds, and his staff gives no order. The list names each intent, or says he is given none. [0174, 19]

A side sees: nothing of this chapter in its game. A world built from a picture is not the side's game; what the side's view gives in that world is the picture's.

## Playing a trial

1. **A course is the commands that make it.** A trial is given, for the side tried, a list of commander's commands (chapter 8, Commands), each with a day of the world, from 1, its first day (1 where none is given): an intent stated, a limit set, a leave given, a plan approved. Not orders to units. Each is handed in for that side on the morning of its day, in the order given, and taken in at the first step of that day, as a game takes a commander's commands; a command for a day outside the horizon refuses the trial. The side's command state is in its own half as it is, so its other plans go on beside the course and draw on the same men, ships and stocks. What the side has handed in for the morning and the engine has not yet taken in is not in its half: whoever asks puts it at the head of the course. A leave is the side's, not a plan's: a course that gives leave to launch gives it to every plan of the side. An empty course tries things as they stand. The course goes to the side tried and to no other, and is no part of a picture or of a draw: the same view, beliefs, figures, seed and trial's number give the same worlds for any course. [0175, 1; amended 1 to 4 and 6]
2. **Its seed and number.** Every trial draws on the seed of the trial's figures (`trial.toml: seed`), never the live game's; its number is given by whoever asks, and the same number is the same worlds. Two live games that differ only in their own seed and in nothing the side can see give the side the same trial. [0175, amended 7]
3. **Its size** is a number of worlds and a horizon in days. Three are named in the trial's figures file, each a name for two numbers, a table of `worlds` and `days`: `trial.toml: small` (8 worlds for 7 days, 56 days of play), `trial.toml: standard` (16 for 14, 224) and `trial.toml: full` (64 for 21, 1,344). Each is a power of two, so that the forces of unknown standing are tried evenly (rule 9 above). The loader refuses a size of no world or no day, and a file without the three sizes or the seed. A trial may also be asked for by its numbers, any count of worlds and any days, at least one; a count of worlds that is not a power of two is played, and the record of the trial says the forces of unknown standing were not tried evenly. The sizes are placeholders; they are work, not time, and the same question at the same size is the same work on any machine. [0175, 2; amended 9]
4. **It plays each world in the order of its number,** from 1 (`sim_trial::begin`, `sim_trial::play`): makes the pictures of its worlds (rule 2 of the pictures), builds each world from its picture, which hands in what the enemy is taken to want, once (rule 14 above), and resolves the days of the horizon, each with both sides' staffs on doctrine (`sim_staff::staffs`, chapter 8), handing in the course's commands of each day on its morning. The horizon is played whole: a world built from a picture has no last day, and a trial of 21 days from day 20 of the 39 plays to day 41. No seat plays in a world of a trial, and no trial is asked for inside one. [0175, 3; amended 8]
5. **A world's play depends on its own number alone.** No world reads another or how many there are: world 3 of a trial of 8 is world 3 of the same trial of 64, day for day. A world's hash holds its trial's number and its own (chapter 1, Every draw is keyed: the domain), so the hashes of two worlds are never equal, though they hold the same state. [0175, 4]
6. **A trial can be stopped.** It is worked a world's day at a time (`Playing::step`); whoever asked may stop asking at any step and drop it. Nothing of an unfinished trial is kept, and a trial begun again is the trial it would have been: a trial stepped to its end and one played out give the same worlds. [0175, 5]
7. **A trial changes nothing.** It is given a side's view, that side's beliefs, the figures, the reference data and the map, and builds worlds of its own. Nothing of the live game is written, on any evening it is asked. [0175, 6; amended 14]
8. **What it gives back** (`sim_trial::Played`) is, for each world in the order of their numbers: its number; the list of what its picture assumed; for each day played, the world's hash at its end and what each side's staff handed in that day; each command of the course the world refused, with its day and the engine's words (the world plays on without it); why it could not be played, where it could not; and the world as it stands at the end. And of the trial: whether its count of worlds was uneven, and the leaves the course gives that the side had not given. It is for the estimate and for the umpire, and no seat is given it. A world of a trial is the trial's own invention: reading it is not reading the enemy. [0175, 7; amended 4, 5, 9 and 10]
9. **A world that cannot be played**: one whose picture is refused when its world is built, or one of whose days the engine fails (its invariants broken at the day's end, chapter 1). Its record has its number, the day (0 where the picture was refused), the engine's words and the days it did play; the other worlds are played. A trial with one says so wherever it is read (`Played::failed`). [0175, 8; amended 11]
10. **Its cost is measured** by `sim-cli time` (workload h) on `stage-3-proof/07a_slice.toml`, the 39 days with each side's two orders to control an island: a trial of the Allied side from the evening before day 1, its course the side's own commands of day 1 in that file, and from the evening of day 20, the live game played to it with both staffs, with an empty course; at each size, with both staffs. The times are in decision 0175, "As built", and wait for the designer. [0175, 9; amended 13]

A side sees: nothing of a trial in its game. A world of a trial is not the side's game.
