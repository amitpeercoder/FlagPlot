# The trial

A trial tries a side's course in many worlds that could be the one it is in, each built from a picture of a world (chapter 1, A world built from a picture). The side's own half of each picture is what its view gives it (chapter 1, A side's own half of a picture). The other half is what it does not know, and a trial must still have an enemy to play against. This chapter says how that enemy is made, by written rules each named in the list of what was assumed, and how a trial is played: one course, in a number of worlds, for a number of days, both sides on doctrine; and the estimate read from a trial's worlds, in counts of worlds; and the window, one course tried at two or three dates in the same worlds; and a seat's trial, how a commander at a seat asks one and what he is shown; and what-ifs from a seat, a trial set beside the side without the thing tried, and the window asked from a seat. [0161, 0174, 0175, 0176, 0177, 0178, 0181]

Nothing here is a rule of play. No rule of a game reads what this chapter makes, and making it, playing a trial or reading an estimate changes no game: no state, no draw of a live game, no hash. The live game's hash after a trial is its hash before. Every quantity is a fixed-point number (chapter 1, Numbers and chance).

## What is built and what is not

Built: the crate `sim-trial`; the pictures of a trial, made from a side's view, what the scenario says that side believed (chapter 1, A game from a scenario) and a trial's figures (`trial.toml`); the enemy's half of each, by the rules below, with the list of what was assumed; and the world of a picture, built with what the enemy is taken to want handed in for him. The playing of a trial: a course, in each world from 1, for the days of its horizon, with both sides' staffs; its three sizes; and its time, measured by `sim-cli time`. The men a side believes may be ashore beyond its estimate, and the estimate of an intent read from a trial's worlds, with its words. The window: its dates, chosen from the side's view or given, its play with the days before a date played once, what it keeps of each date, world for world, and its words. A seat's trial: the tools `trial` and `trials` of a seat with a staff, in a game whose scenario names a trial's figures; the runner that plays it, handed to the session by `sim-cli`; the estimate in a commander's words; the list of estimates a seat was shown. What-ifs from a seat: a second trial of the side without the thing tried (`compare`), the window asked from a seat (`dates`), and what would be tried told with nothing played (`describe`). [0174, 0175, 0176, 0177, 0178, 0181]

Not built, and so not rules: the trial the staff's leave is asked with, and the comparison the staff offers where a limit binds (the next record of step 8 of decision 0161); the lift and the weight of supply a plan needs; stopping a trial from a seat; a commander's direct orders in a course; dates from aircraft due, supply runs due, training and searches, and the same date prepared early and late (decision 0177); worlds played side by side; a trial inside a day; an enemy's land air groups, which a side knows only by the raids it suffers; a kind that is wrong, but where a claim raises it; an enemy's make-up by his own practice, in place of one ship and destroyers; what the enemy knows of us; the works and men a side believes at an enemy base (decision 0160's belief of a base's works is not built). the end of an intent that is not a place (a base kept supplied, waters denied); the lift and the weight of supply in what an estimate says is needed. [0174, 0175, 0176, 0177, 0178 and 0181, "What it leaves for later"]

## The pictures of a trial

1. **What a trial reads.** A side's view; that side's believed forces, intents and men; the trial's figures, from the file a scenario names with its `trial` key; and the trial's settings: a seed (in a trial played, the trial's figures' own, rule 2 of Playing a trial), a trial's number and a count of worlds. Never the world, the umpire's view or the full log. The crate depends on `sim-core` and `sim-staff` only, and a test reads its source: it may name a world only to build one from a picture and to keep the world it built, and no function of it takes a world; what it does to a world is hand in commands and resolve a day with the staffs, and read the world's day, a day's hash and events (for the commands of the course refused) and what the staffs handed in; it may not name the umpire, a whole picture or a rebuild on a live seed, nor take a view of a world (`side_view`) but in two parts: the play, on one line, to record each day's end (Playing a trial, rule 8), and the making of an estimate, which reads each world it played through its two sides' views and the side's command state through its own; nor hand in orders or staff decisions, work a day in its parts, or read a world's order book. [0161, 6; 0174, 1; 0175, amended 12; 0176, 6 and amended 14]
2. **One call makes the pictures** (`sim_trial::pictures`): one for each world, numbered from 1, each with the seed and the domain its world will draw in (the trial and the world) and the list of what was assumed in making it. A second entry (`sim_trial::pictures_from_half`) takes the side's own half beside its view, of the view's side and day, and reads the half's units and belief in place of the view's. [0174, 2]
3. **Refused:** a view that gives no half (a day open; a view cut to a command); a believed force, intent or body of men that is not the tried side's; a view with no search figures; and a side whose own units reach the numbers the made units take: a ship, task force, air group or land unit numbered from 900,001 to 1,000,000. A unit the side made in play is numbered from its side's series, above 1,000,000, and cannot meet them. [0174, 20]
4. **Every draw is the trial's own.** A draw of this chapter is keyed on the trial's seed, the picture's day, no pulse, the trial subsystem (code 9), the thing drawn for by its place in the side's own list (the entity `Place`, code 9: a contact by its place among the side's contacts, a believed force by its place among the side's believed forces), for a pair the second contact's place as the second entity, the rule's own `Draw` value, and the domain of the trial and the world. The values are `TrialContactHex` (14), `TrialPair` (15), `TrialBelievedHex` (16) and `TrialBelievedBase` (17) (chapter 1, Every draw is keyed). The same view, beliefs, seed, trial and world give the same picture, byte for byte. [0174, 3]
5. **The side's own half** is its view's, unchanged. It carries the side's own units held apart until their day (chapter 1, A unit not yet entered), its entries, as its view gives them; so a trial's worlds bring the side's reinforcements onto the map on their days, by the same first step of the day that a game has (chapter 1, The order in which a day resolves, step 0), and a world refuses a picture that holds a unit number both in the state and held apart. The enemy's half is made by rule (below) and holds nothing apart: what the side does not know is coming to the enemy is not made up, so in a trial the enemy's units are those on the map. [0174, 4; 0195, rule 20]
6. **No peek.** Two live games that differ only in what the side cannot see and has not been told give the same pictures, byte for byte, for the same seed, trial and worlds. [0174, 22]

## How a picture's enemy is made

The rules are worked in this order, and each thing made or left out is put in the list of what was assumed, in that order, with its rule and figures. The ships, task forces, air groups and land units made are numbered from 900,001 in the order made, each kind by itself; the enemy's counters of units made in play stand at their first numbers. [0174, 20 and 21]

### The enemy's bases and men

1. **A base for each base of the enemy's on the side's map,** with the number, name, hex and side the map gives it, in the order of their numbers. It is given `trial.toml: enemy_base_supply_t` of supply and `trial.toml: enemy_base_fuel_t` of fuel, no anti-aircraft guns, no ammunition, no repairs of aircraft or ships, no yard, no defences, no reserves, and an airfield, undamaged, with nothing at it. [0174, 5]
2. **A land unit for each estimate of the enemy's men ashore,** in the order of their hexes: one fighting unit of the estimate's men, in its hex, on defend, of the standard experience and every skill at the standard, fed, rested, with no fieldworks, no rehearsal, no march, no formation and no standing order. An estimate's age and grade change nothing. [0174, 6]

### A contact becomes a force

3. **A claim raises a kind and never lowers a count.** A claim of the side's own, made from the air, naming a kind, whose hex is within the merging distance (`search.toml: merge_distance_hexes`, by hex distance) of a contact's place, raises the contact to that kind where it is more important than the contact's; of several such claims, the most important kind, and of equal kinds the first claim held. A claim of ships sunk takes no ship from any contact. [0174, 11]
4. **Two reports that may be one force.** Two contacts may be one where the older (the one of more pulses; of equal ages, the first) could have steamed to the newer: the steps by sea between their reported hexes are no more than the merging distance and the steps of item 7 below for the pulses between their ages, at the cruising speed of the slowest class the older's force would have. The pairs are taken in the order of the side's own numbers, the first contact with the first after it that may be one with it, each contact in one pair at most. For each pair one draw in each world (`TrialPair`), at an even chance: one force, made at the first contact's turn, with the newer's place and age, the more important kind and the larger count; or two. The list says which. No number supports the chance. [0174, 12]
5. **The ships.** A force of `n` ships taken for a kind is one ship of that kind and `n - 1` destroyers; for a transport or an auxiliary, `n` ships of that kind; for a destroyer, `n` destroyers. Each ship is of the lowest-numbered class of its kind that the file of sides gives to the enemy, alone or with the side. Where the enemy has no class of a kind (or the scenario names no file of sides), no ship of that kind is built, and the list says how many were left out. A force none of whose ships can be built is not made. [0174, 7 and 8]
6. **Their state.** Each ship is undamaged, every system working, with the fuel her class holds, her guns' full rounds (guns times rounds a gun), her torpedoes ready where her class has tubes, no supply, no cargo, no men aboard and the standard experience. A carrier has two air groups aboard: fighters, a third of her aircraft capacity, rounded down, and bombers, the rest; each of the lowest-numbered type of its role the file of sides gives to the enemy; all ready, none damaged, at rest, with no fatigue, no pilots counted, nothing claimed (its tally of claims starts at nothing, decision 0180) and no standing order. A group of no aircraft, or of a role the enemy has no type of, is not made, and the list says so. [0174, 9]
7. **Where a force is.** A contact of age 0 is at its reported hex. Older, it is at a hex ships can be in, drawn evenly (`TrialContactHex`, keyed on the contact's place; for a pair that is one, on the newer's) from those within its reach by sea of the reported hex, taken in their own order: the hours of its age, counted back from the picture's evening by the pulses' own lengths (the afternoon 6, the morning 6, the night 12, and so round), times the cruising speed of its slowest class, in miles; and the miles to steps at 40 a step, rounded down. Where no hex is within reach (the reported hex is not one ships can be in), the force is not made. The list gives the hours, the speed, the miles and the steps. [0174, 13]
8. **The force.** One task force for a contact or a pair: of the enemy's side, at its place, stopped, at cruise, at normal readiness, with the standard rules of engagement, no route, no home port, no order, no fatigue. Its mission is by the contact's kind: carrier for a carrier; surface combat for a battleship, cruiser or destroyer; transport for a transport or an auxiliary, whether or not the ship of that kind was built. [0174, 10]

### The forces a side believes in and has not seen

9. **By its standing,** each believed force of the side's list in its order. Believed present: in every world. Believed absent: in none; the list says the side ruled it out. Unknown: the `k`-th unknown force of the list, counted from 0, is in the worlds whose number less one has binary digit `k` set, and in no other, with no draw: the first in the even-numbered worlds, the second in worlds 3, 4, 7 and 8 of every eight, and so on. Over a count of worlds that is a power of two, every combination of the unknown forces is tried equally often. [0174, 14]
10. **A contact may stand for it.** A believed force in a world is not made where the side holds a contact of its kind (after item 3) not already taken for another believed force, which it could be: the first such contact in the side's list where the force's place is not known; else the first whose reported hex is within the reach of item 7 by sea of where it was last known, for the hours since. The list says the contact was taken for it. [0174, 15]
11. **Built as a contact is,** from its kind and its count (items 5, 6 and 8). [0174, 16]
12. **Placed** by item 7 from where it was last known (`TrialBelievedHex`, keyed on its place in the list), with the hours since the evening of the day it was known there, 24 for each day to the picture's (a day of 0, before the first day, counts from the evening before day 1; a day after the picture's counts 0 hours). Where its place is not known: at a base of the enemy's on the side's map, one drawn evenly for each world (`TrialBelievedBase`) from those bases in the order of their numbers; where the map has none, it is not made, and the list says so. [0174, 17]

### What the enemy holds and wants

13. **The enemy is given no belief:** no contact, no claim and no estimate. He finds what he finds in the days played. The list says so. [0174, 18]
14. **What the enemy wants is what the side believes he wants.** The world of a picture is built (`sim_trial::build_world`) from the reference data and the map it is handed, on the picture's seed and domain; then, where the side believes the enemy holds intents, his commands for the first morning are handed in: his leave to prepare and his leave to launch, then each believed intent as a commander's `set_intent`, numbered from 1 in the order given, at the risk a commander has by default. They are taken in at the first step of the world's first day (chapter 8, Commands). With none stated he is given no intent and no leave, holds what he holds, and his staff gives no order. The list names each intent, or says he is given none. [0174, 19]

**The enemy fights supplied.** Where the ground figures give the three figures of a fight that costs supply (chapter 7, What a fight costs), the enemy of a world is taken as supplied in full in his fights ashore and charged nothing for them: the side does not know his stocks, and the bases his landings and marches formed are pictured with none, so without it every enemy pictured would fire at the floor. It is a setting of the world `sim_trial::build_world` builds (`World::set_supplied_in_full`), not state and not hashed, and the list of what was assumed says so (`EnemySupplied`, rule 12 of decision 0209). The side tried is charged as in a game. [0209, 12]

### The men a side believes may be ashore

15. **Believed men** (`[[believed_men]]`, chapter 1, A game from a scenario) are worked after item 12 and before item 13, each body in the order of the side's list. Believed absent: in no world; the list says the side ruled them out. **Set aside,** whatever their standing: where the side's half holds an estimate of their hex of a day after the scenario's start (a count it has had since), or the place is the side's (a base of its own in the hex, none of the enemy's there on its map); the list says which, and they are in no world and take no turn. Believed present: in every world. Unknown: they take their turns after the believed forces of unknown standing, in the order of the list, by item 9's digits: with one unknown force before them, the first body is digit 1. In a world where they are, one fighting unit of their men is made in their hex as the unit of an estimate is (item 2), numbered after the units of the estimates; the list names the unit, the men and the standing. [0176, 1, 2 and amended 6]

A side sees: nothing of this chapter in its game. A world built from a picture is not the side's game; what the side's view gives in that world is the picture's.

## Playing a trial

1. **A course is the commands that make it.** A trial is given, for the side tried, a list of commander's commands (chapter 8, Commands), each with a day of the world, from 1, its first day (1 where none is given): an intent stated, a limit set, a leave given, a plan approved. Not orders to units. Each is handed in for that side on the morning of its day, in the order given, and taken in at the first step of that day, as a game takes a commander's commands; a command for a day outside the horizon refuses the trial. The side's command state is in its own half as it is, so its other plans go on beside the course and draw on the same men, ships and stocks. What the side has handed in for the morning and the engine has not yet taken in is not in its half: whoever asks puts it at the head of the course. A leave is the side's, not a plan's: a course that gives leave to launch gives it to every plan of the side. An empty course tries things as they stand. The course goes to the side tried and to no other, and is no part of a picture or of a draw: the same view, beliefs, figures, seed and trial's number give the same worlds for any course. [0175, 1; amended 1 to 4 and 6]
2. **Its seed and number.** Every trial draws on the seed of the trial's figures (`trial.toml: seed`), never the live game's; its number is given by whoever asks, and the same number is the same worlds. Two live games that differ only in their own seed and in nothing the side can see give the side the same trial. [0175, amended 7]
3. **Its size** is a number of worlds and a horizon in days. Three are named in the trial's figures file, each a name for two numbers, a table of `worlds` and `days`: `trial.toml: small` (8 worlds for 7 days, 56 days of play), `trial.toml: standard` (16 for 14, 224) and `trial.toml: full` (64 for 21, 1,344). Each is a power of two, so that the forces of unknown standing are tried evenly (rule 9 above). The loader refuses a size of no world or no day, and a file without the three sizes or the seed. A trial may also be asked for by its numbers, any count of worlds and any days, at least one; a count of worlds that is not a power of two is played, and the record of the trial says the forces of unknown standing were not tried evenly. The sizes are placeholders; they are work, not time, and the same question at the same size is the same work on any machine. [0175, 2; amended 9]
4. **It plays each world in the order of its number,** from 1 (`sim_trial::begin`, `sim_trial::play`): makes the pictures of its worlds (rule 2 of the pictures), builds each world from its picture, which hands in what the enemy is taken to want, once (rule 14 above), and resolves the days of the horizon, each with both sides' staffs on doctrine (`sim_staff::staffs`, chapter 8), handing in the course's commands of each day on its morning. The horizon is played whole: a world built from a picture does not end on the game's last day, and a trial of 21 days from day 20 of the 39 plays to day 41. It carries the game's days all the same (chapter 1, A world built from a picture), so that its staffs judge the game's last days as the live game's do (chapter 8, Taking a place, Ashore, item 11); on a day past the game's end the days left are 0, never fewer, and its staffs begin no attack ashore. No seat plays in a world of a trial, and no trial is asked for inside one. [0175, 3; amended 8; 0194, 11]
5. **A world's play depends on its own number alone.** No world reads another or how many there are: world 3 of a trial of 8 is world 3 of the same trial of 64, day for day. A world's hash holds its trial's number and its own (chapter 1, Every draw is keyed: the domain), so the hashes of two worlds are never equal, though they hold the same state. [0175, 4]
6. **A trial can be stopped.** It is worked a world's day at a time (`Playing::step`); whoever asked may stop asking at any step and drop it. Nothing of an unfinished trial is kept, and a trial begun again is the trial it would have been: a trial stepped to its end and one played out give the same worlds. [0175, 5]
7. **A trial changes nothing.** It is given a side's view, that side's beliefs, the figures, the reference data and the map, and builds worlds of its own. Nothing of the live game is written, on any evening it is asked. [0175, 6; amended 14]
8. **What it gives back** (`sim_trial::Played`) is, for each world in the order of their numbers: its number; the list of what its picture assumed; for each day played, the world's hash at its end, what each side's staff handed in that day, and where bases stood at its end as the side tried sees them (the hexes in which it holds a base, and the hexes of the enemy's bases on its map, `BasesAt`); the side's plan orders as its staff works them from its view at the end of the first day played; at the end of the day the course's first command is taken in, the side's plan orders again and each landing it has formed, a "ready a landing" in force with the root of its tree, its force, its place and the land units its forces limit names, each with its men ashore and aboard (`TakenIn`); each command of the course the world refused, with its day and the engine's words (the world plays on without it); why it could not be played, where it could not; and the world as it stands at the end. And of the trial: the side tried, whether its count of worlds was uneven, and the leaves the course gives that the side had not given: a leave given among the standing commands is the commander's own and is not counted (A seat's trial, rule 9). It is for the estimate and for the umpire, and no seat is given it. A world of a trial is the trial's own invention: reading it is not reading the enemy. [0175, 7; amended 4, 5, 9 and 10; 0176, amended 1; 0177, amended 7; 0178, 9]
9. **A world that cannot be played**: one whose picture is refused when its world is built, or one of whose days the engine fails (its invariants broken at the day's end, chapter 1). Its record has its number, the day (0 where the picture was refused), the engine's words and the days it did play; the other worlds are played. A trial with one says so wherever it is read (`Played::failed`). [0175, 8; amended 11]
10. **Its cost is measured** by `sim-cli time` (workload h) on `stage-3-proof/07a_slice.toml`, the 39 days with each side's two orders to control an island: a trial of the Allied side from the evening before day 1, its course the side's own commands of day 1 in that file, and from the evening of day 20, the live game played to it with both staffs, with an empty course; at each size, with both staffs. The times are in decision 0175, "As built", and wait for the designer. [0175, 9; amended 13]

A side sees: nothing of a trial in its game. A world of a trial is not the side's game.

## The estimate

An estimate (`sim_trial::estimate`) is what a trial says of one intent of the side tried, named by its number, read from the trial's worlds as played and, where given, a second trial of the same worlds with an empty course, things as they stand. It speaks in counts of worlds and never in chances, and in places, days, men, ships and aircraft and never in points. It is data, with a rendering in words for a reader; reading it changes nothing. [0176]

1. **The end of an intent** is its places being the side's: for "take a place", its hex; for "control an island", every named place of the island; for "hold", its base's hex. An intent of another kind, or one the side does not hold, has no end an estimate can read: its estimate gives the costs and says so. [0176, 4; amended 2]
2. **Met is the staff's own test** (`sim_trial::met`): a place is the side's at the end of a day where the side holds a base in its hex and no base of the enemy's is there on its map. It is read from each day's record (Playing a trial, rule 8), so that a place taken by a landing where the enemy had no base is met as one taken from him is. Days are the world's own; an end already met when the trial begins is met on the first day played. One function, which a live game's view can be put to as a world's record is (`BasesAt::of`). [0176, amended 2 and 3]
3. **Met and kept, and lost again.** An end is met and kept in a world where it is met at the end of the horizon; its day is the first day it was met. It is met and lost again where it was met at the end of some day and is not at the end of the horizon. One met on the last day counts as kept. [0176, amended 4]
4. **How it went:** the worlds played; of them, those in which the end was met and kept, with the earliest, the middle and the latest day met; those met and lost again; and those not met by the last day. A world that could not be played (Playing a trial, rule 9) is counted and named and is in none of the others. Where none could be played, the estimate says so with the count and gives no day and no cost. [0176, 7; amended 13]
5. **What it cost,** over the worlds played, each as the lowest, the middle and the highest: men lost to the enemy (in a fight, to fire, with a ship, by surrender); men lost unfed; ships sunk and lost with a base; ships out of action at the end (a ship afloat whose days of repair are 1 or more, a ship in a yard among them); and aircraft lost, by the list the objectives count. The side's are read from its own events and ships in each world, through its own view; **what the trial's enemy lost,** the same five, through the other side's view of the world, and marked as the trial's own reckoning: not a claim, not a report, and no belief changes. [0176, 8 and 9; amended 12]
6. **The middle** is the value at place ceil(n / 2) of the worlds' values in order, counting from 1, never an average of two. All arithmetic is in whole numbers. [0176, 10]
7. **What it needs and has:** the intent's plan order as recorded at the end of the day the course's first command was taken in, or of the first day where the course has none, in world 1 (or the first world with one): the men wanted and to hand, what it waits for or that it waits for nothing, the day expected ashore, what must be known, and its tasks. Where another world's plan order differs, the estimate names the worlds. [0176, amended 5; 0177, 6]
8. **What was assumed,** grouped by what it is of, not by the numbers given to the things made: an enemy base, the men of an estimate, a believed force or body of men, a contact, a pair, the enemy's belief and intents. Each is one line with the worlds it was made in and, for a thing of unknown standing, the worlds it was not (for a pair, the worlds it was two); a force placed by reach has the count of worlds it was placed in and no list of hexes. With them, the leaves the course gave that the side had not given, and whether the count of worlds was uneven. [0176, 13; amended 9]
9. **Where the worlds part:** by each believed force or body of believed men of unknown standing (the worlds with it and without), and by each pair of contacts that is one force in some worlds and two in others. Each side has its worlds, its counts of rule 4, and the five costs of each side. A parting is given only where its two sides differ in a count, the days met or a cost, and only where each side has a world. [0176, amended 8]
10. **Its other plans,** where a trial of things as they stand is given: for each other intent of the commander's with an end, as the side holds them in that trial's first world played, the worlds in which it was met and kept with the course and without, and the middle day met of each. It is named as displaced where it is met and kept in fewer worlds with the course, or in as many and its middle day is later. [0176, amended 11]
11. **Each world's own figures** are kept: its number, the day its end was first met or none, whether it was lost again, the five costs of each side, and what its picture assumed; the lowest, middle and highest are read from them, and so can a comparison of two courses, world for world. [0176, amended 10]
12. **No chance, no points, no advice.** An estimate gives no share of worlds as a chance, no score and no word on what to do. Its words name counts of worlds ("2 of 4 worlds") and hold no per cent sign, no "chance" and no "points". [0176, 15; amended 15]

A side sees: nothing of an estimate in its game, but what a seat is shown by the tool `trial` (A seat's trial).

## The window

A window (`sim_trial::play_window`, read by `sim_trial::window`) is the same course tried at two or three dates, in the same worlds, with the estimates set side by side: what the side has more of at a later date, what the enemy did with the same days, and how it then went. It is asked for like a trial, by a trial's number, a count of worlds and a size in days, with the intent whose estimates are read, the course, and the side's **standing commands**. It reads what a trial reads and changes nothing. [0177]

1. **A date** is a day of the world on whose morning the course is handed in. The first is the first day of the trial: the course as it would go now. [0177, 2 and 3]
2. **The later dates** are the days, after the first and no later than the last day of the first date's play, on which something the course may use first becomes usable, each on the morning the staff can first use it: a task force or a land unit of the side, the day it may first be used (chapter 1, `available_from`; for a unit held apart until its day, the day it enters, chapter 1, A unit not yet entered, which the take's count reads from the side's entries as it reads a unit on the map [0195, rule 13]); a task force away at a yard, the day it is back. What the course may use is what a take counts (`sim_staff::counted_by_a_take`): where the course gives its intent a forces limit, the task forces and land units it names, and of a kind it does not name, every transport force or every fighting unit of the side; with no limit, every transport force and fighting unit. A ship counts through its task force, whose day is the latest of its ships'. The limit is read from the course's own commands, the last given for the intent. Things due on one day make one date, which names them all. The earliest two such days are the second and third dates; with one, the window has two dates; with none, one, and it says that nothing of the side's that the course may use is due to change. A day due after the third date is named as not tried. The dates are had from the side's view and the course alone, before any world is built (`sim_trial::dates`), and whoever asks is told them before the worlds are played (`WindowPlaying::dates`). [0177, 4 and 11; amended 3]
3. **Dates may be given.** Whoever asks may name one to three dates, each after the one before and none before the first day of the trial, in place of those of rule 2 (`sim_trial::given_dates`). Each date names what came due after the date before it and by it. [0177, amended 4]
4. **The shift.** A date's shift is the date less the first day of the trial. Each command of the course is handed in that many days later than at the first date; a day written inside a command, a latest day or a "not before", is kept as written. The standing commands, what the side has handed in for the morning already, which whoever asks puts at the head of a course (Playing a trial, rule 1), are handed in on the first day at every date and are not shifted. A leave among them is the commander's own: it is not counted as given by the course (A seat's trial, rule 9). A date is played for the window's size and its shift more, so that every date has the same days from the morning its course is handed in. [0177, 5; amended 2; 0178, 9]
5. **The days before a date are played once.** In each world, the first date's course is played from the picture's evening; the days from the trial's first to the eve of the last date are played once, as things stand, with the standing commands alone; and each later date's course is played on from the world as it stood on its eve. A world's draws are keyed on its own day, and nothing of the course is in them, so each date's worlds are, field for field and hash for hash, the worlds of a plain trial whose course has each command shifted and whose horizon is the date's. A window of three dates is three trials' days and the days between. It is worked a world at a time, at every date (`WindowPlaying::step`). [0177, amended 5]
6. **A later date is "decide later", not "prepare now and go later".** Nothing of the course is done before its date: the units it would take lie idle in the days between, and a landing decided at a later date is ashore later than one prepared from the first and held. So a window overstates what waiting costs in days, and each later date says so in its words. [0177, amended 1]
7. **What a window keeps for each date:** its day; the day its course was taken in; what came due on it, by name and kind; the men its take chose, the units of the landings formed under the intent by the end of the day its course was taken in, with their men, in the world of its plan order, and the worlds whose landings name other units; its day expected ashore, where its plan order gives one; what the enemy did with the days (rule 8); and its estimate (The estimate), whose plan order is the one at the end of the day its course was taken in. A plan order gives men wanted and to hand only where the side holds a count of the enemy. [0177, 6 and 7; amended 8 and 11]
8. **What the enemy did with the days:** for each place of the intent's end, on the eve of each date (the evening before its day; for the first date the picture's own evening), read once in each world from the enemy's side of the world: his men ashore there, and the cover of the unit of his there with the least cover, the higher of its fieldworks and, where he has a base there, the base's defences (chapter 7, Cover and fieldworks); each as the lowest, the middle and the highest over the worlds, the cover over the worlds with a unit of his there. It is the trial's own reckoning of an enemy it made, and is marked so. The trial's enemy, given no intent, orders nothing: what he does with the days is what the engine does for him, his men on the defence dig in and eat. [0177, 8; amended 9]
9. **World for world:** for each later date against the first, the worlds in which the intent's end was met and kept at both, at the first alone, at the later alone, and at neither (`sim_trial::world_for_world`). A world that could not be played at either date is counted apart and is in none of the four. Met and lost again counts as not met and kept. Each date ends at the end of its own play. It is one function of two estimates' records of their worlds, for any two courses in the same worlds. [0177, 9; amended 10]
10. **No advice.** A window says nothing of which date to choose. It gives no chance, no points and no word on what to do. [0177, 10]
11. **Its cost** is its dates': three dates at a size are three trials of that size and the days between. It is measured by `sim-cli time` (workload i) on `stage-3-proof/07a_slice.toml`, a window of the Allied side's own commands of day 1 from the evening before day 1, at each size, at the dates it chooses and, where it chooses fewer than three, at the first and 3 and 5 days after it. The times are in decision 0177, "As built". [0177, 11; amended 6]

Not in a window: dates from aircraft due to fly in, from supply runs due, from training or a rehearsal complete and from a search due to report; the same date prepared early and prepared late; an enemy who uses the days. [0177, What it leaves for later; amended 3]

A side sees: nothing of a window in its game.

## A seat's trial

A commander at a seat tries an intent of his own, or a draft he has previewed, before it is taken in, and is shown the estimate (The estimate) in a commander's words. It is a trial of the coming morning, played as a window of one date (The window), on the side's whole view at dusk, or inside a day, at 0600 or 1200, on the view of the state as it stands with the day treated as closed (rule 14). It changes nothing in the game. [0178; 0204]

1. **Who runs a trial.** The session (`sim-agent`) cannot build a world and does not: it is handed a runner by its caller, as it is handed its staffs. `sim-agent` defines the trait (`TrialRunner`, to begin a trial; `TrialUnderWay`, to step it), what is asked (`TrialAsked`) and the narrow answer (`TrialAnswer`); `sim-cli` holds the runner (`seat_trial`), which plays a window of one date over `sim-trial`, reads its estimate and fills the answer. `serve::open` hands the runner to the session only where the scenario names a `trial` file (the trial's figures); a scenario that names none has no trial, and its sessions are as they were. The session steps a trial under way to its end in one call; a trial dropped before its end leaves nothing. [0178, 1 to 6]
2. **The tool `trial`** is listed, with `trials`, only for a seat with a staff that plays a whole side, in a game that has a trial; at a seat of a command, at a seat with no staff and in a game with no trial it is not listed, and a call of it is answered as a call of any tool the seat does not have. Its arguments are `intent`, the number of an intent of the commander's own in force or queued for the morning, or `preview`, the number of a preview the seat was shown, one of the two; `size`, `small`, `standard` or `full`, `small` where none is given; and `compare`, `dates` and `describe` (What-ifs from a seat). [0178, 7 and 13; 0181]
3. **The standing commands** of a seat's trial are what the side would take in on the coming morning without the trial: the side's commands of that day in the scenario, then the seat's queued commands, in that order. None of the other side's, scripted or queued, is among them. They are handed in apart from the course (The window, rule 4). [0178, 8]
4. **The course** for an intent is empty, but for leave to prepare and leave to launch, each where the side neither holds it nor has it given among the standing commands, so that the intent is tried as approved. For a preview it is the preview's command and the same leaves. A preview of a limit is tried for the intent the limit is on; a reserve is tried as the preview reads it. [0178, 9]
5. **A leave among the standing commands is the commander's own,** and is not counted as given by the course (Playing a trial, rule 8; the window's reading of decision 0177, ruling 7, is changed so). A leave the course gives is the side's and frees every plan of the side that waits for it, not the plan tried alone: the answer says so. [0178, 9, amended 1 and 15]
6. **Where it is played.** On the side's whole view at dusk, or inside a day on the view rule 14 names (`Session::play_trial`, taken outside the dispatch of tools), at any seat that plays a whole side, with command levels on or off. It is one date of a window, the coming morning, with the trial's number 1: the same question at the same hour (on the same evening, or at the same stop inside a day) is the same worlds and the same answer, and two questions at one hour are tried in the same worlds. Asked at 0600 and again at dusk, the answers differ, since the state does. [0178, 10 and 12; 0204, 10]
7. **The second trial** is played where `compare` is true, and is the side without the thing tried (What-ifs from a seat). Where `compare` is not given it is played for a new intent, previewed or queued, where the side has another intent of the commander's with an end (take a place, control an island, hold), in force or queued; for a draft of a limit or of a change to an intent that stands; and not for an intent in force, whose answer says the order is in force and that `compare` shows what it costs. Where none is played the answer says the side has no other plan to weigh. [0178, 11; 0181, amended 1 and 2]
8. **It is refused,** with words that say why and nothing played: where the game is over; with both `intent` and `preview` or neither; for an intent that is not the side's own, is the staff's, is withdrawn or has its withdrawal queued; for a preview that is not the seat's or is void (a day resolved, or something queued, since it was shown); for a size the trial's figures do not name; and whatever the runner refuses. [0178, 13; 0204, 12]
9. **What the answer holds.** The count of worlds and days of the size; how it went (counts of worlds, by their numbers); the five costs of the side and of the trial's enemy, each as the lowest, the middle and the highest; what the plan needs and has (the men wanted and to hand, what it waits for, the day expected ashore, the count asked of the enemy and what is held, its tasks); what the other plans lost; what was assumed; the leaves given; whether the count of worlds was uneven; and where the worlds part. It has no field that could hold a unit made for the enemy, a number of an intent or a plan formed inside a trial's world, or a world's own figures beyond the counts. It holds no chance, no per cent and no points. [0178, 4 and 15]
10. **How each thing is named.** A unit, a task force or a base of the side's that the live game has: by the side's own number and name, as the seat's other tools give it. A task force or a base the staff forms inside a trial: "a force the staff would form" with its ships, "a landing place the staff would make" with its place. A task of the plan: by its kind, in the words of the tool `order`, with its place and its day, and no number. A place: as the seat's tools give a place. A force assumed for the enemy: by its kind, its place and the belief of the side's it was made from ("from your contact at ...", "from your estimate of ...", "believed, standing unknown"), never by a number. The enemy's units in a trial are never named to the seat. [0178, 16 and 18]
11. **The words' order:** what was tried and in how many worlds; how it went; what it cost the side; what the trial's enemy lost, as the trial's own reckoning; what it needs and has and when it could go; what its other plans lost, or that there is none; what was assumed; the leaves given; where the worlds part. Where the seat has orders to units queued, the words say they are not tried. A trial asked inside a day says after what was tried that it was asked at that hour and that the rest of that day was not played, and, where the side has men ashore, that what is ashore is a day behind (rule 14). [0178, 17; 0204, 8]
12. **It changes nothing.** No command is queued, no preview is spent and no state, event, draw or hash of the game changes: a preview tried may still be approved. It reads nothing of the live enemy: the same seat in the same game with another enemy behind it is given the same answer. [0178, 14]
13. **The estimates are kept with the session,** not in the state: each is numbered from 1 among its side's, with the moment it was asked, the revision of the seat's queue then and what was asked. The tool `trials` lists them, marking each asked on an earlier evening or before the queue changed, and, given an `estimate` number, gives that one again unchanged. A trial asked inside a day is listed as asked at that hour ("asked at 0600 of day 1"), and one asked at an earlier hour of the same day is marked as one asked on an earlier evening is ("asked at an earlier hour"); each row gives `inside_day`. They are not in a saved game. A game file kept by `play` holds every call and so plays every trial in it again. [0178, 19 and 20; 0204, 9]
14. **Asked inside a day.** In a scenario that lets a seat stop the clock at 0600 and 1200 (chapter 1, Orders inside a day), a trial may be asked there by every form it has at dusk: of an order in force or queued, of a draft, compared, at later dates and described. [0204, 1 to 11]
    - **It is begun on the copy a preview uses:** the game as it stands at that hour with the open day treated as closed (`World::as_if_day_closed`: no pulse still to come resolved, no end of the day run). A trial played and a trial described are both begun on the side's view of it. The command state is the same on the copy as in the game, so what is in force and the leaves held read the same on either.
    - **The first day played is the day after the open day,** and the commands of the course are handed in on its first morning, the dusk at which they would be taken in; the dates of a window start there.
    - **The hours of the day still to come are not played.** In the trial's worlds each force is where it is at the hour asked, with the fuel it has then. Left out: at sea and in the air, the one or two pulses still to come; ashore, the whole of the day, since the march, the fight, training and rehearsal, digging in and a base changing side are worked at the day's end, so a land unit starts a full day behind and a landing's "when it could go" can come out a day late; hits on men that wait for the day's fight ashore, as a picture leaves them out; and orders a scenario gives inside the day. What the night has already done (supply and feeding, arrivals, replacements, repair) is in it.
    - **Orders to units are not tried,** as at dusk: the count of those left out takes in the orders given for the day's pulses as well as those queued, and the words say that they and the holds they make are not in it. A preview shows those holds, so the two differ there.
    - **A contact's age** is read as the picture holds it, in pulses, and in hours as if the pulse last resolved were an afternoon: a contact that has aged through the night is read up to six hours younger than it is; one reported in the pulse just resolved is exact. In the trial's worlds a contact is two pulses younger at 0600, and one at 1200, than it will be at the dusk the trial stands for.
    - **What was asked says so:** `inside_day` is `null` at dusk and inside a day the day and the hour, `{"day": 1, "clock": "0600"}`, in the answer of a trial, of a window and of a trial described, and in each row of `trials`.
    - **The game is as it was:** the copy is never resolved and never hashed, and the trial's worlds are its own.

Not here: the trial the staff's leave is asked with, and the lift and the weight of supply a plan needs; stopping a trial from a seat (the trait allows it, the tool does not offer it); a commander's direct orders in a course (a seat's queued orders to units are not tried, and the answer says so); playing the rest of a day inside a trial asked at 0600 or 1200 (decision 0204, stage 2). [0178, What it leaves for later; 0181, amended 11; 0204, 14]

A side sees: the estimate the tool `trial` gives it, in its game, and nothing else of a trial.

## What-ifs from a seat

A commander asks three things of an order before it is taken in: what if I change one thing, what does this order cost me, and what if I give it later. Each is the tool `trial` with one more argument, and nothing new is played: the trial, the estimate and the window are those above. [0181]

1. **`compare`,** true or false: whether a second trial is played of the side **without the thing tried**, in the same worlds as the first (the same view, beliefs, figures, seed, trial's number and size). What that is: [0181, amended 1]
   - for a preview of a limit, or of a change to an intent in force or queued: the plan as it stands, without the draft. The intent is in both trials;
   - for a preview that sets a new intent, or an intent that is queued and not yet in force: the side without that intent;
   - for an intent in force: the side with the intent withdrawn on the coming morning.
2. **Where `compare` is not given** it is true for a preview of a limit or of a change to an intent that stands; true for a new intent, previewed or queued, where the side has another plan to weigh (A seat's trial, rule 7); false for an intent in force; and false wherever `dates` is given. [0181, amended 2]
3. **The two trials differ in the one thing.** Each is given the standing commands but for that thing, and the same leaves: a leave the course gives the first is given the second too. For the plan as it stands the second has the standing commands as they are and the first has the draft besides; for a queued intent the second has the standing commands less those that set the intent or a limit on it; for an intent in force the second has the standing commands and the intent's withdrawal, handed in on the coming morning before the staff is asked. The standing commands are the scenario's commands of the day for the side and what the seat has queued (A seat's trial, rule 3). [0181, 4; amended 6]
4. **A withdrawal is a withdrawal.** Withdrawing an intent in force cancels its plan and the staff's intents under it; it does not undo what was ordered: a landing being readied, ships under way and men ashore stay as they are. The answer says the order is withdrawn on the coming morning, that what was already ordered stands, and that the withdrawal is the trial's and not the commander's. [0181, amended 3]
5. **What the answer gains beside it,** where a second trial was played: what the second trial is of; what it cost each side there, as the first is given; and, for the plan as it stands, how the intent went there and the two world for world (The window, rule 9): met and kept in both, with the draft alone, as it stands alone and in neither, with the worlds that could not be played counted apart. Where the intent is not in the second trial to have gone, only the costs are given and no count of worlds for it. What the other plans lost is given as before, from the same second trial. [0181, 5 and 6; amended 1]
6. **`dates`:** `chosen`, or a list of one or two days of the game after the coming morning, ascending. The answer is then a **window** (The window), of the kind `window`: the first date is the coming morning, and the later dates are those the window chooses from what the order may use, or those given. A list may begin with the coming morning, which is dropped. A list of three later days, of days not ascending, of a day before the coming morning, after the last day of the first date's play or after the game's last day, or a value that is neither `chosen` nor a list of days, is refused. Chosen dates are bound by the last day of the first date's play. [0181, 9; amended 8]
7. **A window hands in the whole order at each date.** Its course is every standing command that sets the intent tried or a limit on it, in order, and after them the draft where a preview is tried, and then the leaves the side lacks; the rest are standing commands, handed in on the coming morning at every date. So a previewed limit is tried as part of the order given later, and the dates are chosen from the order's own forces limit. [0181, amended 4]
8. **No later date for an order in force,** whether it is named by `intent` or by a preview of a limit on it or of a change to it: `dates` is refused, with words that say a landing in force is held to a day by a "not before", which can be previewed and compared. A window has no second trial: `dates` with `compare` true is refused. [0181, 12; amended 5]
9. **A standing command left behind that names the intent.** Where the commands that set the intent tried are moved to a later date (a window) or left out (the second trial of a queued intent), and another standing command names that intent (an "after" on another intent), the trial is refused, naming the command. An intent in force that is withdrawn is not such a case: the other order then waits, as it would in the game. [0181, amended 7]
10. **The window's answer** gives the dates, given or chosen, and for each date: its day, the day the order was handed in, what came due on it, the men its take chose (the side's own units by its own numbers, with their men), the day expected ashore, the enemy at each place of the intent's end on its eve (men and the least cover at a place, the trial's own reckoning, never a unit), the fields a trial's answer has but the other plans, and, for each later date, world for world against the first. Its words are a commander's, each date apart; each later date says that nothing of the order was done before it, and that a landing decided on that day is ashore later than one prepared from the first morning and held. A window names no other plan. [0181, 13 and 14]
11. **`describe`,** true or false, false where none is given. With it true nothing is played: the answer, of the kind `trial_described`, says what would be tried: the standing commands, the course, what it would be set beside, the dates with what is due on each, and the worlds and days of each trial it would play (a later date's trial is the size's days and its shift more). It is not kept among the estimates and changes nothing. [0181, amended 9]
12. **No advice, and nothing changed.** The answer says nothing of which is to be chosen; it gives no chance, no per cent and no points. A preview tried is not spent: `approve` queues it, as before. A window and a trial with a second trial are each one estimate in the list `trials` gives, which names each one's kind. [0181, 7, 8 and 16]
13. **Its cost** is its trials': a compared trial is two, about 1.6 times one; a window of three dates is three trials and the days between. [0181, What it costs; amended 10]

Not here: the trial the staff's leave is asked with, and the comparison the staff offers where a limit binds; the lift and the weight of supply a plan needs; a later date prepared from the first morning by itself (a "not before" is that, as a what-if); dates from air groups that come due; a what-if of a direct order; the table's "Dry run". [0181, What it leaves for later]

A side sees: the answers the tool `trial` gives it, in its game, and nothing else of a trial.
