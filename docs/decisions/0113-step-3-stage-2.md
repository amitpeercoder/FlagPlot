# 0113: Step 3, stage 2: doctrine for the fighting tasks, and the enemy played by the staff

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 15, 2026, for the designer's approval, as the spec asks of every stage (`docs/step3-spec.md`, "Built in stages"). Confirmed by the designer on Nov 15, 2026, who answered its seven questions as the architect recommended. Nothing is built. Each step gets its hand calculations or test plan before any code, as stage 1 did. Step 1's two rule changes are decision 0114.

Summary: A plan for step 3, stage 2: seven kinds of intent beside supply (cover a place, strike what comes within reach, wear down a base, intercept, escort a run, keep a force fuelled, rest and repair), a doctrine for each on both sides, the numbers of the three risk levels set from the record, two small rule changes the doctrines need, and the proof: a battle of the slice and then a week of it with no unit order from anyone

Standing: Confirmed by the designer, Nov 15, 2026. Not built

## What stage 2 is

The spec's row: "Doctrine for the fighting tasks, on both sides. The enemy played by the staff", and what it shows: "A battle of the slice, and then a week of it, with no unit order from anyone." It also asks stage 2 for the numbers of the risk table and for the day from which a force may be used, and leaves to it whether a strike of a stated size is wanted.

Stage 1 built the contract and one doctrine. Its staff gives no order to an air group, to a fighting ship or to anything of the Japanese side unless a scenario states a command. So today a side played by the staff feeds its bases and does nothing else. Stage 2 is what makes it fight.

It does not land troops, march, bombard a base from the sea or pull out of a place: those are stage 3. It does not try courses against each other: that is stage 4.

## What the commander can say

1. **Seven kinds of intent are added** to keeping a base supplied. Each names its subject and has the six limits of the contract.

| Intent | The commander says | Its measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cover a place | Keep this base, or this hex of sea, covered from the air and from the sea | The share of daylight pulses with a patrol over it; the share of the approaches searched |
| Strike what comes within reach | Attack enemy ships that come within reach of this base or this carrier force | None. It is judged by what it sinks and what it costs |
| Wear down a base | Raid this enemy base | Raids a week, or "as the force allows" |
| Intercept | Meet an enemy force expected at this place, by night or by day | The place and the nights |
| Escort a run | The runs of this supply intent are escorted, and covered from a distance | Not an intent of its own: a setting of a supply intent (decision 9) |
| Keep a force fuelled | This force is not to go below so many days of fuel at cruise | Days of fuel |
| Rest and repair | Keep these groups and ships fit to fight | The fatigue and the damage above which a unit is withdrawn |

2. **An area intent is a list of these.** "Hold Guadalcanal's airfield open; deny the enemy his reinforcement; risk no carrier for it" is, for the staff of stage 2, a cover intent on Lunga, a strike intent for the groups at Henderson Field and the carrier forces, a supply intent with escort, and a forces limit that leaves the carriers out of anything inside enemy air reach. The commander states the list; stage 3 lets him state the area intent alone and has the staff draw the list.
3. **Both sides have them.** A scenario states each side's intents as commands of day 1, as scenarios 36 to 45 do. With a staff on both sides and no order in the file, the game is played from intents alone.

## The doctrines

Each is written down in the rulebook's chapter 8 as the code has it, gives its orders through the orders that exist, and hands in a reason with every order and every day it gives none.

4. **Covering a place from the air.** For the groups the forces limit gives it at a base or in a carrier force: which fly patrol and with what share kept overhead, which search and over what arc, which stand ready to strike, and which rest. Search arcs are set toward where the side's belief and the area limit say the enemy can come from. A fighter group's patrol share rises with the enemy's raids of the last days and falls when none have come.
5. **Striking what comes within reach.** Which groups stand on the naval strike mission, their target priority, their reach, and how old a contact they launch on. The launch itself stays where it is, inside the day, by the rule of chapter 5: the staff sets the standing orders and the men on the spot act on them.
6. **Wearing down a base.** When to raid: the staff counts the aircraft fit to fly, their fatigue, the escort it can send and what the last raid cost, and sets the base strike mission on the days it judges a raid worth it, and rest on the others.
7. **Covering a place from the sea, and intercepting.** Where a surface or a carrier force stands, at what readiness, with what stance, break-off level and keep share, and when it retires: for fuel, for damage, or because the task is done. A force sent to meet an enemy expected by night is routed to arrive after dark and to be clear by day where the enemy's air reaches, by the standing instruction of decision 0096.
8. **Rest, repair and replacement.** Which groups stand down and at what fatigue; which ships go back to a base that can patch them or to the yard; and, from this, **the day from which a force may be used**: a force away, under repair or not yet arrived is committed to nothing before the day the staff works out for it, and the proposal says that day.
9. **Escort and cover of a run.** A supply intent gains two settings: the escort its runs are to have and whether a covering force stands off. The staff puts the escorts in the convoy's own force, by the join order that exists, and times the run by a rule the record supports: to arrive at dusk and be clear by morning, or to unload by day and stand off by night, according to who holds the air (`docs/research/oob/runs-to-guadalcanal.md`). An escort is drawn from the forces limit as a cargo ship is, and competes with every other intent for it.
10. **Fuel.** The staff plans every force's fuel: it sends no force where it cannot get home with the margin its risk level asks, brings a force back to fuel before it reaches that margin, counts the hours fuelling takes (decision 0112), and keeps a base's fuel above its reserve by fuel runs, which are supply runs with a cargo of fuel.

## Two rule changes the doctrines need

Each gets a decision record with a rule card and a hand calculation before it is built. Both are small and both reuse what exists.

11. **How old a contact a group strikes on.** Today a group on naval strike launches at a contact reported in the pulse or in the one before, and at nothing older. The record has strikes sent on reports four, five and seven hours old, and it has them go badly (`docs/research/oob/launch-decisions.md`). A group gains one standing setting: the oldest contact it will launch on, in pulses. The default is today's rule, so no existing game changes.
12. **A strike of a stated size.** Today every aircraft of a strike group that can fly goes. A group gains a share to hold back, as a fighter group has a patrol share. The default is none held, so no existing game changes. The spec left this to stage 2; the record supports it: at Midway one carrier's attack aircraft were held until the other enemy carriers were found.

Not proposed: a rule for escorts apart from the convoy they sail in (question 4), and a rule for rearming (question 6).

## The three risk levels

The spec's table gives five choices that the risk level changes. These are its numbers. Each is a placeholder sized from the rows named, on a handful of battles, and is not checked against any measure.

| The staff's choice | Preserve the force | Balanced | Press | What it rests on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The oldest contact a strike is launched on | Reported this pulse or the one before, and only of a kind worth the strike (a short target list) | This pulse or the one before, as the engine has it today | Up to two pulses older: in the morning, a contact of the day before | Contacts under about an hour and a half old were struck at once at Coral Sea, Midway and Santa Cruz; three strikes on reports four to seven hours old found nothing, found little, or sank a light carrier while the two large ones went unstruck |
| How far it reaches | Four fifths of the type's radius | The radius | Extended reach, at the cost the rules give | Launches at 155 to 210 miles on fresh reports; 230 and 250 miles on old ones |
| Attack aircraft held back | A third | None | None, and the escort is taken from the patrol first | The reserve carrier at Midway; the advice after it to launch "with the absolute minimum loss of time" |
| Fighters kept overhead (the patrol share) | Three quarters | Half | A quarter | A carrier captain's call for more fighters after Coral Sea; no row gives a share |
| Fuel kept to get home | The passage home and half as much again | The passage home and a fifth | The passage home | Destroyers at three days of fuel on 8 August were called critical |
| A force's break-off level | A quarter of its ships | Half | Three quarters | No row. The engine's setting exists; the values are placeholders |
| Scarce ships (carriers, battleships) | Only where the task cannot be done without them, and outside enemy air reach | Where they add to the task | Wherever they are free | The order before Midway to use "strong attrition tactics, only" |

13. **Risk is read from belief, as the spec says,** and never from what the side has not seen. "Outside enemy air reach" means outside the reach of the bases and carriers the side believes the enemy has.
14. **A changed risk level changes the orders and the reason.** That is one of the spec's acceptance cases and is tested for each row.

## Built in six steps

| Step | What is built | What runs at the end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The two rule changes, each with its record and hand calculation | The older scenarios unchanged; two small scenarios on placeholder forces |
| 2 | Cover from the air, and rest: patrol, search arcs, stand-down. The intent, its tools and reasons | The air campaign of scenario 6 with the Allied side given a cover intent and no order |
| 3 | Strikes and raids: the strike intent and wearing down a base. The risk table's first four rows | Scenario 6 with both sides on intents; then the carrier day of the Eastern Solomons |
| 4 | Surface forces: cover from the sea, intercept, break-off and retirement; fuel planning and the day a force may be used | A night action from intents on both sides, on placeholder forces and then on Savo Island's |
| 5 | Escort and cover of a run, fuel runs, repair | A supply run escorted and covered against a force sent to stop it |
| 6 | The enemy played by the staff: both sides' intents in the slice, and the proof | A battle of the slice and then a week of it, staff against staff, with no unit order |

## What it must pass

Each case gets its numbers in a hand calculation or in the test plan of its step, written before the code.

- **Pure decisions.** For each doctrine, views built by the architect with the orders that must come out: a base with four groups and a raid expected; a contact of each age at each risk level; a force at the edge of its fuel; a damaged ship and a base that can and cannot patch it.
- **Properties,** as in stage 1, for every doctrine: nothing ordered that the side does not own or that is held; no plan below a reserve, outside the area or across the forces limit; every order and every day without one has a reason that resolves; the same inputs give the same orders; a side with no intent gives no order.
- **No truth:** two worlds that differ only in what the side cannot see give the same orders, proposals and reasons, for every doctrine.
- **The risk levels:** on the same information the three levels give different orders in a case built for each row of the table; a new report changes the order with the level unchanged; and a choice that went badly still has a reason that stands.
- **Against no orders.** On scenario 6, the Eastern Solomons and a week of the slice, over a thousand seeds: the side played by the staff does better than the same side given no orders, on the objectives scale and beside it on aircraft lost, ships lost, days the airfield was open and supply delivered, by a margin written in step 6's test plan before the run.
- **Against the script.** The staff's orders for the tuned battles are set beside the scripted ones and the difference is reported. As the spec says, it is not a test to pass.
- **The history measures are not judged on staffed games.** They were fitted to the record's own orders. A staffed game is judged by the spec's measures above.
- **The proof of the stage:** the Eastern Solomons, and then days 15 to 21 of the slice, with both sides' intents stated on day 1 and no order in the file or from anyone. Every invariant holds on a thousand seeds, no staff order is refused, and both sides do something: each flies searches, patrols and at least one strike, and each lands supply.
- **Legibility,** by ruling 39 of decision 0107, on a new set of reasons from the fighting doctrines, with a pass mark written before the run.
- **A model's game:** the same model plays one side of the week by intents alone against the staff, as in stage 1's step 4, and its account is kept.
- **Speed** is measured for a day's staff decisions with every doctrine at work, and reported.

## What it costs

- **Seven kinds of intent** in the command state, the tools and the saved game; the state version rises with each that adds state.
- **Two rule changes** with their records, rule cards, hand calculations and rulebook text; the rules label rises with each.
- **A second staff in `sim-staff`,** or the one staff grown: the supply doctrine stays as it is and the fighting doctrines sit beside it. The doctrine's version rises with each step.
- **The rulebook's chapter 8** grows by a section for each doctrine, and the glossary with it.
- **Every staffed scenario of stage 1 is unchanged** where it states no new intent. That is a test of each step.
- **Research still wanted,** none of it a condition of starting: a rule of the time for the size of an escort; fuel stocks at the bases by date; Japanese doctrine in its own words. Each is in `gaps.csv`.

## What it leaves for later stages

- Landing, marching, bombardment from the sea, pulling out, units made ready and bases built up (stage 3).
- Choosing between courses, and anything that needs the unseen enemy filled in (stage 4).
- The area intent stated alone, with the staff drawing the list of intents from it (stage 3).
- Rearming, unless question 6 is answered otherwise.

## Questions for the designer

Answered on Nov 15, 2026, each as the architect recommended: the seven kinds of intent as listed; the contact-age setting and the share of a strike held back are added (decision 0114); escorts sail in the convoy's own force; the proof is the Eastern Solomons and days 15 to 21 of the slice; rearming is left until after stage 2; and it is built in six steps. The questions as they were put:

1. **The seven kinds of intent.** Is this the right list for stage 2? I recommend it as it stands. The one I weighed leaving out is wearing down a base: it is what the Japanese side did every day of the slice from Rabaul, so without it that side has nothing to do in the air.
2. **How old a contact a strike is launched on** (decision 11): add the setting? I recommend yes. Without it the three risk levels cannot differ in the row the spec puts first, and the engine cannot do what both sides did in 1942, to their cost.
3. **A strike of a stated size** (decision 12): add the share held back? I recommend yes, as the smallest form of it: a share, not a count of aircraft.
4. **Escorts.** Do escorts sail in the convoy's own force, with no new rule, or apart from it with a rule of their own for what an escort does? I recommend the convoy's own force for stage 2. A force of transports and destroyers already fights as one force under the surface rules, and anti-submarine work, which is what a separate escort rule would mostly be for, waits on submarines.
5. **The proof.** The Eastern Solomons and then days 15 to 21 of the slice (21 to 27 August, the week that holds it)? I recommend these: it is the one carrier battle inside the slice, and the week around it has the air campaign, the destroyer runs and the first American supply runs all going at once.
6. **Rearming.** Nothing replenishes ammunition today, so a force that has fired is spent for the rest of a game. The research found where ammunition could be had and when, and no rate. Build a small rearming rule in stage 2, or leave it? I recommend leaving it until after stage 2 and saying so: inside a week it changes little, and the reserve of rounds in a force, which the spec's reserve limit names, can be honoured by the staff sending a force home when it is low. Over the 39 days of stage 3 it will matter, and I would plan it then.
7. **How it is built.** Six steps, each with its own record and hand calculations before code, each merged when it runs, as stage 1 was? I recommend yes.
