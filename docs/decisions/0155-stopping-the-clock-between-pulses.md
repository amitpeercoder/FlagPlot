## What is so today

A game day runs from 1800 to 1800 in three pulses: the night, the morning and the afternoon. A side's orders and commands are taken in once, before the night; the staff decides once; an interrupt of `run` stops the clock at the end of its day. A side's events carry their pulse, and a seat may read each with `explain`, but a seat acts only between days.

So a commander cannot act on a morning's sighting until the next evening, and a battle of one day (the Eastern Solomons) is one decision and then a result. On a campaign of 39 days this fits a commander ashore. On the small scenarios a new player is to begin with, it is not a game.

## The direction

1. **A day is shown pulse by pulse, now.** The table plots a resolved day as its three pulses and its end, from what the side was told in each. No rule changes. Done with this record (`table/`).
2. **A commander may stop between pulses for a short list of commands.** A rule, to be planned. The kind of command meant: to hold a thing back, to withhold or give a leave, to change the risk, to withdraw. Not a new end: a new intent is still taken up by the staff when it next decides.
3. **A seat stops by day or by pulse.** A commander ashore over a campaign stops between days; a commander afloat in a short battle stops between pulses. Command levels already let a player sit at any command (decision 0138), so this follows the game's own shape. A scenario says which.
4. **Left out: the staff planning again every pulse.** It would change what stage 2 and stage 3 proved, and most intents take days to matter.

## What the plan must settle

Each of these is open. None is decided here.

1. **Which commands may be given between pulses,** and for each, what state it reads and writes. Whether an order to a unit is among them: a strike is flown by a group's standing mission, so to hold a strike back is today an order to the group and not a command to the staff.
2. **When a command given between pulses takes effect,** and how long the staff takes to turn it into orders. Without a delay a commander who acts every pulse sees and steers more than any commander did. With command levels on, the delays of reports already stand (decision 0135); what is asked is the delay downward. Any figure is a placeholder until a row supports it.
3. **Whether an interrupt may stop the clock at the end of a pulse,** and which: a ship lost, a contact of a kind, a plan that waits for leave.
4. **That nothing changes where nobody commands inside a day.** The condition of the build: with no command given between pulses, every game has the hash it had, so every proof and hand calculation stands. This is the pattern of every stage since stage 2b.
5. **The other side.** The enemy's staff decides once a day. A player who commands each pulse has something it has not. The plan says how that is measured (a model with pulse stops beside one without, reported) and whether the staff is given the same short list.
6. **The tools.** A way for a seat to resolve one pulse and be answered; and a tool that gives a seat its events of a day or a pulse together, in place of reading them one at a time with `explain`, as the table does now.
7. **Saved and recorded games.** A saved game holds each day's orders; one with commands inside a day must hold their pulse, and an old saved game must replay as it did.

## What it costs

Step 1 cost a change to one page. Step 2 is a rule with a new state field or more, a hash version, tools and a proof of its own; it is of the size of a stage's step, and is planned as one. It does not come before the mends owed at the start of stage 4 unless the designer says so.
