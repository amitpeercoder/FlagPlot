# 0120: Raids, and the end "neutralise a base"

Status: settled by the architect on Nov 15, 2026, inside the plan for step 3, stage 2 (decision 0113, the third end and doctrine 6, and step 3 of its seven), and on the designer's direction of the same day that a doctrine belongs to one level (`docs/design/command-levels.md`, decision 0119). It writes the end and its doctrine out, with the hand calculation. Built on Nov 16, 2026 ("As built" below).

Summary: The end "neutralise a base", stated to the area, under which the area's staff gives each of its bases with bombers in reach the task of raiding it; and the raid doctrine at the base's level: which mornings its bombers go, by their fatigue, the weight they can send, their escort and what the last raid cost, with the risk level setting each. Every doctrine now names the level it belongs to, and its reasons say which level decided

Standing: Settled by the architect, Nov 15, 2026, inside decisions 0113 and 0119. Built

## Why

Stage 2's first proof is the air campaign with both sides played from ends. The side at Rabaul has no end it can state: "hold a place" gives it patrols and searches and no raid. This record gives it one, and gives the staff the judgement the script of scenario 6 makes by hand: on which days to raid and on which to rest.

It is also the first doctrine written across two levels, as the designer directed: the area decides that a base is to be raided and by whom, and the base decides how and when.

## A doctrine belongs to a level

1. **Every doctrine names its level,** which is the addressee of the intents it serves. It reads the intent it was given, the forces that addressee commands, the side's picture and its own figures. A doctrine of a higher level gives no order to a unit and sets no setting of one: it forms sub-intents for the level below.
2. **A reason says which level decided:** the addressee of the intent it serves. The tools print it with the chain.
3. **The picture is still the side's.** One picture a side serves every level until stage 2b (decision 0119). Nothing here plays differently for it.
4. **As built so far,** "keep supplied" is the area's and gives orders itself, and "hold a place" and cover are both the base's. Neither is changed by this record.

## The end: neutralise a base

5. **A kind of intent: neutralise a base.** It names a base that is on the side's map and is not the side's. It is addressed to the area. It has no date.
6. **Its doctrine, at the area's level.** Each morning, for each such intent whose plan is released, for each base of the side, in base number order, that it may use and that has a bomber group able to reach the target on normal reach: where no raid task from that base at that target is in force under it, it forms one. Where no base qualifies, it forms none, hands in that it cannot meet the intent, and its reason gives the distance and the reach of each bomber type it has.
7. **What it may use.** With no forces limit on it, an intent addressed to the area may use nothing (decision 0117, 8), with one exception made here: a neutralise intent with no forces limit may use every base of the side for the forming of raid tasks, and each raid task may then use the air groups at its base. A forces limit that names air groups narrows it as any does.
8. **Its measure:** the days it has been in force, the raids flown at the base under it, the aircraft the side claims destroyed on the ground there, and the air raids on the side's own bases in those days. A side cannot tell where a raid on it came from, so the last counts them all. It is met on a day on which no base of the side was raided from the air.

## The task: raid a base

9. **A kind of intent: raid a base.** It names a base of the side and the target. It is addressed to the base. It may stand under "neutralise a base", and a commander may state it himself as a root.
10. **Its doctrine, at the base's level.** Each morning, over the groups it may use that are at the base and not held, and that are its to order by rule 16, in group number order:
    - **A bomber** is a group that is not a fighter, whose type carries a weapon and can reach the target on normal reach. It is set to strike the target on a morning the raid goes and to rest on a morning it does not.
    - **A fighter** is set to combat air patrol with a patrol share of 0, so that every aircraft it can fly is escort. It is given no rest order: it flies only on a morning a raid goes. A fighter group that the base's cover has (rule 16) is left to it, and its escorts are what its patrol share leaves.
    - **Any other group** is given no order, and the reason says so.
11. **Which bombers go.** A bomber group goes unless its fatigue in the morning is above the level its risk gives (the cover doctrine's: 30, 45, 60), or its last raid cost it too much (rule 13).
12. **Whether the raid goes.** It goes on a morning when the bombers that would go can send, between them, at least the weight its risk asks, and, at preserve the force, when at least one fighter at the base can go with them. What a group can send is the aircraft it is able to fly less its held share. Where the raid does not go, every bomber rests.
13. **What the last raid cost.** A bomber group that lost, on its last raid, at least the share of the aircraft it sent that its risk gives stands down for the one morning after it.
14. **The held share** of each bomber group is set from the risk level, where it differs from what the group has.
15. **When it cannot.** On a morning when no bomber group it may use can reach the target, or, at preserve the force, the base has no fighter group it may use or draw escorts from, it hands in that it cannot meet the intent.

16. **Whose a group is,** where a cover and a raid task at one base may both use it. It goes by the kind of the group and not by the rank of the intents, so that a base both defended and raiding does both:
    - a fighter group is the cover's, where a cover of its base is in force, and the raid has the escorts its patrol share leaves; with no cover it is the raid's;
    - a group that can bomb the target is the raid's, though it can also search; the cover doctrine of decision 0115 searches with the groups no raid task in force may use as bombers, which amends its rule 7;
    - rank decides only between two tasks of the same kind that may use one group.

| Set by the risk level | Preserve the force | Balanced | Press |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A group rests above a fatigue of | 30 | 45 | 60 |
| Share of its bombers a group holds back | A third | None | None |
| The least a raid sends | 12 | 6 | 1 |
| A raid without an escort | Does not go | Goes | Goes |
| A group stands down after losing | A tenth of what it sent | A quarter | Never |

The first two rows are decision 0113's. The last three are placeholders that rest on no row: the record has the raids from Rabaul at 18 to 27 bombers with an escort, and has days without one for weather, which the game does not have.

## Rule card

| Rule | Reads | Writes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forming raid tasks | The neutralise intent and its plan; the side's bases and their groups; the types' reach; the target's hex on the side's map | A raid intent under it, or the notice that it cannot be met |
| The raid doctrine | The raid intent and its limits read up the tree; the groups at the base, their fatigue, the aircraft each is able to fly, their held share; the side's own events of the last raid | Orders to the groups: mission, patrol share, held share; reasons; the notice |

No rule of the engine changes. A raid is launched, escorted, met and resolved by chapter 5 as it stands.

## What moves to step 4

Decision 0113 put the doctrine for striking ships that come within reach in this step. The air campaign has no ship in it, so that doctrine would have nothing to act on and nothing to prove it. It moves to step 4, beside "deny an area", where the scenarios have ships, and the rule for a strike's size (decision 0118) is built with it. There the size is decided at the group's level from the task it is under: a task to destroy one force sends everything, and a task with more to hit than one force is sized by doctrine.

Sending a share of a search group, which decision 0115 left to be weighed in this step, is weighed and left out: a group that searches on the days its fatigue allows was enough for step 2's proof.

## The first proof of the stage

Scenario 6, the air campaign over its 28 days, with every order taken out of the file and each side's ends stated on day 1 with both leaves: for the Japanese side, hold Rabaul and neutralise Henderson Field; for the Allied side, hold Henderson Field. Over 1,000 seeds:

- every invariant holds and no staff order or command is refused on any seed;
- on every seed the Japanese side raids Henderson Field on at least 5 days and flies a patrol at Rabaul, and the Allied side flies patrols and searches;
- reported beside it and not judged: the same measures with the scenario's own scripted orders on both sides, and the count of raid days against the script's.

The Allied side flies no strike in it: nothing comes within reach of its bombers. Decision 0113's line that each side flies at least one strike is met for the Japanese side by its raids, and for the Allied side waits for the second proof, which has ships.

## What it costs

- **State:** two kinds of intent. The state version rises; the rules label does not.
- **The staff:** two doctrines, written apart by level; the doctrine's version rises.
- **The tools:** `set_intent` takes the new kinds; reasons and `why` print the level; the command report shows the measure.
- **The rulebook's chapter 8** gains "A doctrine belongs to a level", "Neutralising a base" and "Raiding a base"; the glossary gains the raid task and the level of a doctrine.

## The hand calculation

`tests/scenario_50_neutralise_a_base.rs`: one base with two bomber groups and a fighter group, an enemy base 200 nm away with nothing at it, over 12 days. It gives every order of every morning, every group's fatigue and the aircraft of every raid. The bombs' hits on the empty base are rolled and are not asserted.

## As built

Built on Nov 16, 2026, in one step. What follows is what was built and every place the build read the record more closely or differed from it.

**Versions.** The state version is 29 (`flagplot-state-v29`: the two kinds of intent, codes 4 and 5, added at the end). The rules label stays 12: no game without a neutralise or raid intent plays differently, and the level of a reason is text. The saved game is version 5 (the commands and hand-ins carry the new kinds and the level; a game of version 4 is refused, not converted). The doctrine version is `supply-9`. The agent interface is version 9: an intent's `base` is null for a neutralise, which is a change in meaning.

**State and intake.** `IntentKind::Neutralise { target }`, code 4, addressed to the area, and `IntentKind::Raid { base, target }`, code 5, addressed to `base`. A raid may stand under a neutralise for the same target and under nothing else. The target of either kind must be a base on the side's map that is not the side's: otherwise `set_intent` and `form_intent` are refused with a new refusal, `NotAnEnemyBase`, in the same words for a base the side may not know and one that does not exist. It is tried after "Not your base". A neutralise names no base of the side's own.

**The level of a reason.** A reason's level is `Serves::level`, filled for every reason a staff hands in, from the addressee of the intent served. So stage 1's reasons now say "decided at the level of the area" too. This is text only: no order, event or hash changes with it, and a game saved before it reads each reason with none.

**The neutralise doctrine** is `crates/sim-staff/src/neutralise.rs` and the raid doctrine `crates/sim-staff/src/raid.rs`; the morning's order is the supply doctrine, the leaves, the hold doctrine, the neutralise doctrine, the cover doctrine and the raid doctrine, in that order, so that the cover's order of the morning is read by the raid. Readings the build took:

- **Rule 6, which bases qualify.** Every group at a base counts for whether the base qualifies, held or not, as the hold doctrine counts one. A base qualifies only if a bomber group it may use there has a reach, its type's strike radius, at least the distance. With a forces limit that names groups, a base qualifies only where a named group is, and only the groups named count there.
- **A reason every morning.** The neutralise doctrine hands in a reason every morning: the raid that stands, the raid it forms, or that none can be formed. A cannot-meet notice, resting on the no-order reason, is handed in every morning on which no base qualifies. Where the target is no longer an enemy base on the side's map it gives a reason and no notice, and forms nothing.
- **Rule 10, the raid's groups.** A group is the raid's to read if it is at the base, may be used by the raid, is not held as a whole, is not taken by a raid served earlier, and is the raid's by rule 16. The orders are given in group number order, a mission before a setting, and only where the group differs from what is wanted.
- **Rule 12, the weight.** What the bombers can send is worked with the held share the doctrine wants this morning (the risk's), whatever the group has set. A raid goes only if its weight is also more than none.
- **Rule 12, the escort at preserve the force.** "A fighter can go with them" is read as the escorts the fighters at the base can give this morning being above 0: a fighter the raid has gives all it can fly; a fighter of the base's cover gives what the patrol share the cover wants this morning leaves of what it can fly, cut to a whole aircraft, and none if the cover rests it.
- **Rule 13, what the last raid cost.** "Lost" is the aircraft the group lost in the pulse of its last base strike launch, shot down (`AircraftLost`) or ditched (`AircraftDitched`), as the side's own events show. A group stands down only on the morning right after that raid; a group that flew its last raid earlier does not.
- **Rest.** A bomber that does not go, and every bomber when the raid does not go, is set to rest.
- **Rule 15.** The cannot-meet case is no bomber group at the base that the raid may use, is not held and can reach the target, or, at preserve the force, no fighter group the raid may use or draw escorts from. A fighter of the cover counts here even on a morning the cover rests it.
- **Rule 16.** "A cover of its base" is read as a cover in force of the base that may use the group. By kind: a fighter is the cover's where such a cover is in force, and otherwise the raid's; a group that can bomb the target is the raid's, though it can also search; any other group is the cover's where there is a cover, and otherwise no one's. The cover (`crates/sim-staff/src/air.rs`) leaves a raid's bomber out and says so in its reason, and records for each fighter it has the share it is to fly at, or none where it rests it, for the raid to read.

**The measure** (`sim_agent::neutralise_measure`) is worked from the side's own view: the raids flown are the distinct (day, pulse) pairs with a base strike launch at the target by the side's groups; the claims on the ground are the side's air claims at the target's hex; the raids on its own bases are summed over every base of the side; the days met are the days in force on which none was raided. It is shown in `command_state`, in `report` (as `neutralisations`) and in the summary.

**The tools.** `set_intent` takes the kinds `neutralise` (with `target`) and `raid` (with `base` and `target`), and a scenario's `[[command]]` the same keys. `propose` works for the new kinds and gives the tree the staff would form. `why` and `staff_orders` print the level before the chain.

**What it was checked against.** The hand calculation, `tests/scenario_50_neutralise_a_base.rs` on `data/scenarios/50_neutralise_a_base.toml`: placeholder data, run on the frozen figures as scenario 47 is, with no frozen copy, being new. The main case came out as the calculation has it in every number: the tree (intent 1 the commander's, addressed to the area; intent 2 the staff's, raid base 2 from base 1, addressed to base 1), the orders by day (4, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 2, 2, 0 and 2), every morning's fatigue and 40 at the end, the aircraft of the ten raids and their escorts, the measure (12 days in force, 10 raids flown, no raid on a base of the side, 12 days met), and the levels and chains of the reasons. So did every property, the one about a held group as restated below: preserve, press, no escort at balanced and at preserve, out of reach (280 nm against 250, the notice on day 1), a base held and a base to neutralise (20 bombers and 4 escorts on day 1, and group 2 still the cover's with the neutralise intent ranked first), a raid stated alone, withdrawing the end, the forces limit, the same start and two worlds; "what the last raid cost" and "whose a group is" are tests of the pure decision (`sim_staff::raid::decide`, `stands_down` and `whose`). **A held group, restated by the architect after the build (Nov 16, 2026).** As first written, the property had group 3, with group 1 held from day 3, keep the main case's days.
