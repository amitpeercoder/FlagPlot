# 0202: Carriers serve an order for an end

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 21, 2026, on the designer's finding at the table that an order to deny waters or to neutralise a base never moves his carriers. It changes decisions 0123, 0125 and 0190 where they are named. To be read by a reviewer who has not seen it written, and amended on that reading, before its hand calculation is written and before any of it is built.

Summary: A commander who orders an end, "deny these waters" or "neutralise that airfield", expects his staff to use what he has, his carriers among it. Today those two orders use aircraft at bases and nothing else, and a carrier force moves only when told where to go. This has the staff count a free carrier force among the means of both orders, choose where it works from, and have its aircraft do the task; and it leaves the commander the word on whether it goes.

Standing: Planned on Nov 21, 2026; waits for its review.

## Why

What is so today:

- **"Deny an area" forms tasks for bases.** For each base it may use, a task to strike ships where a bomber's normal reach touches the waters, and a task to search where a searcher's range does. Only groups at a base are read. Where no base reaches, it "forms no task and cannot meet" the order.
- **A carrier force serves a deny only when the commander names it** in the order's forces limit. The staff then gives it a task to operate carriers "in the hex the force is in that morning: the staff does not choose where a carrier force stands" (decision 0125, 2). The force is not moved to the waters. Naming a task force and no air group also shuts every base out of that deny, which nobody meant.
- **"Neutralise a base" forms raids from bases only.** A carrier task cannot stand under it: the pair is refused. The record says so twice: "a raid on a base from a carrier: 'neutralise a base' tasks bases only" (0125) and "that carriers do not raid an airfield by doctrine is left as it is" (0184).
- **The engine can fly a raid on a base from a carrier,** and the staff already orders one under "take a place": the force is sent to the first hex on its way within its bombers' least normal strike radius of the place, and its bombers raid the place when no fresh contact of ships is in their reach.
- **A force has one task of a kind.** A carrier force with a task to operate carriers is given no other.
- **Nothing keeps carriers out of the reach of the enemy's aircraft ashore.** It is recorded as not built.
- **At the table** the designer gave "deny these waters" and "neutralise" in several games and saw his carriers neither move nor be planned for, and a plan that could do nothing shown as "ready and waits for your leave".

## The rules

### Which carrier forces an order may use

1. **An order to deny an area or to neutralise a base may use every carrier force of the side that has no carrier task,** where its forces limit names no task force. Where the limit names task forces, only those, as today.
2. **A forces limit that names a task force and no air group no longer shuts out the bases.** The bases and their groups are narrowed only by a limit that names air groups.
3. **A carrier force already given a task to operate carriers,** by the commander or under another order, is left to it. The commander's own order for a force comes first.
4. **Where two orders could each use a free force,** the order of higher priority takes it, and then the lower number.

### Where the force works from

5. **The staff chooses the station,** replacing decision 0125's rule 2 for these two orders: the first hex on the force's way by sea toward the target from which the task can be done, or the hex it is in if it can be done from there.
   - **For a deny,** the target is the centre of the waters, and the task can be done from a hex whose distance to the centre is no more than the bombers' least normal strike radius less the waters' own distance, so that all the waters are in reach. Where the waters are wider than the radius, the centre itself is to be in reach.
   - **For a neutralise,** the target is the base, and the task can be done from a hex within the bombers' least normal strike radius of it.
   It is the routine that stations a take's carriers today.
6. **The station is the hex of the task to operate carriers,** formed under the order. Everything else is that task's doctrine as it stands: the searches, the patrol, the strikes on ships, closing on a contact and coming back, going home for fuel.
7. **Where no station can be reached** (no way by sea, or the force's fuel is short of the passage there and home at its risk's margin), no task is formed for that force and the reason says which.

### What the aircraft do

8. **Under a deny,** as today for a force named: the waters are in the search arc, and the order's targets limit applies.
9. **Under a neutralise the carrier's bombers raid the base** when no fresh contact of ships is in their reach, by the rule that has a take's carriers raid the place. Ships come first, as there.
10. **The deny's measure counts strikes by carrier groups** serving it, with those from bases.

### Risk

11. **At preserve a carrier force is not sent to neutralise a base.** A raid on an airfield is flown from inside that airfield's reach, and preserve does not put carriers there. The reason says so. At balanced and at press it is sent.
12. **Under a deny it is sent at every risk.** The station is the farthest hex from the waters from which they are all in reach, which is the least exposed the task allows.
13. **No rule is added to keep carriers outside the reach of enemy aircraft ashore.** That stays not built, and is said in the reason where an enemy base with aircraft is within its bombers' reach of the station.

### The commander's word

14. **The task formed is a plan like any other.** It waits for the commander's leave where leave is withheld, is shown in the order's brief with its station, and can be cancelled there; cancelled, it is not formed again while the order stands.
15. **An order of his own for the force takes it out** (rule 3), on the morning it is given.

### What the staff says

16. **"Cannot meet" counts the carriers.** It is said only when no base and no carrier force can serve the order, and names both.
17. **A plan that has formed nothing is not called ready.** That is the table's, below.

## At the table

18. **The words of the two orders** say what will be used: aircraft from your bases, and a carrier force if one is free.
19. **A plan whose staff has formed nothing, or cannot meet the order, says so** where today it says the plan is ready and waits for leave.
20. **The carrier's station is shown** on the chart with the plan.

## Rule card

Reads: the side's own task forces and their carrier tasks; an order's forces, risk and targets limits and its priority; a force's bombers and their normal strike radius; the map's sea paths; a force's fuel and its margin by risk. Writes: a task to operate carriers under a deny or a neutralise, with its hex; the settings that task's doctrine sets today. Draws no random number. No new state: the pair "operate carriers under neutralise" is allowed where it is refused today.

## What it changes in games on file

Any game in which a side has a deny or a neutralise with no task force named, and a carrier force with no carrier task, plays differently. Before it is built each scenario of record and each proof is read for that, and the ones that would move are named to the designer with what moves. The doctrine's version rises. Where a closed proof would move, its scenario states the carrier forces' own orders, as most do already, so that it plays as it did.

## What it does not do

- It does not keep carriers out of the enemy's air reach, or weigh a base's aircraft before a raid.
- It does not use carriers for "hold", "cover" or "keep supplied".
- It does not move surface forces under these orders; "station a force" stays the commander's.
- It does not choose between a deny and a neutralise for one force beyond rule 4.

## What it costs

- **The staff commits the scarcest ships a side has without being told to.** The commander's leave, the plan in the brief and his own order are the checks. A player who gives leave to launch in advance and then orders a neutralise at press will see his carriers go to an enemy airfield.
- **Rulebook:** chapter 8 (deny, neutralise, the carrier doctrine's station, what may stand under what, the forces limit), the glossary ("station"), and the lines of 0125 and 0184 that say the staff does not choose and does not raid.

## Questions for the designer

1. **Carriers by default, or only when asked?** The plan has the staff use a free carrier force unless the commander says otherwise. The other way is a choice on the order, "with the carriers", off unless ticked. The architect proposes by default, with leave as the check: it is what a commander means by the order.
2. **Preserve keeps carriers from raiding an airfield** (rule 11). The architect proposes it.

## Amended on review

A reviewer who had not seen the plan written read it against the code and the scenarios on Nov 21, 2026. Where this and the text above differ, this holds.

**Closed proofs do not move (replaces rules 1 to 5 where they differ, and "What it changes in games on file").**

21. **Rule 2 is withdrawn.** Letting the bases serve a deny that names only task forces would move eleven proof files and the Coral Sea's, and changes what the engine accepts for every order with such a limit. It stays as it is, a known oddity, written in the rulebook. The table sets no forces limit, so a player does not meet it.
22. **A force the commander named keeps the hex it is in.** Decision 0125's rule 2 stands for a force named in the order's forces limit. The staff chooses a station only for a force it took by its own choice.
23. **A carrier force is free only when nothing of the commander's speaks for it:** it has no carrier task, no intent in force is addressed to it, no forces limit in force names it, and it is not held. The staff hands free forces out in one pass over the denies and neutralises together, in priority order and then by number, so that a force given to one order is never taken by another first. This is the test the island's doctrine uses for its carriers today, and that routine is reused.
24. **It is for the commander's own orders.** A deny or a neutralise the staff forms under an island keeps the island's rule for its carriers; this decision adds nothing there.
25. **With rules 21 to 24 no closed proof is expected to move:** in each, every carrier force is named by an order or has an order of its own. The build checks every scenario and recorded game on file by playing them before and after. Any that moves is named to the designer before the change merges, and none is restated to make it pass.

**The commander's word (replaces rules 14 and 15).**

26. **Nothing is formed until the order's plan is released,** as for every order: with leave withheld the carriers stay where they are until the commander approves the plan. What he approves is shown first: the preview and the dry run are played as if released and name the force and its station.
27. **A hold keeps a force out.** A carrier force the commander holds is not taken, and one taken and then held is let go the next morning. No new state is needed: holds exist. A direct order to a force the staff has tasked holds it by the override, as today; a direct order to a free force does not, and the plan paper's last line, which says it does, is corrected.
28. **The staff withdraws its own carrier task** when an order of higher priority that names the force needs it, as it may withdraw a task of its own today.

**The station and the raid (replaces rules 5, 7, 9, 10 and 12 where they differ).**

29. **The reach for a deny** is the bombers' least normal strike radius less 40 nm for each hex of the waters' distance. Where the centre of the waters is a hex ships cannot enter, the way is found to the nearest sea hex of the waters.
30. **A force already within that reach stays where it is,** which may be inside the waters. The plan's claim that the station is the least exposed place is withdrawn: it is the first place from which the task can be done.
31. **No fuel test at forming.** The carrier doctrine already fuels in port before it sails, goes home when short and says when a force with no home port cannot be weighed.
32. **The raid on a base is the take's routine given a target base** in place of a take's place. The neutralise's measure already counts any group's raid on the target, so it needs no change; the deny's is changed as rule 10 says.

**Risk (replaces rules 11 and 13).**

33. **At preserve a carrier force goes to neutralise a base only where the commander's standing leave to launch is given,** as the take's reconnaissance does at preserve. Approving the plan alone does not send it.
34. **The reason names any enemy base on the side's map within the bombers' reach of the station.** Whether it has aircraft the staff cannot know and does not say.

**At the table (replaces items 18 to 20).**

35. **The order's draft and the plan paper read the preview,** which says whether a carrier force would be used and from where; the fixed words under the two orders say only what may be used. The station is drawn with the plan once it is released, as any force's task is. A plan that waits for leave says what approving it would send.
36. **A carrier force's card offers a hold,** "keep this force out of the staff's plans", if it does not already.

**Rule card, corrected:** it also reads holds, and every intent's addressee and forces limit. It allows the pair "operate carriers under neutralise". It adds no state.

## Amended on the designer's word: the station is chosen by risk, the enemy and fuel

On Nov 21, 2026 the designer answered the two questions and set the station's rule. Carriers serve both orders, by default. Their placement is to be settled by the risk, by the enemy known and suspected, and by fuel; and the way they take is to be weighed too. The way is a decision of its own, to follow this one (0203): here a force goes to its station by the shortest way by sea, as every ship does. Where this and the text above differ, this holds; it replaces rules 5, 12, 29, 30 and 33.

**Where the task can be done from**

37. **The candidates are every hex ships can enter from which the task can be done:** for a deny, within the reach of rule 29 of the centre of the waters, or of their nearest sea hex where the centre is land; for a neutralise, within the bombers' least normal strike radius of the base. The hex the force is in is a candidate like any other when it qualifies.

**The enemy, known and suspected**

38. **Each of these casts a circle of danger,** from what the side knows and believes and nothing else:
   - an enemy base on the side's map, to 15 hexes: the distance within which the island's doctrine already treats an enemy base as one that matters. What the base holds the staff does not know, so every enemy base casts it;
   - a fresh contact of ships, of any kind, to 8 hexes: the distance at which a lift is turned back from a carrier contact today (decision 0191). Fresh is as the carrier doctrine has it.
   Both are figures of the doctrine, reused and not new. A candidate is in danger when it lies inside any circle, and its margin is its distance to the nearest edge.

**Fuel**

39. **A candidate is in fuel when every ship of the force has the fuel for the passage to it and from it to the place it fuels, times the margin of the risk:** its home port, or with none the nearest base that can fuel it. A force with no place to fuel is not weighed, and the reason says so, as the carrier doctrine says today. A force in port that lacks the fuel fuels first: the test is made on the morning it would sail.

**The choice, by risk**

40. **Preserve:** only candidates in fuel and out of danger. Of those, the least passage from where the force is. With none, no task is formed for the force, and the reason says which circle or what fuel ruled every candidate out.
41. **Balanced:** as preserve where any candidate is out of danger. With none, the candidate in fuel with the greatest margin, that is, the least deep in danger; and the reason says it stands in danger and of what.
42. **Press:** the candidate in fuel with the least passage, in danger or out.
43. **Ties** go to the greater margin, then to the hex first in the map's order.
44. **So a neutralise is never served by carriers at preserve:** the base's own circle holds every candidate. At balanced the carriers stand at the edge of their reach from it.

**When it is chosen**

45. **The station is chosen on the morning the task is formed and kept.** It is not chosen again when a contact comes or goes, or the force would wander with every sighting; what the force does about a contact is the carrier doctrine's, as it stands. It is chosen again only when the task is formed again. Said as a limit, to be looked at when games have been played with it.

**What the staff says**

46. **The reason gives what was weighed:** the station and its distance and bearing from the target; each circle it lies outside or inside, by name; the passage there and to the place it fuels, the fuel that takes and the margin; and, where the risk ruled candidates out, how many and by what.

**Rule card, as it now stands**

Reads: the side's own task forces, their ships' fuel, class figures and place; the side's own intents, limits and holds; the enemy bases on the side's map, by place alone; the side's contacts, by place, kind and age; the map's sea paths; the bombers' normal strike radius; the two reused figures and the fuel margins by risk. Writes: a task to operate carriers under a deny or a neutralise, with its hex. Draws no random number. Adds no state.

**What it costs, added**

- **A way by sea is found to every candidate,** some hundreds for a wide deny, once when the task is formed. The build measures the day's time with `sim-cli time` before and after and says what it cost.
- **Every enemy base is feared alike.** A base the side has beaten flat casts the same circle as Rabaul. It makes preserve and balanced cautious near any enemy ground, and is the price of the staff knowing only where a base is.

**The search on the way, added on the designer's word**

47. **A carrier force on passage to its station searches the way ahead.** Today the force's search arc is set each morning from where it is, toward every enemy base on the side's map, every contact in its picture and the waters of a deny above it. To those are added, while the force is under way to a station the staff chose: the hexes of its way for the coming day, and the station itself. At the station the arc is as today. So the force looks where it is about to be as well as where the enemy is known to be.
48. **The reason for the arc names what it covers,** as it does today for each base, contact and water: "the way ahead to the station at …".
