# 0202: Carriers serve an order for an end

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 21, 2026 on the designer's finding at the table that an order to deny waters or to neutralise a base never moves his carriers. It was read twice by reviewers who had not seen it written, and the designer set the rule for the station and for the search on the way. This is the plan as it stands after both; its hand calculation is written from this text. It changes decisions 0123, 0125 and 0190 where they are named.

Summary: A commander who orders an end, "deny these waters" or "neutralise that airfield", expects his staff to use what he has, his carriers among it. Today those two orders use aircraft at bases and nothing else, and a carrier force moves only when told where to go. This has the staff count a free carrier force among the means of both orders, choose where it works from by the risk, the enemy and the fuel, search the way there, and have its aircraft do the task; and it leaves the commander the word on whether it goes.

Standing: Planned on Nov 21, 2026, amended on two reviews and on the designer's word, and built on Nov 21, 2026 ("As built"); the readings marked there, and the statements of scenario 116 that do not hold as written, are the architect's to confirm.

## Why

What is so today:

- **"Deny an area" forms tasks for bases.** For each base it may use, a task to strike ships where a bomber's normal reach touches the waters, and a task to search where a searcher's range does. Only groups at a base are read. Where no base reaches, it "forms no task and cannot meet" the order.
- **A carrier force serves a deny only when the commander names it** in the order's forces limit. The staff then gives it a task to operate carriers "in the hex the force is in that morning: the staff does not choose where a carrier force stands" (decision 0125, 2).
- **"Neutralise a base" forms raids from bases only.** A carrier task cannot stand under it: the pair is refused.
- **The engine can fly a raid on a base from a carrier,** and the staff already orders one under "take a place": the force is sent to the first hex on its way within its bombers' least normal strike radius of the place, and its bombers raid it when no fresh contact of ships is in their reach.
- **A force has one task of a kind.**
- **A carrier force searches from where it is each morning,** toward every enemy base on the side's map, every contact in its picture and the waters of a deny above it; not toward where it is going.
- **Nothing keeps carriers out of the reach of the enemy's aircraft ashore.**
- **At the table** the designer gave both orders in several games and saw his carriers neither move nor be planned for, and a plan that could do nothing shown as "ready and waits for your leave".

## The rules

### Which carrier forces an order may use

1. **A commander's order to deny an area or to neutralise a base may use every free carrier force of his side,** where its forces limit names no task force. Where the limit names task forces, only those, and a force so named keeps the hex it is in, as decision 0125 has it: the staff chooses a station only for a force it took by its own choice.
2. **A carrier force is free when nothing of the commander's speaks for it:** it is on the carrier mission, has no carrier task, no intent in force is addressed to it, no forces limit in force names it, and it is not held. This is the test the island's doctrine uses for its carriers today, and that routine is reused.
3. **Free forces are handed out in one pass** over the side's denies and neutralises together, in priority order and then by number, so that a force is never taken by one order while another could claim it first.
4. **It is for the commander's own orders.** A deny or a neutralise the staff forms under an island keeps the island's rule for its carriers, which stations them without weighing danger. That difference is known and is closed when the island's doctrine is next changed.
5. **The staff withdraws its own carrier task** when an order of higher priority that names the force needs it, as it may withdraw a task of its own today.

### Where the task can be done from

6. **The candidates are every hex ships can enter, and the force can reach by sea, from which the task can be done.**
   - **For a neutralise:** within the least normal strike radius of the bombers aboard of the base, in a straight line, as the engine measures a strike's reach.
   - **For a deny:** within that radius, less 40 nm for each hex of the waters' distance, of the centre of the waters, so that all the waters are in reach. Where that leaves less than one hex, the waters are wider than the bombers can cover from anywhere, and the candidates are the hexes within the radius of the centre. Where the centre is a hex ships cannot enter, the nearest sea hex of the waters stands for it.
   The hex the force is in is a candidate like any other when it qualifies.

### The enemy, known and suspected

7. **The staff reads the picture of the command that forms the task,** the area's. What is suspected is what that picture holds as contacts; estimates of men ashore do not count.
8. **Each of these casts a circle of danger:**
   - **an enemy base on the side's map, to 15 hexes.** It is the distance at which a supply run already times its arrival by night because of an enemy base (decision 0145): the same meaning, reused. What a base holds the staff does not know, so every enemy base casts it, an airfield beaten flat and a store of supply among them;
   - **a fresh contact reported as a carrier, to 8 hexes.** It is the distance at which a lift is turned back from such a contact (decision 0191): the same meaning, reused. Fresh is as the carrier doctrine has it.
9. **What the order is aimed at casts no circle against it.** A contact inside the deny's own waters is what the force is sent to strike. The base to be neutralised does cast its circle: a raid on an airfield is flown from inside that airfield's reach, and the risk should weigh it.
10. **A candidate's margin is counted in hex steps.** Outside every circle it is the least distance to a circle's edge. Inside one or more it is negative: the depth in the circle that holds it deepest.

### Fuel

11. **A candidate is in fuel when every ship of the force has the fuel for the passage to it, at the speed the carrier doctrine sails out at, and from it to the force's home port at cruise, times the margin of the risk.** A force stopped at a base that can fuel it is weighed with its ships full, since it fuels before it sails. A force with no home port is not weighed and passes, and the reason says its fuel could not be measured against a passage home, as the carrier doctrine says today.

### The choice, by risk

12. **The passage to a candidate is the number of hex steps by sea from the force.**
13. **Preserve:** only candidates in fuel and outside every circle. Of those, the least passage; then the greater margin; then the first in hex order. With none, no task is formed for the force.
14. **Balanced:** as preserve where any candidate in fuel is outside every circle. With none, the candidate in fuel with the greatest margin, that is, the least deep in danger; then the least passage; then hex order.
15. **Press:** the candidate in fuel with the least passage, in danger or out; then the greater margin; then hex order.
16. **So a neutralise is never served by carriers at preserve:** the base's own circle holds every candidate. At balanced the carriers stand at the edge of their reach from it, unless another base's circle reaches deeper there.
17. **The station is chosen on the morning the task is formed and kept.** It is not chosen again when a contact comes or goes, or the force would wander with every sighting. It is chosen again only when the task is formed again, after a hold is lifted or a higher order has had the force.

### What the force does

18. **The station is the hex of a task to operate carriers,** formed under the order. The pair "operate carriers under neutralise" is allowed where it is refused today. Everything else is the carrier doctrine as it stands: its patrol, its strikes on ships, going home for fuel.
19. **The station is where the force waits, not a fence.** The carrier doctrine still closes on a fresh contact by its own rule: on a carrier at balanced, on any ship at press, and at preserve only where the commander's boldness makes it weigh as balanced. A force stationed outside a carrier contact's circle may close on that contact the same day. That is the doctrine fighting; the station only settles where it stands when there is nothing to fight.
20. **Under a deny the force reads the order's waters and its targets limit,** as a force named in the order does today. The build confirms that a task formed under the deny reads both through the chain, and extends that where it does not.
21. **Under a neutralise the carrier's bombers raid the base** when no fresh contact of ships is in their reach: the take's routine, given a target base in place of a take's place. Ships come first, as there.
22. **The deny's measure counts strikes by carrier groups** serving it, with those from bases. The neutralise's already counts any group's raid on its target.

### The search on the way

23. **A carrier force under way to a station the staff chose searches the way ahead.** To what its arc takes in today are added the hexes of its way for the coming day and the station itself. At the station the arc is as today.

### The commander's word

24. **Nothing is formed until the order's plan is released,** as for every order: with leave withheld the carriers stay where they are until the commander approves. What he approves is shown first: the preview and the dry run are played as if released, and name the force and its station.
25. **A hold keeps a force out.** A carrier force the commander holds is not taken, and one taken and then held is let go the next morning. A direct order to a force the staff has tasked holds it by the override, as today; a direct order to a free force does not.

### What the staff says

26. **The reason for a station gives what was weighed:** the station and its distance and bearing from the target; each circle it lies outside or inside, with the base's name or the contact's number and age; the passage there and home, the fuel that takes and the margin; under command levels, as of when the force's state was known; and what would change the choice: another risk, the contact growing old, more fuel.
27. **Where no station is chosen,** it says which circles held every candidate, or that no candidate was in fuel, or that none could be reached by sea.
28. **"Cannot meet" counts the carriers.** It is said only when no base and no carrier force can serve the order, and names both.
29. **The reason for the arc names the way ahead,** as it names each base, contact and water today.

## At the table

30. **The words of the two orders** say what may be used: aircraft from your bases, and a carrier force that is free.
31. **The order's draft and the plan paper read the preview,** which says whether a carrier force would go and where. A plan that waits for leave says what approving it would send; a plan that would form nothing, or cannot meet the order, says so where today it is called ready.
32. **The station is drawn with the plan** once it is released, as any force's task is.
33. **A carrier force's card offers a hold,** "keep this force out of the staff's plans", if it does not already; and the plan paper's last line, which says a direct order keeps a force out, is corrected.

## Rule card

Reads: the side's own task forces, their mission, ships' fuel, class figures and place; the side's own intents, limits, priorities and holds; the enemy bases on the side's map, by place alone; the contacts of the area's picture, by place, kind and age; the map's sea paths; the bombers' normal strike radius; the two reused distances and the fuel margins by risk. Writes: a task to operate carriers under a deny or a neutralise, with its hex; a search arc's hexes. Draws no random number. Adds no state.

## Games on file

No closed proof is expected to move: in each, every carrier force is named by an order or has an order of its own, so none is free. That was read for the Coral Sea, the five-day Eastern Solomons, the second proof, the stage 2b proof, scenarios 53 to 57 and both recorded games; one of them rests on a force being named only by a forces limit, which rule 2 counts. Not read: every other scenario, and forces that enter in the middle of the 39 days. The build plays every scenario and recorded game on file before and after. Any that moves is named to the designer before the change merges, and none is restated to make it pass. The doctrine's version rises.

## What it does not do

- **The way a force takes** is the shortest by sea. A way that weighs danger and fuel is decision 0203, to follow, for carriers first and then for convoys.
- It does not know what an enemy base holds, so it cannot tell a dangerous airfield from a harmless one.
- It does not weigh the sightings, bases and waters of a search arc against each other; an arc asked to cover all of them may spread thin.
- It does not use carriers for "hold", "cover" or "keep supplied", or move surface forces under these orders.
- It does not let the bases serve a deny whose forces limit names only task forces. That oddity stays, and is written in the rulebook; changing it would move eleven proof files.

## What it costs

- **The staff commits the scarcest ships a side has without being told to.** The commander's leave, the preview, the hold and his own order are the checks.
- **Every enemy base is feared alike.** On the Guadalcanal map an enemy base lies within 15 hexes of Lunga for both sides, so at preserve neither side's carriers serve a deny of the waters round it. At balanced they go, and stand where they are least deep in danger. That is what preserve means here, and the designer should see it in play.
- **A sea distance from the force to every hex is worked once** when a task is formed, and fuel only for the candidates in the order they are tried. The build measures a day with `sim-cli time` before and after.
- **Rulebook:** chapter 8 (deny, neutralise, the carrier doctrine's station and arc, what may stand under what, the forces limit and its oddity), the glossary ("station", "circle of danger"), and the lines of 0125 and 0184 that say the staff does not choose and does not raid.

## The designer's answers, Nov 21, 2026

1. **Carriers serve both orders, by default,** with the commander's leave as the check.
2. **Their placement is settled by the risk, the enemy known and suspected, and fuel,** and the way they take is to be weighed too; the way as a second step is agreed.
3. **A carrier on the move searches** for contacts and for what threatens it.

**Readings** (the architect's to confirm).

1. *The orders.* A deny or neutralise the commander made (`Maker::Commander`) whose nearest forces limit names no task force and no ship. One whose limit names air groups alone takes free carriers too, as rule 1 reads.
2. *A force with no station is left for the next order.* Rule 3's pass gives a force to an order only when a task is formed; one an order finds no station for (preserve, fuel, no sea route) may be taken by an order after it the same morning.
3. *Rule 5.* The staff withdraws its own carrier task where an intent of another tree, ahead of the order in priority order, is addressed to the force or names it in a forces limit. One behind it does not: the task stands, and the later order's forming of its own says that one stands, as today.
4. *A hold* withdraws the staff's task the next morning, so that, lifted, the force is free again and its station chosen afresh (rule 17). Statement 22's "lifted on day 3" is read as the commander's release at the end of day 3, a command of the file's day 4: a command of day N is taken in before the staff's morning of day N, so a release dated day 3 forms the task on day 3.
5. *Fuel.* The way out is the engine's forecast of the passage at the doctrine's speed (its miles and the force's knots), burnt by the class's figures; the way home is the engine's charged miles at the class's cruise burn; the margin is the risk's on their sum. A force is weighed full where it lies stopped at a base of the side that holds fuel above its reserve and whose port fuels a ship, its home port or not. Candidates are tried for fuel only in the order of the choice, and the first in fuel is taken.
6. *"Cannot meet".* The carrier part of its words is added only where the side has a carrier force with a carrier and a group aboard, so the words of a game with none are as before. A force that is not free is named there and in no reason of its own; a force taken by an order ahead in the pass this morning is named in a reason under each order after it.
7. *The way ahead* is the sea path from the force's hex to its station, as many hexes as a day at the speed it goes covers (the engine's forecast of its knots, times 24 hours, over 40 nm), and the station; only while it is bound for the station, by a route given that morning or one it steams on, and not while it closes or goes home. Its words are "the way ahead to the station (q,r) at (q,r)".
8. *The raid under a neutralise* is the take's routine, given the target: its words say "the base intent:N is to neutralise" where the take's say "the enemy's at the place".
9. *The preview.* `preview` of a deny or a neutralise whose plan waits for a leave adds the proposal's reason (as if released, naming the force and its station) to the decisions to give no order with the change. No key is added, so the agent interface stays at version 38. The dry run plays with the leaves the course gives, so a free carrier force goes in it; whether its report names the station was not checked.
10. *The table.* The draft shows the proposal as "If you approve it", and a plan that would form nothing or cannot meet the order in the enemy's colour; the plan paper asks `propose` where a plan waits and says what approving it would send, or that it would form nothing. The hold is offered on a carrier force's card only, and the card's "In whose hands" says which hold keeps it.
11. *A force with no bomber group aboard* has no station, and the reason says so.
