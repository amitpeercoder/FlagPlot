# 0203: The way a force takes

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 21, 2026 on the designer's word that a force's placement and the path it takes should weigh the risk, the enemy known and suspected, and fuel. Decision 0202 settled the placement; this is the path. It was read once by a reviewer who had not seen it written and amended. The designer then asked to talk about how far a force may go round before anything was built, and on Nov 21, 2026 chose a limit on how late the force arrives and a measure of danger by day, in place of a limit on distance and a count of hexes. This is the plan folded into one statement with that choice in it. It is read again by a fresh reviewer before its hand calculation is written and before any of it is built.

Summary: Every ship takes the shortest way by sea between the points of its route, whatever lies beside it. The staff chooses where a force goes and when, and never how. This has the staff choose the way too, for a carrier force it has stationed. By its risk the force may arrive later than the shortest way would bring it: not at all at press, by one pulse at balanced, by a day at preserve. Among the ways that arrive in that time and are in fuel, the staff takes the one that spends the least time by day inside the enemy's reach, and the deeper inside the worse. It gives the engine that way as the points of a route. Convoys and supply runs are a second stage, since they would move closed proofs.

Standing: Planned on Nov 21, 2026; the designer's choice of limits folded in; waits for its second review.

## Why

What is so today:

- **The engine finds the shortest way by sea between the waypoints of a route** and nothing else: "the path is not stored… a route is still its waypoints" (decision 0017). It does not go round anything.
- **A route can be given point by point,** by a commander at the table or by the staff, and the engine follows the points.
- **The staff gives a destination and a speed.** In every doctrine the way is the engine's. The one exception is a route kept inside an area limit: there the staff searches step by step inside the area and adds the waypoints that hold the engine to that way (decision 0107).
- **The staff can already say where danger lies:** a circle of 15 hexes round each enemy base on its map and of 8 hexes round a fresh contact reported as a carrier (decision 0202), from its own picture. It uses them to choose a carrier's station, to time a supply run's arrival by night (0145) and to turn a lift back (0191). It never uses them to bend a way.
- **A side knows an enemy base by its place and its name only.** It holds no estimate of the aircraft there. So the staff cannot say that one base is more dangerous than another.
- **A day is three pulses:** the night, 18:00 to 06:00, and the morning and the afternoon, six hours each. Aircraft strike in the morning and the afternoon.
- **A deny and a neutralise carry no date.** Nothing in the order says when the carriers must be on station.
- **A detour costs fuel and time** through the miles the engine charges, and the staff can forecast both for a route it means to give.
- **Nearly every staff route in the closed proofs ends inside an enemy base's circle.** A change to the hexes a force passes through moves its meetings, its sightings and the test that turns a lift back.

Why a limit on lateness and not on distance: a commander who sends his carriers round an enemy airfield pays in the hour they arrive. A quarter more distance is nothing on a short passage and a day on a long one, and rests on no source. A pulse is the same on both.

Why danger by day and not a count of hexes: an airfield strikes by day. A force that crosses the rim of a circle in the night is in less danger than one that skirts inside it all afternoon, and a hex beside the airfield is worse than one at the rim.

## The rules

### Whose way is chosen

1. **Stage 1 is for a carrier force going to a station the staff chose** under decision 0202: to the station when the task is formed, and back to it after closing on a contact or after fuelling.
2. **The way home for fuel is the shortest,** as today. A force turns home when it is short, so no longer way home is in fuel.
3. **Closing on a contact takes the shortest way,** as today.
4. **Every other route is as today:** a carrier force with a station of the commander's, a stand, a supply run, a lift, a take, a bombardment. Those are stage 2.

### The speed and the clock

5. **The speed is the one the carrier doctrine's rule gives the shortest way** (cruise at preserve; at balanced cruise where the shortest way is twelve hours or less at cruise, else full). Every way is weighed at that speed and the way chosen is sailed at it. A longer way does not change the speed.
6. **The staff's clock for a way:** the force leaves at the start of the night pulse of the day the route is given, and enters the hex of step n after n times 40 nautical miles at that speed. A hex is entered by day when that hour falls in the morning or the afternoon pulse, and by night otherwise.

### What is weighed

7. **The circles of danger are decision 0202's,** read from the same picture on the morning the route is given: each enemy base on the side's map to 15 hexes, but for the base a neutralise is aimed at; each contact reported as a carrier and three pulses old or less to 8 hexes, but for one inside the waters of the deny the force serves.
8. **The danger of a hex** is how many hexes inside a circle it lies, and one more: 1 at the rim, 16 on an enemy base, 9 on a carrier contact. In more than one circle it is the greatest. Outside every circle it is nothing.
9. **The danger of a way** is the sum of the danger of its hexes entered by day. The hex the force starts in is not counted; the hex it ends in is, where it is entered by day. A hex entered by night adds nothing.

### The limits

10. **Lateness.** A way is within the limit when the engine forecasts its arrival, for the waypoints the staff would give:
    - at press, as the shortest way: the shortest way is taken and nothing is chosen;
    - at balanced, no later than the pulse after the one the shortest way arrives in;
    - at preserve, no later than the same pulse of the day after the shortest way arrives.
11. **Fuel.** A way is in fuel when every ship has the fuel for it at the speed of rule 5 and from its end to the force's home port at cruise, times the margin of the risk, as decision 0202 weighs a station. A force stopped at a base that can fuel it is weighed with its ships full.
12. **Both limits are tested on what the engine forecasts** for the waypoints, not on the staff's clock. The engine charges a clear shortest way by the straight line and a way held by waypoints along its hexes, so the two can differ.

### The choice

13. **Of the ways within both limits, the one of least danger;** then the one of fewest steps.
14. **Ways equal in both** are parted by the engine's order of directions at the first step from the start at which they differ, so the same picture gives the same way.
15. **The search** is over a hex and the steps taken to it, on hexes ships can enter, from the hex the force is in. The steps give the hour by rule 6, and so whether the hex is entered by day. It runs to the most steps the clock of rule 6 puts inside the last pulse rule 10 allows. Where the way it finds fails rule 10 or rule 11 on the engine's forecast, the most steps is lowered by one and it runs again; the shortest way is the last tried.
16. **Where no way is in fuel the shortest is taken,** as today, and the reason says so.

### How the way is given and kept

17. **The staff gives the way as the waypoints of the route:** the fewest points that hold the engine's shortest path to the way chosen. The routine that keeps a route inside an area is given the way's own hexes as the area; inside that set the only path is the way itself, and the farthest hex the engine reaches along it is taken each time. Nothing in the engine changes.
18. **The way is chosen when the route is given and kept.** The staff gives no route to a force that is under way and whose route ends at its station, so the engine's route holds the way. Today the carrier doctrine gives the route afresh every morning; built on that, the bend would be thrown away each day.
19. **The doctrine knows a force is bound for its station by the last waypoint of its route,** not by a route of one point.
20. **The search on the way** (decision 0202, 23) reads the engine's path through the waypoints that remain.

### What the staff says

21. **The reason for a route gives the way and what it costs:** the pulse it arrives in and the pulse the shortest way would; how many of its hexes inside the enemy's reach are entered by day and how many by night, of what, and the nearest it passes to each by day; and the same for the shortest way.
22. **Where the shortest way is taken though it lies in danger by day,** at balanced or preserve, the reason says why: no way that arrives in time is clearer, or none is in fuel.
23. **No figure of danger is shown.** The reason speaks in hexes, pulses and distances.

## Stage 2

24. **The same choice for every route the staff gives:** supply runs, lifts and takes, stands, bombardments, and a carrier force at a station of the commander's.
25. **It moves closed proofs.** Almost every such route in the second proof, the stage 2b proof, the stage 3 proof, the Coral Sea and the five days of the Eastern Solomons ends inside a base's circle, and a way bent on its approach changes what the force meets and sights. Stage 2 is planned after stage 1 is played, with a list of the proofs that move and the designer's word on running them again.
26. **Not every route is built by the one routine.** A stand's route to its station and on to a base, and the covering carriers' way, are built apart. The routine is also used to measure which base is nearest, so bending it would change which base is chosen. The arrival timed by night (0145) and the turn-back test (0191) each work the way out again for themselves, and must be made to read the way actually given.
27. **The station and the way chosen together.** Decision 0202 ranks stations by the shortest way to each, so a station whose shortest way crosses a circle may be chosen over one whose way is clear. Stage 1 leaves that as it is. In stage 2 the same search gives the best way to every candidate at once.
28. **Long passages with their fuelling** (decision 0201, stage D) are planned with stage 2: a way and the fuellings on it are one plan.

## Games on file

One scenario has such a force: scenario 116, decision 0202's own. Where a circle lies across the shortest way to the station it chose, as in its statement 6, the way may now bend and that scenario's events move. Its statements name stations and not ways, and are checked to hold. No other game on file has a carrier force stationed by the staff, and none of those may move. The build plays every scenario and recorded game before and after. The doctrine's version rises.

## What it does not do

Each of these was weighed with the designer and left for later. None undoes stage 1 when it comes.

- **It does not know what an enemy base holds.** A side has no estimate of the aircraft at a base, so every base's circle is the same. That needs a belief the game does not have yet.
- **A report does not fade.** A carrier contact casts its full circle to three pulses old and none after, as decision 0202 has it, so the station and the way read the same danger. A circle that widens and weakens with age is a change to both and is taken up for both together.
- **It does not tell being seen from being struck.** Enemy search reaches further than enemy strikes, and surprise is lost at the outer circle. A second circle for search is planned with Midway (decision 0198), which turns on who is seen first.
- **It reads no time from the operation.** A deny and a neutralise carry no date. When carriers cover a landing, the time they must be on station comes from the landing, and replaces rule 10.
- **It does not change speed or wait to cross by night.** The force leaves when the route is given and keeps one speed. Night helps only where the way's own timing puts its hexes in danger in the dark.
- **It does not weigh the way out,** the side's own aircraft overhead on the way, narrow waters, or fuel to fight on station beyond decision 0202's margin.
- **It does not re-route a force already under way** when a new contact appears; turning a lift back (0191) is unchanged.
- **It does not change the engine's pathfinding** or how a commander's own route is followed.

## What it costs

- **A search over hexes and steps each time such a route is given,** and an engine forecast for each way it tries. The map has 1,600 hexes and a way is some tens of steps. The build measures a day with `sim-cli time` before and after.
- **Later arrivals** for a cautious commander, and more fuel.
- **Rulebook:** chapter 8 (the carrier doctrine's routes, the routine that builds a route), the glossary ("way", "danger by day"), and decision 0017's line that a route is its waypoints gains a pointer here.

## What the designer decided

On Nov 21, 2026:

1. **The limit is on how late the force arrives, not on distance.** One pulse at balanced and one day at preserve, to be tuned after games. This replaces the plan's first figures, a quarter longer and twice as long.
2. **Danger is measured by day and by depth.** This replaces the count of hexes in a circle, and brings in what a night crossing is worth without a rule of its own.
3. **Fuel stays the hard bound under both.**
4. **The search circle comes with Midway.** Speed, the side's own air cover, the way out and narrow waters come later.

Still the designer's: stage 2 will move closed proofs. The architect builds stage 1, plays it, and brings the list of proofs that would move before stage 2 is built.

## How the plan changed

- **First review, Nov 21, 2026.** The way is kept by the engine's route and the staff gives no route to a force under way (rules 18 and 19); the limits are tested on the miles the engine charges (rule 12); the way home and closing stay the shortest (rules 2 and 3); the area routine holds any way (rule 17); scenario 116 moves; stage 2 is not one routine (rule 26).
- **The designer's choice, Nov 21, 2026.** Rules 5 to 10, 13, 15 and 21 to 23 are new or rewritten. The first plan weighed each way at the speed the doctrine's rule gave that way; with a limit on lateness the speed is set once from the shortest way (rule 5), so that a longer way cannot arrive sooner by being sailed faster.
