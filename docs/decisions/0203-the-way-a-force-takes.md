# 0203: The way a force takes

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 21, 2026 on the designer's word that a force's placement and the path it takes should weigh the risk, the enemy known and suspected, and fuel. Decision 0202 settled the placement; this is the path. It was read once by a reviewer who had not seen it written and amended. The designer then asked to talk about how far a force may go round before anything was built, and on Nov 21, 2026 chose a limit on how late the force arrives and a measure of danger by day, in place of a limit on distance and a count of hexes. That choice was folded in and the whole read again by a fresh reviewer; this is the plan with that second reading folded in too. Its hand calculation is written next, and then it is built.

Summary: Every ship takes the shortest way by sea between the points of its route, whatever lies beside it. The staff chooses where a force goes and when, and never how. This has the staff choose the way too, for a carrier force it has stationed. By its risk the force may arrive later than the shortest way would bring it: not at all at press, by one pulse at balanced, by a day at preserve. Among the ways that arrive in that time and are in fuel, the staff takes the one that spends the least time by day inside the enemy's reach, and the deeper inside the worse. It gives the engine that way as the points of a route, and it is the engine's own forecast of that route that is weighed. Convoys and supply runs are a second stage, since they would move closed proofs.

Standing: Planned on Nov 21, 2026; the designer's choice of limits and two reviews folded in; waits for its hand calculation and its build.

## Why

What is so today:

- **The engine finds the shortest way by sea between the waypoints of a route** and nothing else: "the path is not stored… a route is still its waypoints" (decision 0017). It does not go round anything.
- **A route can be given point by point,** by a commander at the table or by the staff, and the engine follows the points.
- **The staff gives a destination and a speed.** In every doctrine the way is the engine's. The one exception is a route kept inside an area limit: there the staff searches step by step inside the area and adds the waypoints that hold the engine to that way (decision 0107).
- **The engine enters a hex halfway along the step into it,** and charges a run with no land in the way by the straight line, so a step off the lines of the grid costs less than 40 nautical miles.
- **The staff can already say where danger lies:** a circle of 15 hexes round each enemy base on its map and of 8 hexes round a fresh contact reported as a carrier (decision 0202), from its own picture. It uses them to choose a carrier's station, to time a supply run's arrival by night (0145) and to turn a lift back (0191). It never uses them to bend a way.
- **A side knows an enemy base by its place and its name only.** It holds no estimate of the aircraft there. So the staff cannot say that one base is more dangerous than another.
- **A day is three pulses:** the night, 18:00 to 06:00, and the morning and the afternoon, six hours each. Aircraft strike in the morning and the afternoon.
- **A deny and a neutralise carry no date.** Nothing in the order says when the carriers must be on station.
- **A detour costs fuel and time** through the miles the engine charges, and the staff can forecast both for a route it means to give.
- **Nearly every staff route in the closed proofs ends inside an enemy base's circle.** A change to the hexes a force passes through moves its meetings, its sightings and the test that turns a lift back.

Why a limit on lateness and not on distance: a commander who sends his carriers round an enemy airfield pays in the hour they arrive. A quarter more distance is nothing on a short passage and a day on a long one, and rests on no source. A pulse is the same on both.

Why danger by day and not a count of hexes: an airfield strikes by day. A force that crosses the rim of a circle in the night is in less danger than one that skirts inside it all afternoon, and a hex beside the airfield is worse than one at the rim.

## The rules

### Whose way is chosen

1. **Stage 1 is for a carrier force going to a station the staff chose** under decision 0202, at balanced or preserve: to the station when the task is formed, and back to it after closing on a contact or after fuelling.
2. **At press nothing changes.** The shortest way is taken and the doctrine routes the force as it does today.
3. **The way home for fuel is the shortest,** as today. A force turns home when it is short, so no longer way home is in fuel.
4. **Closing on a contact takes the shortest way,** as today.
5. **Every other route is as today:** a carrier force with a station of the commander's, a stand, a supply run, a lift, a take, a bombardment. Those are stage 2.
6. **Where the shortest way does not arrive** by the engine's forecast (the order refused, no ship able to move, a ship run dry), nothing is chosen and the doctrine does as today.

### The speed and the two clocks

7. **The speed is the one the carrier doctrine's rule gives the shortest way** on the morning the route is given (cruise at preserve; at balanced cruise where the shortest way is twelve hours or less at cruise, else full). Every way is weighed at that speed. A longer way does not change it.
8. **The engine's clock decides.** The forecast of a route reports, for each hex the force enters, the pulse it enters it in. A hex is entered by day when that pulse is the morning or the afternoon. This is a report of what the movement rules already do; it adds no state and changes no rule.
9. **The staff's clock only proposes, and it gives each hex two hours.** In the search of rule 17 the force leaves at the start of the night pulse of the day the route is given. The latest it enters the hex of step n is after n times 40 nautical miles less 20, at the speed: the engine enters a hex halfway along the step into it. The earliest is after the straight line from the start to that hex less 20 nautical miles: the engine charges a clear run by the straight line, so it can be ahead of the count of steps and never ahead of this. A hex counts as entered by night only where both hours fall in the same night; otherwise by day. An hour on the line between two pulses belongs to the earlier, as in the engine. Nothing is decided on the staff's clock.

### What is weighed

10. **The circles of danger are decision 0202's, worked out by the same routine** from the area's picture on the morning the route is given, also for a way given after closing or fuelling: each enemy base on the side's map to 15 hexes, the base to be neutralised among them; each contact reported as a carrier and three pulses old or less to 8 hexes, but for one inside the waters of the deny the force serves.
11. **The danger of a hex** is how many hexes inside a circle it lies, and one more: 1 at the rim, 16 on an enemy base, 9 on a carrier contact. In more than one circle it is the greatest. Outside every circle it is nothing.
12. **The danger of a way** is the sum of the danger of the hexes the engine's forecast has the force enter by day, but for the last. The station's own danger is decision 0202's to weigh and is the same for every way. A hex entered by night adds nothing.

### The limits

13. **Lateness.** A way is within the limit when the engine forecasts its arrival:
    - at balanced, no later than the pulse after the one the shortest way arrives in;
    - at preserve, no later than the same pulse of the day after the shortest way arrives.
14. **Fuel.** A way is in fuel by decision 0202's test for a station, made on the way's own miles: every ship has the fuel for the way at the speed of rule 7 and from its end to the force's home port at cruise, times the margin of the risk. A force stopped at a base that can fuel it is weighed with its ships full. The doctrine's own test of whether a fuelled force goes back to its station at all is unchanged and comes first.
15. **Both limits are read from the engine's forecast** of the waypoints the staff would give.

### The choice

16. **The shortest way** is the engine's own: a route of one point, the station.
17. **The search proposes ways.** For each number of steps from the shortest way's to the most the staff's clock puts inside the last pulse rule 13 allows, it finds the way of that many steps or fewer with the least danger by the staff's clock, then the fewest steps.
    - It runs on the steps ships can take, as the engine tests a step, from the hex the engine's forecast starts the force from.
    - It is worked backward from the station: for each hex and each count of steps taken, the least danger still to come. The way is then built forward from the start, taking at each step the first direction in the engine's order that keeps the least. So a way of no danger is the engine's own, and the same picture gives the same way.
    - A proposal may pass a hex twice or double back; rule 18 takes that out.
18. **Each proposal is made a route:** the waypoints that hold the engine to the shortest path inside the proposal's own hexes, by the routine that keeps a route inside an area. Proposals that give the same waypoints are one.
19. **Each route is forecast by the engine,** and its danger, its arrival and its fuel are read from that forecast. What is weighed is the path the engine would steam, which need not be the proposal hex for hex.
20. **Of the routes within both limits, with the shortest way counted among them, the one of least danger is taken;** then the fewest miles charged; then the fewest waypoints; then the proposal of fewest steps.
21. **Where no route is in fuel the shortest way is taken,** as today, and the reason says so.
22. **A longer way that brings the force to a circle after dark is a way like any other.** The staff does not stop the force or slow it to wait for the night, but among the ways that arrive in time it may take one that is longer for that reason.

### How the way is given and kept

23. **Where the shortest way is the one taken, the doctrine routes the force as it does today,** each morning, with the speed its rule gives that morning.
24. **A bent way is given once, with the speed of rule 7, and kept.** While the force is under way on a route that ends at its station, the staff gives it no route and no speed. The engine's route holds the way. A force that is stopped, or whose route ends elsewhere, is routed afresh and the way chosen afresh.
25. **The doctrine knows a force is bound for its station by the last waypoint of its route,** on the morning the route is given as on the days after, and its search on the way (decision 0202, 23) reads the engine's path through the waypoints that remain.
26. **A kept way arrives when the forecast said. The shortest way, routed day by day, may arrive later than its forecast,** because the doctrine drops a force from full speed to cruise when twelve hours or less remain. The limit of rule 13 is read against the forecast and is a little stricter for it. That is known.

### What the staff says

27. **The reason for a route gives the way and what it costs:** the pulse it arrives in and the pulse the shortest way would; how many of its hexes inside the enemy's reach are entered by day and how many by night, of what, and the nearest it passes to each by day; and the same for the shortest way.
28. **Where the shortest way is taken though it lies in danger by day,** the reason says why: no way that arrives in time is clearer, or none is in fuel.
29. **No figure of danger is shown.** The reason speaks in hexes, pulses and distances.

## Stage 2

30. **The same choice for every route the staff gives:** supply runs, lifts and takes, stands, bombardments, and a carrier force at a station of the commander's.
31. **It moves closed proofs.** Almost every such route in the second proof, the stage 2b proof, the stage 3 proof, the Coral Sea and the five days of the Eastern Solomons ends inside a base's circle, and a way bent on its approach changes what the force meets and sights. Stage 2 is planned after stage 1 is played, with a list of the proofs that move and the designer's word on running them again.
32. **Not every route is built by the one routine.** A stand's route to its station and on to a base, and the covering carriers' way, are built apart. The routine is also used to measure which base is nearest, so bending it would change which base is chosen. The arrival timed by night (0145) and the turn-back test (0191) each work the way out again for themselves, and must be made to read the way actually given.
33. **The station and the way chosen together.** Decision 0202 ranks stations by the shortest way to each, so a station whose shortest way crosses a circle may be chosen over one whose way is clear. Stage 1 leaves that as it is. In stage 2 the same search gives the best way to every candidate at once.
34. **Long passages with their fuelling** (decision 0201, stage D) are planned with stage 2: a way and the fuellings on it are one plan.

## Games on file

One scenario has such a force: scenario 116, decision 0202's own. Where a circle lies across the shortest way to the station it chose, as in its statement 6, the way may now bend, and a bent way keeps its first speed where today the speed is set again each morning; that scenario's events move. Its statements name stations and not ways, and are checked to hold. No other game on file has a carrier force stationed by the staff, and none of those may move. The build plays every scenario and recorded game before and after. The doctrine's version rises.

## What it does not do

Each of these was weighed with the designer and left for later. None undoes stage 1 when it comes.

- **It does not know what an enemy base holds.** A side has no estimate of the aircraft at a base, so every base's circle is the same. That needs a belief the game does not have yet.
- **A report does not fade.** A carrier contact casts its full circle to three pulses old and none after, as decision 0202 has it, so the station and the way read the same danger. A circle that widens and weakens with age is a change to both and is taken up for both together.
- **It does not tell being seen from being struck.** Enemy search reaches further than enemy strikes, and surprise is lost at the outer circle. A second circle for search is planned with Midway (decision 0198), which turns on who is seen first.
- **It reads no time from the operation.** A deny and a neutralise carry no date. When carriers cover a landing, the time they must be on station comes from the landing, and replaces rule 13.
- **It does not stop, slow or hold a force to cross by night.** The force leaves when the route is given and keeps one speed. Night helps only where a way's own length puts its hexes in danger in the dark (rule 22).
- **It does not count a force that lies in a hex from night into day.** A hex is weighed by the pulse it is entered in.
- **It does not weigh the way out,** the side's own aircraft overhead on the way, narrow waters, or fuel to fight on station beyond decision 0202's margin.
- **It does not re-route a force already under way** when a new contact appears; turning a lift back (0191) is unchanged.
- **It does not change the engine's pathfinding** or how a commander's own route is followed.

## What it costs

- **A search over hexes and steps each time such a route is given,** and for each proposal a set of waypoints and an engine forecast: about five proposals at balanced and up to twenty at preserve. The search is small. The routine that makes the waypoints is not: it asks the engine for a path many times for each way. The build measures a day with `sim-cli time` before and after, and comes back to the architect if a day with such a route takes more than a tenth longer.
- **Later arrivals** for a cautious commander, and more fuel.
- **Rulebook:** chapter 8 (the carrier doctrine's routes, the routine that builds a route), the glossary ("way", "danger by day"), and decision 0017's line that a route is its waypoints gains a pointer here.

## What the designer decided

On Nov 21, 2026:

1. **The limit is on how late the force arrives, not on distance.** One pulse at balanced and one day at preserve, to be tuned after games. This replaces the plan's first figures, a quarter longer and twice as long.
2. **Danger is measured by day and by depth.** This replaces the count of hexes in a circle, and brings in what a night crossing is worth without a rule of its own.
3. **Fuel stays the hard bound under both.**
4. **The search circle comes with Midway.** Speed, the side's own air cover, the way out and narrow waters come later.

Still the designer's: stage 2 will move closed proofs. The architect builds stage 1, plays it, and brings the list of proofs that would move before stage 2 is built.

## How the plan changed

- **First review, Nov 21, 2026.** The way is kept by the engine's route and the staff gives no route to a force under way; the limits are tested on what the engine charges; the way home and closing stay the shortest; scenario 116 moves; stage 2 is not one routine.
- **The designer's choice, Nov 21, 2026.** The limit on lateness, danger by day and by depth, and the reason's words. The first plan weighed each way at the speed the doctrine's rule gave that way; with a limit on lateness the speed is set once from the shortest way (rule 7), so that a longer way cannot arrive sooner by being sailed faster.
- **Second review, Nov 21, 2026,** by a reviewer who had not seen the plan, against the movement rules, the carrier doctrine, the station routine and the area routine. Each finding was checked against the source before it was taken.
  - The staff's clock is not the engine's: the engine enters a hex halfway along a step and charges a clear run by the straight line, between waypoints too. So danger is read from the engine's forecast, which gains the pulse of each hex, and the staff's clock only proposes (rules 8, 9 and 19).
  - A search by day and night can double back to wait for dark, and the area routine cannot hold such a way: it takes the shortest path inside the hexes it is given. So the search proposes, the routine makes a route, and the engine's path is what is weighed (rules 17 to 20, 22).
  - The base to be neutralised does cast its circle (decision 0202, 9); the first folding said it did not (rule 10).
  - A kept way keeps its speed, which is a change from today, so it is kept only where the way bends, and press and an unbent way are exactly as today (rules 2, 23, 24, 26).
  - The tie between equal ways fixes how the search is built (rule 17). The station's own hex is left out of the danger (rule 12). The picture is the area's (rule 10). The search starts where the forecast starts the force (rule 17). The shortest way that does not arrive (rule 6). Which fuel test decides (rule 14). The way ahead on the morning a bent route is given (rule 25). What the waypoint routine costs.
- **The hand calculation, Nov 21, 2026.** Working scenario 117 by hand showed that a clock of 40 nautical miles a step runs behind the engine off the lines of the grid: a way the staff took to cross a circle's rim in the dark was entered by the engine in the afternoon. The staff's clock now gives each hex an earliest and a latest hour and counts it by night only where both are in one night (rule 9).
