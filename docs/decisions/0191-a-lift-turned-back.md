# 0191: A lift turned back

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 20, 2026, on the designer's word (decision 0188, "Confirmed", 5: to be planned after the Coral Sea's first run). Read by a reviewer who had not seen it written, and amended on that reading ("Amended on review", which replaces what is above it where they differ) before any of it is built.

Summary: A staff that has sent a convoy of men to take a place never calls it back. It sails on after its escort is sunk and with enemy carriers in reach. This gives the staff a rule to turn such a lift back while it is threatened and to send it on again when the threat has passed: always at preserve the force, only once it has been hurt at balanced, and never at press.

Standing: Planned on Nov 20, 2026, amended on review, amended in the build (an order to do no cargo work) and built on Nov 20, 2026 ("As built"); the readings marked there are the architect's to confirm.

## Why

Scenario 105 (decision 0188). On 7 May 1942 the Port Moresby convoy was turned back when its covering carrier was sunk and American carriers were known to be near, and it never landed. In 1,000 games of staff against staff the game's convoy landed whole in 978, in most of them after Shoho and its own flagship had been sunk.

What is so today, from the rulebook (chapter 8):

- **A take's lift,** once routed to its place, "is given nothing more". No doctrine recalls it. Only a commander can withdraw the intent and order the force home.
- **A threat** is already a thing a staff reads: a contact of ships the side holds within 15 hexes of where a run is going, read on the morning the run is routed. At preserve the force a threatened run with no escort is held in port; at balanced it sails; at press it sails.
- **A lift is timed to arrive by night** where an enemy base is near, at preserve the force and at balanced.
- **A carrier force that has closed on a contact goes back to its station** on a morning it holds none: a staff already reads the contacts each morning and routes a force one way or the other with no memory of its own.

## The rules

1. **Which forces.** A lift of a "take a place" whose plan is released, under way with men aboard and not yet landing. Nothing else: not a supply run, not a lift that has begun to land, not a force a commander holds.
2. **The danger, read each morning** from what the side knows: a fresh contact of ships (no more than 3 pulses old) reported as a carrier, within 15 hexes of the lift's hex or of any hex left on its route. The figures are the staff's existing ones for a fresh contact and for a threat.
3. **Hurt.** A lift has been hurt when, since its plan was released, a ship of the lift's force has been sunk or put out of action, or a carrier of a force formed under the same "take a place" has been. The staff reads this from the side's own events.
4. **By the risk read up the tree:**

   | | Preserve the force | Balanced | Press |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | The lift turns back | On the danger | On the danger, once it has been hurt | Never |

5. **Turning back.** The lift is routed to its source (the base its way back is reckoned to, as today), at its cruise speed. Its men stay aboard. The plan stays released and the intent in force.
6. **Going on again.** On a later morning with no danger, a lift that was turned back is routed to its place again from where it is, timed as a lift is. One that has reached its source waits there and sails again on such a morning.
7. **No memory.** The staff keeps no state for this. A lift is "turned back" when it is under way with men aboard and its route ends at its source and not at its place. Each morning is decided from that, the contacts and the events.
8. **Reasons.** The order that turns a lift back gives the contact, its kind, its age and its distance, the risk, and at balanced what has been lost. The order that sends it on says the danger has passed and gives the newest carrier contact and its age, or that none is held. On a morning a threatened lift is not turned back the staff says why: press; or at balanced, not yet hurt.
9. **A notice.** A lift turned back hands in the notice `lift_turned_back`, which the standing interrupts may stop the clock on, so that a commander at a seat is told and may overrule: by a risk of press on the intent, or by an order of his own.
10. **A commander's order still holds the force,** as today, and a held lift is given nothing by this rule.

## Rule card

- **Reads:** the plan and the lift of a "take a place"; the lift's hex and route; the contacts of ships the side holds, their kind and age; the side's own events of ships sunk and out of action; the risk on the chain.
- **Writes:** nothing but the staff's own orders (a route), its reasons and a notice.
- **Reuses:** the threat of 15 hexes and a fresh contact of 3 pulses; a lift's source; the way a carrier force is routed out and back with no state; the table by risk; notices and standing interrupts.
- **New:** one doctrine step for a take's lift, and one kind of notice. No state, no draw.

## What it costs

- **Games with a take change** where a carrier contact is held near a lift: the stage 3 proof's scenarios and the 39 days are checked, and any daily hash that moves is reported with the reason before the golden files are remade. The rules label rises.
- **A lift may now never land,** turning back and going on until the game ends. That is the rule working; a commander who wants it pressed sets press.
- **It rests on what is reported as a carrier.** A search that calls carriers battleships, as happened in scenario 105's games, turns nothing back. That is the fog, and is left.
- **Rulebook:** chapter 8 (Taking a place) and the glossary; the agent interface's notice kinds and its version.

## Scenario 105

11. **Nothing in its orders changes.** The Japanese take is at balanced. The run of 1,000 games is made again with this rule and decision 0190's together, and reported beside the first: how often the convoy turns back, goes on again, and lands.

## Questions for the designer

1. **At balanced, hurt first.** The architect's choice is that a balanced staff does not turn back on a sighting alone, only once it has lost a ship of the convoy or its covering carrier, as happened on 7 May. A more careful reading would turn back on the sighting, which is what preserve the force does.

## Amended on review

A reviewer who had not seen the plan written read it against the rules and the code of the take and played scenario 105 on forty seeds. Six of his findings were faults that would have broken the rule. Found right: that no doctrine recalls a lift today; the 15 hexes of a threat and the 3 pulses of a fresh contact; that a lift's source is known when it began at sea; that men aboard do not eat; that everything is read from the side's own view.

### Faults, mended

12. **Every lift's route ends at its source,** since a lift is routed to the place and home again. Rule 7's test is replaced: a lift is turned back when it is under way with men aboard and the place is no longer a hex it is to act at on the route it has.
13. **Men would have gone ashore at the source.** A lift keeps its order to unload and works it at any base of its side. Turning back takes that order away; going on gives it again. A lift turned back that reaches its source lies there with its men aboard.
14. **The take already sends a lift on.** A lift that is not routed to its place is given its route there by the take's own step each morning. So rule 6 is today's doctrine and no new one. This rule is placed before that step and stops it on a morning of danger. On a morning with none, the take's step routes the lift on and gives it its order to unload, timed as a lift is.
15. **The covering carrier.** "A force formed under the same take" misses the case it was written for: in scenario 105 Shoho's force has an intent of its own. Rule 3 is replaced: a lift has been hurt when, since its plan was released, a ship of the lift's own force has been sunk, or a ship of a force keeping station on it as its escort, or any carrier of the side. The staff reads each from the side's own events, which name the ship and her force.
16. **"Put out of action" is dropped.** A staff has no reading of it but the umpire's. Hurt is ships sunk.
17. **The way still to go.** The danger is read against the hexes of the engine's sea route from the lift's hex to the place, each hex of the path and not only its corners, and not against the way home.

### The danger, tightened

18. **Eight hexes, not fifteen.** Fifteen hexes is 600 nm, and in a sea this size a carrier is nearly always within it. The danger's distance is 8 hexes (320 nm), a placeholder of the staff's: an aircraft's reach and a night's steaming toward the lift. It is a figure of this rule and not the supply run's threat, which is read at a destination; the record says so because two figures now answer two like questions.
19. **Two clear mornings.** A contact's reported kind changes with each report and the newest stands: one force was a carrier, a transport, a carrier and a battleship in one afternoon. A lift turned back is sent on only after two mornings running with no danger. The staff still keeps no state: it reads the second morning from the contacts' ages (no contact that makes the danger has been reported in the last 6 pulses).
20. **Before it first sails.** At preserve the force a lift is not released to sail on a morning of danger: it is held, as a threatened supply run with no escort is, and the reason says so. At balanced and at press it sails.

### The notice

21. **A new standing interrupt goes with the notice,** and that is command state, in the hash. The rule card's "no state" is corrected: the doctrine keeps none; the commander's switch for the interrupt is one more of a kind there are four of. The notice is handed in on the morning a lift is turned back and not on the mornings it is kept back.
22. **Under an island** a take's risk cannot be set bolder than the island's; a commander overrules there by setting press on the island's intent, or by an order of his own.

### The worked example and the costs

23. **The statements are corrected by the architect before the build:** the covering carrier is sunk and not put out of action; a lift at its source keeps its men aboard because its order to unload is taken away; going on again is on the second clear morning, with the pulse given; the distances are against 8 hexes. Three properties are added: turned back at press implies at balanced implies at preserve the force; an older or a farther contact never makes a danger a newer or nearer one did not; the men aboard are the same after a turning back and a going on.
24. **Games that move:** those with a take and a carrier contact held near a lift. The reviewer names scenarios 67 to 69, 72 to 74, 76, 77, 80 to 83, 86 and 90, the stage 3 proof's three files and the Coral Sea's. Each is checked and any hash that moves is reported with its reason. The trial and the window play staffs forward and change with it. The agent interface gains the notice and the interrupt; the table and `docs/commands.md` name them.

### What it will and will not do for scenario 105, said before any run

25. **The reviewer's estimate, from forty games:** at balanced the convoy would be turned back on about the third morning in most games, having lost Yubari to the support group, and could not land before the fifth day ends. He expects it to land in a quarter to a third of games where it lands in 98 of 100 today, and the Allied side then to be ahead in most.
26. **That is not a contest by either staff's choosing.** It would turn on whether the support group sinks the convoy's cruiser in the night, on what the newest report calls a carrier, and on a game too short for a lift turned back to try again. The rule is built because a staff should not sail a convoy on blind, and it serves every scenario with a landing. What it does to scenario 105 is run and put to the designer; the scenario's length and the danger's figures are not then moved to make the band.

## Amended in the build: an order to do no cargo work

27. **Why.** Rule 13 asks that turning back take a lift's order to unload away. No order could: every load order is refused while a force is under way, and none clears a cargo order, so a lift turned back kept `unload` and landed its men at its source. The architect ruled on Nov 20, 2026 that a new task force order is built for it.
28. **The order.** `no_cargo_work`, with no arguments: "do no cargo work". It is accepted for a force of the side whatever its status (stopped, under way, halted, out of fuel); only the checks every task force order has (the force is the side's) apply. Taking it in sets the force's cargo order to none. It moves no cargo and no man. A force halted at a marked waypoint for its cargo alone has then no cargo work left and steams on at the next pulse's movement, as when its cargo order is complete.
29. **Its place.** A task force order added at the end of the order kinds, never renumbering: the agent's order code 18. The hash walks no order code, and the force's cargo order was already hashed, so the state's layout is raised only for decision 0191's standing interrupt.
30. **Who sees it.** The side's own `OrderAccepted` or `OrderRejected`, as for any task force order. The enemy sees nothing. A commander's `no_cargo_work` is an order of his own and holds the force from the staff, as his other cargo orders do.
31. **Rule card.** Reads: the force. Writes: its cargo order.
32. **The staff** gives it to a lift it turns back, with the turning back's reason; the take's own step gives the unload order again when it sends the lift on.

**Readings made in the build** (the architect's to confirm):

1. **Which lift.** Only the first lift, the force of the take's "ready a landing", before any man of its units is ashore at the place. Later lifts (a second lift, lifts as they stand) and a hold's lifts are not read.
2. **Under way** is the force's status under way. A lift halted at the place with men aboard, or landing, is not read (statement 14: a landing in this engine is begun and done within a pulse, so the "landing" test is met by a lift halted at the place).
3. **The way to the place** is the force's hex and each hex of the engine's sea path along the route the take would give it now from there (`route_between`, in the take's area), whether the lift is on its way or turned back.
4. **The contact that makes the danger** is the nearest, then the newest, then the lowest numbered. The picture read is that of the take's addressee, as for a run's threat.
5. **Hurt** is read from the plan's last `PlanStateChanged` to released; with none in the side's events, from the first event. Escorts are the forces whose charge is the lift now and those named by a `StationKept` on it since the release. It is read only at balanced, the one risk it changes. A ship lost with a base (`ShipLostWithBase`) is not sunk and does not count.
6. **The source** a lift is turned back to is the end's source as its forces limit binds (where it came from), not the source of a hold a landed lift goes on to.
7. **Two clear mornings.** A contact that makes the danger "reported in the last 6 pulses" is one whose age is 6 or less. A lift is turned back when it is under way with men aboard and the place is not ahead on its route; such a lift is kept back while such a contact is held and the risk would turn it.
8. **Before it sails** (item 20) uses the same test as the keeping back: a first lift with men aboard is held at its passage where a contact that makes the danger was reported in the last 6 pulses and the risk would turn it. This is stricter than item 20 in two cases, at preserve the force on the first clear morning after a danger and at balanced for a lift already hurt; with no state a lift at its source that was turned back cannot be told from one about to sail first, and statement 11 asks that the first waits. Recorded in `docs/rules/differences.md`, row 46.
9. **The going-on words.** The figures drop a contact before it is 6 pulses old, so on the morning a lift goes on no contact of the danger is held. The reason says the danger has passed for two mornings where a carrier contact is held near the way, or where the side's events hold a `lift_turned_back` notice for the plan and the lift is not on its way: reading a notice already handed in is reading the side's events, not a state of the staff's.
10. **The notice rests on a reason for giving no order** handed in beside the orders, since a notice can rest only on a command or such a reason.
11. **A held lift** is answered by this rule only on a morning it would turn or keep the lift back; otherwise the take says, as before, that its orders stand or that it is held.
12. **Speed.** Cruise is ordered only where the force has another setting.

**The tests.** Every statement (1 to 17 and 11a) and every property of scenario 107 has a test; all 25 pass and none is ignored. Statement 14's test is weak, by reading 2. The builder added one command to the scenario, `knowledge = none`, without which a take at preserve the force never released its plan for want of a count of the place. Statement 9's contact is 12 hexes from the lift's hex and 5 from a hex of the path; on this map it is also 5 from the place, since on a hex grid a point 5 from the middle of a straight 12-hex leg is that near its end too. Rule 15's escort path (a ship of a force keeping station on the lift) has no test: no statement covers it.

**What it moved.** Every scenario under `data/scenarios` (158 files, the step 2 frozen copies left out) was run with the rule and without it on the same build, and the output of `run --events` compared. One game moved: the Coral Sea, `coral-sea/01a_coral_sea.toml` (staff against staff). On day 3, at balanced, the Japanese convoy (task force 5), under way to (-17,-4) with 5,386 men aboard, is turned back to Rabaul: contact 5, reported as a carrier, 0 pulses old, 5 hexes from its way, and Yubari of the convoy and Shoho, a carrier of the side, were sunk on day 2. On day 4 it is kept back (contact 6, a carrier, 3 hexes from the way); on day 5 it is sent on again and the game ends before it lands. The Japanese side lands no man and the result turns from a decisive Japanese victory (margin 42.46) to a minor Allied victory (margin 11.2). The stage 3 proof's slices, the 39 days and every other scenario with a take (67 to 69, 72 to 74, 76, 77, 80 to 83, 86, 90, the Coral Sea's 01b) did not move: no carrier contact is held near a lift's way. Each test that builds variants of its own was run in the full test run (below).

**Not done.** Later lifts and a hold's lifts are not turned back (reading 1). A lift turned back that goes on from the sea and needs more supply than it has aboard is given a load it cannot take under way, as today for any lift not at its source. Scenario 105's 1,000 games are the architect's to run.
