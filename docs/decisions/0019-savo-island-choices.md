# 0019: Savo Island, the designer's choices

Status: confirmed by the designer, Nov 10, 2026, with the stage 4c plan.

Summary: Savo Island: who was where, standard displacement, and unknown radar treated as not fitted

Standing: Confirmed

## The gap

The sources disagree about who was where on the night of 8 to 9 August 1942, and are silent on some equipment. Decision 0015 says a person settles such things, not a source's grade. The order-of-battle research set out the options in `docs/research/oob/savo-island-choices.md` as proposals. These are the designer's answers.

## Decisions

1. **HMAS Australia is absent.** She had left the southern group for the transport area before the action. USS Chicago leads the southern group and gives no orders.
2. **USS Jarvis is alone, damaged and steaming west.** She is a force of one ship that the Japanese column sights and does not attack. She takes no part in the fighting.
3. **USS Wilson is in the northern group,** in Jarvis's place. USS Hull is in the transport screen. HMAS Hobart is with USS San Juan in the eastern group. None of the eastern group or the transport screens fights.
4. **How much damage a ship can take is measured by its standard displacement,** one figure for each class.
5. **Where no source says whether a ship carried radar on that date, the scenario treats it as not fitted.** This is a working assumption, named in the scenario file ship by ship, and is not a fact. It applies to Bagley, Patterson, Helm, Wilson, Jarvis, Vincennes and Canberra until a source is found. The facts files record these as unknown. The designer left the membership of this list to the architect on Nov 10, 2026. Jarvis and Vincennes were added that day, when the derivation tool showed the facts hold no search radar row for either: Vincennes has fire-control radar rows only. Canberra stays on the list until her fit is settled in the facts, after which the tool reads the fact and the list no longer applies to her.

## Why

- **Choices 1 to 3** follow the Navy's 1943 narrative where it and the 1950 Naval War College analysis differ on placement, as the research recommends, and they match what both agree happened: the southern group fought without its flag officer, and Jarvis was passed.
- **Choice 4:** standard displacement is the one measure sourced for every class involved. What each source's figure actually is, where it is not quite the standard measure, is recorded on the fact rows.
- **Choice 5:** the combat rule needs a yes or no. "Not fitted" is the cautious reading for a sighting bonus: it gives no advantage that no source supports. Chicago, Astoria and both pickets have a sourced search radar, so each Allied group that fought has at least one ship with it.

## What this changes

Who fights whom: the southern group is Canberra, Chicago, Bagley and Patterson. The northern group is Vincennes, Quincy, Astoria, Helm and Wilson. The pickets are Blue and Ralph Talbot. The Japanese column is Chokai, Aoba, Kako, Kinugasa, Furutaka, Tenryu, Yubari and Yunagi.

## Open

- A torpedo type for Kinugasa, Tenryu, Yubari and Yunagi, the gun mark of Furutaka and Kako, and the ammunition every ship carried are facts still being sourced. They are not choices, and are not settled here.
