# 0184: The Eastern Solomons over five days: a small scenario two commanders can both win. A plan for the designer

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 20, 2026, at the designer's asking, for his confirmation. Nothing is built. It is to be read by a reviewer who has not seen it written, and amended, before it is put to him.

Summary: The 39 days are lopsided: in seven games between agents the Allied side won all seven decisively, because its landing arrives on the first day and the Japanese fleet on the seventeenth. The designer asked for a second scenario of about five days, small and interesting, that either side can win. This is the Eastern Solomons, 22 to 26 August 1942: three Japanese carriers against two American and an airfield, with a Japanese troop convoy that must reach Guadalcanal. It is made almost wholly of what the repository holds. It is played from orders by a commander or a staff on each side, has no scripted orders, and its points are set after a first run so that the results are spread. Six questions for the designer

Standing: A plan, written by the architect on Nov 20, 2026, at the designer's asking. Not yet reviewed, not confirmed and not built

## Why

The designer, on Nov 20, 2026: the 39 days "may not be super balanced, but could be ok as a second learning scenario. We need another one which is, say, 5 days, which should be small, yet interesting."

What is so today, read from the repository (gathered for the architect and checked where the plan rests on it):

- **Scenario 5 is one day,** 18:00 on 23 August to 18:00 on 24 August, with about forty scripted orders. It holds both American carrier forces (Enterprise and Saratoga, 16 ships), the Japanese carrier striking force (Shokaku, Zuikaku and 12 ships) and the Ryujo detachment (4 ships), their air groups from rows, and Henderson Field with two Marine squadrons. Every ship in it has ratings derived from fact rows. Fatigue, wear, supply and pilots are off. Its hand calculation, eighteen validation measures, two recorded games and many tests read it: it is not to be changed.
- **The same forces are in the 39 days,** where they sit in scenario 5's hexes until day 18.
- **The Japanese convoy of those days is in neither.** The order-of-battle facts name it (Jintsu, four patrol boats, destroyers, three transports, about 1,500 men of the Ichiki Detachment's second echelon and the Yokosuka 5th Special Naval Landing Force) and give its plan: out of Rabaul on 19 August, a landing set for 22 August and put off to the 24th and the 25th. The sources disagree on how the men were divided. Jintsu, the patrol boats, the transports and two of the destroyers have no derived ratings.
- **Also named by the rows and in no scenario:** Wasp's force, which was away refuelling; Kondo's advance force with Mutsu and Chitose; Mikawa's cruisers.
- **Positions are thin.** Rows give places for 23 to 25 August for several forces, many as grid references with no latitude. For 22 August there is one Japanese position and no Allied one.
- **The map's northern edge is where scenario 5 starts the Japanese carriers,** about 520 nm from Lunga. Truk is off the map. A force at 17 kn makes about 400 nm a day, so there is a day's approach on the map and no more.
- **A scenario is played from orders by staffs on both sides** where it states its islands, whose each class of ship is, and what each side believes of the other; the 39 days does. A unit can be given a day from which it may be used.
- **The kinds of objective are fixed:** enemy ships sunk, crippled or out of action; enemy aircraft destroyed; supply landed at a base; a base open or shut by days; a base held at the end; enemy men lost. There is none for men landed.
- **Every point value in the game is a placeholder.**

## The scenario

### What it is

1. **Five days, 22 to 26 August 1942,** the game beginning on the evening of the 21st. One file, with no scripted order and no intent stated for either side: each side is given a mission, and a commander or a staff does the rest.
2. **Day 1 is quiet.** The Allied carriers, Henderson Field and the Japanese aircraft at Rabaul are there from the start. The Japanese carrier forces and the convoy may be used from day 2, the day the rows first place them near. A new commander has an evening and a day to set his searches, his patrols and his carriers' station before anything can reach him.
3. **Orders inside a day are on,** so that a commander may stop at 0600 or 1200 when he is told of a contact or an attack.

### The forces

4. **The carriers and their air groups are scenario 5's,** ship for ship and group for group, with their rows. Nothing is added to them.
5. **Henderson Field** is Allied, with its airfield built, the two Marine squadrons at their strength of 20 August, and the supply the 39 days finds there in the middle of the month, stated as a figure with its source. The Marines who hold it are there as land units, so that a landing matters; they are given no order to attack.
6. **The Japanese at Rabaul:** the land bombers and fighters the 39 days has there.
7. **The convoy** is one transport force that may be used from day 2: destroyers of classes already derived, and transports. Jintsu, the patrol boats and the two destroyers of classes not derived are either derived from their rows, by the written rule, before the scenario is built, or left out and said to be. The transports are placeholders, named so. It carries the men the rows give, as land units, and supply.
8. **Where the convoy lands** is a Japanese stock on Guadalcanal east of Lunga, at Taivu, as the 39 days has it. The men there at the start are those the 39 days has after the fight of 21 August, as a figure with its source.
9. **Left out, and said so in the file:** Wasp's force, Kondo's advance force, Mikawa's cruisers and Chitose. With them the scenario is neither small nor of ships the game can rate today.

### Where things start

10. **A place and a day are taken from a row** where a row gives both. Where none does, the place is a placeholder and marked: the Allied carriers on 22 August are placed where scenario 5 has them on the 23rd, by the ruling that already does this in the 39 days (decision 0101, ruling G). The Japanese forces come onto the map at its northern edge on day 2.

### What each side is to do

11. **The Japanese mission:** land the convoy's men and supply on Guadalcanal; shut the airfield at Lunga; sink the American carriers.
12. **The Allied mission:** keep the airfield open; stop the convoy; sink the Japanese carriers and keep its own.
13. **Scored by the kinds the game has,** the same for both where they mirror: ships sunk and out of action, with a transport worth points; aircraft destroyed; Lunga open by days for the Allied side and shut by days for the Japanese; supply landed at Taivu for the Japanese. Each side is shown the other's (decision 0183).
14. **The points and the three levels are placeholders, set once from a first run** (below), and stated with what they were set from. They are not changed after that to make a game come out.

### Rules switched on

15. **Fatigue, wear, supply and the land rules are on,** with the live figures, as in the 39 days. Five days is long enough for a search flown every pulse to wear a group out, and a commander should meet that here before he meets it in a campaign.
16. **What each side believes of the other** is stated, as the 39 days states it, so that a dry run can be asked. It rests on no source yet and is marked so.

## How it is made balanced, and how that is checked

17. **A first run, before any point is set:** 1,000 games of staff against staff, each side given the plain orders its mission implies (the Japanese: operate the carriers, keep Taivu supplied; the Allied: operate the carriers, hold and cover Lunga), and the counts of every objective recorded: carriers sunk and out of action each side, transports sunk, men and tons landed, days Lunga was open.
18. **The points and levels are then set once** so that, on those counts, neither side wins more than about six games in ten and each of the three levels is reached in some. They are recorded with the run they were set from.
19. **Reported, not judged:** four games between agents, two with each model on each side, as was done on the 39 days, with their margins beside the staffs'.
20. **If the first run shows one side cannot win whatever the points** (the convoy never arrives, or always does), that is told to the designer with the counts, and the forces are not altered to mend it without his word.

## What is built

- **A scenario file,** in a folder of its own under `data/scenarios/` until it is ready to stand with the numbered ones (decision 0072), with its files of sides and of the trial's figures.
- **Derivations,** if the designer wants Jintsu and the convoy's own escorts (question 3).
- **A test** with a hand calculation, written by the architect when the plan is confirmed: the days each force may first be used, the convoy's passage from where it comes on to Taivu at its speed, the reach of each side's strikes from where it starts, and Lunga's supply over five days. And that the same seed gives the same game.
- **The first run** and its report under `docs/validation/`.
- **A directive for each side,** for the table, in the game's own words.

No rule is added or changed, and scenario 5 is not touched.

## What it costs

- **Data.** The convoy's transports are placeholders. The sources disagree on the convoy's men; the scenario takes one reading and says which. Positions on 22 August are placeholders. What each side believed rests on no source.
- **It is one more scenario to keep,** with a golden file once it stands among the numbered ones.
- **The first run** is about 1,000 games of five days, a few minutes.

## Questions for the designer

1. **22 to 26 August with a quiet first day, or 23 to 27?** The architect recommends 22 to 26: a first day to set up is worth more to a learner than a fifth day after the battle.
2. **Is the convoy's success scored by the supply it lands, or by a new kind of objective, men landed?** The game has none today. The architect recommends building the scenario on supply landed and transports sunk, and adding "men landed" as a small rule of its own only if the first run shows the convoy's purpose is not felt.
3. **Jintsu and the convoy's own escorts: derive them now, or stand in destroyers the game already rates?** The architect recommends standing in rated destroyers for the first build and commissioning the derivations beside it. It keeps the first game days away and not weeks.
4. **Wasp, Kondo, Mikawa and Chitose left out.** The architect recommends it: with them it is not small.
5. **A person's seat is the Allied one, with the staff playing Japan.** The architect recommends it for a learner: the Allied side has the simpler task on day 1. Either seat can be taken.
6. **Points set once from a first run of staffs.** The architect recommends it over setting them by judgement: it is the one way to say "either side can win" with a count behind it.

## Coral Sea

The designer's other thought. It is the better first battle in history, two carriers a side and an invasion to stop, and it needs what the repository does not have: the order of battle from sources, ratings for Lexington, Yorktown, Shoho and their escorts and at least one aircraft type, and a map that reaches Port Moresby. The architect recommends commissioning that research now, so that it can follow this scenario.
