# 0222: An order's limits at the table, and a supply source the staff chooses

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 23, 2026, on the designer's questions at the table, reading the plan's paper for "Control New Guinea", whose task to hold Port Moresby said "This task names no source" and "the commander may name a source in the forces limit, or state keep supplied himself": "What does it mean, this task names no sources: how can the player name sources? How can the player set limits?" It is to be read by a reviewer who has not seen it written before anything is built.

Summary: An order carries eleven limits in the engine: what the staff may use, where, when, in what rank, what it strikes first, what it must know. The table lets a player set one, the risk. So the staff's reasons tell him to do things the table does not offer. This puts the limits on the order's paper, each at the staff's choice until he sets it, makes a reason that names a limit lead to that limit's control, and has the staff choose a supply source itself where none is named, so that the commonest such reason goes.

Standing: Planned on Nov 23, 2026; to be reviewed.

## Why

What is so today (rules chapter 8, The eleven limits; decisions 0107, 0111, 0117, 0141, 0159, 0190; the table's page):

- **The engine's limits on an order:** forces (ships, task forces, air groups, land units, submarines, and a source base for supply), area, priority, protected ships, risk, the targets to strike first, and for a landing not before a day, wait for a unit, after another order, and the knowledge needed. One more, the reserve, is a base's.
- **The tool that sets a limit takes them all,** and an agent at a seat sets them.
- **The table's order paper says "Forces: the staff's choice."** and offers the risk. It sets a forces limit by itself in one case, a source for an order to hold or to keep supplied given from a base's card. A plan's paper can change the risk.
- **The staff's reasons speak of limits freely:** "its forces limit names no source", "a forces limit that names it", "outside the order's area". A player reads what would change a thing and cannot do it.
- **A task to hold a place under an order to control an island has no source,** because the staff forms it and names none, and the staff then forms no supply for it and says the commander may name one.

## The rules

### The limits on the order's paper

1. **The order's paper gains a part, LIMITS, folded, between the order and when it acts.** Closed, it says in one line what is set ("The staff's choice in all." or "Forces: Task Force 16, VB-6 · Area: within 200 miles of Lunga"). Open, it has a line for each limit the kind of order can carry, each at "the staff's choice" until set.
2. **Forces: "use only these".** The player touches pieces on the chart or names in a list: task forces, air groups, land units, boats. What he has named is listed with a way to take each off. Ships singly are not offered in this stage.
3. **Supplied from:** a base of his side, touched on the chart or chosen from a list, for an order that keeps a place supplied or holds one.
4. **Area: "keep within".** A place and a distance, drawn as a circle on the chart while he sets it; the limit given is the hexes within it. One circle in this stage.
5. **Rank:** first, second and so on among his orders, shown as a list of them that he reorders; an order he has not ranked comes after those he has.
6. **Strike first:** the kinds of ship in the order they are to be struck, and for an order to deny waters whether ships in those waters come first. Offered where the order's tasks strike ships.
7. **For an order to take a place or control an island:** not before a day; wait for a unit; after another of his orders; and what must be known of the enemy first (nothing, a count, a count no older than a number of days).
8. **Protected ships and a base's reserve are not on the order's paper.** The reserve is on the base's card, as a line he can change. Protected ships wait for a use.
9. **Limits are set with the order when it is signed, and changed afterwards from the plan's paper,** whose head shows each limit that is set with CHANGE, as it shows the risk today. A change is a command like any other: it is listed under SIGNED and may be taken back until the clock runs.
10. **What a limit set would refuse is said in the paper before signing,** from the tool's own refusal, as NOT SENT is.

### A reason leads to the limit

11. **Where a reason says a limit holds a thing back or would change it, the limit's name is a link** that opens the order's paper at LIMITS with that limit's control in view. This is of a kind with the link to an order that blocks (decision 0221, the staff's reading).
12. **The words are the table's:** "the forces you named" for the forces limit, "the base it is supplied from" for a source, "the waters you kept it within" for the area. "Forces limit" is not said to a player.

### The staff chooses a source

13. **Where an order to hold a place has no source named on it or above it, the staff chooses one** and forms the supply, as it would had the commander named it. The source is the base of the side from which the supply doctrine's own projection gives the place the most days of supply on the day it would run short, among bases with a port and with supply above their reserve; the nearest by sea where they tie; the lowest number then.
14. **Its reason says which base it chose and why, and what it passed over.** Where no base serves, it says so as now.
15. **A source the commander names is used and none is chosen.** An order to keep a place supplied that he gives himself is as now.
16. **The doctrine's version rises.** Staffed games on file are compared before and after: where a hold today forms no supply for want of a source, the game changes, and each such game is listed with what moved.

## What a side learns by it

Nothing. The limits are the side's own, and the source is chosen from the side's own bases.

## Rule card

For rule 13. Reads: the order's forces limit up the tree; the side's bases, their ports, supply and reserves; the supply doctrine's projection for the place. Writes: a task to keep the place supplied from the base chosen, as the doctrine forms one today. Draws: none.

## Games on file

Rules 1 to 12 move none. Rule 13 moves every staffed game in which a hold has no source: the 39 days and others, to be found and listed in the build. Recorded games are remade; the records of closed proofs are not altered.

## What it does not do

- **It does not let a player name single ships.**
- **It does not draw an area of any shape but a circle.**
- **It does not add a limit the engine lacks.**
- **It does not have the staff choose forces differently;** only a supply's source.

## What it costs

- **The LIMITS part of the order's paper and of the plan's head; the chart's picking of pieces, a base and a circle; the links from reasons; the reserve on a base's card.** The page's checks gain each.
- **One rule of the staff, with its scenario and hand calculation,** written by the architect before it is delegated.
- **Rulebook:** chapter 8 (Holding a place; the forces limit) and the glossary.

## Questions for the designer

1. **Should the staff choose a supply source by itself (rule 13),** or only say that none is named and lead him to name one? The architect recommends that it choose: feeding a place is the staff's work.
2. **Which limits he wants first,** if not all at once. The architect recommends forces, supplied from and area first; rank, strike first and the landing's four after.

## Amended on review, Nov 23, 2026

Read by a reviewer who had not seen it written; the architect checked its first finding on the Coral Sea's bases and on the staff's code. What follows is the plan as it now stands and replaces the rules above.

### What the review found

- **The case that began this was misread.** Under an order to control an island the staff already chooses a source of supply and its ships (decisions 0151, 0152 and 0163): the side's base off the island with the most supply above its reserve. "Control New Guinea" in the Coral Sea finds none because Port Moresby is the Allied side's only base. The reason shown then tells the player to name a source, and there is none he could name.
- **A forces limit that names some things strips the rest.** Outside a landing and an island, an air group serves an order only where every forces limit above it names it; an order to deny waters whose limit names task forces and no air group has no base at all; a carrier force named in a limit is given its task where it lies that morning, where a free one is stationed by the staff. One touch on a carrier force would drop every land-based air group from the order and move where the carriers work.
- **Several limits are read by nothing on most orders.** An area binds supply routes and a landing's lifts and is not read by a hold, a deny, a stand, a patrol or a carrier's station; targets are read where ships are struck and nowhere else; protected ships by supply alone; land units and boats only by the orders that use them.
- **A forces limit and an area cannot be put back to the staff's choice:** the tool has no way to clear either.
- **The staff's reading before signing cannot take the limits in:** a preview is of one command, and a limit on an order not yet signed is refused.
- **A reason is words:** nothing in it says which limit it speaks of.
- **Rule 13 could not be built as written.** The staff may not set a source under a commander's hold; a source with no ship fails the hold's plan and its cover with it; the measure proposed needed a planner's run for each base and was undefined where nothing runs short; and it would have been a third rule for choosing a source beside the island's and the table's own.

### First, the reason that misled

1. **Where a hold has no source and no base of the side could be one, the staff says that and offers nothing:** "No other base of ours holds supply: Port Moresby lives on what it has", with its days of supply. It says a source may be named only where a base that could be one exists, and names those bases. This is built at once, by itself.

### The engine, before the table

2. **A commander's forces limit binds only the kinds it names, on every kind of order,** as it does today under a landing and an island (decisions 0149 and 0151) and for boats (decision 0218). Naming a task force leaves the air groups, land units, boats and source to the staff; naming an air group leaves the task forces to it. A carrier force named in a limit is still stationed by the staff unless the commander gives it the task himself. Staffed games on file whose limits name forces are compared before and after and what moves is listed.
3. **A forces limit and an area may be cleared,** by the command that sets them given with none, as the limits that tell a plan to wait are cleared.
4. **A preview and a signing take an order with its limits as one,** so that the staff's reading is of the order as it will be given, at the risk and with the limits chosen.
5. **A reason says which limits it speaks of:** a list at its end of the kind of each limit and the order it is set on, filled where its words of what holds it or what would change it name a limit. The doctrine's version rises.
6. **The supply doctrine reads an order's rank up the tree,** so that supply the staff forms under a ranked order is ranked with it.
7. **An area binds what it says it binds or is not offered.** No doctrine is changed to read it in this decision: it is offered only on the orders where it is read.

### The staff chooses a source, if the designer agrees

8. **For a hold of the commander's with no source on it or above it, the staff chooses by the island's rule, unchanged:** the side's base not in the place's hex, that the staff may take, with the most supply above its reserve, the lower number where they tie; chosen once and kept; its ships the transport forces stopped there with nothing aboard.
9. **The staff may then set that source and those ships on the supply it forms,** which is a change to what a limit set by the staff may name under a commander's order, as decision 0149 has it under a landing.
10. **The supply so formed has a plan of its own,** so that its failure does not end the hold; and **with no ship to carry it the staff forms none and says why.**
11. **The table's own choosing of a source (`supplyForces`) goes,** the source being chosen in one place and said in a reason.
12. **A source the commander names is used.**

### The limits on the order's paper

13. **A line is offered only where it changes what the staff does for that kind of order:**

14. **Forces and "supplied from" are one limit of the engine and are sent as one.** "Use only these" lists by kind, and a kind with nothing named says "the staff's choice".
15. **Rank is on YOUR ORDERS in the stop paper,** not on each order's paper: the orders in a list he reorders, sending a rank only for those he has ranked.
16. **Limits are changed afterwards from the plan's paper,** each shown in its head with CHANGE and with "back to the staff's choice". A change is a command under SIGNED and may be taken back until the clock runs. A task the staff formed for a force no longer named stands until its order is withdrawn, and the paper shows it so.
17. **A reason's limit is a link to that limit's control,** from rule 5's list and not from its words. The table's words for each kind are "the forces you named", "the base it is supplied from", "the waters you kept it within", "what it strikes first".
18. **Single ships are not named by a player.** Ships in port in no force at the source are the staff's to use.

## The designer's answers, Nov 23, 2026, and what they add

His words: "I think - staff chooses and user can change it. But if an agent can change limits - I think we should allow the player to set them as well. Maybe we should also have an orders screen, where orders can be prioritized."

1. **The staff chooses a supply source where none is named, and the commander may change it.** Rules 8 to 12 are confirmed. The source the staff chose is shown on the order's paper under "supplied from", marked as the staff's choice, with CHANGE.
2. **What an agent at a seat can set, a player can set.** Every limit the tool takes is reachable at the table, which adds to rule 13:
   - **protected ships,** on an order to keep a place supplied, where they are read;
   - **single ships** in "use only these", for a supply's ships in port and wherever a forces limit may name a ship (rule 18 is withdrawn);
   - **a base's reserve** of supply and of fuel, on the base's card.
   A limit is still offered on an order only where something reads it there. Where the tool would take a limit that nothing reads on that kind of order, the table does not offer it, and each such case is a row of open work: either a doctrine comes to read it or the tool comes to refuse it. A player is never given a control that does nothing, and an agent is not to be left one either.
3. **An orders paper.** YOUR ORDERS in the stop paper opens a paper for reading, THE ORDERS, that lists every order of the commander's in force and signed: what it is, its state (waiting for leave, launched, cannot be met), its limits in a line, and its rank. Orders are put in rank by moving them up and down; an order not ranked comes after those that are. From each line its plan's paper opens, and it can be withdrawn. Rule 15 is this paper. A rank is sent only for the orders he has ranked, and what a rank does is said by the staff's reasons where it decided something ("Task Force 16 went to order 2, ranked before order 3").

The second question put to him (a forces limit that binds only the kinds it names) he did not speak to; it follows from his second answer, since "use only these" must mean what it says for a player, and it is built as rule 2 has it, with the games on file that move listed in the build.

### How each rule was built

- **Rule 2.** A kind is bound by a limit where the limit names one of it or lists it in `none_of`, or where it names nothing and lists nothing at all. `ForcesLimit` gains `binds_ships`, `binds_groups`, `binds_land_units`, `binds_submarines`, `binds_source` and `names_nothing`; `CommandState` gains `reads_as_bound` (a take or an island on the chain), `ship_limits_on_chain`, `nearest_ship_limit` and `source_on_chain`. On a chain under a take or an island every reading is what it was.
- **Rule 3.** `set_limit` of forces or area with `cleared: true` (a scenario's `cleared = true`) and nothing but the intent clears it: it is a command like the setting, refused for an intent not the side's (`NoSuchIntent`), an amendment of a plan in force (one `PlanAmended`, even where nothing was set), never "Wider than its parent", and not refused where nothing is set. A forces limit with no list, no source and no `none_of` is "use nothing", as before.
- **Rule 4.** `preview` of a `set_intent` takes `limits`, a list of set_limit bodies without `intent`; the batch is taken in on the copy and worked as one, and a limit the engine refuses is answered as that refusal with `of_batch` and nothing numbered. `approve` of that preview queues the order and its limits, all or none (`limits_queued`). `take_back` of the order takes its limits with it, as decision 0221 already did. A trial of such a preview tries the whole batch.
- **Rule 5.** `Reason` gains, at its end, `limits`: each a `ReasonLimit` of a `LimitKind` (forces, area, priority, protected, reserve, risk, not_before, wait_for, after, knowledge, targets, source; codes 1 to 12) and the intent it is set on (`CommandState::limit_set_on`: the nearest on the chain that sets it, for the risk the one that binds, else the root), or the base of a reserve. About ninety reasons are filled. It goes through the agent's JSON, the schemas (`command_defs.schema.json#/$defs/reason_limit`), saves (version 25), the export (`limits` on `staff_reason`) and the replay. A reason is not world state: it is not hashed, and its debug text leaves the list out, so every digest the earlier tests keep is what it was. No reason's words changed but the two below that the rulings gave.
- **Rule 6.** `priority_order` in `morning.rs` reads the rank up the tree: a supply under a ranked order is ranked with it, and a rank on the supply itself is read first.
- **Rule 7.** No doctrine reads an area that did not; the table below is the code as it stands.
- **Frozen figures.** `scripts/compare-scenarios.sh --against 9bdc3062`: 254 runs, 252 the same with the hashes cut; scenario 55 differs, live and frozen, in the words of its station's reason alone.
