# 0140: A day from which a force may be used, formations, and the staff's setup of the 39 days: step 1 of stage 3

Status: settled by the architect on Nov 17, 2026, inside the plan for step 3, stage 3, that the designer confirmed the same day (decision 0139, step 1 of its eight, and items 7 to 11). It changes one sentence of `docs/step3-spec.md`, "Two setups of the campaign", as the spec says it may be changed. Built on Nov 17, 2026: see "As built".

Summary: Step 1 of stage 3: a task force, a ship, an air group and a land unit may have a day from which its side may use it, before which no order to it is taken and the staff leaves it out; a side knows its own forces' days and not the enemy's; a land unit may belong to a formation, and formations have parents, as data; and the 39-day slice is set up a second time with no scripted order, each force with its day from the row the script's day rests on

Standing: Settled by the architect, Nov 17, 2026, inside decision 0139. Built, its tables awaiting the architect's ruling (As built)

## Why

The 39 days are scripted: each force is sent out on the day the record gives. Every force is in the scenario from day 1, because nothing brings a force in later. Take the scripts away and a staff has, on 7 August, ships and men that were not there to use for weeks. A staff that plans an operation must also know what it will have and when, which is the reinforcement schedule in its first form.

## The day

1. **A task force, a ship, an air group and a land unit may each have a day from which its side may use it,** given by the scenario. With none, it may be used from day 1, as now.
2. **Before its day no order to it is taken,** from the staff or from the commander. It is refused with the day. A ship takes the later of its own day and its task force's; an air group aboard a ship and a land unit aboard a ship take the later of their own and the ship's.
3. **Before its day the staff leaves it out:** it is in no pool, it is no force a task may use, and no doctrine gives it an order. A reason that would have used it says the day it may be used from.
4. **It is still there.** It lies where the scenario put it, at the settings the scenario gave it, and the rules act on it as on any unit: it can be seen, attacked, and it defends itself. The game has no way to bring a force onto the map later, and this step adds none.
5. **A side knows its own forces' days.** The side's view gives them, and the tools show them: "available from day 9". Nothing gives a side the enemy's days.
6. **After its day a unit is the side's to use as it sees fit.** No date of any operation is fixed by a day.

## What it changes in the spec

`docs/step3-spec.md`, "Two setups of the campaign", says of the day: "It is a dated entry in the forces limit." It becomes a property of the force, set by the scenario. A forces limit belongs to one intent, and stage 3 has the staff draw its own intents from "control an island"; a day that lived in a hand-written limit would be lost the moment the staff wrote the limit. The row "A day from which a force may be used" of "What it builds on" is this record.

## Formations

7. **A land unit may belong to a formation,** and a formation may have a parent formation: a battalion under a regiment under a division. A scenario states them. The loader refuses a parent that is not there and a formation that is its own ancestor.
8. **No rule reads a formation in this step.** The side's view gives the tree, so that step 3 of this stage can choose and move a formation whole. A formation is not a command (decision 0139, 10).
9. **In the staff's setup each formation and each parent comes from a row** of `data/oob` (`land_units.csv`, the parent rows of `land_unit_facts.csv`). Where no row gives a unit a parent it has none: a gap, not a guess.

## The staff's setup of the 39 days

10. **A second file,** made from `data/scenarios/07_full_slice.toml`: the same map, bases, forces, groups, units, arrivals and figures, with every scripted order taken out and no intent stated. It is kept out of the loops that walk `data/scenarios/*.toml`.
11. **Each unit's day is the day of its first scripted order** in the scripted setup, with the row that order's day rests on cited beside it. A unit aboard a force takes the force's day. A unit the script never orders has no day, and may be used from day 1.
12. **A day with no row behind it is declared made up,** and marked a placeholder: the placeholder convoys and night forces of the slice are such.
13. **The architect rules on the list before the step is merged.** The build makes the table of every unit, its day and its row, and the table of the formations and their rows; where the script's first order is not the day a force was there to use (the Rabaul air groups, which the script first orders on day 15 and which flew on 7 August), the table says so and the architect settles it, with the reason on the row.
14. **With no intent stated it plays as the scripted file with its orders taken out:** the same events. A day changes nothing until someone would give an order.

## Rule card

- **Reads:** a unit's day; the day of the game.
- **Writes:** nothing but the days and the formations, from the scenario.
- **Reuses:** the engine's intake and its refusals; every doctrine's test of what a task may use.

- **The staff (item 3).** `crates/sim-staff/src/available.rs` is the staff's one test, built on `SideView::may_use`, with the one wording "may be used from day N". ### Readings taken

1. **A task force's day is the later of its own and each of its ships' own.** Item 2 says a ship takes the later of hers and her force's; an order to a force moves every ship in it, and the hand calculation's "task force 1 from day 2 and ship 1 from day 3: nothing is ordered before day 3" needs the force to wait for its ship. A force made in play has no day of its own and takes its ships'.
2. **A land unit takes the later of its own and that of each ship with its men aboard,** each ship's day read as item 2 reads it. A unit partly ashore and partly aboard waits for the latest.
3. **What an order is "to".** The order's own unit, the ships a base's order to form a force names, and the land unit a load order names. A detach order to a force names ships of that force, whose days the force's day already holds. An escort order's charge is not ordered and is not read.
4. **Commands are not refused for a day.** A commander may name a unit before its day in a forces limit or commit a ship to a run before its day: planning ahead is his. Any order to it before its day is refused all the same. The staff's commitments come from its pool, which leaves a waiting ship out.
5. **The tools show the day while it has not come:** `SideView::may_use` false. On the day itself the unit is plain.
6. **A land unit's formation is the `data/oob` entity it is.** A battalion in the scenario belongs to the battalion's formation, whose parent is its regiment, as scenario 65's variant has two units in "Placeholder Battalion 1" and "Placeholder Battalion 2". Each formation's number is its row's id in `land_units.csv`, and each parent is that row's `parent` column, which the parent research fills only where a row states it; the other parent-kind rows (task organisation, attached to, listed under) are cited and not used.
7. **"Hands in one reason" on day 1 of scenario 65.** The staff hands in one no-order reason and no order. It also hands in the two `approve` commands of its plan with their reasons, the same commands it hands in on day 1 of the game without the day. The test asserts the one no-order reason, no order, and the same commands in both games. The architect may say whether "one reason" meant that.
8. **"Where a reason lists the pool it names the ship with its day," in scenario 36.** With ship 2 from day 5 and ship 1 free, the staff's reasons list the free ships it uses ("ship:1") and never the pool whole, so ship 2 is named in none; the test asserts nothing is committed or loaded on ship 2 before day 5 and that any reason naming it gives its day. A second test, ship 1 from day 3 and ship 2 from day 5, has no ship free, and the reason reads "ship:1 may be used from day 3, ship:2 may be used from day 5". If the architect meant every reason on a pool to name its waiting ships, that is a change to the staff's words.
9. **Reasons "the same but for the day".** The test compares each reason of day N+1 with the game-without-the-day's day N after putting each day number back by one; nothing else differs.

### The staff's setup of the 39 days

`data/scenarios/staff/07_full_slice.toml`, in a folder of its own so that it is out of the loops that walk `data/scenarios/*.toml` (decision 0072). It loads, runs its 39 days with no order from anyone and none refused, and gives the events `07_full_slice.toml` gives with its orders taken out (scenario 65's test, and by hand with `sim-cli run --events`). It has 35 days: 25 task forces and 10 air groups. No ship and no land unit has a day of its own: no scripted order names a ship, and every land unit first ordered after day 1 is aboard a force and takes its day. It has 22 formations, 10 Allied and 12 Japanese; 21 of the 25 land units have one.

It is made by a small script kept outside the repository, `make_staff_setup.py` (Python 3.9, standard library only, deterministic), run from anywhere as `python3 -I make_staff_setup.py <repo> [tables.md]`. It reads `data/scenarios/07_full_slice.toml` as text and, to check what it cites, `data/oob/land_units.csv` and `land_unit_facts.csv`. It: takes out all 167 `[[order]]` tables and the comment blocks attached to them (it asserts the span holds only orders, comments and blank lines); moves each relative path one folder deeper and checks each resolves; changes the `name` and adds a header; gives each task force and each air group not aboard a ship `available_from = N`, N the day of the first order whose `task_force`, `air_group` or `land_unit` key names it (a load order's `unit` is a cargo, not a target), with a comment citing the row, the decision or "made up (placeholder)", where a unit aboard a force takes the force's day and gets no key, and day 1 gets none; adds the `[[formation]]` tables and each land unit's `formation`, from its tables of the rows (`TF`, `AG`, `LU`, `FORM` at the top of the script); and asserts the result has no `[[order]]` and no `[[command]]`. To change a day's citation or a formation, edit those tables and run it again; the day itself is computed. The test of scenario 65 checks that each day is the day of the first scripted order, or one the architect rules (its constant `RULED`, empty until he does).
