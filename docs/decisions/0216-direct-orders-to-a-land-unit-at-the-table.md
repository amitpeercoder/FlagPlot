# 0216: Direct orders to a land unit at the table

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 22, 2026, on the designer's question at the table, "Do we have an option to command land units?", and his word on the answer: "Yes, plan it." It is to be read by a reviewer who has not seen it written before anything is built.

Summary: The engine takes three orders for a land unit: its posture, a march, and what it does on a quiet day. The table offers none of them: a player moves his men only through an operation order that the staff carries out. This puts the three on a land unit's card, as a force's and an air group's cards have theirs, and says on the card whose hands the unit is in.

Standing: Planned on Nov 22, 2026; to be reviewed.

## Why

What is so today (decisions 0090, 0094, 0097, 0099, 0105 and 0156; rules chapters 1, 7 and 8):

- **The engine's orders to a land unit are three,** each a standing order: posture, defend or attack, and for an attack what the unit does when its attack is spent, hold or fall back; a march to a neighbouring hex joined by a land link, or its own hex to stop; and its activity on a day it neither marches nor fights, stand ready, train, or work, with a job where the unit works.
- **Each is refused where it cannot be:** posture and a march for a unit with no men ashore; an activity a working unit does not take.
- **Inside a day only a land unit's posture may be ordered,** at 0600 and 1200, where the scenario has orders inside a day.
- **The tool that gives orders takes all three.** An agent at a seat can give them now.
- **The table's cards give orders to a force and to an air group,** and say "In whose hands" each is. A land unit's card shows its men, place, posture, activity, fatigue, food and experience, and gives no order.
- **How the staff treats a land unit the commander has ordered** is for the reviewer to read from the code: whether such a unit is held from the staff as a force is (decision 0156), and whether the staff's own orders to it, in a take or a hold, would replace the commander's the next dusk.

## The rules

1. **A land unit's card gains an order form** for a unit of the player's own, under the heading ORDER, as an air group's has:
   - **Posture:** defend or attack; with attack, when spent: hold or fall back.
   - **March:** to a place the player touches on the chart, offered only among the hexes joined to the unit's by a land link, which the chart marks while he chooses; and "halt" where it is marching.
   - **Activity:** stand ready, train or work, with the jobs the unit can do where it works.
2. **The form shows what stands,** as the air group's does: the posture, march and activity in force are the ones marked.
3. **An order the engine would refuse is not offered,** and where one is refused all the same the card says the engine's reason. A unit with no men ashore is offered the activity alone. Inside a day the posture alone is offered.
4. **The orders are given with the day's orders,** as a force's direct orders are: drafted on the card, shown among what the player has signed, and able to be taken back before the clock runs.
5. **The card says in whose hands the unit is:** the staff's, where an order of the staff's working names or uses it; the commander's, where he has ordered it or holds it; and no one's.
6. **A direct order takes the unit out of the staff's hands, as it does a force,** and the card says so before the player signs, in the words the force's form uses. The unit goes back to the staff when the player releases it. Whether the engine does this for a land unit today is the reviewer's first question; if it does not, that is a change to the staff's rules, with its own rule card, and is added to this plan before it is built.
7. **Nothing of a rule changes otherwise.** The table gives what the tool already takes.

### Rest

Added the same day on the designer's word: "near the fatigue mark, should such a command exist as rest, allow the player to give that command. That will take the unit out of staff hands until it is recovered enough."

8. **A card that shows fatigue offers REST beside it,** for a land unit, an air group and a task force of the player's own.
9. **Rest is the orders under which that kind of unit loses fatigue, and a hold with an end.** For a land unit: defend, no march, stand ready. For an air group: the rest mission. For a task force: normal readiness, as the rules of its fatigue have it. With them the unit is held from the staff, as "keep this force out of the staff's plans" holds it.
10. **The hold ends by itself when the unit is rested:** at the first dusk at which its fatigue is at or below `rested_fatigue`. The unit is then the staff's again, and the day's report says it is rested and released. The commander may end the rest sooner by releasing it or by giving it another order.
11. **A unit that cannot rest where it is says so and is not offered it:** a land unit under fire or in a fight takes no rest (decision 0106), and the card says that it is in contact.
12. **This is a rule and not the table's alone.** A hold that ends on a condition is new to the command state: it is hashed and named, the hold's kind is added at the end, an agent's tool takes it, and the staff's rules say what a held unit's order does while it rests. One figure is new, `rested_fatigue`, a placeholder, 10: the fatigue below which the table calls a unit fresh.

Rule card for rest: reads a held unit's fatigue and `rested_fatigue`. Writes the hold's end. Draws: none.

## What a side learns by it

Nothing new. A player sees his own units' orders as he sees them now in the diary.

## Rule card

None, unless rule 6 needs the staff changed. The table reads the unit, its orders in force, the land links of its hex and the command state; it writes nothing but orders through the tool.

## Games on file

None moves: no game on file gives a rest, and the form is the table's. The layout rises for the hold's end and recorded games are remade for it.

## What it does not do

- **It does not let a player order a unit aboard ship to land,** or load one. Lifts are the staff's, by an operation order.
- **It does not give a march of more than one hex.** The engine's march is to a neighbouring hex; a longer one is given a hex at a time.
- **It does not let one order go to several units at once.**
- **It does not say what an attack is to take.** A unit on attack fights the enemy in its hex, as the rules have it.

## What it costs

- **The card's form, the chart's marks for a march, and the words for whose hands a unit is in.** The table's screen checks gain the form.
- **Rulebook:** chapter 8 (holds, a hold that ends when a unit is rested) and the glossary's "Hold"; and more if rule 6 needs the staff changed.
