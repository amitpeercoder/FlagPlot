# 0216: Direct orders to a land unit at the table

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 22, 2026, on the designer's question at the table, "Do we have an option to command land units?", and his word on the answer: "Yes, plan it." It is to be read by a reviewer who has not seen it written before anything is built.

Summary: The engine takes three orders for a land unit: its posture, a march, and what it does on a quiet day. The table offers none of them: a player moves his men only through an operation order that the staff carries out. This puts the three on a land unit's card, as a force's and an air group's cards have theirs, and says on the card whose hands the unit is in.

Standing: Planned on Nov 22, 2026; reviewed and amended the same day; the three orders built on Nov 22, 2026 (see "As built"), rest not yet built.

## Why

What is so today (decisions 0090, 0094, 0097, 0099, 0105 and 0156; rules chapters 1, 7 and 8):

- **The engine's orders to a land unit are three,** each a standing order: posture, defend or attack, and for an attack what the unit does when its attack is spent, hold or fall back; a march to a neighbouring hex joined by a land link, or its own hex to stop; and its activity on a day it neither marches nor fights, stand ready, train, or work, with a job where the unit works.
- **Each is refused where it cannot be:** posture and a march for a unit with no men ashore; an activity a working unit does not take.
- **Inside a day only a land unit's posture may be ordered,** at 0600 and 1200, where the scenario has orders inside a day.
- **The tool that gives orders takes all three.** An agent at a seat can give them now.
- **The table's cards give orders to a force and to an air group,** and say "In whose hands" each is. A land unit's card shows its men, place, posture, activity, fatigue, food and experience, and gives no order.
- **How the staff treats a land unit the commander has ordered** is for the reviewer to read from the code: whether such a unit is held from the staff as a force is (decision 0156), and whether the staff's own orders to it, in a take or a hold, would replace the commander's the next dusk.

## The rules

1. **A land unit's card gains an order form** for a unit of the player's own, under the heading ORDER, as an air group's has:
   - **Posture:** defend or attack; with attack, when spent: hold or fall back.
   - **March:** to a place the player touches on the chart, offered only among the hexes joined to the unit's by a land link, which the chart marks while he chooses; and "halt" where it is marching.
   - **Activity:** stand ready, train or work, with the jobs the unit can do where it works.
2. **The form shows what stands,** as the air group's does: the posture, march and activity in force are the ones marked.
3. **An order the engine would refuse is not offered,** and where one is refused all the same the card says the engine's reason. A unit with no men ashore is offered the activity alone. Inside a day the posture alone is offered.
4. **The orders are given with the day's orders,** as a force's direct orders are: drafted on the card, shown among what the player has signed, and able to be taken back before the clock runs.
5. **The card says in whose hands the unit is:** the staff's, where an order of the staff's working names or uses it; the commander's, where he has ordered it or holds it; and no one's.
6. **A direct order takes the unit out of the staff's hands, as it does a force,** and the card says so before the player signs, in the words the force's form uses. The unit goes back to the staff when the player releases it. Whether the engine does this for a land unit today is the reviewer's first question; if it does not, that is a change to the staff's rules, with its own rule card, and is added to this plan before it is built.
7. **Nothing of a rule changes otherwise.** The table gives what the tool already takes.

### Rest

Added the same day on the designer's word: "near the fatigue mark, should such a command exist as rest, allow the player to give that command. That will take the unit out of staff hands until it is recovered enough."

8. **A card that shows fatigue offers REST beside it,** for a land unit, an air group and a task force of the player's own.
9. **Rest is the orders under which that kind of unit loses fatigue, and a hold with an end.** For a land unit: defend, no march, stand ready. For an air group: the rest mission. For a task force: normal readiness, as the rules of its fatigue have it. With them the unit is held from the staff, as "keep this force out of the staff's plans" holds it.
10. **The hold ends by itself when the unit is rested:** at the first dusk at which its fatigue is at or below `rested_fatigue`. The unit is then the staff's again, and the day's report says it is rested and released. The commander may end the rest sooner by releasing it or by giving it another order.
11. **A unit that cannot rest where it is says so and is not offered it:** a land unit under fire or in a fight takes no rest (decision 0106), and the card says that it is in contact.
12. **This is a rule and not the table's alone.** A hold that ends on a condition is new to the command state: it is hashed and named, the hold's kind is added at the end, an agent's tool takes it, and the staff's rules say what a held unit's order does while it rests. One figure is new, `rested_fatigue`, a placeholder, 10: the fatigue below which the table calls a unit fresh.

Rule card for rest: reads a held unit's fatigue and `rested_fatigue`. Writes the hold's end. Draws: none.

## What a side learns by it

Nothing new. A player sees his own units' orders as he sees them now in the diary.

## Rule card

None, unless rule 6 needs the staff changed. The table reads the unit, its orders in force, the land links of its hex and the command state; it writes nothing but orders through the tool.

## Games on file

None moves: no game on file gives a rest, and the form is the table's. The layout rises for the hold's end and recorded games are remade for it.

## What it does not do

- **It does not let a player order a unit aboard ship to land,** or load one. Lifts are the staff's, by an operation order.
- **It does not give a march of more than one hex.** The engine's march is to a neighbouring hex; a longer one is given a hex at a time.
- **It does not let one order go to several units at once.**
- **It does not say what an attack is to take.** A unit on attack fights the enemy in its hex, as the rules have it.

## What it costs

- **The card's form, the chart's marks for a march, and the words for whose hands a unit is in.** The table's screen checks gain the form.
- **Rulebook:** chapter 8 (holds, a hold that ends when a unit is rested) and the glossary's "Hold"; and more if rule 6 needs the staff changed.

## Amended on review, Nov 22, 2026

Read by a reviewer who had not seen it written. Where this differs from what is above, this holds.

### Orders to a land unit

- **Rule 6 is settled: the engine already does it.** A commander's order to a land unit that an order in force may use holds the unit from the staff when it is taken in (decision 0141, item 14), at dusk and inside a day, and the staff's doctrines leave a held unit alone. Nothing in the staff changes and rule 6 needs no rule card.
- **The form holds the unit first, as the force's and the air group's forms do:** it sends the hold and then the order, so that a unit no order yet uses is the commander's too and stays so if an order that could use it comes later. The card then says "Yours: you keep it out of the staff's plans". It goes back to the staff when he releases it.
- **"In whose hands" is as the force's card has it:** the commander's where it is held, otherwise the staff's.
- **What is offered follows every refusal the engine has:** a working unit is offered defend and work alone; a unit with no man ashore the activity alone; a unit with no man ashore and none aboard, one before its day and one held apart no form at all; a march only to the hexes the tool lists as joined to the unit's by land.
- **When spent has three answers,** as the engine has: hold, fall back, or left to supply (hold where the unit can be fed where it stands, otherwise fall back).
- **Work offers the four jobs and "the staff's choice of job",** and says where the unit's hex has no base of its side, in which it makes no work.
- **An order signed is not taken back singly.** The table has no such thing for a force's orders either; rule 4's words on it are withdrawn.
- **The form says, before signing, what an order costs that a player would not expect:** a march or an attack leaves the unit's fieldworks; a march takes only the men ashore.

### Rest

- **Rest is offered for a land unit and an air group, and not for a task force.** A force's fatigue cuts only its chance of sighting, and a force at normal readiness is alerted, and tires, whenever a contact is near: a rest order could not promise rest.
- **A land unit's rest is defend and no march.** Its activity is left as it is: activity plays no part in rest, a working unit takes no other, and a training unit would lose its training.
- **An air group's rest is the rest mission.**
- **Rule 11 is withdrawn.** A unit defending in a fight does rest; what stops rest is marching, firing on attack, and being under bombs or shells, which is not known beforehand. Rest is not offered to a unit with no man ashore, or where the figures of fatigue are not loaded.
- **The hold is of a new kind, a rest hold,** its code added after the two there are. It ends at dusk, before the commander's orders of that dusk hold anything and before the staff is asked, where the unit's fatigue at the end of the day before is at or below the figure. An event records that the rest ended, seen by the unit's own side, and the evening's report says it.
- **A later order of the commander's to the unit ends the rest and keeps the unit his:** the rest hold becomes an ordinary hold by order, and does not end by itself. A search arc or another setting of an air group that leaves it on the rest mission does not count.
- **Rest for a unit already held** makes its hold a rest hold: it will end.
- **The figure is one for each kind,** `rested_fatigue` in the ground figures and in the fatigue figures, each a placeholder of 10, the level below which the table says "fresh". The frozen copies do not gain it and no frozen game has a rest hold.
- **With command levels on,** a rest hold and its end are in the command's own state as any hold is.
- **The card says, before signing, what a rest costs:** that an order using the unit waits for it. A unit that a landing is about to lift holds up the units behind it.
- **A resting unit that cannot recover** (under fire each day, or aboard) stays held until the commander releases it; the card shows its fatigue, and the report says nothing until it is rested.

Rule card for rest: reads a rest-held unit's fatigue and its kind's `rested_fatigue`. Writes the hold's end, or its kind where the commander orders the unit. Draws: none. The layout rises; the rules label and the agent's interface rise; recorded games are remade; the records of closed proofs are not altered.

Rulebook: chapter 8 (holds; a rest hold and when it ends) and the glossary ("Hold", "Rest hold").

A scenario of its own and its hand calculation are written by the architect before the rest is delegated. The form for the three orders needs none and may be built first.

## As built, Nov 22, 2026: the three orders

Built: "Orders to a land unit" as amended on review. Rest is not built: it waits for the architect's scenario and hand calculation.

- **The card** of a land unit of the player's own (`landOrder` in `table/index.html`) says "In whose hands" as the force's card does, with "Return it to the staff" where it is held, and carries an ORDER form: posture (defend, or attack with "when spent": hold, fall back, left to supply), march ("Not marching" or "Marching to" where it is, a list of the places of the tool's `march_to` by name and compass word, and halt where it marches), and activity (stand ready, train, work with the four jobs and "the staff's choice of job"). What stands is marked.
- **The chart** marks the `march_to` hexes while a march is chosen; a touch on one chooses it.
- **What is offered:** a working unit, defend and work; a unit with no man ashore, the activity alone; a unit with no man ashore and none aboard, one before its day and one held apart, no form. Work says where the unit's hex has no base of its side that it makes no work there. The costs are said before signing where they apply: that an attack or a march leaves its fieldworks (only where it has fieldworks), and that a march takes only the men ashore (only where men are aboard).
- **Holding first:** at dusk the form sends the hold of the land unit and then the order, as the force's and the air group's forms do. A refusal is said "NOT SENT" with the engine's reason cut short.
- **The tools lacked nothing.** `table/check_screen.py` tries the form's calls (hold, posture with when spent, march, work with a job, a refusal and the release) against the tool.
- **With decision 0219** an order given at 0600 or 1200 that is not a posture is kept for 1800, and the form says "Acts at 1800, for the coming day." before and after signing.
