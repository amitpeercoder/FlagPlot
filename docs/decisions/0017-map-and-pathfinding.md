# 0017: The real map, and pathfinding

Status: confirmed by the designer, Nov 10, 2026, as stage 4b of scenario 4.

## The gap in the spec

The spec asks for a hex map of the slice at 40 nm per hex, with a stored latitude and longitude per hex, and says its size is "to be fixed when the map is drawn". It does not say how a hex gets its type, what projection puts a place in a hex, or how a route finds its way round land. Decision 0005 left pathfinding until the real map.

## Decisions

### The map

1. **The map is generated by a tool from open coastline data** and committed as data. The source is Natural Earth, 1:10 million, land polygons and minor islands, version 5.1.1, public domain. The source files are not committed. Their checksums are recorded in the generated file's header.
2. **Projection.** Hex (0,0) is centred at 12 degrees south, 160 degrees east. A point's offset from there is:

   - east, in nm = (longitude - 160) x 60 x 0.978148
   - north, in nm = (latitude + 12) x 60

   0.978148 is the cosine of 12 degrees. Hexes are pointy-topped and 40 nm between centres, and north is decreasing r (decision 0012), so a hex centre (q, r) lies 40 x (q + r/2) nm east and 34.641016 x r nm south of the origin. A point belongs to the hex whose centre is nearest.
3. **This projection is for the slice only.** East-west distances are true at 12 degrees south and up to about 5 percent off at the northern and southern edges. The whole Pacific needs its own scheme, in step 5.
4. **Extent.** Every hex whose centre lies from 1 to 24 degrees south and from 145 to 172 degrees east.
5. **A hex's type comes from the share of its area that is land,** measured on one lattice of points 2 nm apart laid over the whole map. Each point belongs to the hex whose centre is nearest:
   - 80 percent or more: land. Ships cannot enter.
   - More than none and less than 80 percent: coast. Ships can enter, and it can hold a base.
   - None: deep sea.

   The share is stored for every hex. Shallow sea is not used yet: there is no depth data.
6. **A hex stands for its largest body of water.** Inside each hex the sea points of the lattice fall into bodies of water: groups of points joined north, south, east or west. The largest is the hex's main body. Where two are the same size, it is the one holding the point furthest north, then furthest west.

   **A hex that holds a listed anchorage stands for the water the anchorage is on.** Its main body is the body of water nearest the anchorage's sourced position, if that body has at least 5 lattice points. The list is `data/map/south_pacific_anchors.csv`. Lunga Point is on the north coast of Guadalcanal, on Ironbottom Sound. Without the list, its hex stood for the larger water off the south coast, and was closed toward Savo Island and Tulagi to the north. If two listed anchorages in one hex are on different bodies of water, the map tool stops with an error naming both, and one has to be dropped from the list.
7. **Two neighbouring hexes are joined only if their main bodies of water touch:** a sea point of one main body lies next to a sea point of the other. Otherwise the side between them is closed, and ships cannot cross it. The closed sides are worked out by the map tool and stored with the map. Nothing is drawn by hand.

   So a hex with a long island across it belongs to the sea on one side of the island, and a ship in it cannot step to a hex that belongs to the other side. A strait stays open because the water joins there.
8. **Sites are not on the generated map.** Port and airfield sizes for real bases are order-of-battle facts and come with them.
9. **The map is one row per hex** in a CSV file: q, r, type, land percent, latitude and longitude of the centre, how many bodies of water it has, and its closed sides. This is the format decision 0009 expected.

### Pathfinding

10. **A task force follows a shortest path over hexes ships can enter, never crossing a closed side.** The distance from a hex to a target is the fewest such steps. From any hex, the next step is the neighbour whose distance to the target is one less, the first such neighbour in the fixed direction order.
11. **On open water this is the path decision 0005 already gave.** The greedy step and the shortest-path step are the same when nothing is in the way. Every earlier scenario keeps its numbers.
12. **The path is not stored.** The map does not change, so the next step is worked out from the map each time and always comes out the same. A route is still its waypoints.
13. **A route is rejected if any waypoint cannot be reached** from the one before it: it is off the map, is land, or lies across land with no way round. This replaces the rejection for a straight path that crosses land.
14. **The turn-back choice of decision 0005 uses path distance,** not straight-line hexes.
15. **Search and other rules that measure distance still use straight-line hex distance.** An aircraft does not go round an island.

   *Nov 14, 2026: the distance an aircraft flies is the straight line between hex centres, by decision 0040.*

## Why

- **Generated, not drawn.** A map traced from another game's map would be that game's data. A map generated from public-domain coastlines can be regenerated, checked and extended.
- **A share of land, with a high bar for "land".** With a low bar, Guadalcanal and every other island would close their hexes to ships and the Slot would be a wall. With a high bar, only New Guinea and the large islands' interiors are closed.
- **Closed sides, because the high bar alone is wrong.** The first map had no closed sides. Nearly every hex of New Britain, New Ireland, Bougainville, the Solomons and New Caledonia came out as coast, open in every direction. A ship could cross New Britain from its north coast at (-9,-12) to its south coast at (-10,-9) in 3 steps. With closed sides that is 7. This was found on inspecting the generated map, before it was merged, and the designer chose the fix on Nov 10, 2026.

  The architect's first example of the defect was wrong and is recorded here so it is not repeated: a path from Rabaul to the Solomon Sea in 160 nm. That path does not cross New Britain. It leaves Blanche Bay and rounds the east side of the Gazelle Peninsula, and is about right.
- **Derived, not drawn.** WitP:AE also closes hex sides. Ours come from the coastline data by a stated rule, so they can be regenerated and checked.
- **Not storing the path** adds no state and nothing to the hash. A stored path would be a second copy of what the map already says.

## Legibility test

Run on Nov 10, 2026. A Haiku model was given this record, the event logs of the island scenario and the real-map transit, and the six sides of Lunga Point's hex. It answered all thirteen questions correctly: why the force steamed 400 nm, why it went south and not north (worked at the hex where the choice is made), what happens to an unreachable or land waypoint, why the path is not stored, why the real-map transit needed one extra hex, why a hex that is 59 percent land is open with four sides closed, and predictions for a hex of 85 percent land, for two anchorages on opposite coasts, and for an aircraft measuring across an island.

What it found unclear led to two changes above: which coast of Guadalcanal each sentence meant, and what happens when two listed anchorages disagree. It also noted that the logs do not say a force's hex is the nearest centre. That is decision 0005, and belongs in the briefing text when briefings exist.

## Deferred

- A hex with water on both sides of an island counts as one side only: the larger, or the side of a listed anchorage. Two anchorages on opposite sides of one hex cannot both be served.
- Going round an island costs less than it should. The hex at the east end of Guadalcanal has one body of water that wraps round the tip, so north and south join inside it.
- Straits narrower than the lattice can show, about 2 to 4 nm, may come out closed. They go on the same list.
- **Hex steps overstate distance.** A step is 40 nm in one of six directions, so a course between two of them zigzags, and due north or south costs about 15 percent more than the straight line. Noumea to Espiritu Santo is 405 nm straight and 520 nm by hex. Speeds and ranges from sources therefore do not convert as directly as the spec assumed. This is true of any hex map and is left for step 2 to weigh. *(Nov 14, 2026: settled for ships by decision 0058: a ship is charged straight-line miles where the way is clear)*
- Shallow water, reefs and anchorages.
- Port and airfield sizes for real bases.
- A cache for path distances, if the 180-day soak needs it.
- Weather, and terrain beyond land or sea.
- The whole Pacific.
