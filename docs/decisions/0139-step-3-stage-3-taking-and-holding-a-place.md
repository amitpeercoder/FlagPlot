## What stage 3 is

Stages 1 and 2 gave the staff supply and the fighting at sea and in the air. A commander can say "hold this place", "deny these waters", "neutralise that base", "keep this base supplied", and the staff does it on both sides.

What he cannot yet say is the thing the campaign is about: take that island and keep it. The rules for it are built and proved by scripted scenarios: land units carried by sea, loading and rehearsal for a landing, the fight ashore, a base that changes side, marching, bombardment, training, skills, work, an airfield built and mended, a beaten side that falls back. No staff uses them. Stage 3 writes the doctrines that do, and then plays the whole 39 days with nobody ordering a unit.

## What the commander can say

Three things are added to the ends of decisions 0116, 0117, 0120 and 0123.

1. **Take a place,** by a date or as soon as may be: a base or a hex of land. It is met when the place is the side's.
2. **Leave a place:** what is there is taken off and flown out. It is met when nothing of the side's is left that could be taken off.
3. **Control an island,** the area intent stated alone: the island's named places are held and their garrisons supplied above their reserve (`docs/step3-spec.md`, "The play it must support"). The staff draws the rest of the tree from it: what must be taken, held, supplied, denied and neutralised. A commander may still state any of those himself.

"Hold a place" is widened: today it forms cover from the air. With men ashore it also sets their posture, digs them in, builds the defences and keeps them fed.

## An operation is a tree with phases

Decision 0116, items 15 to 17, said a plan is the tree of an intent with its order in time. Stage 3 builds that for "take a place":

| Phase | What the staff forms under it | It ends when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare | The units chosen, trained and rehearsed; the shipping gathered and loaded for the landing; the base it sails from built up; what it needs to know looked for | Every requirement is met, or the commander is told which is not and by how much |
| Move | The embarkation, the passage and its escort, the cover, the bombardment before the landing | The force is off the beach |
| Land and fight | The landing, the march, the attack or the defence | The place is taken, or the staff reports that it cannot be |
| Follow up | The supply that follows, the garrison, the field put to use | It hands over to "hold a place" |

The leaves of stage 1 stand: leave to prepare lets the first phase run, and nothing sails without leave to launch. A proposal shows the tree before it is formed (0116, 16).

## Reconnaissance, as little as the stage needs

The designer's direction (`docs/design/intelligence.md`, "Reconnaissance as part of an operation") is that a commander who orders a place taken has said he needs to know what is on it. Today a side learns of the enemy's men ashore only once its own men are in the hex. A staff that plans a landing would plan it blind.

Stage 3 builds the smallest part of that direction that mends this:

4. **A source and a grade on a count of the enemy ashore:** nothing seen; an aircraft sent to look; men in contact. Three of the note's five.
5. **A reconnaissance mission for an air group:** it flies to a place and reports the men there, with an error wider than men in contact have. The figures are placeholders.
6. **A plan's need to know:** "take a place" is not ready to launch until it holds a count of the grade its risk asks for, or says plainly what it goes without. At press it goes on nothing better than an old report, and says so.

Left for later: patrols and men put ashore to watch, how hard a reconnaissance is pushed, being seen and what the enemy makes of it, and harassment.

## The staff's setup of the 39 days

The slice is scripted today: each force is sent out on the day the record gives. The spec ("Two setups of the campaign") asks for a second setup with no scripted order.

7. **A day from which a force may be used,** as a dated entry in a forces limit. Not built; it is the first thing this stage builds.
8. **Each force's day is the day the record first has it sent,** from the rows the scripted setup already rests on, and declared as made up where no row gives one. A side knows its own forces' days and not the enemy's.
9. **Each side's intents are stated by the scenario,** written by the architect before any run and set down in the proof's record, as decision 0128 did.
10. **Land formations get parents:** regiment and battalion under a division, as data (decision 0116, 14), so that the staff can choose and move a formation whole. A formation is not a command in this stage.
11. **The scripted setup stays as it is,** for the measures against the record.

## What it must pass

Set before anything is run. Each step's own scenario passes its hand calculation, and with no intent stated every staff still does nothing. Then, on the staff's setup of the 39 days, both sides from the intents the scenario states, release build, live figures, seeds 1 to 1,000:

1. **It runs.** Every invariant holds on every seed; the same start gives the same game. Judged.
2. **No order is refused that the staff should have known would be.** No staff order is refused on any seed. Judged.
3. **The Allied side does something.** It lands men on Guadalcanal on 900 seeds or more, takes the airfield on 800 or more, lands supply there after the landing on 800 or more, and flies from the field on 600 or more. Judged.
4. **The Japanese side does something.** It lands men on Guadalcanal after the Allied landing on 800 seeds or more, attacks the airfield ashore on 600 or more, and bombards it from the sea or sends a surface force into its waters by night on 600 or more. Judged.
5. **Better than a side given nothing.** Each side from its intents, against the other from its intents, has a higher median margin on the objectives scale than the same side given no orders. Judged.
6. **Legibility.** Twenty-four reasons picked by a rule written before the run, four from each of six of the doctrines above: a reader given a reason alone says what the staff is trying to do and what would change its mind. Pass: 20 of 24. Judged.
7. **Speed.** A staffed day of the 39 days takes no more than 40 ms, the median over the campaign, on the machine the earlier measures were made on. Judged.
8. **The operation, on placeholder forces** (the spec's built invasion): the parts that need no trial. An island asked for by a date; preparation authorised with leave to launch withheld; a raw unit trained and then rehearsed, the fitter of two units picked for the assault, a working unit set to the job the operation waits on, with nobody giving an activity by hand; a quiet interval; leave asked; the landing, the fight and the supply that follows, reported against the plan; a save and a resume twice with nothing changed. Judged, by its hand calculation.

**Reported and not judged:**

- **Who holds the airfield on day 39,** and each side's losses. The ground figures are placeholders that one tuning round has touched and that cannot yet fit the Tenaru and the ridge together (decision 0109). Who wins ashore is not yet something the game can be held to.
- **The same run with command levels on** and the live report figures.
- **The staff's orders beside the script's,** day by day, as the spec asks.
- **A model's game** of the 39 days by intent alone, as in stage 2.

Nothing is tuned to pass. A condition that fails is reported with its seeds and its cause from the staff's own reasons, and goes to the designer.

## Risks

- **The fight ashore is not tuned.** A staff can be right and lose, or wrong and win, on figures nobody trusts yet. The conditions above ask what each side does and not who wins.
- **It is the largest stage so far:** eleven doctrines, nine steps.
- **Thirty-nine days find what a week does not.** Stage 2's week passed; a staff that runs out of ships, fuel or rested groups on day 30 has not been seen yet.

## Amended by the designer, Nov 17, 2026

Step 3's record (decision 0142) has the commander name the transport force, the units and the base they sail from; the staff picks among them. The designer read it and set out the order a staff works in: the enemy first, then what is needed, then the lift, then the risk to the force on the way, then whether the men can be kept fed and reinforced, and only then the landing made ready. Asked whether the staff should assemble the force itself in this stage or in stage 4, he said: in stage 3, before the proof. Three things follow.

1. **A new step 7: the force assembled by the staff.** "Take a place" may be stated with no forces named. The staff then works in the designer's order:
   - **The enemy:** its estimate of the men at the place, with its grade (step 6).
   - **The men needed,** by the risk, and the units that give them, chosen by what each is good at from every unit the side may use that is not held and serves no higher intent.
   - **The lift:** the transports those men need, from the ships the side may use; and the base they sail from.
   - **The risk on the way:** the escort, the cover and the bombardment the risk asks for, from the forces the side may use (steps 5 and 6).
   - **Whether it can be kept up:** item 2.
   - **What it lacks,** in numbers, where it cannot find one of these: "this needs four transports and the side has two". At preserve the force it then forms nothing; at balanced and press it goes with what it has and says so, as decision 0142 has it for men.

   A forces limit still narrows the choice, as any limit does. The step has its own record and hand calculation first. Steps 7 and 8 of the plan as confirmed become 8 and 9.
2. **A check that the place can be held, before leave to launch is asked,** built with the supply that follows in step 4. It gives the daily need of the men to be ashore, what the base they sail from holds above its reserve, how long a run there and back takes and what it carries, and so whether the men can be fed. The supply that goes with the landing is then what lasts until the first run that follows can arrive, with a margin by the risk; the 15 days of decision 0142, item 16, stand only until this is built.
3. **The reasons of "take a place" are given in the designer's order:** the estimate, the men wanted, the ships, what is short, the days. The architect's, as wording.

**The proof.** In step 9 each side's stated intents name no forces. The conditions and their pass marks are as confirmed; none is changed.

**Not in this stage:** weather; ammunition as a stock apart from supply (the game has one); reinforcement as an act of the staff beyond the units a first lift left behind, which step 4 sends after; and looking ahead to an operation not yet stated. The last is stage 4's, with the choice of the day and the beach.
