# 0226: What is known of the enemy, on one paper

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 23, 2026, on the designer's words at the table: "we have an oob screen for our own forces. Could/should we have the enemy's oob as well, which is based upon collected claims/observations. Or maybe it's some sort of an overall collected intelligence screen with whatever is established regarding the enemy before and along the scenario?" The architect answered that it should be a paper of what is known and claimed, not an order of battle, and the designer said to plan it.

Summary: A commander at the table can open a list of his own forces. Of the enemy he has a contact's card, a place's card, the chart and the evening's report, each showing one thing, and nothing that puts them together or keeps what a contact said after it is dropped. This adds one paper, THE ENEMY, opened from the STAFF menu, that gathers what the side holds of the enemy: what it was told before the game, what it has seen, what it claims to have done to him, what it believes is ashore, his bases, and his submarines. It is built from what the side's own tools already answer. It adds no knowledge and makes no estimate. The staff's estimate of what the enemy has left, and reports that say more than a count and one kind, are named as the two steps after it and are not planned here.

Standing: Planned on Nov 23, 2026; to be reviewed.

## Why

What a side holds of the enemy today (rules chapters 1, 3, 7 and 10; `docs/engineering/truth-and-belief.md`):

- **Contacts:** each a number, a hex, a count of ships, the heaviest kind seen, an age, and the report that last made or refreshed it. A report holds no identity, so a side cannot tell that two sightings are of one force. A contact not refreshed is dropped, and a strike that finds nothing drops it.
- **Claims:** what its own people believe they did: ships believed sunk, hits believed scored and the kind of ship attacked, aircraft believed shot down and destroyed on the ground, with their types where made out. Claims run high by the rules.
- **Estimates of men ashore:** a count for a hex, with its day and the report it came from; and the opening estimate a scenario gives.
- **The enemy's bases:** number, name, hex and the side that holds it, for every base on the side's map.
- **Reports of submarines and suspected waters.**
- **What it was told before the game:** the directive's account of the enemy.
- **Its own events:** every report, claim and sighting it was ever given, by day.

What the table shows of that: the first five each on a card or the chart, for what is held now. Nothing shows what was held and is no longer, and nothing sets a claim beside the contact it was made on.

Why not an order of battle: a list of the enemy's forces would need to know which sightings are one force, which the engine withholds on purpose, and would put claimed sinkings beside seen ships as if they were of one certainty.

## The rules

1. **One paper, THE ENEMY, opened from the STAFF menu,** on a sheet as the plan's paper and the war diary are. It reads the side's own answers and nothing else. A watcher of a game reads the paper of the seat watched.
2. **It has six parts, in this order, each left out where it has nothing:**
   - **AS WE WERE TOLD:** the directive's sentences on the enemy, word for word, and the opening estimates of men ashore.
   - **SEEN:** the contacts held now, newest first, each as the chart's card says it (count, heaviest kind seen, place, age) and linked to its card; then **LOST SIGHT OF**, the contacts dropped, each with the day and pulse it was last reported, what it then said, and why it was dropped where the side was told (a strike found nothing; no report since).
   - **CLAIMED:** by day, what the side's own units claim: ships believed sunk and hit, with the kind attacked and the contact struck where the claim names one; aircraft believed shot down and destroyed on the ground, with types where made out. A total is given for each kind of claim, under the words "claimed, not confirmed". No total is taken from one part and subtracted from another.
   - **ASHORE:** each estimate of the enemy's men, with its place, its age and where it came from.
   - **HIS BASES:** the bases on the side's map, each linked to its place, with what the side holds of it (an estimate of men, aircraft believed destroyed on the ground there).
   - **HIS SUBMARINES:** the reports held, with their age, and the waters suspected, as told.
3. **Every line says how it is known and how old it is,** in the words the cards already use, and every place and contact in it is a link, as everywhere on the table.
4. **Nothing in it is an estimate of the staff's.** It says what was reported and what was claimed. It does not say how many carriers the enemy has left, whether two contacts are one force, or whether a claim is true.
5. **Nothing in it is new knowledge.** Each line is one the side could already read on a card, on the chart, in the evening's report or in its war diary. A test puts every handle and number the paper shows against the side's own answers.
6. **The staff speaks as "we"** in any sentence of its own ("We lost sight of it on 5 June"), and the paper explains no rule.
7. **No points and no result** are shown in it during play.

## What it needs

- **From the page:** the paper, its part in the STAFF menu, and the links.
- **From the tools:** the contacts dropped and the claims of earlier days are in the side's events, by day. If reading them day by day is too slow for a long game, one tool gives them gathered (the side's reports of contacts with what each said and when it was dropped; the side's claims by day). It gives nothing the side's events do not, and is a reading: no state, no hash. The build says which it needed.

## What a side learns by it

Nothing. See rule 5.

## What it does not do, and what comes after

- **It makes no estimate of the enemy's strength.** That is the next step: the staff's estimate of what the enemy has, by kind, as a range with its basis, made from the same six parts by a written rule. It is a rule and is planned on its own.
- **It does not make a report say more.** A report is a count and one kind. Counts said as "about", and what a force is made of, are a change to what a report holds (rules chapter 3) and are planned on their own, after the estimate.
- **It keeps no picture by command level** beyond the seat's own: the paper is the seat's.

## The designer's answer on the name, Nov 23, 2026

"Yes, Intelligence or something even more militaristic (I don't recall the term, something with 'SIG'...)". The term he had in mind is most likely SIGINT, signals intelligence, which is knowledge got from the enemy's radio. This paper holds sightings, claims and estimates and nothing from radio, so that name would say something untrue of it. The paper is named INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY, the name a staff of the period gave the paper that gathers what is known of the enemy, and it is opened from the STAFF menu as INTELLIGENCE. Where this record says THE ENEMY, read INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. If radio intelligence is ever built (`docs/design/intelligence.md`, standing sources), it gets a part of its own in the paper under its own name.
