# 0226: What is known of the enemy, on one paper

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 23, 2026, on the designer's words at the table: "we have an oob screen for our own forces. Could/should we have the enemy's oob as well, which is based upon collected claims/observations. Or maybe it's some sort of an overall collected intelligence screen with whatever is established regarding the enemy before and along the scenario?" The architect answered that it should be a paper of what is known and claimed, not an order of battle, and the designer said to plan it.

Summary: A commander at the table can open a list of his own forces. Of the enemy he has a contact's card, a place's card, the chart and the evening's report, each showing one thing, and nothing that puts them together or keeps what a contact said after it is dropped. This adds one paper, THE ENEMY, opened from the STAFF menu, that gathers what the side holds of the enemy: what it was told before the game, what it has seen, what it claims to have done to him, what it believes is ashore, his bases, and his submarines. It is built from what the side's own tools already answer. It adds no knowledge and makes no estimate. The staff's estimate of what the enemy has left, and reports that say more than a count and one kind, are named as the two steps after it and are not planned here.

Standing: Planned on Nov 23, 2026; to be reviewed.

## Why

What a side holds of the enemy today (rules chapters 1, 3, 7 and 10; `docs/engineering/truth-and-belief.md`):

- **Contacts:** each a number, a hex, a count of ships, the heaviest kind seen, an age, and the report that last made or refreshed it. A report holds no identity, so a side cannot tell that two sightings are of one force. A contact not refreshed is dropped, and a strike that finds nothing drops it.
- **Claims:** what its own people believe they did: ships believed sunk, hits believed scored and the kind of ship attacked, aircraft believed shot down and destroyed on the ground, with their types where made out. Claims run high by the rules.
- **Estimates of men ashore:** a count for a hex, with its day and the report it came from; and the opening estimate a scenario gives.
- **The enemy's bases:** number, name, hex and the side that holds it, for every base on the side's map.
- **Reports of submarines and suspected waters.**
- **What it was told before the game:** the directive's account of the enemy.
- **Its own events:** every report, claim and sighting it was ever given, by day.

What the table shows of that: the first five each on a card or the chart, for what is held now. Nothing shows what was held and is no longer, and nothing sets a claim beside the contact it was made on.

Why not an order of battle: a list of the enemy's forces would need to know which sightings are one force, which the engine withholds on purpose, and would put claimed sinkings beside seen ships as if they were of one certainty.

## The rules

1. **One paper, THE ENEMY, opened from the STAFF menu,** on a sheet as the plan's paper and the war diary are. It reads the side's own answers and nothing else. A watcher of a game reads the paper of the seat watched.
2. **It has six parts, in this order, each left out where it has nothing:**
   - **AS WE WERE TOLD:** the directive's sentences on the enemy, word for word, and the opening estimates of men ashore.
   - **SEEN:** the contacts held now, newest first, each as the chart's card says it (count, heaviest kind seen, place, age) and linked to its card; then **LOST SIGHT OF**, the contacts dropped, each with the day and pulse it was last reported, what it then said, and why it was dropped where the side was told (a strike found nothing; no report since).
   - **CLAIMED:** by day, what the side's own units claim: ships believed sunk and hit, with the kind attacked and the contact struck where the claim names one; aircraft believed shot down and destroyed on the ground, with types where made out. A total is given for each kind of claim, under the words "claimed, not confirmed". No total is taken from one part and subtracted from another.
   - **ASHORE:** each estimate of the enemy's men, with its place, its age and where it came from.
   - **HIS BASES:** the bases on the side's map, each linked to its place, with what the side holds of it (an estimate of men, aircraft believed destroyed on the ground there).
   - **HIS SUBMARINES:** the reports held, with their age, and the waters suspected, as told.
3. **Every line says how it is known and how old it is,** in the words the cards already use, and every place and contact in it is a link, as everywhere on the table.
4. **Nothing in it is an estimate of the staff's.** It says what was reported and what was claimed. It does not say how many carriers the enemy has left, whether two contacts are one force, or whether a claim is true.
5. **Nothing in it is new knowledge.** Each line is one the side could already read on a card, on the chart, in the evening's report or in its war diary. A test puts every handle and number the paper shows against the side's own answers.
6. **The staff speaks as "we"** in any sentence of its own ("We lost sight of it on 5 June"), and the paper explains no rule.
7. **No points and no result** are shown in it during play.

## What it needs

- **From the page:** the paper, its part in the STAFF menu, and the links.
- **From the tools:** the contacts dropped and the claims of earlier days are in the side's events, by day. If reading them day by day is too slow for a long game, one tool gives them gathered (the side's reports of contacts with what each said and when it was dropped; the side's claims by day). It gives nothing the side's events do not, and is a reading: no state, no hash. The build says which it needed.

## What a side learns by it

Nothing. See rule 5.

## What it does not do, and what comes after

- **It makes no estimate of the enemy's strength.** That is the next step: the staff's estimate of what the enemy has, by kind, as a range with its basis, made from the same six parts by a written rule. It is a rule and is planned on its own.
- **It does not make a report say more.** A report is a count and one kind. Counts said as "about", and what a force is made of, are a change to what a report holds (rules chapter 3) and are planned on their own, after the estimate.
- **It keeps no picture by command level** beyond the seat's own: the paper is the seat's.

## Amended on review, Nov 23, 2026

A fresh reader was given this record, the rules on what a side may know, the agent's tools and the page. The architect accepts the findings. Where this section and the rules above differ, this section holds.

### What the review found

- **A claim names no contact.** It holds no identity of an enemy unit, and its kind is the kind the contact was taken for when the strike was launched, not a kind attacked. Tying a claim to a contact would be the paper linking what the side was never told is linked.
- **Rule 5 was untrue of the plan's own parts.** A total of claims over days and a list of contacts lost are on no card today. Gathering what a side holds is allowed (the measures of "neutralise" and "deny" already sum a side's claims over days), but it is not "a line the side could already read".
- **What a side was told before the game cannot be read back by the page.** Opening estimates and opening contacts are recorded on day 0, the `events` tool gives no day 0, and a later report replaces an opening estimate in belief.
- **Reading the side's events day by day is too slow and too coarse.** Each call walks the whole game, each is written to the transcript, and events carry words, which the page would have to parse. Claims need no events: belief holds every claim. The plan left this to the builder; it is the architect's to decide.
- **The directive is the table's file, not an answer of the seat's,** and nothing marks which of its sentences are about the enemy. Choosing them would be an editor's reading.
- **With command levels on, "the paper is the seat's" fails:** the seat's contacts are its command's picture and are numbered apart from the side's events, a picture's ageing records nothing, and a lower seat's list of enemy bases holds the side's own other bases.
- **One force is several contacts over time, and one ship is several claims.** Counting either misleads.
- **The paper left out the main evidence of the enemy: what he did to us.**
- **The two next steps were in the wrong order** by the design note's own layers.

### The paper, as amended

1. **A tool gives it.** `intelligence`, in `sim-agent`, reads the asking seat's picture and the side's own events, day 0 among them, and nothing else. It keeps no state and is in no hash. A watcher has it for the seat watched. The page draws what it answers and parses no words. It raises the agent interface by one.
2. **Its parts, in this order, each left out where it has nothing:**
   - **SEEN:** the contacts held, newest first: ships reported, the heaviest seen, where, how old.
   - **LOST SIGHT OF:** each contact dropped, with the day and pulse of its last report, what that report said, and which of the two things the side was told: no report since, or a strike found nothing there. Its last place can be shown on the chart.
   - **CLAIMED:** every claim the seat's picture holds, by day: ships believed sunk, hits believed scored, with what the target was taken for where the claim has it; aircraft believed shot down and destroyed on the ground, with types where made out. Under it one line of totals by kind of claim (sunk, hits, shot down, on the ground) and never by kind of ship, headed "claimed, not confirmed".
   - **WHAT THE ENEMY HAS DONE:** by day, the raids on the side's bases and the attacks on its ships and forces, with the aircraft types recognised where the side was told them, and the surface actions met.
   - **ASHORE:** each estimate of the enemy's men held, with place, age and source; and an estimate since cleared, said as found empty.
   - **ENEMY BASES:** only the bases of which something is held: an estimate of men there, or aircraft claimed destroyed on the ground in its hex from the air (the rule the measure of "neutralise" uses). A base that has changed side is listed under the side it has now, with the claims made while it was the enemy's said as of then.
   - **ENEMY SUBMARINES:** the reports held, with their age, and the waters suspected, as told.
   - **BEFORE THE GAME:** the contacts and the estimates of men the side was given at the start, as given; and a link to the directives. No sentence of a directive is quoted.
3. **Words.** A contact is "a report" or "a contact", never "a force", and contacts are never counted as forces. A claim's kind is "taken for". The enemy is "the enemy", as the cards say, not "his".
4. **Rule 5 is replaced:** the paper is worked from the seat's own answers and events and nothing else. Its test: two worlds that differ only in what the side cannot know give the same answer, byte for byte; and every number and handle in the answer is one the seat's other tools give or the side's events hold.
5. **With command levels on,** the paper has SEEN, ASHORE, ENEMY BASES (the enemy's only) and ENEMY SUBMARINES from the seat's own picture, and CLAIMED from the seat's picture. LOST SIGHT OF, WHAT THE ENEMY HAS DONE and BEFORE THE GAME are given only at a seat that plays the whole side, and the paper says of the others "not kept at this command". No game at the table has command levels on today.
6. **When the game is over** the paper still shows belief only. What was true is the end paper's.
7. **What it leaves out, said in the rulebook and not on the paper:** the enemy's objectives as told (they carry points); radio intelligence (not built); enemy ships by name (never told).
8. **The plan's paper already shows one reading of the enemy,** "what we took the enemy to have", from a dry run's assumptions. This paper does not repeat it and does not claim the table makes no reading anywhere.
9. **On the chart:** SHOW ON CHART marks the last place of each contact lost. A lost contact's line opens what is known of how it was last reported.

### What comes after, in the design note's order

1. **Claims checked against what is seen later, and a record of what the side believed each day** (the note's first layer). It gives LOST SIGHT OF and CLAIMED something firmer than a tally.
2. **Reports that say more, each with its source:** a count said as "about", what a force is made of.
3. **The staff's appreciation of the enemy's strength,** made one with what a dry run assumes of him. "Appreciation" and not "estimate", which already means the estimate of men ashore.

### The architect's statements for the tool's test and the page's check

**The tool's test** is `crates/sim-agent/tests/intelligence_tool.rs`. Its doc comment holds the statements, 0 to 16, and the hand calculation; each test function is named for its statements and is marked as not built. It adds no scenario. It runs on five that exist, each on the frozen step 1 figures as every test of that crate does:

| Scenario | Seed | For |

| `24_shells_and_estimates.toml` | 1942 | Both seats: a contact made, refreshed and dropped for its age; a base shelled; an estimate ashore made, replaced and found empty; an opening estimate; claims from the sea; a base found |
| `05b_strike.toml` | 11 | A claim with what the target was taken for, and a contact a strike found nothing at |

The numbers the tool gives are gathered, not worked, so the test puts each line against the line the side's own tools gave (the briefing of each morning, `list_contacts`, `list_bases`, `explain`) and works by hand only what a file or a frozen figure fixes: the opening contact and estimates as given, and the pulse in which a contact reaches the age of 5 and is dropped. No scenario at hand has a base that changes side with claims made on it, so that line of item 2 is built and not tested; the first scenario that has one adds the statement.

**What the plan left to the builder, ruled here.**

1. **The answer names its parts.** It has a list `parts`, the names of the parts that hold something in the paper's order (`seen`, `lost_sight_of`, `claimed`, `enemy_has_done`, `ashore`, `enemy_bases`, `enemy_submarines`, `before_the_game`), one key for each of them, and `not_kept` for the parts a seat is not given. Reason: the page then draws the order it is handed and decides nothing, and the order can be tested.
2. **A line is of the type the tool that already says that thing uses,** with its day and pulse added where that type has none. Reason: one wording of a contact, a claim, a raid and an estimate across the tools, and the test of provenance is then an equality.
3. **The tool writes no sentence.** Its only words are those of suspected waters, as told. Reason: the page parses no words (item 1).
4. **Order.** Contacts held: the least age first, and of two of one age the higher number first. Contacts lost: the latest dropped first. Claims and what the enemy has done: the latest day first, and within a day in the order told. Enemy bases: by number. Reason: the plan's "newest first" for contacts, carried to the dated parts so the paper reads one way.
5. **What a lost contact's line holds:** the day, pulse and content of the last report that made or refreshed it, and of the dropping its day and pulse and one of two words, `no_report_since` with the age it was dropped at, or `strike_found_nothing` with the place flown to. A contact given at the start and never refreshed has a last report of day 0 with no pulse.
6. **What the enemy has done** is four things and no other: air raids on the side's bases, shellings of them from the sea, attacks on its ships (from the air, by gunfire and torpedo, a submarine's among them), and the fights at sea its forces met, each naming the side's own force only. A fight ashore is not in it: it is the side's own affair of the day, in the evening's report, and what it tells of the enemy is the estimate under ASHORE.
7. **Enemy bases: a claim from the sea lists no base.** Item 2 says "from the air (the rule the measure of 'neutralise' uses)" and that is kept: one rule for what is claimed at a base. A bombardment's claim is in CLAIMED with its place. A base found and then found empty holds nothing and is not listed; ASHORE says it was found empty.
8. **A report of a submarine that was dropped is not shown.** Item 2 says "the reports held". It holds no count and no kind, so its line would say only that something was once reported there.
9. **BEFORE THE GAME is as given, on every day,** whether the contact has since been dropped or the estimate replaced; such a contact is also under SEEN or LOST SIGHT OF. Suspected waters told before day 1 are under ENEMY SUBMARINES, where item 2 puts them, and not here.
10. **"A seat that plays the whole side"** (item 5) is, with command levels on, the area seat under the easier setting (`area:easier`), whose picture is the side's own belief, so its contacts are numbered as the side's events number them. The area seat as it stands by default holds the area command's picture, which a report reaches late or never; given the side's events it would be told what its command has not been. It and a seat at a task force or a base are told "not kept at this command" for the three parts.
11. **Totals** are four numbers, always given where there is a claim; the page writes those that are not 0.
12. **The tool takes no argument,** is in the seat's tools and the watcher's, has a schema of its own, and raises the interface from 51 to 52.
13. **The page asks it when the paper is opened,** not at load and not on every refresh. Reason: item 1's point that each call is written to the transcript.
14. **A paper with no part** says one line of the staff's, "We hold nothing of the enemy yet.", and no heading.

**The page's check** is a function of `table/check_screen.py`, by the page's text, by the page's own functions run in node on an answer, and through a bridge on a real game, as its other checks are. Its statements:

1. **Where it is.** The STAFF menu has "Intelligence", which opens a sheet titled INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY. The paper is drawn from one call of the tool `intelligence`, made when it is opened; the function that draws it calls no other tool, reads no `events` and matches no pattern against words.
2. **Order.** Given an answer with every part, the headings are, in this order and each once: SEEN, LOST SIGHT OF, CLAIMED, WHAT THE ENEMY HAS DONE, ASHORE, ENEMY BASES, ENEMY SUBMARINES, BEFORE THE GAME. The page takes the order from the answer's `parts`. Given an answer without a part, that heading is not drawn. Given an answer with none, the paper says "We hold nothing of the enemy yet." and draws no heading.
3. **How known and how old.** A contact's line gives its ships reported, what the heaviest was taken for, its place and its age, in the words the contact's card uses for them. An estimate's line gives the men, the place, where it came from and its age in days, in the words the place's card uses. A report of a submarine gives its place and age, and suspected waters their place, their distance and the words as told.
4. **Links.** Every contact held, every place and every base on the paper is a link made by the functions the cards use, and a unit of the side's own is named and linked as on the diary. No handle of the form word:number is shown.
5. **LOST SIGHT OF.** Each line says the day and part of the day of the last report, what it said, and one of two things: no report since, or a strike found nothing there. The line is not a link to a contact's card, which the game no longer has; it opens what is known of how it was last reported. While the answer has a lost contact the sheet's SHOW ON CHART is shown, and it marks the last place of each on the chart, with a mark that is not a held contact's; with no lost contact the button is hidden.
6. **CLAIMED.** By day, the latest first. A claim of ships says what the target was "taken for" where the answer has it. Under the days is one line headed "claimed, not confirmed", with the totals by kind of claim (sunk, hits, shot down, on the ground), those that are 0 left out. The paper draws no total by kind of ship, takes no claim from a count of ships seen, and links no claim to a contact.
7. **WHAT THE ENEMY HAS DONE.** By day, the latest first: raids on our bases, shellings from the sea, attacks on our ships and the actions our forces met, with the aircraft type where it was recognised and "type not made out" where it was not, as the diary says it.
8. **ENEMY BASES** shows only the bases the answer gives, each linked to its place, with the estimate of men there or the aircraft claimed destroyed on the ground there from the air, and the day of each.
9. **BEFORE THE GAME** shows the contacts and the estimates as the answer gives them, and one link that opens the directives. No sentence of a directive is on the paper: the function that draws it does not read the directives' text.
10. **Not kept.** For each part the answer names in `not_kept`, the heading is drawn with the one line "Not kept at this command." under it.
11. **Words.** Run in node on an answer with every part, the text drawn: says "report" or "contact" and never "force" of a contact, and gives no count of forces; says "the enemy" and never "his"; has the staff speak as "we" in any sentence of its own; does not hold the word "intent"; and holds no sentence that explains a rule (none saying why claims run high, when a contact is dropped, how an estimate can be wrong, or what "taken for" means). "claimed, not confirmed" is the only qualifier on the paper.
12. **No points and no result.** The same text holds none of "points", "victory", "margin" or "score". The paper reads nothing of the game's result, and when the game is over it is drawn from the tool as before.
13. **A watched game.** The paper opens for the seat watched, through the watcher's read of the same tool.
14. **Through a bridge.** On the table's game of scenario 128, at the Allied seat with the three days played, the page's call answers and the paper drawn from it has ENEMY SUBMARINES, with a report and the suspected waters in the words told, and WHAT THE ENEMY HAS DONE, with the submarine's attacks on the carrier, one on each of days 1 and 2. On the 39 days at the first evening it has BEFORE THE GAME with the link to the directives.

### The architect's rulings on the build of the tool, Nov 23, 2026

1. **A submarine's attack on a ship is given by the tool, not by the briefing.** Statement 5 said the attacks were "as the briefing has it"; the briefing leaves a salvo from a submarine out, as a later phase of decision 0218, and it stays out: adding it would change the briefing's text and data, and with them its goldens and the recorded games that hold a briefing, for a paper that does not need it. The statement is changed. A day of `enemy_has_done` has a fifth list, `submarine_attacks`, read from the side's own events: for each salvo a submarine fired at a ship of the side's, `against` (the side's ship), `place`, `pulse` and `event`. It gives no torpedoes, no hits and nothing of the boat, which the side is not told; the damage is the ship's own affair, on its card. This is the one line of the tool that is not a type another tool already has (ruling 2 above), until the briefing's part for what was fired from the sea is built, which then takes this line as it is. On the paper it reads as an attack on the ship by a submarine, with the ship linked, under WHAT THE ENEMY HAS DONE; the page's statements 7 and 14 are read so. The test's doc comment (statement 5 and scenario D's hand calculation) is amended to match.
2. **Statement 7's "number" is the aircraft the claim gives,** as built: each of a base's `claims` is `{day, number}`. The event's number is not wanted there: the claim is in CLAIMED with it.
