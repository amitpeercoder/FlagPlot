# 0192: A station kept from the sea

Status: a plan by the architect, written on Nov 20, 2026, on the designer's word ("plan and build 1 and 4 next"). To be read by a reviewer who has not seen it written, and amended on that reading, before any of it is built.

Summary: A staff stands a surface force at a hex only by sending it out from its home port at night and bringing it home in the morning. A force that begins at sea with no home port is "away from home" and is given nothing, for the whole game. This lets such a force be stood at its station from where it is: it goes there when it would sortie, stays there, and goes to the nearest base of its side when its fuel says it must.

Standing: Planned on Nov 20, 2026, amended on review and built on Nov 20, 2026 ("As built"); the readings marked there, and two statements of scenario 108, are the architect's to confirm.

## Why

Scenario 105 (decision 0188). The Allied support group begins at sea with the carriers. The order to deny the waters with it formed a stand task, and every morning the staff answered that the force "has no home port and is not stopped at a base of the side, so the staff gives it no order". It lay idle for five days until the architect moved it by an order of the commander's own. A scenario should not need a hand-written order to do what an intent says.

What is so today (chapter 8, Standing at a hex by night):

- **A stand task** names a force and a station. Each morning the staff decides whether the force sorties tonight: at press every night, at balanced and at preserve the force on a day with a threat (a contact of ships within 15 hexes of the station).
- **The force must be stopped at its home port,** or at a base of the side, which is then made its home port. Otherwise it is away from home and is given no order.
- **A sortie** is a route to the station and home again, at the speed that brings it there within the night, with fuel for both ways times the risk's margin.

## The rules

1. **A force at sea with no home port stands from where it is.** For a stand task whose force has no home port and is not stopped at a base of the side, the staff no longer answers "away from home". On a morning the force would sortie (the test is unchanged), it is routed to the station, one way, the station marked to stand at.
2. **Its speed** is cruise where the engine's measure brings it to the station within 12 hours, and otherwise full. It goes whether or not it arrives within the night: it has no port to wait in.
3. **At the station it stays.** On later mornings it is given no route while it is there. It is at battle readiness on a day it would sortie and at normal on a day it would not, as today. Its rules of engagement are the risk's, as today.
4. **On a morning it would not sortie and is not at the station,** it is given no route and stays where it is. The reason says so.
5. **Fuel.** The fuel it needs to go is the engine's measure of the passage to the station and from the station to the nearest base of the side by sea, at the speed chosen, times the risk's margin. A force with a ship short of that does not go, and the reason gives the figures. With no base of the side it can reach by sea, the fuel is not weighed, as a carrier force's is not (chapter 8, Fuel first).
6. **Going in for fuel.** At the station or on the way, on a morning a ship has less than the engine's measure of the passage to that nearest base at cruise, times the margin, the force is routed to that base and the base is set as its home port. From then on it is a force with a home port, and stands as today.
7. **A force with a home port is as today.** Nothing changes for it, whether it is at home or away.
8. **Reasons** give, beside what a stand's reason gives today, that the force has no home port and stands from where it is, the base its fuel is weighed to and that passage, and on going in for fuel the ship that is short.
9. **A force the commander holds** is given nothing, as today.

## Rule card

- **Reads:** a stand task and its force; whether the force has a home port and where it lies; the contacts within 15 hexes of the station; the fuel aboard; the engine's measure of a passage; the bases of the side.
- **Writes:** nothing but the staff's existing orders (route, speed, readiness, rules of engagement, home port) and their reasons.
- **Reuses:** the test of whether a force sorties; the choice of speed; the fuel margin by risk; the order that sets a home port; the mark to stand at a hex.
- **New:** one branch of the stand doctrine. No state, no event of a new kind, no draw.

## What it costs

- **A game with a stand task for a force at sea with no home port changes:** such a force now moves. Any existing scenario with one is found and its daily hashes checked; where they move the reason is reported before golden files are remade. The rules label rises.
- **A force kept at a station by day is under the enemy's aircraft,** where a sortie by night was not. That is what standing there means; the risk and the commander decide.
- **Rulebook:** chapter 8, Standing at a hex by night, and the glossary's "home port".

## Amended on review

A reviewer read the plan against the stand doctrine's code and ran the scenarios that have a stand. Found right: the claims of "what is so today"; where the branch belongs; that the engine has the sea route, the passage forecast and the nearest base by sea that the rule needs; that a commander may state "stand at" himself and set its risk. None of the scenarios he ran reaches the new branch, so none of their hashes moves.

11. **It goes to its station at every risk.** As written a balanced force with no threat lay idle where it began, though the plan itself said it has no port to wait in. Rules 1 and 4 are replaced: a force with no home port is routed to its station on the first morning it is not there and not already under way to it. The test of a sortie decides its readiness and no longer whether it goes: battle readiness on a day it would sortie, normal otherwise. At preserve the force it does not go, and a force at the station is given nothing, while a contact that outnumbers it stands within 15 hexes of the station; the reason names it.
12. **No mark.** The last hex of a route is never treated as marked. The force is routed to the station, arrives and lies stopped there. The reasons say that and nothing of a mark.
13. **Under way, it is left alone.** A force already under way to its station is given no route.
14. **Fuel is weighed to a base that can fuel it:** the nearest base of the side by sea that holds fuel above its reserve and gives a rate, the lower number on a tie. Rule 6 sends it there and to no other. With no such base the fuel is not weighed, the force does not go in, and the reason says no base of the side can fuel it. (In scenario 105 Port Moresby holds no fuel, so the support group stays out.)
15. **Ammunition is read in the same pass as fuel,** as today: a force short of it by the risk's share does not go to its station, and goes in to that base if it is at the station.
16. **A force with a home port, stopped at sea on no route,** is stuck for ever today: it is "away from home" and is given nothing. It is now routed to its home port at cruise, and from there stands as today. Where its home port is no longer the side's, the staff says so and treats it as a force with no home port; today it says nothing at all.
17. **Known:** a force lying at its station burns no fuel and is open to the enemy's aircraft by day, and fights any force that enters its hex.
18. **The worked example is corrected by the architect:** statement 6 (balanced goes at once; its readiness waits for the threat), statement 7 (the fuel is tested at press with a margin of one, and B holds fuel and gives a rate), the mark is dropped, and statements are added for rules 13, 14 and 16 with two properties: a force that goes at balanced goes at press; more fuel aboard never stops it going.
19. **Scenario 105:** task force 2's intent is "stand at (-13,-2)" with no risk set.

**Readings made in the build** (the architect's to confirm):

1. **At the station** is the force's hex being the station. **Under way to it** is a force not stopped whose route ends at the station (or, with no waypoint left, whose leg ends there). A force under way elsewhere, with no home port, is routed to the station.
2. **The speed** is cruise where the engine's measure at cruise arrives within the night's 12 hours, and otherwise full, whether or not the passage at full speed arrives. A first build fell back to cruise where full speed would run a ship dry, and scenario 108's property 4 caught it: a ship with more fuel, able to reach the station at full speed, was weighed at full and stopped, where one with less went at cruise. Where the passage at full runs a ship dry and no base is weighed to, the force goes at full and the engine stops it where it runs dry. Where ships cannot get to the station by sea at all, it is given no route and the notice that the intent cannot be met is handed in.
3. **"Gives a rate"** (rule 14) is read as "the engine would fuel a ship there": the port's rate (at a berth where it has one, else by lighter) is given, or is not given, which is fuelling with no limit (chapter 2, Port size). Read literally, no base on the frozen figures, which give no rates, would ever count. The test that matters is fuel above the reserve.
4. **The nearest base** is by the engine's sea route from the station (the miles `route_between` charges), the lower number on a tie, as a lift's source is found. A base at the force's own hex is not taken.
5. **The fuel weighed to go** is the engine's measure of the route to the station and on to that base, with no mark, at the speed chosen, times the margin; a ship the measure runs dry is short, as for a sortie (decision 0130). **The fuel weighed at the station** is the measure of the passage from the station to that base at cruise, times the margin.
6. **On the way** (rule 6) is read as overridden by rule 13: a force under way to its station is not sent in, nor is a force away from the station that is short; it does not go, and stays where it is. Only at the station does a short force go in (`docs/rules/differences.md` row 48).
7. **Ammunition** short at the station with no base to go in to: the force stays, and the reason says so.
8. **Preserve the force** with a contact that outnumbers it: the force is given nothing, neither readiness nor rules of engagement, whether at the station or away from it; radio silence, which is ordered before the home port is read, is still ordered as today. A force already under way to its station is left alone, and its readiness and rules of engagement are still set.
9. **Readiness** is by the test of a sortie on every morning the force is given anything, going in included.
10. **Going in** is routed at cruise, one way, with no mark, and the home port order comes first, then rules of engagement, readiness, speed and route.
11. **A short force away from the station** hands in no notice: the rule gives none, and the force may be fuelled where it is by a commander's order.
12. **Stopped at sea** (rule 16) is: stopped (not under way, not halted, no leg ahead), no waypoint left on its route, and at no base of the side. A force with a home port stopped at another base of the side is away from home, as today. The route home is one way, with no mark, at cruise; the speed order is given only where the force has another setting.
13. **A lost home port** (rule 16, the second sentence): the code treats a home port that is not one of the side's bases as none, and says so in the reasons. The engine clears a home port taken by the enemy, and the loader refuses a home port of the enemy's, so no game reaches these words (`docs/rules/differences.md` row 47). A force with a lost home port stopped at a base of the side has that base set as its home port, as a force with none does.

**The tests.** Every statement and property of scenario 108 has a test: 21 pass and 2 are ignored. Two unit tests are added to the stand's in `sim-staff`.

- **Statement 10a, its second half, ignored:** with B taken by the enemy no game can hold H with B its home port (reading 13). The test is written as the statement says and does not load.
- **The first property, ignored as slow:** it walks every `data/scenarios/*.toml` and asserts that no staff reason has the new branch's words. Run by hand, it passes. The on and off comparison below is its proof.
- **Statement 1, "the same at press":** at press the force is set to battle readiness, as statement 6 says it is every morning at press. The test at press checks the route and the reason only.
- **Statement 8 at press:** with the margin one, a destroyer "cut below the measure of the passage from X to B at cruise" cannot make that passage: it is sent in and runs dry one hex short of B, and from then on is a force away from home, given no order. So statement 9 cannot follow statement 8 at press. The test checks statement 8 as written, and statements 8 and 9 together at balanced, where the destroyer's 27 t is below the 30 t the passage times 1.2 needs and above the 25 t it burns: it goes in, fuels at B and then sorties to X by night and comes home.
- **Statement 8, "the next morning":** the test begins with G at X and the destroyer's fuel already cut, so G goes in on the first morning.
- **Statement 7, a ship's fuel cut:** where a ship runs dry the engine's measure ends there, so the reason says "against more than 40 t needed, running dry short of the passage", and the other ships' needs are shorter too. The figures 90 t and 45 t are asserted only with no ship cut.
- **Statement 10, H under way:** an order of the commander's to H holds it, so H begins stopped at sea, is routed home by rule 16 on day 1 and is answered "away from home" on day 2.
- **Distances:** the engine's sea route from B to X is 200 nm with X at 4 hexes, as the statement says; 183 nm at 8 hexes and 280 nm at 12 (the map's coasts).
