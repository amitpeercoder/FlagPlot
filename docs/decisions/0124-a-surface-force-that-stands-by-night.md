# 0124: A surface force that stands by night: the task force as an addressee, the night stand, fuel as policy

Status: settled by the architect on Nov 16, 2026, inside the plan for step 3, stage 2 (decision 0113, doctrines 7, 8 and 10, and step 4 of its seven), written by level as decision 0120 has it. It writes one rule of the engine, one task and its doctrine out, with the hand calculation. Built on Nov 16, 2026 ("As built" below). It is the second part of step 4. Carrier forces at sea, with their own patrol, search and strike, are the third part and get their own record before the second proof.

Summary: A task force becomes an addressee; under "deny an area" and "hold a place" the area's staff gives each surface force the end names the task of standing at a hex by night; the force's level decides each morning whether to sortie, from the threat it believes in, its fuel and its risk, and sets its route, readiness and rules of engagement; and one rule is added so that it can: a force may halt at a waypoint marked to stand at, for the night, and steam on at morning

Standing: Settled by the architect, Nov 16, 2026, inside decisions 0113 and 0120. Built

## Why

A side's surface forces do nothing unless a scenario orders them. Stage 2's second proof needs both sides' ships to be sent, to stand where the enemy is expected by night, to fight by the risk they were given and to come back for fuel, all from ends.

Orders are taken at the start of a day, which is the start of its night. A force can be sent to a hex and it will arrive in the night, and then nothing takes it away again until the next day's orders: it sits through the daylight within reach of the enemy's aircraft. The record has the opposite: forces that came up after dark and were gone by morning. A route may already mark waypoints to act at, where a force halts for cargo or to bombard and then steams on (decision 0096). One more kind of mark gives the night stand.

## The rule: standing at a waypoint for the night

1. **A route may mark waypoints to stand at** (`stand_at`), as it marks waypoints to act at. The last waypoint is never marked.
2. **A force halts at a waypoint marked to stand at when it reaches it in the night pulse,** whatever its mission. Its work there is the night. It records the halt as chapter 2 has it. A force that reaches the waypoint in a daylight pulse passes it.
3. **It steams on at the start of the morning pulse's movement,** with the pulse's full hours, as a halted force does when its work is done.
4. **Halted, it is stopped:** it meets what enters its hex and what it finds there, by chapter 4.
5. **A side can ask the choice beforehand,** as it can for the other halts.

A route with no such mark is unchanged, so every game played before this rule is the game it was.

## The task force as an addressee

6. **An intent may be addressed to a task force** of the side (decision 0116, 12 and 13). It may use that force's ships and nothing else.
7. **A kind of intent: stand at a hex.** It names a task force of the side and a hex. It is addressed to the force. It may stand under "deny an area", where the hex is the centre of the waters, and under "hold a place", where it is the base's hex; and a commander may state it himself as a root.
8. **Forming it, at the level above.** Under a deny or a hold intent whose forces limit names task forces, the doctrine of that level forms a stand task for each named force that is on the surface combat mission and has a ship afloat, in task force number order, where none for that force is in force under it. A force is named in a forces limit by the commander: with none named, no stand task is formed. Under "hold a place" this is the base's level giving a task to a force, which is "cover from the sea".

## The doctrine, at the force's level

Each morning, for a stand task whose force is not held:

9. **The threat.** There is a threat when the side holds a contact of ships within 15 hexes of the station. At preserve the force, only a contact that reports no more ships than the force has afloat counts.
10. **Whether it sorties tonight.** At press, every night. At balanced and at preserve the force, on a day with a threat.
11. **Fuel comes first.** The fuel the sortie needs is the passage to the station and home, by the engine's own measure, times the margin its risk gives. A force that has less aboard does not sortie, and its reason gives the two figures and the day from which it may be used: the day after it will have fuelled where it lies, by the hours fuelling takes there (decision 0112), or no day where the base cannot fuel it, and then the notice that the intent cannot be met.
12. **The sortie.** The force must be stopped at its home port, which is the base it lies at when the task is formed, set by order where it has none. It is routed to the station and home again, the station marked to stand at, at cruising speed where that brings it to the station within the night's 12 hours, and at full speed where only that does. Where neither does, there is no sortie, and the notice is handed in. Where the station is the force's own hex, there is no route.
13. **Readiness and rules of engagement.** On a day it sorties, or stands at its own hex with a threat, the force is set to battle readiness; on a day it does not, to normal. Its rules of engagement are set once, from the risk: the stance engage and the break-off level of the table; the straggler instruction and the keep share are left as they are.
14. **Away from home.** On a morning the force is not stopped at its home port (it is still coming back, or was sent elsewhere by a fight), it is given no order, and the reason says where it is and when it is expected.
15. **Reasons** carry the contact and its distance, the fuel aboard against the fuel needed, the hours to the station, and the level.

| Set by the risk level | Preserve the force | Balanced | Press |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| It sorties | On a threat it is not outnumbered by | On a threat | Every night |
| Fuel needed, of the passage out and home | One and a half times | One and a fifth | Once |
| Break-off level | A quarter | A half | Three quarters |

The fuel and break-off rows are decision 0113's. The 15 hexes are a placeholder: a day's steaming at 25 knots.

## What it leaves out

- **Being clear of the enemy's air by day.** The force steams for home at morning and is at sea, within reach, for as long as the passage takes. Leaving in the dark needs orders inside a day, which the game does not have.
- **Coming up from far away.** A station more than a night's steaming at full speed from the force's home is not met. Moving a force's home forward is the commander's, until a later step.
- **Retiring for damage.** The rules of engagement and the straggler rule do what they do today. Sending a damaged ship to a base that can patch it or to the yard is the staff's in the third part.
- **Carriers,** and a surface force that escorts or covers a run (step 5).
- **Where the enemy is expected** beyond a contact near the station: the enemy's habits and his bases are stage 2b's and stage 3's.

## What it costs

- **State:** a route's marks to stand at, a new kind of halt, the task force addressee and one kind of intent, in the hash and named beside it. The state version rises and the rules label rises.
- **The staff:** one doctrine at the force's level, and the forming rule in the deny and hold doctrines.
- **The rulebook:** chapter 2's halts gain the night stand; chapter 8 gains "A task force as an addressee" and "Standing at a hex by night"; the glossary gains station and night stand.

## As built

Built on Nov 16, 2026, with decision 0125, in one step. What follows is what was built and every place the build read the record more closely or differed from it.

**Versions.** The state version is 31 (a route's `stand_at` after its `act_at`, a halt's work `night` after its `cargo`, the addressee task force, code 4, and the kinds stand at a hex, code 9, and operate a carrier force, code 10, each its force and then its hex). The rules label is 14, raised by the night stand. The saved game is version 7, the doctrine `supply-11`, the agent interface version 11.

**The rule.** `TaskForceOrder::SetRoute` gains `stand_at`, the marks to stand at; intake refuses a mark that is not a waypoint with the refusal the marks to act at have. The engine's halt is found by one function for both marks (`movement::waypoint_work` and `halt_index`): at a waypoint marked to act at the work is what it was; at one marked to stand at, the night, when the pulse is the night. A halted force with the night as its work steams on when the pulse is not the night, so at the start of the morning's movement. A route with no mark to stand at gives what it gave in every pulse: every scenario built before gives the events and end states it gave. The events' words: a halt "to stand for the night", a steaming on because "the night is over"; the replay file writes `night` only where it is true.

**The choice beforehand (rule 5).** The halt and steam-on questions now carry the pulse they are asked for (`MovementQuestion::Halt { force, pulse }` and `SteamOn { force, pulse }`), since the answer for a mark to stand at depends on it. The side's list of questions asks both for the night, the first movement of the day it gives orders for; the tools write the pulse in `asked`.

**The addressee.** `Addressee::TaskForce`. A staff's order to a task force is inside the forces limit when the intent it serves is addressed to that force, whatever the limits above (intake's check). A commander's order to a force an intent in force is addressed to holds it, as an order to a force in a forces limit does (the override). An intent addressed to a force may use the air groups aboard the force's ships, where every forces limit on its chain names the group or holds the force; no other group.

**Forming (rule 8)** is `crate::stand::form_force_tasks`, read from the nearest forces limit on the end's chain (its own, for a root). Under "deny an area" it runs after the bases' tasks, and a task formed or standing counts as serving the end, so the end's notice that it cannot be met is not handed in; under "hold a place" it runs first, before the cover from the air. A stand task under "deny an area" stands only at the centre of the waters (`may_stand_under`); under "hold a place" intake checks that its hex is the base's.

**The doctrine** is `crates/sim-staff/src/stand.rs`. Readings the build took:

- **Stopped** is not under way, not halted and on no leg. **The home port** is the force's own where it has one; else, stopped at a base of the side, that base, set by an order that morning. A force with neither is given no order, with a reason, as one away from home (rule 14).
- **The threat:** the distance is in whole hexes from the contact's estimated hex to the station; the nearest that counts is named, the lowest number on a tie. At preserve the force a nearer contact that does not count is named in the reason with its ships against the force's.
- **The speed is chosen before the fuel** (rule 12 before 11): the engine's forecast to the station at cruise, then at full speed, the first within 12 hours. The fuel each ship needs is the engine's forecast of a route to the station and home with no mark (`SideView::forecast_route`, new), at that speed, times the margin. A force with neither speed in the night has no sortie and the notice; the fuel is then not weighed.
- **Where the base can fuel it:** its fuel stock holds what the ships short lack of their need; the hours are the longest ship's lack of a full load at the port's berth rate where it has a berth, else its lighter rate, none where no rate is given; the day it may be used is the day after the one on which those hours end, counted from the start of the day: today and at least one day more, so with no rate the next day. Where the base cannot, the notice that the intent cannot be met. The notice is handed in on each morning that holds, not on the first alone.
- **Readiness at its own hex** (the station is the hex it lies at): battle on a night it would sortie (a threat, or press), normal otherwise.
- **Order of the orders:** home port, rules of engagement, readiness, speed, route. A reason with no order is handed in on each morning the force is not routed; a morning with an order has its reasons on the orders.
- **A scenario task force may state its stance** (`stance = "avoid"`), which the hand calculation's force needs.
