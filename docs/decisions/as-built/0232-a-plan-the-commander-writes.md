### The order's paper: YOUR PLAN (rule 27)

11. **The part.** A fold, YOUR PLAN, after LIMITS and before ACTS, on the paper of a new order to the staff whose end is "seize and hold" an island, and on no other paper. Not in a watched game.
12. **Its lines are read from the staff's reading,** and from nothing else: one line for each entry of `preview`'s `with_change.tree.sub_intents` whose `kind` is `take_place` and whose `parent` is the order's number, in the order the reading of the bare order (no `under`) gives them. That is the staff's own list of the places the side does not hold; the page does not work out what the side holds. A line is named by its place, "The landing at Lunga", the place said as the page says any place.
13. **The words.** Closed, the fold's head is `YOUR PLAN · ` and then: `The staff plans every landing.` where nothing is written; `Yours: the landing at Tulagi, the landing at Lunga.` where something is, in the order sent; `Every place is ours: there is no landing to write.` where the reading has no such line. Open, a line not written says `left to the staff` and has the button `WRITE IT`; a line written has its own lines and the button `LEAVE IT TO THE STAFF`, which puts the whole landing back.
14. **A written landing's own lines,** each at the staff's choice until set, with the controls and the way back (BACK TO THE STAFF’S CHOICE) the LIMITS part gives a take today: `Ships and forces` (task forces only: no single ship is offered or sent), `Land units`, `Sails from` (the forces limit's `source`), `Not before`, `Wait for`, `After`, `Knowledge`, `Risk`, and `By the end of` (a day, the take's `by_day`). Air groups, an area, targets and protected ships are not offered on a written landing. `After` offers the other written landings of this draft, by name, and the other orders of his it offers today; it does not offer a landing that already waits, itself or through another, for this one.
15. **What is sent.** `preview` of the order, its `limits` as today, and `under`: one entry for each written landing, `{intent_kind: "take_place", hex, intent, by_day?, limits}`, with `limits` as the limits model gives them for these lines (forces, not_before, wait_for, after, knowledge, then risk where it is not at the staff's choice). The entries stand in the order of the lines, but that a landing another waits for stands before it. Each carries its number: the order's number as the last reading gave it (`intent`), plus its place in `under` counted from 1; an `After` that names a landing of the draft is sent as that number. Where a reading comes back with another number for the order, the page asks again. The page sends no force, base, day or limit the player did not set.
16. **The model is pure,** beside the limits model and cut and run in node by the check as that is: `planLines(tree, order)` gives the lines of item 12; `writtenUnder(order, written)` gives `under`; `planSummary(lines, written)` gives the words of item 13. The names are fixed here because the check calls them.

### One reading, one signature (rule 28)

17. **THE STAFF'S READING is the preview of the whole draft,** drawn by the plan paper's functions from its tree: his landings are tasks marked YOURS (item 23), with what the staff would form beneath each; the rest are THE STAFF'S.
18. **A refusal is said at its line.** `of_batch.position` 1 is the order; then the order's limits; then each landing followed by its own limits. A refusal of a landing or of one of its limits opens YOUR PLAN at that landing, marks the line, and says `The landing at Tulagi: Risk: ` and the engine's reason through the page's refusal words (for the landing itself, `The landing at Tulagi: ` and the reason).
19. **SIGN AND SEND is one `approve` of that preview.** The page checks `limits_queued` against the order's limits sent and `under_queued` against each landing's, and sends nothing else. SIGNED shows one entry, the order's (the queued `set_intent` with no `parent`; what its `goes_with` lists is shown in its line and not as entries): the order, its limits, then `Yours: the landing at Tulagi, the landing at Lunga.` TAKE BACK is one `take_back` of that entry and takes all of it.

### The dry run's two columns (rule 29)

20. **When.** Whenever a trial's answer has `beside.of` of `left_to_the_staff` or `given_back_to_the_staff`, whatever was asked: DRY RUN on a draft with a written part (`trial` with `preview` and `size`; `compare` is not sent, and is true by rule 24), and DRY RUN on the paper of item 25 (`trial` with `intent`). A draft with nothing written is read as today.
21. **What is shown,** by one pure function, `twoPlans(j)`, from the answer's fields alone: `asked.worlds`, `last_day`, `cost`, `enemy_lost`, and `beside.of`, `.order`, `.with_your_plan`, `.how_it_went`, `.cost`, `.enemy_lost`, `.world_for_world`. No line of `text` is cut, matched or split for it.
    - The heading `Your plan beside the staff’s`, and under it `The staff’s plan is this order with every landing left to the staff.` or, for a landing given back, `The staff’s plan is this order with the landing at Lunga given back to the staff from 1800.`
    - One table of two columns, `Your plan` and `The staff’s plan`. Its first row is `Island won and held`: the count of runs in `met_and_kept` of `beside.with_your_plan` and of `beside.how_it_went`, each said as the page says a count of runs, with the day or days where there is one. Then `Won and lost again` and `Not won by day N` (N the answer's `last_day`), each only where either column has a run. Then the five costs of the side, `Your men lost to the enemy` and so on, from `cost` and `beside.cost`; then the five of the enemy of the dry run, `The enemy’s men lost, in the dry run` and so on, from `enemy_lost` and `beside.enemy_lost`; a cost's row only where either column's highest is above nothing, the same rows in both columns.
    - One line under it: `Run for run: won and held with both plans in 5; with your plan alone in 0; with the staff’s plan alone in 2; with neither in 1.`, the counts of `beside.world_for_world`, and where `apart` is not empty the count of runs that could not be played at one of the two.
    - Where the order itself was tried the sheet does not say its end a second time above the table; where a landing was tried, how the landing went stands above it as today.
22. **No advice.** Neither column is marked, ordered, coloured or worded as the better; no cost is summed across rows; no chance, per cent or points. The rest of the sheet (where the runs part, what it waits for, what the enemy was taken to have, the staff's report) is as today.

### The plan's paper (rule 30)

23. **Every task line under an order is marked `YOURS` or `THE STAFF’S`,** from the field `maker` and no other thing: of the intent in `command_state` for a plan in force, of the entry of `propose`'s tree for a plan that waits. Never from the words of a reason or of a plan order. The order's own line carries no mark.
24. **A line of his** (a `take_place` with `maker` "commander" and a parent) shows its limits as item 14 names them, each with CHANGE and BACK TO THE STAFF’S CHOICE: the paper for one limit that exists, opened for his landing's number (`preview` of a `set_limit` on it, the staff's reading, one `approve`). Its day, where he gave one, is shown and not changed there. It has GIVE IT BACK TO THE STAFF.
25. **GIVE IT BACK TO THE STAFF** opens the order paper as a withdrawal (item 10): TO the staff; THE ORDER `Give the landing at Lunga back to the staff.`; ACTS; DRY RUN where the game has a trial, which is `trial` with `intent` his landing's number and shows the two columns; SIGN AND SEND, which is the tool `withdraw_intent` for that number and nothing else. SIGNED says it as `The landing at Lunga: given back to the staff`, with TAKE BACK.
26. **A place the staff planned** is marked THE STAFF’S and offers nothing new. Writing a landing under an order already signed is in the tools (rule 22) and not on the table in this step.

### A reason that leads to his node (rule 31)

27. **A reason turns on a node of his** where, by numbers alone: its `serves.intent` is a node of his; or its `limits` list names one; or its words carry the handle `intent:K` of one. A node of his is an intent with `maker` "commander" and a parent, from `command_state`, or a landing of the draft, from the reading's `under`. The page does not read the words "the commander's" for it.
28. **Where it leads.** A handle of his node in a reason's words is said as `your landing at Lunga` and is a link. A reason that turns on a node of his, shown anywhere but under that node's own line, ends its WHY with `YOUR PLAN` and a link for each such node, `The landing at Lunga`. In a plan's paper the link opens that plan's paper at his node's line; in the reading of a draft it opens YOUR PLAN at that landing. A limit of his node in a reason's list is under YOUR LIMITS as today (decision 0222, rule 17) and leads to that limit's line on his node's line.

### What the page may not do

29. It is given one seat's tools and reads nothing else: no replay, save, batch file or event log. Of the new parts: whose a node is comes from `maker`; the lines of YOUR PLAN from the reading's tree; the two columns from the answer's fields. None of them is made by cutting or matching the engine's sentences. It says no number or handle of the engine's (a landing is "the landing at Lunga", never an intent's number). It shows no points, chance or per cent and gives no advice. No caption explains a rule. It chooses nothing for the player.
