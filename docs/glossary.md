# Glossary

One meaning for each word the rules use. The complexity review after scenario 5 found these used in more than one sense. Decision records, event texts and briefings follow this page; where an older record says otherwise, this page is right.

| Word | Means | Does not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Air distance** | How far an aircraft flies between two hexes: the straight line between their centres, in whole miles (decision 0040). Every range, radius and sortie distance, and every tool that measures one, uses it | The number of hexes between them times 40, which is longer off the grid's axes |
| **Sea distance** | How far a ship is charged for steaming between two hexes by the map's shortest sea path: the sum of the charges of its steps. Where the way is clear it is the air distance; where land forces a detour the steps are charged 40 miles until the way is clear (decision 0058) | The hex count of the path times 40 miles |
| **Step charge** | What the step from one hex centre to the next is charged, in miles, 1 to 40: the miles it brings the force closer to its waypoint in a straight line where the way is clear, 40 where it is not (decision 0058) | |
| **Clear way** | From a hex, a sea path to the waypoint with as many steps as the hex distance: no land forces a detour (decision 0058) | |
| **Readiness** | What a force is ordered to be: normal or battle (decision 0021) | Whether it is alert at this moment |
| **Alert** | The state of a force whose lookouts see at full effect. A force at battle readiness is always alert | An order |
| **Ready** | A force's state as a fight begins, kept for the whole fight: at battle readiness, or alert since before the present pulse began. A force that is not ready fires its guns at a share, `unready_gun_share`, in every round, and its torpedoes at a share, `unready_torpedo_share` (decisions 0068, 0071) | Alert. A force alerted in this pulse sees at full effect and is not ready |
| **Alerted** | How a force at normal readiness came to be alert: by a fresh contact nearby, by being fired on, or by an alarm. A contact never alerts or stands down a force at battle readiness | |
| **Alarm** | The roll a force that is not alert makes when a fight with gunfire happens in its hex | |
| **Stance** | A force's ordered way of meeting an enemy: engage or avoid (decision 0014) | |
| **Avoiding for the pulse** | What a force does after it breaks off or slips away: it takes the avoid stance until the pulse ends, whatever its orders (decision 0022) | A change to its orders |
| **Break off** | To end a fight because the share of a force's ships sunk or heavily damaged has reached its break-off level, or because it is avoiding and was fired on | To move. A force that breaks off keeps to its route |
| **Slip away** | To end a fight unseen: an avoiding force that has seen an enemy that has not seen it | |
| **Keep share** | The share of its fit ships a force must keep when it detaches stragglers (decision 0016) | |
| **Patrol share** | The share of its aircraft a fighter group keeps over its own hex (decision 0027) | |
| **Sight** | What an air search does to a force: it sights it, and a report follows | |
| **See** | What a force does in a fight, round by round | |
| **Report** | What a side is told. It can be wrong. Every change to what a side believes is a report | The truth |
| **Contact** | What a side believes about an enemy force: a hex, a count, a kind and an age. Made and refreshed by reports. Each report gives a hex, a count and a kind, any of which can be wrong; a later report of the same contact replaces them, so its kind and count can change from report to report | The force itself |
| **Position** | A force's hex: the nearest hex centre. A group aboard a ship is at its ship's force's position | How far along a leg it is, which is working data of movement |
| **Radius** | How far out an aircraft type goes and comes back. `search_range_nm` is the largest range a search arc may name, and the engine refuses an arc beyond it; it is the search radius, derived on its own from the range in the scouting condition (decision 0075). `strike_range_nm` is what a group's reach is measured from. Both columns are named range and hold radii | Range |
| **Reach** | How far an air group's strikes and escorts may go: normal, the type's strike radius, or extended, the radius and a share more. Set by order, normal until then (decision 0059) | Radius |
| **Covering hex** | Under "take a place", the hex a carrier force is sent to: the first hex along the shortest sea route from where the force is to the place within the least strike radius, at normal reach, of the bomber groups aboard (decision 0148) | The hex of a force's own task under a hold or a deny, which the commander gives |
| **Extended reach** | The reach of a group that has been ordered to it: the radius times (1 plus the extended reach share), cut to a whole mile. A sortie beyond the radius may cost aircraft that run out of fuel on the way home | Normal reach |
| **Available from** | The day from which a side may use one of its units: a task force, a ship, an air group or a land unit, given by the scenario; with none, day 1. It is the later of the unit's own day and those of what carries it. Before it no order to the unit is taken and the staff leaves it out; the unit is there all the same, and is seen, attacked and defends itself (decision 0140) | The day a unit arrives on the map. Nothing brings a unit onto the map later. Nor a date of an operation |
| **Formation** | A grouping of land units stated by a scenario, with a parent formation it belongs to, if any: a battalion under a regiment under a division (decision 0140). Data: no rule reads it | A command. A land unit |
| **Strike contact age** | The oldest contact, in pulses, an air group launches a naval strike on, set by order. With none set the figure `strike_contact_max_age_pulses` serves. A contact is not aged in the pulse it is reported in, so at 0 a group launches on a contact of this pulse or the one before. Not read by a base strike (decision 0114) | Contact age, Strike held share |
| **Strike held share** | The share, from 0 to 1, of the aircraft an air group is able to fly that a naval strike or a base strike holds back, set by order. 0 until set. The aircraft held are not counted as flown. Not a patrol share, which splits a fighter group (decision 0114) | Patrol share, Strike contact age |
| **Strike sizing** | A setting of an air group, on or off, set by order and off until set. A group with it on sends no more at a naval strike's contact than the contact needs, less what the side's groups before it have sent there in the pulse, and launches nothing when every contact it could strike is served. It does nothing where no kind has a figure, as in the frozen step 1 figures. Not read by a base strike (decision 0118) | Strike held share, which holds back a share of every strike |
| **Search once a day** | A setting of an air group, on or off, set by order (`set_search_once`) and off until set. A group on the search mission with it on searches in the morning pulse and not in the afternoon, and rests that afternoon as a group that did not fly. The staff sets it on at preserve the force and at balanced and off at press for a base's cover and its search of waters, and on at preserve the force alone for a carrier force's searcher (decision 0131, rule 6 as amended) | Search figure, what a search costs in fatigue |
| **Search figure** | `fatigue.toml: search_fatigue_per_100_nm`: the fatigue a search sortie costs for each 100 nm, in place of `air_fatigue_per_100_nm`; with none given the general figure serves, as in the frozen step 1 figures. The live figure is 0.14 (decision 0131) | Air fatigue per 100 nm, which every other sortie takes |
| **Take a turn at the search** | What a bomber group that can search does on a morning it is the least tired, then the lowest numbered, of its force's or its task's bomber groups that can search and fly, where the force, or the base's cover or search task, has no group that can only search: it searches that day and does not strike (decision 0131, 7 and 8). At a base, only where the lending task has two or more such groups | Search group, which can only search |
| **Need of a contact** | The aircraft a sized naval strike sends at a contact: the ships it reports times the figure of `air_combat.toml: [strike_aircraft_per_ship]` for the kind it is taken for (cruiser 9, destroyer 3, transport 3, auxiliary 3). A kind with no figure (carrier, battleship) needs everything: a group sends all it can send at it. Worked from the contact as the side holds it (decision 0118, 2) | The aircraft a group can send, or the aircraft the contact really needs
| **Target list** | The kinds of ship a strike group goes for, in order, set by order. The default is all six kinds in the order of importance. A group is launched only at a contact taken for a kind on its list, and attacks over the target the ship its list ranks first, the kinds not on it following in the order of importance (decision 0063) | Target priority, priorities |
| **In the air** | Describes the aircraft of a group that flew in the pulse and were not shot down, until the wear phase of the pulse brings them home (decision 0059) | Aboard |
| **Landed elsewhere** | Describes aircraft that were in the air when their own deck was lost and landed at another carrier or a base, joining or forming a group there (decision 0059) | Rebased |
| **Rebase** | An order that moves an air group at a base to another base of its side that has an airfield taking aircraft, at once. Its ready aircraft and pilots go with it, its damaged aircraft are written off, and it flies nothing that day (decision 0091) | Landed elsewhere; a group moving with its ship |
| **Transfer range** | How far an air group may be rebased: twice the longer of its type's search radius and strike radius, by air distance between the two bases (decision 0091) | Reach; radius |
| **Ditched** | Describes an aircraft that ran out of fuel on the way home from a sortie beyond its radius, or that found no deck to land on, and came down in the sea. It is gone from the group for good and its crew may be recovered or lost (decision 0059) | Shot down, written off |
| **Range** | How far an aircraft flies one way, as a sourced fact. Only the derivation tool reads it. The engine reads the radius columns, whose names say range (corrected against the code, which reads `search_range_nm`) | |
| **Crippled** | A ship afloat, at sea, in port or at the yard, whose propulsion or flight deck is out. The objective `ships_crippled` counts them; degraded does not count (decision 0054) | Heavily damaged; slowed |
| **Heavily damaged** | A ship with flotation damage of `surface_combat.toml: crippled_flotation` (50) or more. It counts toward its force's break-off level and has no other effect. The figure, the break-off event's text and its breakdown factor still say crippled | Crippled |
| **Straggler** | A ship whose propulsion is degraded or out. The straggler instruction acts on it | Heavily damaged |
| **Degraded, out** | The two damaged states of a named system. Degraded halves what the system does; out stops it | |
| **Raid** | The strikes launched in one pulse from one place at one hex, with their escorts | One air group's strike |
| **Mission** | What an air group is ordered to fly: rest, search, naval strike, combat air patrol. A task force also has a mission; two are read: a force on the bombardment mission fires at an enemy base (decision 0092), and a force on the escort mission may be given a charge (decision 0121). No other mission of a task force is read by a rule | |
| **Placeholder** | A figure invented so that the engine runs. Never historical | |
| **Settled** | A choice between sourced figures, made by the architect and written down with its reason | Stated by a source |
| **Pulse** | One of the three parts of a game day, resolved in this order: night, morning, afternoon. A pulse is the same across the whole map (decision 0001). Air searches and strikes fly only in the morning and afternoon: the night pulse is dark, its search phase is empty and no strike is launched in it | Darkness. The night pulse is dark, and light is asked of one function |
| **Phase** | A named step the day runs in a fixed order, inside a pulse or after the last pulse. Some phases are empty slots | A pulse; the phase of a plan to take a place |
| **Draw** | One random number taken from a stream keyed on the seed, day, pulse, subsystem, entity and rule. A chance succeeds if its draw is below it (decision 0003) | A number from a generator that advances as the game runs |
| **Hash** | The 64-bit summary of the state at the end of a day. Two runs agree if their daily hashes agree (decision 0004) | A security measure |
| **Saved game** | A file holding the scenario's name and hash, the hash of each data file, the seed, who held each seat, the orders as handed in, the commander's commands as handed in, what each side's staff handed in (its notices among it), the daily hashes, the version of the state's layout, the rules label and the doctrine version. It is version 4 since decision 0117 (version 3 from decision 0107). It holds no state (decisions 0043, 0100, 0107) | A snapshot a game can be resumed from |
| **Rules label** | The number a saved game records for the rules it was played under, beside the version of the state's layout. It began at 1 (decision 0100), is 2 since decision 0103, 3 since decision 0104, 4 since decision 0105, 5 since decision 0106, 6 since decision 0111, 7 since decision 0109, 8 since the architect's ruling on decision 0106, 9 since decision 0110 and 10 since step 3 of decision 0107, and is raised by a change of rule that changes what an existing game would do; a replay under another label is refused before it runs | The version of the state hash's layout, which changes with the state, not the rules |
| **Point of contact** | Where a choice is made by the men who see what is truly there: the pilot over the target, the captain in the fight. Five choices are made there (which enemy force a raid attacks and whether it finds anything, which ship a strike group attacks, how a patrol divides between raids, whether an avoiding force slips away, who fires at whom) and stay with the rules (decision 0100, 4) | A choice a side can ask before it is made |
| **Choice a side can ask** | A choice the rules make for a side's own unit inside a day from what the side knows, worked by one function the engine also calls; a side asks it through its view and is told what the engine would choose now (decision 0100). Chapter 1 of the rulebook lists them | A forecast of what will happen |
| **Replay** | Running a saved game again from the start with no player and no staff asked, handing in what was saved, and failing on the first day whose hash differs. With `--check-staff` it also asks the doctrine's staff on each saved day and fails on the first difference from what was saved (decision 0107) | The replay file the viewer reads, which is an exported run |
| **Seat** | A side's place in a game. A person, an agent or the script holds it (decision 0050). With command levels on it may be at the area command, a task force or a base, and is shown what that command holds (decision 0138) | A unit |
| **Unit** | A task force, an air group or a land unit: what an order is addressed to. Chapter 7 of the rulebook says "unit" for a land unit alone | A ship |
| **Issuer** | Who gave an order: the player, the staff or the local commander. It is part of the order's origin | Who holds the seat |
| **Origin** | Who issued an order and the intent it serves. Every order carries one (decision 0008) | |
| **Staff** | Code outside `sim-core` that, asked once a day with a side's view and before the day's orders, hands in orders, staff commands, decisions to give no order and notices, each with a reason. The engine passes it in and a side may have none. Every runner gives both sides the supply staff of `sim-staff`; a side with no intent in force has a staff that does nothing. Its orders carry the issuer staff (decision 0107) | The local commander. Older rows and records say "the staff" of a rule that acts for the commander with no order and no reason, such as a tired air group stood down (**Stood down**, **Rest threshold**) and forward repair: those are rules of the day, and not this staff |
| **Local commander** | A rule that gives an order for a unit on the spot, inside a day. Today it is the straggler rule's order to head for the home port (decisions 0016 and 0055) | The staff |
| **Intake** | The step at the start of a day that numbers, checks and applies or refuses the orders waiting (decision 0008) | |
| **Standing order** | An accepted order that stays in force until another order of its slot replaces it | An order carried out and finished |
| **Amendment** | A numbered change to a plan that is approved: the commander's new intent, or a new forces, area, priority, protected or risk limit of its intent, kept in the plan with its day. A reserve is no amendment (decision 0107) | A change made to a draft, which enters its baseline when it is approved |
| **Baseline** | What a plan was approved under: its intent's kind and the intent's limits on the day the plan was first approved. It never changes (decision 0107) | The intent as it stands, which an amendment may have changed |
| **Command** | A change to a side's command state, handed in beside orders and checked and accepted or refused with a reason as an order is. The commander's are to set or withdraw an intent, set a limit, set a leave, approve a plan, cancel one, hold, release, set an interrupt and acknowledge one; the staff's are under **Staff command** (decisions 0107 and 0117) | An order, which is for a unit and works in the world |
| **Command state** | A side's intents (with their parent, maker and addressee) and their limits, its two leaves, its plans, its commitments of ships, its holds and its interrupts. It is hashed, changed only by a command, and seen whole by its own side and not at all by the enemy (decisions 0107 and 0117) | A belief, which is written only from reports |
| **Command (in the chain)** | With command levels on, a part of a side that holds a picture of the enemy: its area command, one of its task forces that has a ship (or one away at a yard to rejoin it) or one of its bases. A ship, an air group and a land unit are not commands; each belongs to one. A task force that has ceased to be stops being one (decision 0134) | The commander's **command** above (a change to the command state, taken in and accepted or refused), or a unit's order |
| **Area command** | The command at the top of a side's chain, made when the game starts with command levels on. Every task force and base is under it unless a scenario names another superior. It reads for the agent's briefing and tools, and for a staff doctrine whose intent is addressed to the area (decision 0134), and with report figures it reads the side's task forces and bases as last reported (decision 0136) | The commander |
| **Picture** | One command's contacts, claims and estimates of the enemy's men ashore: a copy of the side's belief at the start, changed only by a report applied through `BeliefState::apply_report` and by ageing. Without report figures every report reaches every picture at once, so every picture is the side's (decision 0134); with them each picture holds the reports its command held first or was passed (decision 0135). With command levels off a command's picture is the side's own belief | A map, or the truth |
| **Command levels** | The scenario switch `command_levels = true` (off by default) that gives each side an area command and each task force and base a command with a picture of its own (decision 0134) | A level of the intent tree (chapter 8), which is the addressee of an intent |
| **Road** | How a command came to hold a report: its own (it made it), or passed up, sideways or down, on time or a pulse late (decision 0135; shown by `sim-cli events --report`, decision 0138) | |
| **Easier setting** | The area seat shown the side's own picture and its forces exactly, instead of the area command's picture and its forces as last reported (decision 0119, answer 5; decision 0138, 5) | |
| **Report figures** | The file `data/reference/reports.toml`, named by a scenario's optional `reports` key: the chances that a report passed up, sideways or down arrives on time, a pulse late or never, and the radio reach of each kind of ship and of a base. Every figure is a placeholder. With none, every report reaches every command at once (decision 0135) | The search figures |
| **Hold a report first** | To be the command whose picture a report is applied to in the pulse it is made, because it saw the thing: the group's command for a search, the command of the groups that flew for a strike, each task force that fought, a base for a raid on it or a ground count in its hex. Every other command is passed the report (decision 0135) | To hold a base, or to hold (an order or a hold intent) |
| **Radio reach** | How far a command's word carries sideways, in nautical miles between hex centres: a task force's is that of its ship with the longest, by kind, and a base's is its own. The area command has none (decision 0135) | A ship's air or sea range, or a search distance |
| **Report sent** | The event that records a command passing one report on: the sender and, for each listener, the direction and whether it arrives on time, a pulse late or never, with the draw. Its cause is the report's own event. A side sees its own and the enemy sees none (decision 0135) | A report received, which is a report's arrival in the belief of a side |
| **Heard** | Applied once to a command's picture: a command hears a report the first time it arrives and ignores it by any other road, and remembers it until it is as old as a contact may be kept (decision 0135) | Seen or sighted |
| **State report** | A command's report to the area command of its own state, made at the end of each pulse by every task force and base command (the afternoon's at the end of the day): for a task force its record, ships and air groups, for a base its record, air groups and land units. It is drawn once with the `up` chances of the report figures and is held on time, held at the next report moment or never; a late or lost one is an event the side sees (decision 0136) | A sighting report, a claim or any report of the enemy, which a picture holds |
| **Radio silence** | A task force's setting, off unless a scenario, the commander's order `set_radio_silence` or the staff at preserve the force turns it on. A silent force sends no state report and none of its own reports, and keeps them; it breaks silence in a pulse in which it fights, is attacked or launches a strike or raid, and then sends what it kept that is not too old and that pulse's state report. Nothing listens to radio yet, so it buys nothing (decision 0136) | A hold of a force from the staff, or a force's readiness |
| **As last reported** | The way the area command reads a unit of a command under it: the state the latest state report that arrived gave, with the pulse it is of, and not the truth. A reason of an area doctrine says "(as of the morning of day 1)" where it is older than the pulse just ended. A command reads its own units exactly (decision 0136) | A contact, which is the side's picture of the enemy |
| **Commander (of a command)** | The man a scenario names for one command of a side, with command levels on: a name, an initiative rating and a note of what the rating rests on. Every rating is a placeholder. He goes with his command; a command with none named is steady (decision 0137) | The commander, the player or agent who gives a side's commands and orders. A local commander, a rule |
| **Initiative** | A commander's rating: cautious, steady or bold. It is read by one rule, the closing of a carrier force on a contact of its own, which weighs that contact at one level of risk more careful (cautious) or bolder (bold) than the task's (decision 0137) | Anything else a force does: its speed, fuel, search, strike settings and break-off are its task's risk's. A refusal |
| **A contact of its own** | A contact in a command's picture whose last making or refreshing there was by a report the command held first: its own search, strike or fight, or a starting contact the scenario says it saw (`seen_by`). Every other contact of the picture is one it was told of: sent to it from above or a neighbour, or a starting contact every command was told of (decision 0137) | A contact the side holds. With command levels off there is no such mark |
| **Commitment** | A ship given to a plan, and to one of its runs if named, from one day to another, both included. A ship holds at most one on any day. Only the staff makes or ends one, and no rule of the day reads it (decision 0107) | A reserve, or a cargo order |
| **Commitment of stock** | The tons at a run's source still to be loaded for it: its load less what is aboard while it loads there. No record of its own: it is the run, and a new run of any plan draws only on the stock above the reserve and above these tons (decision 0107, ruling 12) | A reserve, which is the base's |
| **Days of supply** | A side's supply stock in a hex over its planned use there for the day, cut at six decimals; none where the planned use is 0 ("no planned use, no figure") and none where the side holds no base there. Read when the staff is asked. It is `planned_days_of_supply` in the briefing's base entry and `days_of_supply` in the agent's list of bases and a base's entity line (decision 0107) | The briefing's older `days_of_supply`, which the base entry and the supply warning keep: the base's stock over yesterday's spending, cut to one decimal; and the land units part's, the stock over the day's charge for its aircraft and men |
| **Displaced demand** | An intent that needs a run and cannot have one, or has its load cut, because a ship of its pool or stock at its source is committed to another plan. Its reason and its proposal name its shortfall in days at the reading it checked and each commitment that caused it, by plan, run, ships or tons, and days (decision 0107, ruling 14) | A shortfall with no other plan's commitment in it, which is the intent's own |
| **Doctrine version** | The version of the doctrine whose staff was passed in: `"supply-8"` from step 2 of stage 2 (the air doctrine; `"supply-7"` before it, `"supply-6"` before decision 0112, `"supply-5"`, `"supply-4"` from step 4 of stage 1, `"supply-3"` from step 3, `"supply-2"` from step 2, `"supply-1"` in step 1), `"none"` in games saved before a doctrine existed. A saved game and the header of a `play --game` file record it. It is checked when a `play --game` file is resumed, which is refused under another version, and not when a game is replayed (decision 0107) | The rules label, or the version of the state's layout |
| **Intent** | What the commander wants: an **end**, a state of the world on one of the side's bases, set once and standing until withdrawn. Ten kinds are built: keep a base supplied (a base, a floor in days of supply and optionally an interval between shipments), hold a place and cover a base from the air (each a base and nothing more), neutralise a base (an enemy base as its target), raid a base (a base of the side and a target) (decision 0120), deny an area (a hex and a distance), strike ships from a base and search waters from a base (a base each) (decision 0123), stand at a hex and operate a carrier force (a task force of the side and a hex each) (decisions 0124 and 0125). The commander numbers each intent he sets, and a withdrawn number is never used again. An intent has a parent, a maker and an addressee (**Intent tree**) (decisions 0107, 0116, 0117) | An order, or a mission |
| **End** | An intent that is a state of the world the commander wants, met or not met, on something on the map: "the airfield stays open", and not "fly patrols". The commander states ends. Hold a place is the one end built beside keeping a base supplied (decision 0116, 1) | A means: what the staff does to meet an end is a **Task** |
| **Task, sub-intent** | A means the staff forms to meet an end, which is itself an intent, lower in the tree, with a parent. A **sub-intent** is an intent with a parent. Seven tasks are built: cover from the air, formed under hold a place, a raid, formed under neutralise a base, bombarding a base, formed under neutralise a base or take a place (decision 0146), striking ships and searching waters, formed under deny an area, standing at a hex and operating a carrier force, formed under deny an area or hold a place, ready a landing, formed under take a place, and keep supplied, formed under hold a place (decision 0144). Its maker is the staff and it belongs to its root's plan (decisions 0116, 4 and 6, 0117, 0124 and 0125) | A **Doctrine**, which is how the staff decides, or an order, which is given only at the leaves |
| **Intent tree, root** | The intents of a side, each with its parent: an intent with none is a **root**. The commander's `set_intent` makes a root and the staff's `form_intent` makes a sub-intent. A tree has one plan, its root's (but a take, and a supply intent under a hold, formed under an island have a plan of their own, decision 0151). Withdrawing an intent withdraws everything beneath it. A limit not set on an intent is read from the intent above it, and a limit set on a sub-intent may only narrow what its parent allows (decision 0117) | The order of the plan in time, which is a plan's phases and is not built |
| **Maker** | Who made an intent: the commander, by `set_intent`, or the staff, by `form_intent`. The commander may not set or withdraw an intent the staff made: he shapes it by its limits, by holds, and by the intent it stands under (decision 0117, 1 and 5) | The issuer of an order, or the owner of a plan |
| **Addressee** | The level an intent is given to, and so what it may use where no forces limit says otherwise. It is set by the kind: the area for keeping a base supplied, which commands nothing without a forces limit, and a base for holding a place, covering a base and raiding a base, which commands the side's air groups based there; neutralising a base is the area's too, with the one exception that it may use every base of the side to form raid tasks (decisions 0116, 12, 0117, 2 and 8, and 0120, 7). A **task force** is an addressee too, for the intents that stand a force at a hex and operate a carrier force: it commands its own ships and the air groups aboard them (decisions 0124, 6, and 0125, 1). A land formation is not built as an addressee (decisions 0116, 12, 0117, 2 and 8, and 0124) | The base an intent names, which is its subject: a supply intent names a base and is given to the area |
| **Short of ammunition** | A ship whose rounds aboard are below the share of her outfit her risk gives, or who has fired more of her tubes than the share her risk allows (preserve the force: three quarters of her outfit and no tube fired; balanced: a half and half her tubes; press: a quarter and never). A force is short when any ship of it is, does not sortie and is not a free escort until it has rearmed (decision 0127). A ship of a class whose guns carry the marker for ammunition that is not limiting is never short of rounds | **Lacking** ammunition, which is any round or tube below full: a ship may lack some and not be short |
