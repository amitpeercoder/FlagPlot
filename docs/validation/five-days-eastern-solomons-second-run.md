## Did each order do what it was for

Seen in the game on the scenario's own seed, 1942:

- **The convoy leaves.** It lands all 1,500 men on the night of day 2 and arrives at Rabaul at 04:54 on day 5.
- **The bombers are escorted.** The Tainan Air Group flies to Lunga with them on days 1 to 3 and claims aircraft shot down there.
- **Lunga's dive bombers strike ships.** The staff forms a task to strike ships from Lunga under the deny. On day 1 VMSB-232 flies at a contact at (4,-9) in the morning and finds nothing, then at the Ryujo detachment at (7,-9), 200 nm, in the afternoon: 1 hit, 6 of its 12 aircraft lost by the end of the day. It hits Kagero once on day 3. It strikes whatever contact is in reach, and the carriers are in reach.

That game ends Allied 22.69, Japanese 8.87: an Allied major victory.

## What the counts say

1. **The mended orders work as orders and move the result toward the Allied side.** The Allied side wins 963 games where it won 846.
2. **The escort does not shut Lunga.** It is shut on some day in 8 games, against 14.
3. **The convoy still lands** in 997 games; in 3 it lands nothing. No transport is sunk. Leaving saves destroyers (Kagero is sunk in 404 games, against 527) and scores nothing.
4. **The dive bombers at Lunga join the carrier fight.** A Japanese carrier is sunk in 561 games against 388, Ryujo in 419 against 277; an American carrier in 285 against 355. The run does not say how much of that is theirs.
5. **The points the Japanese side cannot reach are the same two:** the 10 for Lunga shut, got in 8 games, and the landing, worth 0.45.
