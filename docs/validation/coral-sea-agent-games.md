# The Coral Sea: four games between agents

Scenario 105, decision 0188, file d (`data/scenarios/coral-sea/01d_coral_sea.toml`): neither side given orders, each side commanded by an agent through the game's own tools, the two playing at the same time. Played on Nov 20, 2026, at the architect's asking, on an engine with decisions 0190 to 0193 built. These are decision 0187's guard three: four games, two models, each on each side twice.

The games' records (a saved game and a transcript each) are kept by the architect's session and are not in the repository. Each saved game replays to its end. The figures below are the umpire's results as each game's final briefing gave them to both commanders, who agree.

## What decided each

1. **Games 1 and 2.** Each Japanese commander set the take of Port Moresby to press on the first evening, so that the convoy would not turn back, and to go on no count of the garrison. Both convoys landed on the night of day 4. In game 1 the Allied commander denied the waters with the waters first, and his aircraft sank two transports with 1,200 men; 4,186 still landed. In game 2 the Allied side lost the convoy after day 2 and never found it.
2. **Game 3.** The Japanese commander's take waited for a count of the garrison that he never sent anyone to make. On day 3 he measured the convoy's passage, found it could no longer arrive, and sent it home. The Allied commander used direct orders throughout and no intent.
3. **Game 4.** The convoy sailed. The Allied commander stood his cruisers in the hex of Port Moresby itself, and they fought the convoy there on day 4 and after, sinking Yubari and three destroyers. His carriers sank Shokaku on day 1. No man landed.

## What the commanders reported of the game

Each was asked what confused him or was missing. What more than one said, or what bears on a rule:

- **A contact's reported kind changes from report to report** (all eight). Strikes went at contacts that were not there, and twice at one on the commander's own station.
- **The staff orders men ashore to attack on the last day,** when the place cannot be taken and the attack only loses men the enemy is scored for. Both Japanese commanders who landed overruled it and kept the men dug in. A fault of decision 0193 as built: it does not weigh the days left.
- **No warning of fuel.** Two commanders ran destroyers nearly dry at full speed (to 25 t and 36 t). The staff weighs fuel only against a home port, and these forces have none.
- **The staff's carrier search is aimed at the enemy's base,** not at the waters he is likely to be in. Two commanders set the arc themselves.
- **A carrier force that has closed on a contact stays on it** while it is in reach, whatever its station. One commander could only move it by a direct order, which took its air groups from the staff too.
- **A plan that waits says little.** A take that waits for a count gave no order and no warning that it could no longer arrive in time.
- **The tools list is too long to read:** about 2,200 lines.
- **The passage forecast's hours are counted from the game's start,** not from the leaving (three).
- **In game 4 the convoy lay in the hex of Port Moresby for two days and landed no one,** fighting the Allied cruisers there in every pulse. Its commander took it for a missing order to unload. The record shows the force in a fight each pulse and never halted to work cargo. Whether a force may land men while an enemy force fights it in the hex is not looked into here.

## Conduct

Three commanders reported slips against their briefs. Two opened the script that carries their calls to the game, and one listed his folder; the script holds his own seat's address and nothing of the game. One wrote a file outside his folder and deleted it. None read anything of the game or of the other side. The architect does not think a result is touched, and records it.

## What this report does not do

Four games are few. They show that the result follows the commander more than the side; they do not measure by how much. No figure of the scenario is set from them.
