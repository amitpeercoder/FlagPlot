# On what information a strike was launched, 1942

Status: research report for the architect, read on 2026-11-15. Nothing here is settled. The rows are in `data/oob/engagement_events.csv`, ids 2101 to 2207 (107 rows), six new sources (ids 1356 to 1361 in `sources.csv`) and eleven gaps in `gaps.csv`. No row was needed in `air_unit_facts.csv` or `attributes.csv`: every statement is an event of an engagement and fits `engagement_events.csv`. "Miles" in the naval sources are taken as nautical, and each row that carries a `range_m` says so.

How the pages were read: through the ordinary web fetch tool, which returns the passage asked for as fragments of at most 125 characters. Where a row's `as_stated` is a fragment, the note on the row says so. The ONI narrative of Savo Island and the Eastern Solomons was read from the saved text of the printed volume (govinfo), in full for the Eastern Solomons. Times are as each source prints them; none was converted. The reports of the ships at Midway use zone plus 10; the ONI narrative of Midway prints times about two hours earlier for the same events (Enterprise launching begins 0906 in the Enterprise report and 0700 in ONI), which is consistent with zone plus 12, but the passages read do not state ONI's zone, so ONI's times are not converted here.

## What was asked

For the carrier strikes of 1942 and the land-based strikes against ships: the hour of the contact report, what it said, the hour the strike was ordered and the hour it left, the range, how old the contact was, whether a second report was waited for, what was held back (fighters kept for patrol against fighters sent as escort; attack aircraft kept in reserve), and the doctrine in its own words. The subtraction for the age of a contact is done in the tables below, from the two hours the source gives; no row carries an age.

## The launch decisions

"Age" is the launch hour minus the hour of the report, worked here from two hours of one source's clock. Where the source gives the hour of the sighting and a later hour of receipt, both are shown. A dash means the sources read do not give the figure. Row ids are in square brackets; ids below 2101 are rows already in the data.

## Doctrine and standing instructions, in their own words

None is a written rule of the fleet on when to launch. USF-74 and USF-77 were not found (see the gaps).

- Operation Order 2-42 of Task Force 17, 1 May 1942 (Fletcher): "Locate and destroy, by bombing and torpedo attack, enemy forces reported" (Air, para. 2); "Trail any enemy surface units encountered, reporting contact promptly." (Search Group, para. 4); "Maintain absolute radio silence except for enemy contact reports." (Communications, para. 6(1)); the Attack Group is to "Destroy enemy forces encountered and defend carriers against air and submarine attack while this force is concentrated." (para. 3). The order gives no attack radius, no composition of strike groups and no instruction to hold aircraft in reserve [2204 to 2206].
- Nimitz's instruction to the task force commanders before Midway: "strong attirtion tactics, only, be employed and that our carriers and cruisers not be unduly reisked" (the page's spelling) [2165].
- Fletcher to Spruance, 0807, 4 June: "proceed southwesterly and attack enemy carriers when definitely located" [2141].
- Nimitz after Midway: "Aircraft should be launched and attack completed with the absolute minimum loss of time." (para. 78) [2166]; "Early, accurate, and continuous information of the enemy is essential for successful attack by carrier groups." and "Contact once made must be held and tracking information broadcast." (para. 79) [2167]; "carriers should still maintain an alert search with their own planes, accepting reduction in offensive power for greater security." (para. 79); "The TBF is much improved, but still cannot attack ships defended by fighters without fighter support." (para. 72) [2168].
- Spruance after Midway: "Early and accurate information of movements of an enemy force to be attacked is essential for successful carrier operations." (para. 9(j)) [2149]. ONI quotes Spruance and Fletcher: "the side which is able to strike the first blow against enemy CV's whose planes are on board wins." [2169].
- Yorktown's captain after Coral Sea: "Each carrier should have a minimum of 27 fighters; 36 would be none too many" and "the fighter escorts were seriously hampered by their lack of range." [2118]. Hornet's captain after Midway: "VF which accompany the attack group should have the same cruising range as the aircraft they accompany." (strike-ranges.md). ONI on Coral Sea: "Insufficient fighter planes prevented adequate protection of either our attack groups or ships of the task force." [2132]; on Midway: fighters "still too few for their duties of combat patrol and escorting attack groups." [2170].
- The reason for the escorts and the patrol, Coral Sea, 4 May: "Only four fighters were launched for protection of attack groups as there was practically no air opposition." [2103]. Midway, 6 June: "Eight fighters were sent too as a precaution against possible air opposition." [2163].
- Point option and range, from the Lexington on 8 May: "Since the distance to the enemy was so great (180 miles) I personally told Lieutenant GAYLOR ... to make the trip if gasoline permitted" [2126]; the Yorktown's captain recommended a change of point option "so the fighters, when launched, would have a shorter distance to go to escort VT" [2116].
- Japanese: no standing doctrine was found. The interrogations give decisions: "we did not know how many ships you had, so it was decided not to attack until morning." (Yamaoka, on 7 May) [2136]; "It was therefore decided that we would await the return of the Midway attack unit" (Nagumo's report) [2171].

## Held back and cancelled, with the reason given at the time

- Coral Sea, 7 May, afternoon: no second attack "until more had been learned of the two other enemy carriers" [2128]; an attack after "an extensive search" would have met "insufficient daylight" [2104]; a night attack was not made because the information was "too doubtful" (Naval War College) [2133] and the force was to stay concentrated [2129].
- Coral Sea, 8 May, afternoon: another air attack was given up because the damaged carriers' speed fell to 30 and 24 knots and "our ships might be subject to a strong carrier air attack before dark" [2130].
- Midway, 4 June: the Yorktown's group waited because only two carriers had been reported, then half of it left for fear of being caught with all aircraft aboard [2151, 2152, 2156].
- Eastern Solomons, 24 August: Saratoga's strike held for "more definite word" and for the second search [155, 2188]; Enterprise's strike on Ryujo dropped because the aircraft could not return before nightfall [2190].
- Santa Cruz, 25 October: Hornet's aircraft kept in reserve [2176]; 26 October: no strike on the 0110 or 0410 reports; a third group planned for 1100 was forestalled [2183].
- Guadalcanal, 13 November: an attack group "held in readiness while the area was being combed" [2195]; 14 November: no pre-dawn group launched [2201].

## Judgments made at the time or soon after

- "it was deemed expedient to allow the planes to continue, and this proved a wise decision" (ONI on Fletcher, 7 May, after the false report) [2127]; "As it turned out this was a fortunate mistake." (Yorktown air operations, p. 3).
- "it was apparent that he was in better position to receive an air attack with his attack planes in the air" (Naval War College, on not recalling the groups; low confidence on page) [2134].
- "The weakness of his analysis in this case appears when it is realised that sunset was at about 1816" (Naval War College on Hara's launch of about 1615 on 7 May; low confidence on page) [2135].
- "our TBDs gallantly attacked without waiting for the arrival and support of our dive bombers." (Spruance) [2147].
- "should have had the two large carriers for targets", on the 1435 launch of 24 August, and "a hopeless mission" for the 1510 launch of 23 August (ONI conclusions) [2192, 2193].
- "Not wishing to break radio silence, Admiral Kinkaid did not recall the planes." (25 October) [2178].

## Where sources disagree

- Coral Sea, 7 May, the first report. Yorktown air operations: 0845, "2 CV's and 4 CA's" [2107]. ONI: 0845, "2 enemy carriers and 4 heavy cruisers" (p. 13). Lexington enclosure C: 0926, "2 aircraft carrier, 4 cruisers", bearing 325, "about 180 miles" [2119]. Lexington fighter director: 0835 [2120]. The lead's reading that the Lexington captain gives 0835 was not found: the captain's narrative is missing in the page, and 0835 is the fighter director's entry.
- Coral Sea, 7 May, the launch. ONI: Lexington about 0925, Yorktown from 1014 [404, 405]. Lexington enclosure C: about 0953 [2121]. Fighter director: 0925 [2120].
- Coral Sea, 7 May, what the false report really was. Yorktown: "2 DD's instead 2 CV's" [2111]. ONI: "2 heavy cruisers and 2 light cruisers" (p. 14).
- Coral Sea, 8 May, the range and bearing. Yorktown: 120 miles, bearing 006 at 0835, then 175, bearing 028 at 0847 [2113, 2114]; Lexington: 170 (captain), 175, bearing 020 (air operations), 180 (air officer) [2124, 2126]; ONI: about 170 [406]. The launch: Yorktown 0900 to 0915; Lexington 0900 to 0920 (captain), 0907 to 0925 (air officer), 0925 (ONI, from another source) [407, 2125].
- Midway, 4 June, the first reports. Enterprise: 0734 and 0803 [2139, 2140]; Spruance: 0740 [2145]; Yorktown: 0800 [2150]; Hornet: 0810 [Hornet report]. The clock zone of the Hornet's entry is the same (zone plus 10) as the Enterprise's, so the reports as the ships logged them differ by 36 minutes; the Hornet's launch hour is printed "090".
- Midway, the clock. ONI's times for the launches are about two hours earlier than the ships' (ONI 0700 begins launching; Enterprise 0906). The pairing of ONI's 0838 with Yorktown's completion at 1106 on the TF 17 clock suggests the same two hours; neither is converted.
- Midway, 5 June, the report. The fetch tool gave "179°32' E." for the position in ONI's quotation of Nimitz's report; the Enterprise gives "179° - 32' W". The ONI text was not read in full.
- Eastern Solomons, 24 August, the launch hour. ONI 1435 [188, 396]; cv6.org action report about 1400 [189]; Wikipedia 1340 [191]; combinedfleet 1315 [1246]. ONI itself says (p. 42) the Saratoga group "were launched" at about 1130 after refuelling and also (pp. 43 to 44) that it was "held in readiness" until 1435; the narrative does not reconcile the two [2187].
- Santa Cruz, 26 October: the battleship sighting is 0730 in the timeline and 0717 in the narrative (p. 36 and p. 41).

## What is still a gap

- Written fleet doctrine of 1941 and 1942 (USF-74, USF-77, the Pacific Fleet edition) on when to launch, at what range, on what report and how much to hold. Not found online.
- The hour the strike was ordered, for most strikes. It is given for the Yorktown on 7 May (0845) and 8 May (0848), and nowhere for the other carriers.
- The Lexington captain's own narrative of 7 May; the PDF of the Lexington report (`CoralSeaAAR02.pdf`) did not open (over the fetch tool's 10 MB limit), and the transcription of it is marked as missing text.
- The Rabaul strikes against the ships off Guadalcanal on 7 and 8 August: their hours, and the report each left on. Only the 0652 contact report and a request to augment searches by land attack planes are held (low confidence) [2203].
- Strikes from Henderson Field against ships, August to November 1942: no Marine order or log found, and few launch hours.
- The Japanese carrier launch of Midway (1024 or 1030), and which clock each Japanese source uses.
- Fighters held for patrol against fighters sent as escort, by carrier and strike. Escort counts are given for most strikes; patrol counts for a few.
- Not opened: the Naval War College analysis of Midway in the passages that bear on launching; *Pacific Counterblow*, chapter 2; the Navy history office's page on carrier against carrier (timed out). The lead rows for Bombing Squadron Five's report, *Marines at Midway* and the Hornet's own report of 5 June were not needed for a row.
