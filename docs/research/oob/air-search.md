# Air search, South Pacific, Aug 1942 - Feb 1943

Status: research note. Design input for scenarios 3 to 5; it proposes nothing binding.

How to read it: drafted by a research subagent from pages read on 2026-11-10, then reviewed. Each statement carries its source. The Navy's own history site would not load that day, so some points rest on Wikipedia or a single website; those are marked "unverified", "W" or "WP lead" and should be checked against a primary source before a rule relies on them.

Wikipedia is flagged "(WP lead)" wherever used. Coverage gaps are listed at the end of the findings.

## US carrier scouting

- On 24 Aug 1942 Enterprise sent 20 SBDs on a dawn search covering 290 to 070 degrees out to 200 miles. They found nothing and landed at 1050. A 23-plane afternoon search at 1315 covered 270 to 090 degrees out to 250 miles. [Battle of the Eastern Solomons, ONI Combat Narrative, 1943, https://ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/USN-CN-ESols/USN-CN-ESolomons-4.html, "Events of the 24th: Air Search and Patrol"]
- At Santa Cruz, Enterprise sent 12 scouts out in pairs at 1430 on 25 Oct to search sector 280 to 010. [Battle of Santa Cruz Islands, ONI, 1943, https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/USN-CN-SantaCruz/USN-CN-SantaCruz-3.html, "The Approach"]
- Scouts were also fighters' targets. Japanese fighters repeatedly attacked the search planes on 26 Oct. [ONI Santa Cruz, https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/USN-CN-SantaCruz/USN-CN-SantaCruz-5.html]

## US land-based and patrol aircraft

- On 25 Oct a shore-based patrol plane reported two battleships, four heavy cruisers and seven destroyers at 1120. At 1250 the tender Curtiss relayed a sighting of two Japanese carriers. [ONI Santa Cruz, SantaCruz-3.html, "The Approach"]
- Six B-17s from Espiritu Santo attacked Japanese ships on 25 Oct. [ONI Santa Cruz, https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/USN-CN-SantaCruz/USN-CN-SantaCruz-4.html]
- (WP lead, unverified) A radar-equipped PBY sighted the carriers at 0310 on 26 Oct, but Kinkaid received the report at 0512, so he held his strike. Another PBY sighting on 25 Oct was about 355 nmi away, beyond carrier-aircraft range. [Battle of the Santa Cruz Islands, Wikipedia, 2026, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Santa_Cruz_Islands, "Search patterns"]
- (WP lead, unverified) At Eastern Solomons, Catalinas from Ndeni made the first Ryujo sighting at 0935. [Battle of the Eastern Solomons, Wikipedia, 2026, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Eastern_Solomons, "Search and reconnaissance"]
- Navy history says airborne radar let PBYs work at night from 1942 and that VP-12 flew from Henderson Field. This was read only in a search summary of an archived NHHC PDF, which would not load. Treat it as unverified. [Search summary of NHHC pict-m6.pdf, 2012 archive, https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20120916195105/http://www.history.navy.mil/download/pict-m6.pdf]

## Savo Island: the Hudson story (contested)

- Two RAAF Hudsons from Milne Bay sighted Mikawa at about 1020 and 1110 on 8 Aug. The reports reached the fleet at 1845 and 2130. The cause of the delay is described as unknown. [Battle of Savo Island, Wikipedia, 2026, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Savo_Island, "Prelude" (WP lead)]
- The first report read "three cruisers, three destroyers, two seaplane tenders or gunboats." The real force was five heavy cruisers, two light cruisers and one destroyer. Turner and Crutchley read the tenders as a seaplane base heading for Rekata Bay. [The First Battle of Savo Island, John C. Date, Naval Historical Review, 1989, https://navyhistory.au/the-first-battle-of-savo-island-89-august-1942-a-correction-to-the-history/2/]
- The second Hudson, flown by Milne, reported "two heavy cruisers, two light cruisers and an unidentified vessel." Its crew landed and were debriefed at 1404. [Date, 1989, same URL, page 2]
- Date argues the first crew did report at once and that Morison's story of delay is wrong. [Date, 1989, https://navyhistory.au/the-first-battle-of-savo-island-89-august-1942-a-correction-to-the-history/] Wikipedia says the crew tried to radio Fall River without reply and flew back to deliver the report at 1242, and that Morison's "tea" claim has been discredited. These accounts disagree on whether the first signal was sent or acknowledged, so do not model one version as settled. [Savo, Wikipedia, "Prelude" (WP lead)]
- Turner asked McCain for extra Catalina reconnaissance. It was not flown and Turner was not told. Fifteen cruiser scout planes stayed on deck. [Savo, Wikipedia, "Prelude" (WP lead); Date, page 2]
- Mikawa launched four floatplanes. They returned about noon and reported two Allied groups. Three more flew a last reconnaissance at night and dropped flares. [Savo, Wikipedia, "Japanese response" (WP lead)]

## Coastwatchers

- Coastwatchers on Bougainville radioed raid warnings: Mason signalled "27 bombers" on 7 Aug and Read signalled "45 bombers" on 8 Aug. Feldt's book, as quoted, gives about two hours' warning. [Coastwatchers - Thank God Such Men Lived, Ken Wright, Naval Historical Review, 2010, https://navyhistory.au/coatswatchers-thank-god-such-men-lived/2/]

## Radar and fighter direction

- At Eastern Solomons, Enterprise radar picked up a large raid at 88 miles at 1632 and again at 44 miles at 1649. First fighter contact came about 33 miles out. A crowded fighter radio circuit degraded control. Post-action study: 8 pilots never engaged and 12 met enemy fighters first. [ONI Eastern Solomons, ESolomons-4.html, "Air Combat Patrol"]
- At Santa Cruz, vectors put the CAP at 7,000 feet while it stood at 12,000 feet, so it found bombers above it. [ONI Santa Cruz, Appendix B, https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/USN-CN-SantaCruz/USN-CN-SantaCruz-B.html]

## Reporting failures

- A contact on the Ryujo group reached Enterprise 68 minutes late (sighted 1440, received 1548), through frequency congestion and interference. [ONI Eastern Solomons, ESolomons-4.html]
- A scout sighted two large carriers at 1500 but never transmitted. The sighting became known only when the plane landed, about 1840. [same]
- The ONI conclusions say the report on Zuikaku and Shokaku never arrived, and Saratoga's strike went after Ryujo. [ONI Eastern Solomons, https://ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/USN-CN-ESols/USN-CN-ESolomons-8.html]
- Enterprise's torpedo planes found no ship. One pilot took Roncador Reef for ships. [ONI Eastern Solomons, ESolomons-4.html]
- At Santa Cruz, Widhelm's position broadcast was not received by the second wave. [ONI Santa Cruz, https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/USN-CN-SantaCruz/USN-CN-SantaCruz-6.html]

## Japanese search

- The cruiser floatplane was the main scouting arm. The E13A1 "Jake" carried one 7.7 mm gun and was easy prey for fighters. [Eyes of the Imperial Fleet, Jon Guttman, HistoryNet, 2019, https://historynet.com/eyes-of-the-imperial-fleet/]
- On 24 Aug a Chikuma floatplane sighted Task Force 16 at 1400 and was shot down. A second report at 1430 located the force. [Guttman, 2019, same URL]
- At Santa Cruz, two of four Tone floatplanes were shot down. [Guttman, 2019]
- A USSBS interrogation says that after Midway the seaplane tenders were converted to carriers, so floatplanes could no longer do long-range search. [Interrogation Nav No. 60, Capt. Toyama Yasumi, USSBS, 1945, https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/USSBS/IJO/IJO-60.html]
- Not found in any fetched source: H6K/H8K flying-boat practice, and carrier attack planes held back for strike.

## What a rule would need to model

- Search arc, radius and number of aircraft, and the gaps between sectors.
- Search aircraft type: endurance, speed, radar, vulnerability to fighters.
- Sighting versus identification: ship type and count errors, position error.
- Contact-report delay from radio congestion, relay stations or a silent crew, separate from the sighting itself.
- Whether a report reaches the deciding commander at all.
- Weather and visibility, including squalls.
- Who owns the search (theatre air command versus task force) and unfulfilled search requests.
- Coastwatcher and radar warning lead time.
- Fighter-direction quality: circuit load, plot errors, altitude and bearing mismatch.
- Commander's reading of an ambiguous report (the seaplane-tender assumption).

## Search distances in the carrier actions of 1942, and the Japanese search signals of 23 to 24 August (13 November 2026)

For the step 2 coordinator's commission on the Japanese search at the Eastern Solomons (decision 0073). Rows: `engagement_events.csv` 1133 to 1210, with a new kind `search_order` for a search as ordered or planned, beside `search_launch` for a search as flown and `sighting` for the distance at which a contact was made. Distances printed in miles are entered in metres as nautical miles, as the earlier search rows are; the note gives the figure as printed. The reading was done by Sonnet researchers; text quotes were checked by program, and the handwritten Japanese pages were read twice by readers who did not see each other's reading.

### The Ryujo detachment and the signals

From the vanguard's battle report (JACAR C08030051500) and the 8th Cruiser Division's war diary and battle report no. 3 (C08030048300):

- No signal numbered 328 is on any page read. The detachment was set up by Mobile Force operations signal no. 3 (機動部隊信令作第三號): battle type 2 has Tone, Ryujo, Tokitsukaze and Amatsukaze as a detachment under the 8th Cruiser Division commander, to support the reinforcement force and attack Guadalcanal. A signal entered at 2345 on the 23rd orders the detachment to separate at 0200 on the 24th and go south west of the main body. The division's diary has the separation at 0200.
- The diary's report enters no aircraft launch by Tone on the 24th. Ryujo's launches are her two attack groups (6 attack planes and 6 fighters; then 9 fighters at 1048); the first group's time is read 1020 by one reader and 1000 by the other, and the same report gives 1100 and 1200 elsewhere. The vanguard's report has contact aircraft from Hiei and Chikuma, one each, at about 1350. Nothing read supports the website row that has Tone launching searchers with the vanguard at 1100.
- Ryujo was given the third dawn search of the 24th by signal no. 13 (7 aircraft, 250 nm) and relieved of it by signal no. 15 at 2345, which gave the task to Shokaku.

### What a numbered search method fixes

Signal no. 10 gives Shokaku search method 14 with 8 aircraft, base line 140 degrees, 10 degrees between lines, 250 nm out and a 25 nm lateral leg. Signal no. 13 gives method 17 three times with different numbers: 11 aircraft at 7 degrees to 300 nm; 12 at 10 degrees to 250 nm; 7 at 10 degrees to 250 nm. So the method number does not fix the aircraft count, the angle or the distance. Method 8 and "left half" (左反面) in the 0900 order are not defined on any page read; that order's distance is cut at the page edge and its number is read 19 by one reader and 16 by the other.
