# Eastern Solomons, 23 to 25 August 1942: what the sources give a scenario

Status: research note for scenario 5. Read on 2026-11-10. The rows behind every statement are in `data/oob/engagement_events.csv` (145 rows), `engagement_positions.csv` (86) and `engagement_outcomes.csv` (152), each with its locator. Where this note and the rows differ, the rows are right.

## Sources, and how far to trust them

| Source | What it is | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ONI Combat Narrative, 1943 | Wartime account from US reports | The US side: searches, radar, fighters, strikes. Its figures for Japanese ships and losses are claims |
| Enterprise action report, 5 September 1942 | The ship's own report | Times, radar ranges, fighters in the air, hits, ammunition |
| USSBS, *Campaigns of the Pacific War*, 1946 | Short post-war summary with Japanese statements | Japanese losses and damage |
| combinedfleet.com ship records | Modern compilation from Japanese records | Japanese launches and losses. The pages for Zuikaku, Hiei, Kirishima and Mutsuki would not load |
| Wikipedia | | Ryujo's launch, the Japanese withdrawal and the sinking position, at low confidence only |

Not read: Lundstrom, Frank, Morison, Hammel, the Japanese official history, Saratoga's and North Carolina's own reports.

**Clocks.** The 1943 narrative says all its times are zone minus 11½. The Enterprise report gives the same clock times and states no zone. The Japanese-record compilation and Wikipedia run 30 to 40 minutes earlier for the same events (the attack on Enterprise at 1642 against 1711), which is what a different zone would produce, but no source read says which zone they use. **Times are entered as each source prints them. Nothing has been shifted.** A scenario should take the US clock and treat the Japanese-record times as offsets from it.

## The search and detection sequence

Times from the Navy narrative and the Enterprise report unless marked.

### 23 August

- 1040 (0950 by another account): a search plane sights the troop convoy 250 miles north of Guadalcanal.
- 1510: Saratoga launches 31 dive bombers and 6 torpedo planes at it. A Marine group leaves Henderson Field at 1645. The convoy had turned away at about 1300 and neither finds anything.
- About 1700: Fletcher is told the Japanese carriers are still north of Truk. At about 1830 he sends the Wasp group south to refuel. It takes no part.

### 24 August: finding each other

| Time | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 0630 | Enterprise launches 20 dive bombers to search 290 to 070 degrees out to 200 miles. They find nothing |
| 0935 | A patrol flying boat sights the Ryujo group: one carrier, two cruisers, one destroyer, 281 miles from the US carriers. Enterprise has the report at 1017. The Enterprise report gives the sighting as 1005 |
| 1100, 1320 | Fighters shoot down three Japanese scouts near the task force |
| 1309 or 1315 | Enterprise launches 23 aircraft to search to 250 miles |
| about 1400 | A floatplane from Chikuma finds the US carriers and reports before it is shot down (Japanese-record time) |
| 1435 | Saratoga launches 30 dive bombers and 8 torpedo planes at Ryujo. The Enterprise report says about 1400 |
| 1440 | Enterprise search planes sight Ryujo at 198 miles. The report reaches the ship at 1548 |
| 1500 | Two search planes sight Shokaku and Zuikaku at 198 miles and attack, for near misses. **The report is never received.** Fletcher learns of it at about 1840 |
| 1445 to 1600 | The Japanese carriers launch two strikes (Japanese-record times). The second never finds its target |
| 1620 | Saratoga's strike hits Ryujo |

The pattern is the reverse of Savo Island. Both sides found the other by air search in daylight, but the US strike went to the small carrier, because the report of the two large ones did not get through.

### 24 August: the attack on Enterprise

| Time | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 1632 | Radar picks up a large flight at 88 miles, bearing 320. The contact fades at once |
| 1649 | Radar regains it at 44 miles. 38 fighters are in the air |
| 1655 | A fighter section sights the raid at 33 miles: about 36 bombers at 12,000 feet with fighters above. 53 fighters are now up of 54 on the two carriers |
| 1708 | Enterprise finishes launching the 25 aircraft on her deck, to clear it |
| 1711 | Dive bombing begins. It lasts about four minutes, a plane every seven seconds |
| about 1715 | Over. Three hits and several near misses on Enterprise. North Carolina is attacked and not hit. Saratoga, 8 miles away by the narrative and 10 to 15 by the Enterprise report, is not attacked |

The fighters had 39 minutes of radar warning and most of them were airborne, yet the dive bombers reached the ship. The sources give three reasons: the radar's height estimate was low, the fighter radio channel was jammed with chatter so directions could not be passed, and many fighters were held low or far out.

### 25 August

Marine dive bombers from Henderson Field hit Jintsu and the transport Kinryu Maru in the morning. B-17s sink the destroyer Mutsuki as she stands by. The landing is called off. Sources give five different times and aircraft counts for the Marine attack.

## What is still missing

- **Squadron strengths on 24 August.** Only totals were found: 54 fighters on the two US carriers, and at Henderson Field about 20 fighters and 12 dive bombers. The numbers launched give a floor for the Japanese carriers (27 dive bombers each) and nothing more. Per-squadron figures in the data still rest on Wikipedia.
- **Japanese positions and times from Japanese records.** See above.
- **Bomb sizes and the time of each hit on Enterprise.** The detailed damage report was not found.
- **Ammunition for North Carolina, Atlanta and Portland.** Claims only.
- **Ryujo's strike on Henderson Field.** Launch time and numbers come from one compilation and Wikipedia.

## What a rule would need to model

- **Air search that finds part of the enemy.** Sector searches at 200 to 250 miles found the bait and, later, the main body. What was seen depended on which sector was flown and when.
- **Reports that arrive late or never.** Sighting to receipt took 42 minutes for the first Ryujo report and 68 for the second. The sighting of the two fleet carriers never arrived. A strike goes to what the commander knows about, which is not what has been seen.
- **Committing a strike before the picture is complete.** Saratoga's whole strike was in the air against the small carrier 25 minutes before the large ones were sighted.
- **Radar warning of an air raid, with gaps.** First contact at 88 miles, lost for 17 minutes, regained at 44. Height was estimated and was wrong.
- **Fighter direction as the limit, not fighter numbers.** 53 fighters up against 37 attackers, and the dive bombers still got through, because the fighters could not be put in the right place at the right height.
- **Claims far above losses.** By a factor of about three on the US side here.
- **Deck state.** Enterprise launched everything on deck three minutes before the bombs arrived, so the hits found no fuelled aircraft there.
- **A carrier out of the fight for weeks from three bombs, without sinking.** The steering failed an hour and a half after the hits, with the ship still in range of a second strike.
- **Refuelling as absence.** A third of the US carrier strength was away fuelling on the day of the battle.
