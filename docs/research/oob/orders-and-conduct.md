# Orders and conduct at Savo Island and Cape Esperance

Research for the step 2 coordinator's commission of 13 November 2026. Three questions: what Crutchley's special instructions to the screening group said; what Chicago did, and where she was, from about 0147 to 0245 on 9 August 1942; and what Scott ordered at Cape Esperance, and who ordered the Japanese turn away, at what loss. This page says what the rows hold, what was found and what was not. It settles nothing.

The reading was done by Sonnet agents: readers quoting from saved text, clerks keying the records, and an auditor checking each staged row against its quote (340 of 353 passed, 13 were corrected, none was rejected). The Japanese pages were read twice by readers who did not see each other's reading; a row from them is entered only where both readings agree. A row cited below is cited as `outcomes`, `events` or `positions` with its id.

## Crutchley's instructions

The full text of the "Special Instructions to the Screening Group" was not found. No row holds a continuous passage of it. What the rows hold is Bates's paraphrase, one ONI summary, and a few phrases that Gill puts in quotation marks. Where the three give the same clause in different words, the words are shown.

| Clause | What the source says | Rows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Task | The three main groups were to destroy or beat off hostile surface forces trying to enter Iron Bottom Sound. Two principles: beat the enemy off before he sighted or reached the convoy, and concentrate Allied forces to avoid confusion; meet him to seaward of the area between Savo and Sealark Channel, the engaging force staying interposed. (Bates, paraphrase) | outcomes 3515, 3520, 3521 |
| Warning | Air reconnaissance was expected to warn of the approach. Radar pickets were to report a surface force at once, shadow it and report often; destroyers in contact might be ordered to illuminate when cruisers were about to engage. (Bates) | outcomes 3516 to 3518, 3522 |

Why two groups. Bates reports Crutchley's reason: the three American cruisers had not operated under him before the Koro rehearsals, so he used them as a separate group, and he had never had a chance to meet their captains or give them his standing instructions (outcomes 3532, 3533). Gill quotes Crutchley saying he decided to block one Savo entrance with the three heavy cruisers he knew he could command and leave the other to the three American ships (outcomes 3699). Bates finds it dubious that no chance arose and says it cannot be determined why he divided his forces (outcomes 3534, 3541, 3542).

Who was told what when Crutchley left. Bates says that at 2055 Crutchley directed the commanding officer of Chicago to take charge of the Australia group and carry out the basic plan while he closed the McCawley, and said he did not know whether he would return (outcomes 3544 to 3546; events 1211). Gill says he handed charge to Bode at 8.55 p.m. (outcomes 3706). Bates and Gill both say he did not tell Riefkohl in Vincennes, and Bates adds that he did not direct that officer to take command (outcomes 3547, 3571, 3707). Bates says no battle plan was issued that provided for coordination, and that Riefkohl received no orders and did not know Australia was absent or that he was senior in the western screen (outcomes 3566, 3558, 3559, 3567). Riefkohl had objected by dispatch that the two groups could become foul of each other, and had asked for the Australia group's night plan without getting it (outcomes 3539, 3540). Bates adds that Crutchley did not tell CTF 62 or either group commander he would stay in Area XRAY (outcomes 3556). Gill's reason for the silence to Riefkohl, which he marks as presumed, is that the instructions already made clear the northern group should act independently if engaged (outcomes 3707).

Who put Canberra in the lead. Bates: at 2123 Chicago's commanding officer directed the formation to remain as it was with Canberra leading, as Crutchley had ordered (outcomes 3550; events 1212). Gill: Bode directed Canberra to lead in place of Australia (outcomes 3706); Canberra led "by order of Chicago" (outcomes 3708). ONI says only that Bode was left in command though Canberra acted as guide (outcomes 3578). The sources differ; no row settles which.

What the analysis says the instructions did not provide for. Bates says they were written on the conviction that aircraft would locate the enemy early enough for destroyers to concentrate into a squadron (outcomes 3528). He says no provision can be found for night action against a surprise raid detected only within gun range, and that this was the situation at Savo (outcomes 3529, 3530). He adds that the action Helm should take was not set out in the special instructions (outcomes 3561), and that no night battle plan was prescribed for the screening force (outcomes 3570). A quoted later comment calls the radar guard's orders faulty in requiring them to shadow and in saying little about searchlights (outcomes 3536, 3537). After the action began, Crutchley ordered Australia to patrol seven miles west of XRAY and ordered the destroyers not engaged to concentrate on her. The order went in a cipher not generally understood (outcomes 3568, 3569, 3588, 3589). Henley read it correctly at 0206; Selfridge, with ComDesRon Four, took it as an order to concentrate north-west of Savo (outcomes 3590, 3591).

## Chicago, 0143 to 0245

Several points differ between sources and both versions stand. ONI says Chicago's track chart shows her on 283 when hit; Bates has her steadying on 283 after the torpedo passed (outcomes 3679, 3605). Bates and ONI differ on which battery fired (above) and on how far astern of Canberra she was. Bates says the engagement she saw about 0200 was Jarvis and Yunagi, from badly garbled text; ONI says it cannot say what it was, since the only known engagement beyond Savo was Ralph Talbot's about 0220 (outcomes 3638, 3689). Gill has Chicago steering about north-west by west while the battle swept north-east and north, and says no enemy report except Patterson's reached the northern group (outcomes 3709, 3710). Gill, citing Morison, says Chicago made no enemy report (outcomes 3711).

Reasons, in three kinds.

- The ship's own, as Bates reports Chicago's action report: she swung further to port because a destroyer in position to fire torpedoes was seen (outcomes 3601); she stood west to investigate the gunfire and to enter it if occasion permitted (outcomes 3639); the report says "All firing ceased, no ships visible" (outcomes 3643).
- The analyst's. Bates gives several reasons why Bode did not lead the group: Australia might return early and he did not choose to shift position twice in the dark; he had no staff and was tired; he appears to have expected no action despite the 1900 dispatch (outcomes 3551 to 3554). He calls staying in the rear understandable but not sound (outcomes 3555). He says Bode did not warn the task force, did not order Patterson back, let each ship act independently, and made no thorough estimate (outcomes 3612, 3613, 3617, 3622). ONI says he had not yet sized up the situation, and that Chicago had gone west while the enemy passed east and could take no effective action (outcomes 3680, 3690). ONI says only Patterson read the situation correctly (outcomes 3691).
- Conjectures. The notes mark these "possibility only": that the star shells toward Patterson were to learn how far the Japanese had come in (outcomes 3607); that Bode could not do both duties or forgot he was group commander (outcomes 3614); that he felt he might meet others in the south channel, or was too far away to help (outcomes 3629, 3630); that cloud south of Savo hid the Japanese (outcomes 3636); that he slowed because he thought the ships to the west had gone beyond Savo (outcomes 3645); that at 0229 he realised he had given his group no orders (outcomes 3656).

It says doubt over own ships' positions led the group commander to order cease fire (outcomes 3771).

## The Japanese turn

Both readings of the Japanese pages agree on these points; the rows hold only what both read.

- No page names who gave the order to deploy right and fight, or the further right turn, or the decision to break off (outcomes 3834, 3835, 3837). The decision is in the honorific passive. An older row, events 509, names Goto; the second readings find no subject written, and that row is now `disputed`.
- Each ship's turn: Aoba turned further right, with maximum battle speed ordered (outcomes 3835), and the staff table has her reversing to starboard with torpedoes at 2148 (outcomes 3847). Furutaka put her helm to port, with gun and torpedo action ordered (outcomes 3851); the staff table has a right reversal at 2152 (outcomes 3848). Kinugasa went to port at once on seeing enemy fire (outcomes 3852). Hatsuyuki reversed to port after contact and followed Kinugasa (outcomes 3853). No page read gives a turn for Fubuki (outcomes 3850).
- Reasons for breaking off after fifteen minutes: the commander and many staff were killed or wounded, communication was hard, and the position was very unfavourable (outcomes 3836). The staff paper says the first shell hit the flagship's bridge and the senior staff were killed (outcomes 3840). Afterward communications stayed poor and the force could not be gathered (outcomes 3842).
- What the enemy was taken for: the ships ahead were thought possibly to be the own landing unit (outcomes 3838); the staff paper says they were misjudged as Nisshin and Chiyoda, then in transport work (outcomes 3839).
- Losses: Furutaka and Fubuki sunk (outcomes 3854, 3855, 3858); Furutaka 33 killed (outcomes 3856); Kinugasa 1 wounded (outcomes 3857); Hatsuyuki rescued about 400 men from Furutaka, against 513 in another page of the same report (outcomes 3845); Furutaka covered Aoba and took the concentrated fire (outcomes 3841).

The American sources say little of any turn. They saw a destroyer run parallel, turn away and be lit (outcomes 3811), and one turn about 90 degrees to starboard to open the range (outcomes 3812). They saw a burning target retire to the north-west (outcomes 3825) and an enemy destroyer retire in flames (outcomes 3828). They record rapid closing, then high-speed withdrawal when damaged (outcomes 3806). None names who ordered anything. The notes on 3810 and 3830 say so. Their reasons for the enemy's slow fire are conjecture: a junction arranged, no effective radar, two forces, fear of hitting own ships, or a belief they were being fired on by the ships San Francisco's plane had reported (outcomes 3810, 3829 to 3831).

Where the two readings disagree (from the readers' key; none is entered as a row unless both agree):

- Speed ordered at 2143: 26 or 24 knots. Aoba's further right course: 270 or 70 (outcomes 3835 notes it). Furutaka's sinking time: 0028 or 0020. The count of ships at first sighting.
- Whether Kinugasa turned "away from the enemy" in the staff paper: one reader clear, the other doubts the characters; no side is given.
- When Hatsuyuki left to rescue: both read 2330, one reader doubts it could be 330 (outcomes 3845).
- The page where "deploy south" stands in the telegram, and how it differs from "deploy right" in the narrative.

Older rows set against the new readings. Events 504 (Guadalcanal fine-weather message: older 2130, both new readers 2030), 509 (the giver), 514 (the 2150 hit on torpedo tubes: older Aoba, both new readers Furutaka) and 523 (Fubuki 700 m from Aoba: both new readers 70 degrees at 2 km) and positions 506 (the same screen positions) are now `disputed`. Events 525 (Hatsuyuki's rescue start: older 2230, new readers 2330) is annotated and keeps its `preferred` standing because the second reader doubts the first digit. Fubuki and Hatsuyuki at 70 degrees and 2 km is also what outcomes 3843 and 3844 hold, with a note that page 38 gives 3000 m. The two Japanese papers also differ on Aoba's casualties (71 killed and 72 wounded in one, 64 and 37 in the other), per the key; Furutaka's 33 killed is the same in both (outcomes 3856).

## Leads not entered

These come from a web reading round and are not rows. Both are tertiary.

- Kamikaze-class torpedo mounts. ja.wikipedia (神風型駆逐艦, second class): three twin mounts as built, the No. 3 mount removed by 1944 in Yunagi and Hatakaze. en.wikipedia (class and Yunagi pages): positions given, with the aft tubes removed "early in war", undated. ## Not found

- The Hepburn report. The reading round found no text of it.
- The full text of Crutchley's special instructions, only Bates's, ONI's and Gill's extracts.
