# Japanese unit records: daily air losses over Guadalcanal, and Eastern Solomons from the ships' own reports

Status: research report for the architect, read on 2026-11-12. Nothing here is settled. The rows are in `data/oob`: `engagement_outcomes.csv` ids 980 to 1236 (the air campaign: 980 to 1158 and 1191 to 1236; Eastern Solomons: 1159 to 1190), `engagement_events.csv` ids 327 to 395, `engagement_positions.csv` ids 447 to 472, and 33 new gaps.

## What was read, and how far to trust it

The Japanese navy's aircraft unit combat action records (飛行機隊戦闘行動調書) and several ships' war diaries and action reports are online at the Japanese national archives (JACAR) as 400 dpi scans of the handwritten originals. Each air mission has a printed form with boxes: aircraft sent, time of take-off and return, enemy met, shot down and probable, and own losses by kind (shot down, badly damaged, missing, self-destroyed, hit), killed and wounded.

**Second readings.** Seven forms were read twice, by the researcher and then by me, and every figure agreed: Tainan on 26 August, 2 September and 13 September; Kisarazu on 12 September; Shokaku's fighters on 30 August; Shokaku's first wave and Zuikaku's first-wave fighters on 24 August. That is seven of about ninety forms. The rest were read once. Rows say where a character was unclear and are graded down there.

**What these records are.** A unit's own count of its losses is the best evidence there is. Its count of enemy aircraft shot down is a claim like any other.

**Totals, 21 August to 16 September, from the forms:** 29 aircraft missing or self-destroyed and 7 forced landings, 36 in all, with about 60 more hit. The sum is mine. It leaves out the floatplanes of 14 September, the flying boats, and any unit whose forms were not found. Wikipedia's figure from Lundstrom for 21 August to 11 September, 31 destroyed and 7 badly damaged, is of the same order.

**US claims against these losses.** On the eleven days with both a US claim and a Japanese form (26, 29, 30 August; 2, 5, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 September) US claims add to about 100 to 120, by which history is taken, against 29 lost and 7 forced down on those days. That is about **three to one**, a little above the two to three found before from weaker sources. The arithmetic is mine.

**Japanese claims against US losses.** The forms claim about 100 aircraft shot down over the same days, and about 60 to 80 destroyed on the ground (8 on 25 August and about 10 on 2 September, each reported by both bomber units and so perhaps the same aircraft twice; 12 on 26 August; 8 on 29 August; 24 on 12 September). US losses in the data for those days are far smaller: single figures in the air on most days, and four aircraft found destroyed on the ground in the whole period. The US side of this is still thinly sourced, so no ratio is offered, but two things are safe to say: **Japanese fighter claims were at least as inflated as American ones, and Japanese claims of aircraft destroyed on the ground were inflated far more.**

**Where the forms correct the data.**
- 26 August: three Zeros lost, not one or two.
- 30 August: the fighters that fought were Shokaku's from Buka; they lost four, against a US claim of 14 to 19.
- 13 September: the four Zeros were lost on the reconnaissance escort, not the afternoon raid.
- Half the bomber sorties of the period turned back for weather. A raid launched is not a raid delivered.

## 2. Eastern Solomons from the Japanese records

**The search as flown, 24 August** (Tokyo time, which runs two and a half hours behind the US clock of the battle):

| Time | Who | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0415 | Shokaku | 7 attack planes to 250 nm, one per line. Saw one surfaced submarine. All back by 0810 |
| about 0435 | Zuikaku | 12 attack planes, to 200 nm on one sector and about 230 nm on another. All back 0907, nothing seen |
| 0900 | The vanguard | 4 floatplanes, to 330 nm, by the vanguard's own narrative |
| 1205 | Chikuma's No. 2 aircraft | "Enemy large force sighted; being chased by fighters", then silence. The vanguard put the enemy 40 nm south-east of Stewart Island. Ryujo's detachment overheard the report |
| about 1322 | Hiei and Chikuma | Each ordered to launch a shadowing floatplane |
| 1450 | A Chikuma aircraft | Enemy 40 nm east-north-east of Stewart Island |

The dawn groups of eleven and four floatplanes ordered the evening before do not appear in the vanguard's narrative. Whether they flew is not established; most of that report's signal log is unread.

**The strikes, from the carriers' own forms:**

| Group | Launched | Aircraft | Lost | Claimed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shokaku, first wave | 1255 | 18 dive bombers, 4 fighters | 11, with 19 killed. Read twice | 4 shot down and 1 probable; six or more bomb hits on a new-type carrier |
| Zuikaku, first wave | 1300 | 9 dive bombers, 6 fighters | 8 dive bombers, 3 fighters. The fighter form read twice | 4 shot down; a carrier hit |
| Shokaku, second wave | 1355 | 9 dive bombers, 3 fighters | Not read | None; found nothing |
| Zuikaku, second wave | 1400 | 18 dive bombers, 6 fighters | 1, for fuel | None |
| Ryujo, on Henderson Field | 1020 and 1048 | 15 fighters, 6 attack planes | 5 | 13 shot down and 2 probable |

First wave: **22 of 37 lost**, against 24 in the data until now. Ryujo launched from 24 degrees, 110 nm from Ndai Island, by the cruiser division's report (22 degrees, 105 nm by her own form).

**Positions with a latitude and longitude:** the vanguard at 4°N 157°E on 22 August 0600, 5°S 162°30'E on 23 August 1800 and 6°S 163°E on 25 August 0400; Ryujo sank at 6°10'S 160°50'E at 1800 (now read from the primary page); the supply group at 1°58'S 160°28'E. The carriers themselves still have none. **The grid code was not broken:** nine coded positions are recorded, and no page gives a coded position and a latitude and longitude for the same moment.

**Also recorded:** Ryujo's damage (one torpedo to port amidships, machinery and steering gone, list over 20 degrees); the times of the attacks on Tanaka's convoy on 25 August; Kumano's propeller damage.

## Second pass, 2026-11-12: the vanguard's signal log

The rest of the 11th Squadron's action report (JACAR C08030051500, pages 1 to 3 and 12 to 69) was read: the signal log of the vanguard for 23 to 25 August. Rows: `engagement_events.csv` ids 424 to 489, 20 position rows, 8 outcome rows, 8 gaps. Two pages were read a second time by me and agreed: the amendment that cancels the dawn floatplane group, and the sighting report.

**The dawn floatplane groups did not fly.** The amendment to the search order, received at 2050 on 23 August, has a third item the 7th Cruiser Division's copy did not show: "the first search group is not used; the third search group is used" (第一索敵隊ヲ使用セズ第三索敵隊ヲ使用ス). The first group was the eleven cruiser floatplanes to 300 nm. The third was the seven carrier aircraft, which fits Shokaku's own form: seven attack planes at dawn. The fourth group, the older floatplanes, was cancelled at 0018 on the 24th.

**The search as flown, 24 August** (Tokyo time):

| Time | Aircraft | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dawn | Shokaku 7 and Zuikaku 12 attack planes | Nothing |
| 0900 | Chikuma, 2 floatplanes | Her No. 2 sighted the enemy at 1207 and was lost |
| 0940 | Kumano, 2 floatplanes, all she had | Nothing reported |
| 1318 | Hiei and Chikuma, one shadowing aircraft each | Chikuma's No. 1 reported the enemy at 1450 |
| 1515 | Kirishima and Nagara, one older floatplane each, for night contact | Both lost |

The order for the 0900 search, received at 0346, was for six aircraft on a base line of 150 degrees. Four are shown launching. Tone, Suzuya and Kirishima show none in the log. No "Chikuma No. 5" appears in it.

**The sighting, word for word.** From the carrier force's search aircraft, originated 1207, received 1208: "large enemy force sighted" (敵大部隊見ユ). At 1210: "we are being pursued by enemy fighters". Nothing more. Chikuma relayed the enemy's approximate position as a grid code at about 1315; the carrier force's battle summary says the enemy's strength and position were unknown and the strike was sent toward the aircraft's own estimated position, "about 260 nm, direction 153 degrees, from the carriers" at the time of the sighting. **That is the first Japanese figure for the range at which the first wave was launched** (it left at 1255 to 1300, some fifty minutes later), and it is well beyond the 150 to 210 miles of the US launches. It is from one reading and is graded medium.

**Floatplanes lost in the battle,** from the report's closing table: Chikuma's No. 2, Kirishima's and Nagara's older floatplanes missing; a Kirishima observation plane capsized on recovery; Hiei's observation plane burned after combat damage. That is five, against the three the official history is quoted as giving.

**The grid code is still unbroken.** Twenty-seven coded positions are now recorded. No key fits them all. The log names the table (地点標示法) but does not contain it. The one pair close in time is the vanguard: 6°S 163°E at 0400 on the 25th and grid ke-ri-tsu 22 at 0500. The carriers' positions remain grid codes only: ke-mu-ke 55 at 0500, ke-ya-ke 44 at the 1300 launch.

**Not resolved:** many signals have no time of origin, so some rows carry the time of receipt; the bombing of Kirishima is timed three ways in the same report.
