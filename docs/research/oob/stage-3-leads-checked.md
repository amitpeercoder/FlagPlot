# Five leads for stage 3, checked against their sources

Read on 2026-11-17. An outside research pass of 17 November 2026 answered five questions: reports and radio, estimates of the enemy ashore, night forces and bombardments, sizing and supplying a landing, and carrier cover for a landing. Its answers were leads only. Each claim was checked by opening the source it cited and reading the passage. A row was written only where that passage says the fact, and the row cites what was read. Where a source already on file stated the same fact, no new row was written; the row already on file is named below.

This page reports what the rows say. It draws no conclusion about game figures; the architect draws those.

## 1. How long a report took to reach a commander, and radio silence

**What the rows say.**

- **Savo Island, 8 August.** An article of the Naval Historical Society of Australia (Date, 1989) gives the Hudson's sighting of Mikawa's force at 1026 (row 2324). It says Japanese records log Mikawa's receipt of the aircraft's message at 1027 (2325). It reports a South West Pacific Area headquarters station report (No. 350) at 1817 (2326), read by Crutchley at 1839 over Bells (2327) and by Turner at 1845 over Fox (2328). It says Enterprise had the report on her status board in the early afternoon (2329). The article relays a 1988 paper by Captain Bonnot that was not read. It is graded C.
- **Santa Cruz, 26 October.** A patrol plane sighted Zuiho at 0410 (2330), and Kinkaid did not have the report until 0612 (2331). The narrative says the report was held up until about 0600 for a reason it does not know (row 2180, already on file).
- **Eastern Solomons, 24 August.** The sighting at 0935 and its receipt at 1017, Saratoga's interception at 1158, the 1440 contact that reached Enterprise only at 1548, and the 1500 sighting were all on file already (rows 133, 149, 153, 154, 160, 163 to 165). New rows: the narrative's cause for the 1548 delay, fighter direction on the scouts' frequency and interference at high speed (2332), and that no report of the 1500 sighting reached Enterprise (2333).
- **Midway.** Nagumo's message No. 336 was sent at 0555 and received by Combined Fleet at 0630, under the chronology's 5 June heading (2339, 2340). When radio silence must be kept, aircraft return and drop a message for contacts within 100 miles (2336). Captain Aoki said under interrogation in 1945 that the carriers kept radio silence on the way to Midway (2337). Nimitz's message "Reports must get through promptly" is quoted in a 1993 history, undated (2338).
- **TBS.** Turner, quoted by Dyer, says a fault in McCawley's antenna cut her TBS range from the usual 20 miles to about 8 (2334). Exchanges with ships off Tulagi, 15 miles away, had to be relayed through the outer screen (2335). The fault was found and mended only about November 1942.
- Kinkaid's choice of 25 October not to recall his strike so as not to break radio silence was on file already (row 2178).

**Disputes.** Bates (rows 10 and 11) has Crutchley receiving a report at 1645 to 1657 and Turner at about 1900. Date's times belong to station report No. 350. The two may be different messages; neither source settles which. The Date rows are therefore unconfirmed, not disputed.

**What the sources said that the lead did not.** The Date article names station report No. 350 and does not quote Morison; it paraphrases him. The Nimitz passage is on printed page 60, not 59, and gives no date: row 2338 takes 3 June from where the passage stands. Dyer's TBS passage is in the body text; footnote 51 only cites Turner's letter of 1950.

**Not found.** A complete chain of sighting, sending, relay and receipt times for any report. Day and night ranges of the 1942 sets. Written standing orders on radio silence for either navy. Intervals for reports of position, fuel and losses.

## 2. Each side's estimate of the enemy ashore, and the later count

**What the rows say.** Most of this subject was on file already from the land-forces pass (`land-forces.md`). Rows already on file cover:

- Turner's 1,850 and 5,275 (rows 162, 163, 230, 232) and their total, 7,125 (211, 212).
- Ghormley's 3,100 (205, 208, 213) and the division's 8,400 of 20 July (206, 210).
- The later figures of 780 and 2,230 (Dyer), and of 3,457 and 2,571 (Shaw) (143, 144, 221 and others).
- The Japanese view of a landing of perhaps 2,000 (727, 728) and the 17th Army's 7,500 of 19 September (894).
- The Tasimboko figures: perhaps 300, at least 4,000 and Clemens's several thousand (87, 91, 93).
- Vouza's 250 to 500 (79).

New rows:

- The division's estimate made in Wellington on 11 July, a total of 5,000 (1584), and its revision of 27 July with the same total and a different breakdown (1585). Both are from Zimmerman's footnote 8, whose parts are rows 159, 160 and 209.
- How Ghormley's 3,100 was learned (1587): from the coast watchers' information and aerial photographs (Dyer).
- How the 8,400 of 20 July was learned (1588): from MacArthur's headquarters estimate, with aerial reconnaissance and coastwatchers (Miller). Miller says it far exceeded the true strength of about 3,000.

**Disputes.** These are the estimates and counts already on file: the July estimates (5,000, 8,400, 7,125, 3,100) against the later reconstructions (about 2,000 to 2,900 on Guadalcanal and Tulagi). Nothing new was added to them.

**Not checked.** Vouza's 250 to 500 as given in the Marine Corps University Press study (2018). usmcu.edu refused the fetch, and no other copy opened. The same figure is on file from Shaw (row 79).

**Not found.** The dates of the observations behind each estimate. The estimates Ichiki and Kawaguchi themselves attacked on. Any count of the force Vouza or Clemens's scouts reported.

## 3. Japanese ships off the airfield by night, and bombardments from the sea

**What the rows say.** The bombardment rows are under the new engagement `lunga-bombardments-1942`.

- **23 and 24 August.** Zimmerman has Kagero shelling the perimeter on the night of 23 August (2400). On the 24th she was back with four more destroyers, causing only minor damage (2401, 4143). Zimmerman prints one name as "Musuki", read as Mutsuki.
- **6 September.** combinedfleet's Yudachi record has Yudachi and Ariake, with Uranami and Shikinami in brackets as printed, bombarding the airfield on 6 September after failing to find a reported convoy (2403).
- **8 September.** Sendai shelled Tulagi (2404).
- **12 September.** At 2130 Sendai, with Shikinami, Fubuki and Suzukaze, bombarded the ridge (2405).
- **13 September.** Yudachi, Uranami and Murakumo fired between Lunga and Cape Taivu to cover the retreat (2408).
- **18 September.** Sendai with four destroyers shelled Lunga (2409).
- **The communiqués.** The Navy communiqué No. 124 reports shelling on the nights of 12 to 13 and 13 to 14 September without naming the ships (2406, 2407). It reports one vessel hit by shore batteries without saying which night (4144).
- **Rounds.** Except in October, no row gives hours of firing, rounds or effect for these raids.
- **October.** Kongo and Haruna bombarded Henderson Field on the night of 13 to 14 October (2410 to 2413). They killed 41 men and damaged many aircraft (4142). Kinugasa and Chokai fired 752 8-inch shells on the night headed 15 October (2414, 4138). The communiqué reports shelling on the night of 14 to 15 October (2415).
- **The fire of 7 August**, under `watchtower-landing`:
  - From ONI: Quincy opened on Kukum at 0613 (2416); Ellet, Astoria and Vincennes fired on their areas (2417 to 2419).
  - From Zimmerman: Monssen fired 60 rounds at a Florida Island hill between 0727 and 0732 (2420, 4145), and Buchanan and San Juan fired 100 rounds each (2421, 4146, 4147).
- **The run in.** On 30 August Yudachi left the Shortlands at 1000. She was 150 miles from Lunga Point at dusk, began a high-speed run in, and arrived off Cape Taivu at 2230 (2423, passage 394).
- **Savo Island.** Quantock has Mikawa's order to retire at 0220 (2422). This agrees with Bates (row 110).

**Disputes.**

- **Rounds fired by Kongo and Haruna.** Miller gives 918 rounds of 360 mm for both ships, 625 armour-piercing and 293 high explosive (4133). combinedfleet gives 435 for Kongo (4134) and 433 for Haruna (4135). Haruna's parts as combinedfleet prints them, 189 and 294, add to 483, not 433.
- **Duration.** Zimmerman and Miller give 80 minutes (4139, 4140). combinedfleet has the firing begin at 0137 and the retirement at 0230 at 29 knots, 53 minutes (4141, inferred; the retirement is not the end of firing).
- **Date.** combinedfleet heads the entry 13 October. Miller has the force entering Sealark Channel shortly before midnight of 13 October.
- **Ships on 24 August.** Zimmerman names five destroyers. The USSBS row already on file (251) names three.

**What the sources said that the lead did not.** The Yudachi entry for 4 to 5 September names only Yudachi, Sendai, Umikaze, Kawakaze and Suzukaze in the lines read. It states an intention to bombard, not that firing took place (2402, kind `other`). The communiqué's shore-battery hit is not tied to the night of 12 to 13 September. The Toa Maru's fuelling of the three heavy cruisers at Shortland on 6 September was on file already (`place_facts.csv` row 269).

**Not checked.**

- Yudachi's 8 to 9 September sortie south of Savo at 2115.
- Her departure at 1105 on 6 September and her return at 1130 on 7 September: the lines fetched did not include these hours.
- The search of 11 September by three destroyers off Lunga and Tulagi. The passage was read and says what the lead says, but no row was written because the sortie also carried troops; it can be added.
- The paragraph number of communiqué No. 156.

**Not found.** A complete list of the bombardments with hours, rounds by calibre and effect. Rounds kept back for surface action while bombarding, for either navy. US rounds fired by Quincy, Astoria, Vincennes and Ellet on 7 August.

New rows:

- Miller's statement that after 9 August the division was without air cover or naval surface support (1633).
- Miller on the destroyer-transports' run of 15 August with aviation ground crews and supplies (573).
- Zimmerman's definition of combat loading, unloading in the order an assault needs (574).

**Disputes.** None new.

**What the sources said that the lead did not.**

- **FTP 167.** The doctrine's statements are in paragraphs 112 and 113 of the body text, not 110 and 111; the contents list numbers them 110 and 111.
- **The rations of the two companies.** They come from officers' letters of 1948 and 1949 quoted by Zimmerman on page 40, not from a primary document.
- **The run of 31 August.** Zimmerman's sentence reads as the four destroyers of the 29th plus Amagiri, Kagero and Suzukaze. The run is on file with eight ships (row 156); the lead said seven.

**Facts with no file, kept here for the architect.**

- **FTP 167, Landing Operations Doctrine** (Change 1, no year printed), chapter I, section II, read on the HyperWar copy (https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/ref/Amphibious/Amphibious-1.html):
  - Paragraph 112(2): "superiority of force, particularly at the point of landing, is essential to success".
  - Paragraph 112(3): "Numbers alone cannot afford the required superiority."
  - Paragraph 113(2): "An extensive overseas expedition presupposes marked superiority on the sea and in the air".
  - It gives no ratio. These are doctrine, not a battle; no file holds land or amphibious doctrine. The `pacific-air-doctrine` engagement is for air doctrine.
- **Rabaul, January 1942.** Shaw and Kane, *Isolation of Rabaul*, part V chapter 1, page 445 (https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USMC/II/USMC-II-V-1.html; source `shaw-kane-usmc-ops-v2`): Rabaul had "a small garrison of about 1,350 men, a reinforced Australian infantry battalion", and "the 5,000-man Japanese landing force". There is no Rabaul land entity, so no row was written.

**Not found.** A numerical ratio of attackers to defenders in a 1942 manual. The men carried in each named transport. A threshold in days of rations behind the order of 12 August. All three are in `gaps.csv`.

## 5. Where carriers stood to cover the landing, and what they did

**What the rows say.** Wasp's own action report of 14 August 1942 (a new primary source, read from the page images of the scan) gives:

- **Positions.** Her launching position on 7 August was 84 miles, 240 degrees from Tulagi (position 556). The air support force at dawn on 8 August was 87 miles, 167 degrees from Tulagi and 34 miles from Cape Henslow (557).
- **The first launch, 7 August.** At 0530: 16 fighters and 15 scout bombers (2450, 2451). The first combat patrol went up at 0557, before sunrise at 0631 (2452). Four, then eight, fighters stayed over the carriers (2453, 2454).
- **Later on 7 August.** At 0700, 9 TBFs with four 500-lb bombs each (2455). A continuous group of 4 fighters and 9 scout bombers was kept over Transport Group Yoke (2456). At about 1030 every fighter was put in the air for Enterprise's fighter director (2457).
- **8 August.** At 0600, 12 scout bombers searched to 220 miles (2458). Eight fighters were kept over the transports until 1200 (2462, 4203). Nine fighters were launched at 1140 at the fighter director's request (2461). Wasp made 89 launches and 86 landings that day (4200).
- **Claims.** An estimate that 15 patrol and 7 fighter seaplanes were destroyed before they could take off (4201, 4202).
- **The captain's judgements.** Control of the three carriers by prearranged schedules kept one carrier ready to launch at all times (4206). The procedure is "a correct one when enemy air opposition is weak"; against strong opposition, other methods should be used before a landing (4207). The plans Wasp launched under are named (4208).

Zimmerman has Wasp's Flight 101 of 15 fighters strafing at 0614 and Flight 102 of 15 dive bombers bombing at 0618 (2459, 2460).

**The withdrawal.** ONI says the carriers asked late on 8 August to retire, with fuel running low, 20 of their 99 fighters lost, and the enemy showing considerable air strength (2463, 4204). Zimmerman has Fletcher reporting a shortage of fuel and the loss of 21 of his 99 planes (2464, 4205). Carter has Fletcher fuelling on 3 and 4 August (2465), and again on 10 August, 500 miles south of the unloading area (2466). Zimmerman's figure of 17 days of fuel was on file already (`ship_facts.csv` rows 1826 to 1828).

**Ryujo, 24 August.** Cox, read on a reprint, has Nagumo detaching Ryujo before dawn to cover Tanaka's convoy (2467). He has her launching 15 Zeros and 6 Kates at Guadalcanal at 1230 (2468); the row on file from Wikipedia (182) has 1220. Both rows are low, because the reprint is graded C. The NHHC original (`nhhc-hgram-010`) did not open.

**Disputes.**

- **Losses before the withdrawal.** ONI's 20 of 99 fighters (4204) against Zimmerman's 21 of 99 planes (4205).
- **The first strike.** Wasp's report launched 16 fighters; Zimmerman's Flight 101 strafed with 15. These may count different things, so neither row is marked disputed.

**What the sources said that the lead did not.**

- **The launches of 7 August.** The scan prints them as "22?". The last digit reads 3 or 5, not clearly 5. No row was written. The landings, 221, are clear but were not entered alone.
- **The withdrawal passage.** Zimmerman's "21 of 99" passage stands just after his page 50, not 51.
- **The 500 miles.** It is Carter's, not in Zimmerman's footnote.
- **The screening units.** Miller's "about a hundred miles to the east" names no point of reference, so no row was written.

**Not found.**

- Separate positions for Saratoga and Enterprise.
- Each fighter's time on station.
- Japanese distances from carrier to convoy.
- A 1942 doctrine, for either navy, giving a carrier's distance from enemy airfields or its days on station before fuelling.
