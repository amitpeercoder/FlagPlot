## US torpedoes

**Mark 14 (submarine).** Three separate faults. Depth: Lockwood's net test at Frenchman Bay on 20 June 1942 showed runs about 11 ft deeper than set; BuOrd conceded deep running on 1 August 1942. The same article gives two dates for the cure (August 1942 "resolved" versus the depth sensor moved to midbody in late 1943), so the sources conflict. [Mark 14 torpedo, Wikipedia (lead only, flag W), n.d., https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_14_torpedo, "Problems"]

Magnetic exploder: prematures reported in 1943 (Tunny, Pompano, Trigger); CINCPAC ordered it deactivated 24 June 1943, but Southwest Pacific boats kept it until about the end of 1943. Contact exploder: Tinosa's nine duds on 24 July 1943; Kahoolawe drop tests from 31 August 1943 showed most warheads failing at 90 degrees; new pistols went to sea September 1943. [Same, W]

**Mark 15 (destroyer).** The Mark 15 shared the Mark 6 exploder and the deep-running problem with the Mark 14. Wikipedia also says wartime zinc in place of cadmium plating clogged strainers and caused erratic runs. The article gives November 1943 as the point the exploder problems were resolved. [Mark 15 torpedo, W, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_15_torpedo, "Problems"] navweaps lists the Mark 15 as the standard destroyer weapon, about 9,700 built 1940-44, but gives no fault detail. [22.4" Mark 13 page, navweaps, https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WTUS_WWII.php]

**Mark 13 (aircraft).** Early models needed a drop at about 50 ft and 110 knots, which exposed the aircraft. A July 1941 exercise saw one of ten VT-6 torpedoes run hot, straight and normal. A mid-1943 analysis of drops above 150 knots found 36 percent failed to start, 20 percent sank, and only 31 percent ran satisfactorily. [navweaps, same URL, "Mark 13"] Fixes: nose drag ring and tail shroud ring (shroud ring tested mid-summer 1944), later limits of 2,400 ft and 410 knots. It had no magnetic exploder at the start of the war. [navweaps, same URL; Mark 13 torpedo, W, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_13_torpedo, "Wartime development"] No source read gave a source giving a 1942 failure rate or a Midway-specific figure.

## US destroyer doctrine

The 1934 War Instructions stayed in force into the 1942 Solomons fighting: destroyers attacked at once with guns and kept torpedoes for capital ships. Searchlight range covered US launch positions but not those of Japanese Type 93 ships. [Mark 15 torpedo, W, "Doctrine"] The claim that US cruisers carried no tubes is unverified; no fetched page stated it.

At Vella Gulf (6-7 Aug 1943) Moosbrugger's division was freed from the rule of staying close to cruisers, fired 24 torpedoes in 63 seconds from the shadow of Kolombangara, and held gunfire until hits landed. Three Japanese destroyers sank, one hit by a dud escaped, no US ship was hit. [Battle of Vella Gulf, W, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vella_Gulf, "Battle"] At Cape St. George (25 Nov 1943) Burke's column used radar to fire at about 5,500 yards; three Japanese destroyers were lost and no US ship. [Battle of Cape St. George, W, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cape_St._George, "Battle"]

## Japanese torpedoes

**Type 93.** Range about 40,000 m at 36-38 knots, 32,000 m at 40-42, 20,000 m at 48-50; 490 kg warhead; kerosene-oxygen. [navweaps Japanese torpedoes, https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WTJAP_WWII.php] Oxygen gave far less exhaust gas and so a weak bubble trail; the design served a plan of night attacks by light forces, and firing from about 20 km surprised Allied officers, who often suspected submarines. [Type 93 torpedo, W, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_93_torpedo] At Tassafaronga the drum-carrying destroyers left reloads ashore, so each had eight tube torpedoes only. [Battle of Tassafaronga, W, "Background"]

Premature detonations: about a third of those fired at Java Sea exploded early or on a wake; a Japanese inquiry blamed crews setting fuzes too sensitive. [navweaps, same URL] Hazard aboard: the Shimose warhead was shock-sensitive; Mikuma at Midway, Furutaka at Cape Esperance, and others were lost to their own torpedo explosions, while Chikuma jettisoned hers before bombs hit at Santa Cruz. [Type 93 torpedo, W]

**Type 91 (aircraft).** About 2,000 m at 41-43 knots, charge 150 kg (Mod 1) rising to 240 kg (Mod 3, 1942). [navweaps Japanese torpedoes] **Type 95 (submarine).** 405 kg charge, about 12,000 m at 45-47 knots; the Type 96 variant used 36 percent oxygen to cut starting problems. [navweaps, same] Whether Type 95 is the weapon I-19 used at the Wasp is as stated by combinedfleet.

## Episodes

- **Savo Island (9 Aug 1942):** Japanese cruisers fired at about 01:38-01:44; Chokai put two torpedoes into Vincennes, Tenryu two into Quincy, Chicago was hit once with a second dud. US destroyer torpedo attacks were ineffective (Patterson's order unheard; Bagley fired at the departing column). Whether Canberra was hit by Japanese or Bagley torpedoes is contested between sources. [Battle of Savo Island, W, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Savo_Island, "Battle"]
- **Wasp (15 Sep 1942):** I-19 fired six; two or three hit Wasp (the BuShips report treated the third blast as secondary), one North Carolina, one O'Brien. [USS Wasp, W, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wasp_(CV-7), "Loss"; I-19, combinedfleet, https://www.combinedfleet.com/I-19.htm] Lansdowne's first two Mark 15s did not explode; with magnetic exploders disabled and depth set to 10 ft the last three detonated. [USS Wasp, W]
- **Hornet (26-27 Oct 1942):** nine US torpedoes "mostly failed to explode", the type unnamed there; Japanese Long Lances then finished her. [USS Hornet (CV-8), W, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Hornet_(CV-8), "Santa Cruz"]
- **Tassafaronga (30 Nov 1942):** about 44 Type 93s were fired, 6 hit (a cruiser sunk, three damaged). US destroyers fired 20 Mark 15s (one source says 24) and none hit. [Battle of Tassafaronga, W, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tassafaronga, "Action"]

## What a rule would need to model

- Range, speed and wake visibility as separate torpedo traits, with the Japanese long-range option as a trade of speed for reach.
- Reload availability (tube count versus stowed reloads) and whether reloads were embarked.
- Per-side, per-period reliability: running depth, exploder type and doctrine in force, changing by date.
- Whether magnetic or contact exploders are fitted and set, and the failure modes of each.
- Launch envelope for aircraft torpedoes (speed, height) and its effect on failure.
- Chance of a launching ship's own torpedoes detonating when it is hit.
- Detection and attribution: the target often cannot tell torpedoes from submarines.
- Doctrine as a constraint on whether destroyers are allowed to fire early.
