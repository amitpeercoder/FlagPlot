Status: research report for the architect, read on 2026-11-12. Nothing here is settled. The rows are in `data/oob`: `engagement_events.csv` ids 297 to 326, `engagement_positions.csv` ids 435 to 446, `engagement_outcomes.csv` ids 910 to 979, and 24 new gaps.

## 1. The Japanese search of 24 August, from the signal log

**The carrier force commander's order of 23 August, 1730** (rows 297 to 300):

| Group | Aircraft | Distance | Spacing | By |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First | 11 | 300 nm (older Type 94 floatplanes, if they had to be used, 250 nm) | 7 degrees | As the vanguard commander arranges |
| Second | 12 | 250 nm | 10 degrees | Zuikaku, on base bearing A less 90 degrees |
| Third | 7 | 250 nm | 10 degrees | Ryujo |
| Fourth | not given | | | The vanguard commander, over water searched before sunrise. Cancelled at 0005 on the 24th |

An amendment at about 0200 on the 24th set A to 150 degrees and the launch to 0500, and let the second group shorten its left half to 200 nm.

**The vanguard's own order of 23 August, 2040** (rows 301, 302): eleven Type 0 floatplanes under Kumano's air officer, launching 0330. Lines 1 to 3 Chikuma, lines 4, 6 and 8 Tone, lines 5 and 7 Kumano, lines 9 and 10 Suzuya. That names ten lines for eleven aircraft; the eleventh was not made out. A further group of three Type 94 floatplanes and Hiei's Type 0, under Kirishima's air officer, at 0400, to 150 nm (Hiei's to 200), 40 degrees apart.

**What followed, from the same log:** at 1028 the morning search had seen no enemy. Two aircraft of the division launched at 0920, and Kumano's two returned at 1500 having seen nothing. A Chikuma signal gives "approximate position of the enemy large force" as a grid code at 1325. An evening signal passes the aircrews' report: one new-type carrier, one battleship and several cruisers and destroyers, 141 degrees and 18 nm from an island group, course 110, speed 25.

**What this changes.**
- The ordered distance was 300 nm for the cruiser floatplanes and 250 nm for carrier aircraft. That replaces the figure borrowed from Midway.
- Thirty aircraft were ordered out at dawn, not seven. The "seven floatplanes at 1100" of the English compilation does not appear in this log; the log may simply not carry it.
- Tone was assigned three lines but was detached with Ryujo at 0200. Whether her lines were flown is not known.
- The log's clock is not stated. It fits Tokyo time: Ryujo's attack is 1350 in Japanese quotes and 1620 on the US clock.

**Still missing:** the key to the grid codes, so no Japanese position has a latitude and longitude except Ryujo's sinking (6°10'S 160°50'E, the 8th Cruiser Division's diary as Japanese Wikipedia quotes it); the text of the sighting reports; when the carriers received them.

## 2. Japanese losses at Eastern Solomons

From the official history as Japanese Wikipedia quotes it (rows 958 to 976, all low confidence because the book was not read):

| | Launched | Missing | Forced landings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First wave | 37 (27 dive bombers, 10 fighters; Shokaku 22, Zuikaku 15) | 20 (17 and 3) | 4 |
| Second wave | 36 (27 and 9; Shokaku 12, Zuikaku 24) | 4 dive bombers | 1 |
| Ryujo's strike on Henderson Field | 21 in two launches | 5 | 2 |

Total for the battle: 59 carrier aircraft (30 fighters, 23 dive bombers, 6 attack planes), Ryujo's included, and 3 floatplanes: **62**. The two carriers had 100 aircraft left on 25 August. No Japanese source found supports the totals of about 40 or 75 already in the data. **Recommendation: 62, at low confidence until the official history itself is read.**

Japanese claims recorded: a carrier and a battleship set on fire and a second carrier burning; "ten or more" aircraft at Guadalcanal.

## 3. Japanese daily losses over Guadalcanal

No Japanese record was read. What was found is secondary, mostly Wikipedia in Japanese and English naming the books it cites, plus one researched article and one publisher's sample page (rows 910 to 957):

| Date | Japanese sent | Japanese lost | Japanese claimed | US claimed (already in the data) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 21 Aug | 26 bombers, 13 fighters | 0 | 8 fighters | 1 |
| 26 Aug | 16 or 17 bombers, 9 fighters | 1 bomber ditched; 1 or 2 fighters | | about 13 |
| 2 Sep | 18 bombers | 0 bombers | | 7 |
| 11 Sep | 26 bombers, 15 fighters | 1 bomber, 1 fighter | | 6 or 7 |
| 12 Sep | 25 or 26 bombers, 15 fighters | 3, 4 or 6 bombers; 1 fighter | 11 to 13 in the air, 20 on the ground | 15 or 8 |
| 13 Sep | 9 fighters on reconnaissance; 27 bombers and 12 fighters | 4 fighters; 2 bombers | | 11 |
| 14 Sep | 19 floatplanes and 2 float fighters | 3 or 8 | 5 in the air | about 10 |

On these days US claims run two to three times the Japanese losses, which agrees with the first pass. Japanese claims now have three days (21 August: 8 fighters claimed, against US rows of two fighters crashed and one dive bomber lost that day, cause not split; 12 September; 14 September). The 12 September claim of 20 aircraft on the ground is the first ground claim found.

These rows do not replace the 49 rows from the unsourced chronology; they sit beside them. Where a day now has a figure with a named book behind it, the chronology's row for that day could be dropped.
