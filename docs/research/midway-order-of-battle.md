# Midway, 3 to 7 June 1942: order-of-battle facts

Read on 2026-11-20 for a future small scenario of about four days. This pass gathered facts only: rows in `data/oob`, under the rules of `data/oob/README.md` and `docs/research/oob/database-design.md`. It derives no rating, writes no scenario, changes no derivation list or map, and settles no dispute. The rows already on file for the battle (the strike launches, searches, ditchings, launch decisions and losses of earlier passes, ids such as 410 to 420, 1133 to 1186, 1252 to 1258, 1391 to 1420, 2139 to 2173, 2219 to 2239, 2287 to 2304, 2336 to 2340 and 3943 to 3956) were read first and not repeated. The engagement `midway` (id 1004) was already in `engagements.csv` with the dates 4 to 6 June and zone 10 (the American carriers' clock); both were left alone, and the new rows run from 27 May to 7 June.

To keep clear of another research pass working on the same files for the Coral Sea, every new id is at least 5,000 above the highest id of its file on `main` when this pass began.

**Rows on file that changed.** Four rows already on file changed their standing to preferred, with a sentence added to the note and nothing else, because a row of this pass agrees with them and the rule on support then requires it (`aircraft_type_facts.csv` 232, the TBF-1's power; `fits.csv` 47 and 411, the TBF-1's bomb load, and 1324, the SBD-2's bomb); `oob-check` would not pass otherwise, and no derived file changes for them. Two things were done so that no generated file in `data/reference` changes, which this pass may not touch:

- The Navy's characteristics sheet for the TBF-1C and TBF-1 (1 July 1943) gives a range, a maximum range, a speed and an engine that differ from unconfirmed rows on file (231, 290, 292, 301), and row 290 is named by the Eastern Solomons list's search exception with its standing. Marking those four disputed would change that list's generated header. So the three new rows that differ from them (5785, 5789, 5790) are entered as `alternative`, which leaves the old rows as they were; this is not a judgement that the Navy sheet is the weaker source, and the architect should settle it. The other new rows from the sheet join disputes already on file.
- The sheet's row for the TBF-1's torpedo load (one Mark 13) would have joined fit row 410, which the Eastern Solomons list reads, and so changed that list's companion file of sources. It was left out (it was to be `fits.csv` 6814) and is named here for the architect to add when the list is next regenerated.

**How the rows were checked.** Eight researchers wrote the rows into separate staging files, each fetching every page through one helper that saved a text copy and recorded its address. Every one of the 2,119 fact, fit, forces and engagement rows written was then checked by script against the saved text it cites: its quotation (or each piece of it, where two pieces from one page are joined by "...") was found in the text after collapsing white space, and the address in its locator was the one the text was saved from. 2,090 rows passed that way. 29 rows were read from page images (Bates's Table 2 of the land-based aircraft, eleven rows; the TBF-1C and TBF-1 characteristics sheet, eighteen rows, one of which was then left out): the lead read both images and checked each of the figures against them. A second script looked for rows whose figure was not in the quotation; it found 72, of which most are a figure in words, a conversion or a sum shown in the note; eight strike rows had a count that came from elsewhere, and those were corrected (the count emptied with a pointer to the row that gives it, or a second quoted piece added). What was not checked: whether each quotation supports the meaning given to it in every case beyond those 72, and the notes, which were read only where a check flagged a row.

## Disputes a scenario builder has to settle

1. **The clocks.** Four clocks run through the sources and every row names its own. The Nagumo report and Japanese Monograph 93 keep Tokyo time and date the American 4 June as 5 June. Bates, CINCPAC, Ramsey and the NAS Midway diary keep zone +12 (Midway). The carriers' and task forces' reports keep zone +10, which is the engagement's reference zone. The ONI narrative, Rentz/Heinl, the War Damage Report and the Yorktown report do not say. The cross-checks found agree (Tokyo 1031 is 1331 in Bates; Tokyo 0758 is 1258 zone +10), but no time on file was converted. Order #161, cancelling the occupation, is 2355 Tokyo on 5 June and 0255 zone +12 in Bates; Yorktown sank at 0701 zone +10 or 0501 zone +12.
2. **The Japanese carriers' air groups.** No Japanese record of aircraft by carrier and type was reachable (the Nagumo tables are missing from the transcription). Bates gives the four carriers 234 aircraft, 90 fighters, 72 dive bombers and 72 torpedo planes, citing an ONI review of 1947. By carrier, Navypedia (A6M, D3A, B5N) and Wikipedia disagree: Akagi 21/21/21 or 24/18/18, Kaga 21/27/18 or 27/20/27, Hiryu 21/21/21 or 18/18/18; Soryu only Wikipedia, 18/16/18 and two D4Y1-C. The Zeros of the 6th Air Group carried for Midway (three each in Soryu and Hiryu in Wikipedia, six in all) are not in a primary source read.
3. **The times and sizes of the strikes.** The Midway strike: 107 aircraft by carrier or 108 in the narrative, attack times 0334 to 0343 by unit against a window of 0345 to 0410 in the same report. Hiryu's first strike had 6 fighters (narrative) or 5 (Carrier Division 2's report); her second left at 1031, 1020, 1030 or 0906 Tokyo by different passages, and 1331 in Bates. The first PBY report of the carriers on 4 June: 0530 (Bates), 0545 and 0552 (Ramsey), 0537 and 0552 (Rentz). The B-17s on 4 June: 16 (Ramsey) against 15 (row 2223 on file). The Marine attack on Mikuma and Mogami on 5 June: 0805 (Bates) or 1220 (Ramsey). The TF 16 and TF 17 meeting of 2 June: 1530, 1600, 1630 or 1730.
4. **Midway's aircraft and losses.** Bates's Table 2 gives 121 aircraft at Midway at 2400 on 3 June (14 PBY-5, 16 PBY-5A, 17 B-17, 4 B-26, 6 TBF-1, 19 SBD, 17 SB2U-3 of which five out of commission on 31 May, 21 F2A-3, 7 F4F-3). VMF-221 operational 26 (Rentz) or 27 (CINCPAC, Ramsey); VMSB-241 16 SBD-2 and 11 SB2U-3 (Rentz, Bates) or 13 and 12 engaged (Ramsey); MAG-22's dive bombers 34 (Craven and Cate) or 36 (Bates). Lost: 132 (Bates), 148 (CINCPAC as Bates quotes it) or about 150 (CINCPAC); VMSB-241 7 SBD (Ramsey) or 8 (CINCPAC).
5. **The landing force and the Yorktown's end.** The Japanese landing force: 1,500 naval landing troops and 1,000 Army (Heinl), 2,800 and 3,000 (Japanese Monograph 93's plan, which elsewhere gives 2,000 and 3,000), or about 5,000 (Wikipedia); 13 transports named by Bates, 12 by Wikipedia (with Kiyosumi Maru and without Bates's Seicho), 16 in the monograph's table. The landing date: 7 June Tokyo-dated (the monograph) and "N-day, June 6th" (Bates), probably the same day. Yorktown: listed 23 degrees (War Damage Report) or 26 (her report, DANFS) when abandoned; one or two torpedoes on 6 June; sinking position 30 36 N 176 34 W (CINCPAC) or 30 46 N 167 24 W (her report: a probable transposition of 176 24 W, Bates's figure).

Others, smaller: Zuiho in the Main Force's Aleutians Support Group (Bates) or the Occupation Force (the monograph); Kongo and Hiei first section (Bates) or second (ONI); Bates lists Shinonome, Sagiri and Natsushio, which Nishida has sunk in December 1941 and February 1942; I-164 (Bates) or I-162 (Wikipedia); Mikuma's sinking at about 1300 (CINCPAC), after dark (Bates) or 1930 (combinedfleet); Mikuma hit two or three times in the 0950 attack (Bates) or near-missed (combinedfleet); the collision at 0342 (Bates) or 0200 (Mogami's captain); Hammann hit once or twice; the 20 mm guns of the 3d Defense Battalion's detachment, 18 (Heinl) or 12 (Bates); Midway's aviation fuel tanks, 22 of 2,500 gallons (*Building the Navy's Bases*) or 31 (the diary), and the fuel lost in the explosion of 22 May, printed "375,00 gallons"; Hornet's 20 mm guns on 4 June, 24 (Navypedia) or 30 (Wikipedia); Akagi's and Kaga's speed, endurance, complement and armour after their rebuilds; Conyngham's launch year; and many aircraft figures of the F2A-3, SB2U-3 and B-26, whose sources differ on nearly every weight and range.

## Gaps

- **The Japanese side from Japanese records.** The Nagumo report's tables of organization, aircraft and results are not in its transcription; the Mobile Force's positions in it are grid codes with no key. So the carriers' aircraft by type, the per-carrier launch times and the Mobile Force's track in latitude and longitude rest on Bates and on compilations. Floatplanes of Mikuma, Mogami and Kamikawa Maru: not found.
- **combinedfleet.com did not answer** (all but the Mikuma and Mogami records timed out, and the Wayback Machine refused connections that day). The transports' tonnage and types, the carriers' and I-168's tabular records, and the dated fits of 1942 are missing for that reason.
- **US squadrons on hand.** Enterprise's and Hornet's squadrons by number on hand on 4 June are not in the reports read; only group totals and the numbers launched. The patrol and B-17 squadrons at Midway by squadron: not found. Whether the carriers' dive bombers were SBD-2 or SBD-3 is not stated (entered as SBD-3, low and inferred).
- **The men per transport, and the Ikki and construction units' sub-units and sizes.** Not found.
- **Midway's stocks** of aviation fuel, food and ammunition, the garrison's total with the air and Navy men, and the guns of the 5-inch and 7-inch batteries by battery.
- **Positions:** Hammann's sinking position; I-168's track and firing time; the Main Force and Second Fleet beyond a few points.
- **Classes:** the Kuma class has no facts; no attribute holds a carrier's lifts or hangar decks (Navypedia's lift sizes are in `gaps.csv`); Hosho's B4Y has no type key; whether Ise, Hyuga or Fuso had radar in June 1942.
- **Aircraft:** no F2A-3 fuel or endurance, no B-26 fuel or radius, no D4Y1-C figures for the model flown; the National Museum of the US Air Force's fact sheets refused every request.
- Submarines are listed as forces only, with no class or facts, apart from I-168's and Nautilus's actions as events. The Aleutians are one event row for each side.

## What to look for next, and where

- The Nagumo report's original tables (the ONI Review of May 1947 that Bates cites, or a scan of OPNAV P-23-100) for the air groups by carrier and type and the losses by cause.
- combinedfleet.com tabular records of the carriers, transports and I-168, on a day the site answers.
- Bates's track diagrams (page images of the downloaded scan) for the Mobile Force's and the Main Force's tracks.
- The squadron reports on midway1942.com not read here (VF-6, VB-6, VT-6, the Hornet squadrons, VMF-221, VMSB-241) for aircraft on hand.
- Parshall and Tully, *Shattered Sword*, and Lundstrom, *The First Team*, are the books that would settle most of the disputes above; not in hand.

## The map

The present map (`data/map/south_pacific.csv`) covers 1 S to 24 S and 145 E to 172 E. It holds no part of this battle: Midway is at about 28 13 N 177 22 W, north of the equator and east of the date line.

Every position in these rows lies from 26 15 N (the Minesweeping Group) to 37 02 N (the Mobile Force on 3 June), and from 164 20 E (the Transport Group on 6 June) eastward across the date line to 172 45 W (TF 16 and TF 17 meeting on 2 June) or 167 24 W if the disputed Yorktown sinking position is kept. Point Luck is at 32 N 173 W, Kure Atoll about 60 miles west-north-west of Midway.

A map for the four days therefore has to cover at least **25 N to 38 N and 163 E to 166 W** (a span of 31 degrees of longitude that crosses the 180th meridian, so the map's longitudes must run on past 180 without a break, for example as 163 to 194 degrees east). That box also holds Kure, Laysan, Lisianski and French Frigate Shoals (23 52 N 166 17 W, which needs the southern edge at 23 N). To start the Occupation Force from where it sailed, the map would have to reach Saipan (15 N 145 E) and Guam (13 N 145 E); to show Wake (19 N 166 E) it needs 19 N; Pearl Harbor (21 N 158 W) and Hashirajima in the Inland Sea are far outside any sensible box and are better entered as off-map. At 40-mile hexes the core box is about 20 hexes north to south and 40 east to west.
