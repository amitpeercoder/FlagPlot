# Larger scenarios

**Nov 28, 2026: the direction has moved on.** The designer chose one game of 1942 on one wide map, from Papua to Midway (`one-map-1942.md`); Guadalcanal to the end of November, proposed below, is now the last part of that note's third stage. Of the needs in the table below, forces that arrive by date (decision 0195), supply from off the map (0196), replacement men (0197), submarines against warships and a second map (0199) have since been built. The rest of this note stands as it was written.

A design note by the architect, begun on Nov 20, 2026 at the designer's asking ("start researching and looking into larger scenarios. I'm leaving that to you"). It is not a plan and nothing here is built. It says what a larger scenario would need, which candidates there are, and what the architect proposes to do first. Facts were gathered from the code and the records by a team and the ones the proposals rest on were checked by the architect; a figure marked "measured" was measured on Nov 20, 2026.

## Where the game is

- **The largest scenario is the 39 days:** Guadalcanal, 7 August to 14 September 1942. 133 ships, 30 task forces, 34 air groups, 25 land units, 7 bases.
- **The map** is 1,600 hexes of 40 nautical miles, from 1 S to 24 S and 145 E to 172 E. Decision 0017 says its projection is for this slice only, and that the whole Pacific needs a scheme of its own.
- **Speed is not the limit.** A day of the 39 days resolves in well under a millisecond without staffs at work (measured), and 180 days of the soak scenario in about a quarter of a second (decision 0100). A dry run at full size takes about 19 seconds (measured), and that grows with days and worlds.
- **What grows with a campaign's length** is the event log: a side's whole history is read again each day in several places (`docs/open-work.md`), and a late world's copy is mostly its log. At 39 days this does not matter. At 120 days it would have to be mended first.
- **Order-of-battle facts** are on file for 1942 from May to November: the Coral Sea, Midway, Savo Island, the Eastern Solomons, Cape Esperance, Santa Cruz, the two night actions of November, Tassafaronga, and the air campaign of August and September. The depth is in August and September. No book was in hand; the largest single source is low grade.

## What a larger scenario needs that the game lacks

Longer first, then wider. Each is a rule or a body of data, and each would be planned by its own decision record.

| Need | Today | Why it matters as a scenario grows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ships and land units that arrive by date** | Not built. A scenario brings in aircraft and pilots by date and nothing else; every ship and unit is on the map on day 1 | In 39 days it is hidden by starting forces at the edge. Beyond that a campaign is its reinforcements |
| **Supply from outside the map** | Not built. A base has a stock and runs carry it between bases; nothing refills a rear base | A long game drains the rear bases to nothing |
| **The history read once, not every day** | Known cost, not mended | Days of play slow as the game lengthens |
| **Land unit replacements** | Deferred (decision 0102) | Men lost are never made good |
| **Submarines** | Not built; planned for before the campaign runs past 14 September | They sank and crippled carriers in exactly these months |
| **Weather** | Not built; deferred until a measure misses | It decided who found whom in several of these battles |
| **Terrain in the fight ashore** | Not read | Long land campaigns turn on it |
| **Air group experience** | Waits on research (decision 0064) | Attrition of trained aircrew is the story of late 1942 |
| **A wider map** | The projection is for the slice | Midway, Truk and Pearl Harbor are off it |
| **Rearming and wear from steaming** | Partly built | A fleet at sea for months |

Ship repair with yards off the map, saved games, command levels and the staff are built and would carry over.

## The candidates

1. **Guadalcanal to the end of November 1942** (about 115 days, the same map). It adds Cape Esperance, Santa Cruz, the November night actions and Tassafaronga, each of which has facts on file. It needs arrivals of ships and units by date, supply from off the map, the history mended, and submarines. Every one of those is also needed by anything larger.
2. **The South Pacific, May to November 1942** (about 200 days, the same map). The Coral Sea joined to Guadalcanal, with Port Moresby and the Papuan fight. It needs all of the above and a land campaign in New Guinea, for which there are no facts.
3. **Midway.** A different sea: a second map and its scheme, and data for forces not yet read. A short battle, so none of the long-campaign rules. It would be a third small scenario for play, not a larger one.
4. **The Pacific, 1942.** The full build of the pillars' last step. It needs the economy, a map scheme for the ocean and a complete order of battle. Not a next step.

## What the architect proposes

**The next larger scenario is the first candidate: Guadalcanal to the end of November.** The map exists, most of the battles have facts, and it forces exactly the four rules that everything larger needs. A wider map without those rules would be a bigger board with the same game on it; `docs/pillars.md` warns of that.

It is reached in an order in which each step is useful by itself:

1. **Forces that arrive by date** (ships, task forces and land units), as a scenario's data. The smallest of the four and the one the small scenarios would also use: a carrier that comes late is the honest way to vary a start.
2. **A rear base fed from off the map** on a schedule, as `docs/design/economy.md` sketches for the map's edge.
3. **The history read once.** No rule changes; a game plays the same.
4. **Submarines**, which decision 0102 already places here.
5. **Then the scenario,** in the three stages the Coral Sea plan uses: the data and its research (the forces from 15 September to 30 November, by date of arrival), the scenario, and what its runs show. It is a scenario of record first; a version for play would follow under decision 0187.

Weather, terrain and air group experience are not put before it. Each waits for what the long scenario's runs show is missing, as decision 0102 ruled.

## What this does not settle

- **When.** Step 3 of the build has stage 5, the proof, still to come, and the designer declined once to pull later work forward. Steps 1 and 3 above are small and serve the scenarios for play now; the rest is a stage of its own and is for the designer to place.
- **How long a person will play.** The 39 days is already hours at the table. A 115-day game needs the staff to carry more of the routine and the table to compress quiet days. That is the onboarding and pacing question (`docs/design/onboarding.md`), not a rule.
- **Agents at that length.** No time or cost of an agent playing the 39 days is on record. It is measured before a longer game is offered to one.
