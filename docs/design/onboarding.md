# Onboarding

A design note by the architect, begun on Nov 20, 2026 at the designer's asking ("maybe start thinking about the onboarding experience"). It is first thoughts for discussion. Nothing here is built or planned.

## The problem

The game's premise is its teaching problem. A player commands by orders and a staff carries them out. So a newcomer's first question is not which button to press. It is: what am I supposed to decide, and what happens if I decide nothing? A manual does not answer that, and the designer has ruled that the table carries no captions explaining mechanics.

What a newcomer meets today: a screen that asks for a scenario, a side and an opponent, then a chart with forces on it, a situation sheet and an order sheet. Nothing says what a day is, what a contact is, or that the staff will act on an order without being told how.

## What onboarding should leave a player knowing

In this order, since each rests on the one before:

1. **You are a commander with a superior and a staff.** You are told what is wanted; you say what you want done; the staff does it and tells you why.
2. **Time moves in days of three parts, and you choose when it moves.**
3. **You know your own forces exactly and the enemy only by reports,** which can be old or wrong.
4. **An order stands until you change it.** You do not steer ships.
5. **The staff's reasons are there to be read,** and a dry run shows what a plan is likely to do before you commit to it.

## Three ideas

### Teach through the role

The first thing on the table is a directive from above and the staff's first proposal, which the player approves, changes or sends back. Within a minute he has done the central act of the game and seen its result: forces moving on an order he gave in a sentence. This uses what the game has (directives, proposals, approval) and adds no caption.

### A ladder of scenarios

Each rung adds one thing and is short enough to finish in a sitting:

| Rung | Scenario | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | One day, one carrier force each (the one-day Eastern Solomons, cut down) | A day, a search, a contact, a strike |
| 2 | Five days, the Eastern Solomons | A convoy, a base, forces that tire |
| 3 | Five days, the Coral Sea | A landing to stop or carry out; when to turn back |
| 4 | The 39 days | A campaign: supply, the fight ashore, weeks of attrition |

Difficulty (decision 0189, planned) fits the ladder: the first rungs start with the gentlest opponent and the staff helping most.

### The staff explains itself

The game already records the staff's reason for every order, in words. Asked "why did the bombers rest?", the staff answers from that record. That teaches the rules from inside the fiction, at the moment the player wants to know, and it is the simulation speaking, not a tutorial. What it needs is the reasons put in a commander's words, since today they are written for the umpire.

## The first five minutes

The one place the role cannot carry it alone: what the chart shows, what a contact is, how time moves. The proposal is a guided first day on rung 1, in the voice of the chief of staff: he presents the chart, the directive and his proposal, and says what he will do when the player gives the word. It is spoken once, can be skipped, and is never shown on a later game.

## What to find out before planning any of it

- **Watch a newcomer.** One or two people who have not seen the game, on the table as it is, saying what they think is happening. Where they stall is the list of what to build.
- **Read what the agents said.** Each agent that played wrote what confused it. A model reading the briefing cold is a fair stand-in for a newcomer reading it.
- **Decide what a first game is for:** to finish a small battle in twenty minutes, or to understand command by orders. The ladder assumes the second.

## Questions for the designer

1. Is the chief of staff a voice in the game (he presents, proposes and explains), or does the table stay a set of sheets?
2. Should rung 1 be a scenario made for teaching, with forces cut down, or the real one-day battle with the staff doing more?
3. Is a newcomer to be watched before anything is built?
